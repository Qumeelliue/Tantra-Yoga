# Definition

In the movement of the social cycle, **one class (varṇa) is always dominant**.
Ánanda Sútram 5-1: *"Varṇapradhānatā cakradhārāyām."*

# Explanation

History rotates through four ages, each defined by the dominant collective
mentality:

| Age | Dominant class | Character |
|-----|----------------|-----------|
| **Shūdra Age** | shūdra | manual labourers; no well-knit social order |
| **Kṣatriya Age** | kṣatriya | the strong and brave — clan leaders, warriors |
| **Vipra Age** | vipra | intellectuals |
| **Vaeshya Age** | vaeshya | capitalists |

During the **Vaeshya Age**, exploitation reduces warriors and intellectuals to the
level of manual labourers, provoking a **shūdra revolution**. Since the shūdras
have neither a well-knit order nor sufficient intellect to govern, power passes to
the **brave leaders** of that revolution — beginning a *second* Kṣatriya Age. Thus
Shūdra → Kṣatriya → Vipra → Vaeshya recur in succession, each cycle closing with
revolution and the next beginning anew.

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — the full narrative

The 1959 chapter *The Place of Sadvipras in the Samája Cakra* (II-10) narrates the
same cycle in full, adding the ages' genesis and the change-terminology:

- **Shūdra Age → Kṣatriya Age.** Primitive humanity was individualistic (no
  society, no family); the strong ruled by muscle — "though life was brute, it was
  not brutal" (no intellectual exploitation, no developed acquisitive sense). If
  *shūdra* = one who lives by manual work, this was the Shūdra Age. Physical
  leadership then passed hereditarily (father→son, mother→daughter) and formed a
  well-knit class — the **kṣatriyas**; the age of rule by physical valour was the
  Kṣatriya Age.
- **Vipra Age.** Intellectual demand grew; heroes had to sit at the feet of
  physically-common men to learn arms, strategy, medicine, administration; real
  power passed to the intellectuals, who "performed no labour themselves" and were
  "parasites." To keep power among the few they imposed superstitions, rituals,
  faiths, and irrational ideas (the caste system is the example) — the Middle Ages.
- **Vaeshya Age.** Trade and transport of goods rose in importance; the *vaeshyas*
  gained supremacy. Laissez-faire capitalism concentrates the means of production
  in a few hands; the instinct of acquisition develops; the majority are duped or
  neglected into "exploited slaves of the capitalists."
- **Shūdra revolution.** Capitalism turns the majority into shūdras (manual
  labourers); dejection and dissatisfaction on a large scale make them organize
  against "unnatural impositions"; their leaders — "physically and mentally
  better-equipped," i.e. also kṣatriyas — overthrow the structure by force, and
  "after a period of chaos and catastrophe" the next Kṣatriya Age begins.
- **The change-terminology:** gradual change of ages = **evolution** or *krānti*;
  the transitional period = *yuga saṃkrānti*; one complete cycle = *parikrānti*; a
  reversal by force inspired by a negative theory = *vikrānti* (counter-evolution);
  a swift reversal by brute force = *prativiplava* (counter-revolution) — "just like
  the negative pratisaincara of Brahma Cakra." (These terms are also mapped in
  [Social Change](/prout/social-change.md).)
- **Stagnancy means death.** The world "is going in eternal motion, and such a
  motion is the law of nature and the law of life. Stagnancy means death. Hence no
  power can check the social cycle of evolution" — it can only retard or accelerate
  the transition.

# In *Human Society* (1967) — the revolution's sociology

*Shūdra Revolution and Sadvipra Society* (HS 2/4) details who makes the revolution
and what follows:

- **Shūdra revolution needs vikṣubdha shūdras.** "It is not the work of people who
  have a shūdra mentality to bring about revolution" — the shúdra-minded are
  "playthings of the vaeshyas." Revolution comes from shūdras who are **kṣatriya- or
  vipra-minded** — called the **middle class** (*madhyavitta samāja*) or *vikṣubdha*
  ("disgruntled") — who are "a constant source of uneasiness for the tyrannical
  vaeshyas." An increase in their number "is an early omen of a possible shūdra
  revolution." Obstacles: shūdra-mindedness, national/religious traditions, fatalism,
  the welfare-state idea, Gandhism, and misreadings of *niṣkāma karmavāda* and
  *janmāntaravāda*.
- **The second rotation.** After the shūdra revolution the leaders become "the
  kṣatriyas of the second rotation." The cycle repeats: if the kṣatriyas exploit, the
  sadvipras fight them and establish the Vipra Age of the second rotation; and so on.
  "Nobody can stop the rotation of the social cycle, not even the sadvipras" — they
  stay in the nucleus, "wakefully and vigilantly observing."
- **Thesis → antithesis → synthesis inside the cycle.** "If the post-revolutionary
  Kṣatriya Age is called thesis, the steps taken by the sadvipras against the
  kṣatriyas who descend into exploitation are called antithesis. The post-revolutionary
  Vipra Age which evolves out of this conflict is called synthesis" — which becomes the
  thesis of the next stage. (Cf. the dialectical framing in [Social Change](/prout/social-change.md).)
- **Democracy and the shūdra-minded.** "The capitalists like democracy… because in the
  democratic system they can easily purchase the shūdra-minded shūdras who constitute
  the majority." "If sadvipras alone had the right to vote, there would be no
  difference between the real world and the heaven people imagine." Democratic progress
  is "tortoise-like"; democratic socialism is "theoretically not bad," but the high
  moral standard it needs makes it "impossible… in the real world."

# Notes — fidelity

- The four varṇas here are **collective mentalities / phases of social dominance**,
  not the hereditary caste system. Preserve this sociological reading; do not
  conflate with birth-caste.
- II-10's "viplava" sense differs from the sūtra scheme: here *viplava* is not named
  separately (evolution = krānti; revolution = the shūdra revolution), while the
  *vikrānti*/*prativiplava* reversals are defined exactly as in [Social Change](/prout/social-change.md).
  Keep both terminologies source-specific.
- PiaN 6/2 frames the same rotation in dialectical terms (thesis → antithesis →
  synthesis, within relativity) — see the dialectical framing in
  [Social Change](/prout/social-change.md); HS 2/4 uses the same triad *inside* the
  second rotation.
- **"Vikṣubdha shūdras" / "middle class"** is defined by mentality (kṣatriya-/vipra-
  minded shūdras), NOT by income — preserve this non-economic definition.
