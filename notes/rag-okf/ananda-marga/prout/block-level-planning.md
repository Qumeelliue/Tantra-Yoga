# Definition

**Block-level planning** is PROUT's **basic level of planning** in the decentralized
economy — economic planning "of ascending order, starting at the block level, and
including all the levels of a socio-economic unit" (block → district → state →
national → global). There should be a block-level planning board in every block, and
a provision for block-level planning in the constitution. (1981, PiaN 12/9.)

# Explanation

- **Four factors of planning** (PiaN 12/9):
  1. **Cost of production** — must be systematically determined (agriculture must be
     reorganized "on the same basis as industry," i.e. through the cooperative
     system) and kept at the minimum; no production unit may exceed its market value.
  2. **Productivity** — the economy must have its own innate power to produce more
     and more; money should be "kept rolling rather than hoarded"; maximum production
     for collective needs, based on consumption, with full employment.
  3. **Purchasing capacity** — the genuine measure of economic advancement; per-capita
     income is "a deceptive and defective measure… popularized by capitalist
     economists to fool people." Guaranteed by minimum requirements, stable prices,
     periodic wage increases and increasing collective wealth. "There will be no limit
     to purchasing capacity."
  4. **Collective necessity** — existing collective needs and future requirements of
     the unit; most importance to the minimum requirements, without neglecting
     meritorious people and those with special needs.
- **Block demarcation must be scientific, not political.** Blocks are to be
  reorganised by physical features (river valleys, climate, topography, soil, flora/
  fauna), socio-economic requirements and physico-psychic aspirations. "Each block
  should be made economically sound so that the entire socio-economic unit will be
  self-sufficient."
- **Intra- and inter-block planning.** *Intra-block* planning is for a single block's
  all-round growth; *inter-block* planning coordinates several adjoining blocks on
  problems that traverse boundaries (flood control, river-valley projects,
  communication, higher education, afforestation, key industries, water supply, power,
  organised markets).
- **Short-term and long-term plans** at every level: short-term ≤ **six months**,
  long-term ≤ **three years**, complementary to each other.
- **Immediate goals:** guarantee minimum requirements, eliminate unemployment,
  increase purchasing capacity, make socio-economic units self-sufficient. "The only
  way to solve unemployment… is by developing block-level industries."
- **Floating population** is to be settled or returned to its origin at the block
  level.

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** per-capita income is "deceptive and defective"; purchasing
  capacity is the real index; block-level planning is "essential for economic
  decentralization."
- The **≤6-month / ≤3-year** maxima are stated exactly — preserve them.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** a detailed hierarchy of what the
  district/national/global boards do beyond "planning of ascending order"; the
  chapter focuses on block and inter-block levels.
