# Definition

**Economic democracy** is PROUT's demand that **economic power be vested in the
common people** and that the **minimum requirements of life be guaranteed to all**.
PROUT's slogan: **"To end exploitation we demand economic democracy, not political
democracy."** (1986, PiaN 21/5 = PE 24.)

# Explanation

- **Political democracy is a hoax.** Liberal democracy (USA, GB, France, Canada) and
  socialist democracy (USSR, China, Vietnam, Eastern Europe) are both *political*
  democracies based on economic and political **centralization**. Political democracy
  "has no doubt granted voting rights, but it has snatched away the right of economic
  equality" — producing gross disparity, unemployment, food shortages, poverty.
  Sarkar calls it "a unique system of exploitation," "a great hoax," "a puppet show"
  where power-hungry politicians pull the strings.
- **Defects of the electoral farce:** universal suffrage by age is an unsound
  yardstick; elections are won by party affiliation, money, patronage, and antisocial
  practices; "even bandits and murderers stand for election." The prerequisites of
  successful democracy are **morality, education and socio-economico-political
  consciousness** — which the masses lack, so cunning politicians manipulate them.
- **Economic vs political power are bifurcated.** In economic democracy PROUT
  advocates **political centralization and economic decentralization**: political
  power is vested with the moralists, economic power with the local people. The
  administration's goal is to remove all obstacles to meeting people's economic needs.
- **The four requirements** (II-5/PE-24):
  1. the minimum requirements of the age — food, clothing, housing, education,
     medical treatment — guaranteed to all;
  2. increasing **purchasing capacity** guaranteed to each individual, with local raw
     materials used for local prosperity and industrial centres built where raw
     materials are;
  3. the power of all economic decisions placed in the hands of the local people
     ("economic liberation is the birthright of every individual");
  4. outsiders strictly prevented from interfering in the local economy, stopping the
     outflow of local capital.
- **Universal scope.** Economic democracy serves not only human economic liberation
  but "the universal well-being of all — including plants and animals."

# The prerequisites of democracy (PiaN 6/2)

The 1988 lecture *Dialectical Materialism and Democracy* (PiaN 6/2) grounds the
democracy-critique:

- **Three requirements for democracy's success:** (1) **mass education** — "real
  education means proper, adequate knowledge and the power of understanding… an
  awareness of who I am and what I ought to do," not mere literacy; (2) **morality** —
  "democracy cannot succeed unless 51% of the population rigidly follow principles of
  morality"; (3) **social, economic and political consciousness**.
- **Why political democracy fails:** without these, "government of the people, by the
  people and for the people will only mean government of fools, by fools and for
  fools." Age is "not the yardstick of wisdom and education" (universal suffrage by
  age is unsound — the Licchavii precedent: only the leaders, not the general public,
  had franchise). Democracy "induces sentiments like provincialism, communalism,
  casteism," and "the ghost of immorality lies hidden in today's democratic system."
- **PROUT = progressive socialism.** "The social system that will come into being,
  keeping parallelism and harmony with time, space and person, will be called
  progressive socialism. Our Prout is that very progressive socialism." The minimum
  requirements "will go on increasing day by day" with the age (dhoti-and-sandals →
  cycle/car → eventually a rocket).
- **Even successful democracy is not enough** — "the only solution is an enlightened,
  benevolent dictatorship — that is a morally and spiritually conscious dictatorship"
  (cf. [Sadvipra Boards](/prout/sadvipra-boards.md)). Yet "unless an alternative, better
  and more agreeable theory or system is evolved, we will have to accept democracy in
  preference to other systems."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** political democracy is "a great hoax," "a unique system of
  exploitation"; "the days of political democracy are numbered." Do not soften into a
  reformist endorsement of liberal democracy.
- **Not "socialism as communism":** both liberal and socialist democracies are
  rejected as *political* democracy; economic democracy is distinct from both.
- **PiaN 6/2:** PROUT is "progressive socialism" — Sarkar's own term — and even a
  successful democracy is superseded by "an enlightened, benevolent dictatorship."
  Keep both framings.
- The discourse appears in **two works** (PiaN 21/5 and PE 24) with identical text —
  both cited as sources.
