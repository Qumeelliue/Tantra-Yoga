# Definition

An **ideal constitution** is, in PROUT, a guidebook "in which all such rules,
regulations and principles for the proper conduct of a state are codified" so that
the state renders "all-round services to the people for their rapid progress." Sarkar
analyses common constitutional defects and proposes concrete reforms, culminating in
a **Charter of Rights** for a world constitution. (1986, PiaN 12/11.)

# Explanation

- **The state and its guidebook.** The state is a vital institution with sovereign
  power; "accumulation of power is dangerous if it is not guided by some rules and
  basic principles" — hence the constitution. The first written constitution was
  framed by the **Licchavi Dynasty of Vaeshali** (northern Bihar) over 2500 years ago,
  the first republican democracy.
- **Common constitutional defects** — a constitution "should be fair and just," with
  "least bias… towards any particular ethnic, linguistic or religious group."
  Sarkar's fiscal critiques (India): no check on unbarred capitalist exploitation
  (1947 brought "brown exploitation" after white), no guarantee of increasing
  purchasing capacity, the president cannot check fiscal matters, no provision for
  inter-block planning, no clear concept of balanced economy. Psychological loopholes:
  imposition of a regional language as the national language ("English imperialism has
  been followed by Hindi imperialism"), disparities in the law (Hindu vs Muslim
  Codes), no law against destruction of flora and fauna, unclearly defined
  centre-state relations, unscientific Scheduled Tribe/Caste lists.
- **Thirteen reforms** (PiaN 12/11), incl.: the president may dissolve a minority
  ministry or parliament; a six-month/two-year emergency limit; the president need not
  act on a lame-duck ministry's advice; the president/prime minister must be of high
  moral character (no divorcee/multiple-spouse); parliament amends the constitution
  only with a 7/8 majority; all living languages equal before the state; equal rights
  before the law; a review board for economic progress; a case may be filed even
  against the president/prime minister; self-determination only by plebiscite under
  central supervision; free primary education; the law and constitution the same for
  the whole country (e.g. no special rights for Kashmir).
- **The Charter of Rights** (for the world constitution) — at least four areas:
  1. complete security for all plants and animals;
  2. each country guarantees purchasing power to all citizens;
  3. four fundamental rights — spiritual practice (Dharma), cultural legacy,
     education, indigenous linguistic expression;
  4. any practice conflicting with **cardinal human values** must be curtailed —
     "cardinal human values must take precedence over all other rights."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** per-capita purchasing-power guarantee is a constitutional must;
  "cardinal human values must take precedence over all other rights"; the Samskrta /
  all-living-languages positions (Samskrta proposed as national language for India;
  otherwise no regional language imposed) are stated.
- The **13 reforms** and **4-point Charter** are enumerated exactly in the source —
  preserve the numbering.
- **Scope / could not verify:** the chapter is India-focused for its examples but the
  Charter is universal; no separate "world constitution" text beyond the four areas.
