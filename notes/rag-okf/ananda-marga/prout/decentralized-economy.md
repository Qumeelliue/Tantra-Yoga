# Definition

**Decentralized economy** is PROUT's principle that the economy must be built from
the bottom up — **production for consumption, not production for profit** — with
all resources and industries of a [socio-economic unit](/prout/socio-economic-groupifications.md)
controlled by the **local people**. PROUT's maxim: **"Production for consumption,
not production for profiteering."**

# Explanation

- **Why decentralization.** Centralized economy — whether capitalist or communist —
  can never end exploitation or solve the problems of remote villages: "Successful
  planning can never be done by sitting in an air conditioned office thousands of
  miles away from the place where planning is to be undertaken." Centralization means
  industry for profit; decentralization means industry for consumption. Both
  capitalism and communism ("state capitalism") are "fruits of the same variety."
- **The five principles** (PiaN 21/4):
  1. **Local control of resources** — all resources of the unit, especially those
     producing the minimum requirements, must be in local hands; "local people" are
     those who have merged their interests with the unit's (not a racial/caste
     definition); outsiders may not interfere, lest a "floating population" cause the
     outflow of local capital.
  2. **Production based on consumption, not profit** — commodities sold in the local
     market, money circulated locally, purchasing capacity continuously increased.
  3. **Production and distribution organized through [cooperatives](/prout/cooperatives.md)** —
     agriculture, industry and trade managed cooperatively, with private ownership
     abolished in stages; "the cooperative system and decentralized economy are
     inseparable."
  4. **Local people employed in local enterprises** — full employment solved locally;
     no surplus or deficit labour.
  5. **Non-locally-produced commodities removed from the local market** — locals
     utilise locally produced goods; local industries survive and thrive.
- **Sector structure:** key industries under the local government on a **"no profit,
  no loss"** basis; medium industries as cooperatives; small/cottage industries in
  private hands producing non-essential (luxury) goods, in adjustment with the
  cooperative sector. Agriculture has **the same status as industry**. Power is a key
  industry supplied at cost; taxation is a levy on production rather than income tax.
- **Trade:** export of raw materials is not supported — only finished goods, only to
  units that cannot produce them, directly through cooperatives and not for profit.
  "Free trade should be encouraged once self-sufficiency is attained."
- **Economic transformation (PiaN 16/7):** decentralization removes regional
  disparity; wealth is distributed almost equally; people need not leave home to
  work. Block-level infrastructure is the feasible unit; key industries are
  centralized only where unavoidable, and decentralization of key industries is to be
  implemented whenever circumstances permit. **Block-level planning** (PiaN 12/9)
  starts planning from the lowest level using local knowledge.

# Notes — fidelity

- **"Production for consumption, not production for profit"** is the defining maxim —
  direct teaching; keep it sharp.
- **Both capitalism and communism** are rejected as centralized — "capitalism and
  communism are fruits of the same variety" (PiaN 16/7). Do not read PROUT as a
  pro-capitalist or pro-communist middle course.
- "Local people" is defined by **merged interests, not ethnicity/birthplace** —
  preserve this sociological definition.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** an explicit quantitative threshold for
  "30–45% agriculture" is present in 21/5 (as a statement about development, not a
  planning rule); treat carefully as Sarkar's framing, not a universal law.
