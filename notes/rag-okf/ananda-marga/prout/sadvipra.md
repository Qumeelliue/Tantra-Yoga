# Definition

**Sadvipras** are those who are *staunch moralists and sincere spiritualists* and
who want to end immorality and exploitation by the application of force. Ánanda
Sútram 5-2: *"Cakrakendre sadviprāh cakraniyantrakāh"* — "Located in the nucleus of
the social cycle, sadvipras control the social cycle."

# Explanation

The sadvipras **do not belong to the periphery** of the
[social cycle](/prout/samaja-cakra.md) (they are not partisans of any one class);
they control society while remaining **firmly established in its nucleus**. The
cycle still rotates — but when the dominant class (warriors in the Kṣatriya Age,
intellectuals in the Vipra Age, or capitalists in the Vaeshya Age) **degenerates
into rapacious exploiters** instead of acting as benevolent administrators, the
sacred duty of the sadvipras is to **protect the righteous and the exploited and
subdue the wicked** — by force if necessary.

By the criteria of 5-15, social control belongs to those who are **spiritually
elevated, intelligent and brave all at the same time** — the sadvipric qualities.

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — the sadvipra's method

The 1959 chapter *The Place of Sadvipras in the Samája Cakra* (II-10) defines the
sadvipra and his duty in detail:

- **Definition by Yama-Niyama.** "Those spiritual revolutionaries who work to
  achieve such progressive changes for human elevation on a well-thought,
  pre-planned basis, whether in the physical, metaphysical or spiritual sphere, by
  adhering to the principles of Yama and Niyama, are sadvipras." Only those who by
  nature adhere to the ten commands in normal and spiritual conduct are sadvipras
  (see [Yama & Niyama](/practice/yama-niyama.md)).
- **The duty — prevent exploitation.** "The function of a sadvipra shall, therefore,
  be to see that the dominating or the ruling classes do not have any scope for
  exploitation." When a class turns exploiter, both the few (exploiters, degenerated
  by excess) and the many (exploited, energy consumed by mundane problems) decline —
  so "it is essential that no one be given any scope to exploit the rest of the
  society."
- **Active participants, not passive witnesses.** "Sadvipras are not inactive
  witnesses. They are active participants to see that no person or class exploits
  the rest." They strike at the source of power of the class tending to become
  exploiter:
  - if the **kṣatriya** class is exploiting → sadvipras may resort to **physical
    force**;
  - if the **vipra** (intellectual) class is dominating → a **revolution in the
    intellectual field**;
  - if the **vaeshya** class is dominating → contest and **win elections** (the
    vaeshya rules by democracy, which enables undue accumulation).

# In *Human Society* (1967) — the shūdra revolution and the sadvipra society

The 1967 chapter *Shūdra Revolution and Sadvipra Society* (HS 2/4) grounds the
sadvipra in the mechanics of revolution:

- **Sadvipra = "a person who is a moralist and a spiritualist and who fights against
  immorality."** The pioneers of the shūdra revolution must "learn to be disciplined,
  take proper revolutionary training, build their character, be moralists; in a word,
  they will have to become what I call sadvipras."
- **Vikṣubdha shūdras — the revolutionary middle class.** Shūdra revolution depends on
  shūdras who, though economically shūdras, are **kṣatriya- or vipra-minded**
  ("middle class," *madhyavitta samāja*; alternatively *vikṣubdha*, "disgruntled").
  Shúdra-minded shūdras "do not and cannot bring about a shūdra revolution." The
  capitalists "are afraid of the labour leaders, the vikṣubdha shūdras." Increasing
  their number is an early omen of revolution; sadvipras must ensure their number does
  not decrease through unemployment or birth control.
- **The sadvipra is impartial but stern:** "A sadvipra will not launch a movement
  against honest people, even if he or she does not like them. But a sadvipra will
  definitely take action against dishonest people, even if he or she likes them."
- **Most sadvipras come from the middle class** (vipra-/kṣatriya-minded shūdras); rich
  moralists may be sadvipras but must "come down to the level of the middle class" —
  they cannot keep wealth acquired by sinful means while following Prout.
- **The sadvipra society.** "So far… no sadvipra society has been formed; the social
  cycle is rotating in a natural way." In every age the dominant class "first governs,
  then starts to exploit." Sadvipras keep a vigilant eye on each post-revolutionary
  age; when it shows signs of exploiting, "they will immediately create an antithesis."
  "They will not allow society to follow the process of natural evolution. They will
  bring about social revolution whenever necessary" — "the sadvipras will have no rest."

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1958, 1959) — the Yama-Niyama criterion and the benevolent dictatorship

The *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* add two framings:

- **Sadvipras are defined by Yama-Niyama.** (DNE 3, *Synthesis and Analysis*,
  27 Jul 1958.) "I call sadvipras only those people who strictly and sincerely
  follow the principles of Yama and Niyama." "In the collective efforts of
  sadvipras lies humanity's best hope of establishing peace and progress."
  Science in the hands of static people is disappointing: "if science remains
  completely in the hands of static people, the outcome will be disappointing.
  The only beneficial result will be that ... people will eventually understand
  that only sadvipras have the capacity to be the leaders of society."
- **"A [benevolent] sadvipra dictatorship is an absolute necessity."** (DNE 3.)
  "The establishment of a classless society is only possible for those who accept
  Parama Puruṣa [Supreme Consciousness] as the goal of their lives ... This can
  only be done by sadvipras, and therefore, for the future welfare of the
  universe, a [benevolent] sadvipra dictatorship is an absolute necessity." For
  the same reason "it is desirable that [scientific research] should be carried
  out under the supervision of sadvipras."
- **DNE 26 — the II-10 chapter in the DNE collection.** *The Place of Sadvipras
  in the Samája Cakra* (DNE 26, 4 June 1959) is the **same discourse** as Idea
  and Ideology II-10 (the II-10 section above). Its DNE print corroborates the
  II-10 text and adds the full Yama-Niyama definitions verbatim (ahiṃsā — "not
  causing suffering to any harmless creature through thought, word or deed";
  satya — "no relative application"; asteya — "to give up the desire of acquiring
  what is not rightly one's own"; aparigraha — "the non-acceptance of such
  amenities and comforts of life as are superfluous for the preservation of the
  physical existence"; Brahmacarya — experiencing His presence in every
  objectivity, "when the unit mind resonates with Cosmic will"; and the five
  Niyama incl. Iishvara praṇidhāna as "an auto-suggestion of the idea that each
  and every unit is an instrument in the hands of the Almighty" — see
  [Yama & Niyama](/practice/yama-niyama.md)). The sadvipra's duty and methods are
  restated identically: "Sadvipras are not inactive witnesses. They are active
  participants to see that no person or class exploits the rest. For this they
  may have to resort even to physical violence, because the sadvipras will have
  to strike at the source of the power [of the class] which is tending to become
  the exploiter" — physical force against an exploiting kṣatriya class, "a
  revolution in the intellectual field" against the vipra class, and, in the
  vaeshya age, "contest and win elections, because the vaeshya class rules by
  democracy."

# Notes — fidelity

- A sadvipra is defined by **moral + spiritual qualification**, not by class
  membership or office. Keep this; do not read it as a party or a priesthood.
- II-10's "spiritual revolutionaries … by adhering to Yama and Niyama" is a
  **direct, fuller definition** than the sūtra's "staunch moralists and sincere
  spiritualists" — both are grounded; keep the chapter's wording verbatim.
- **HS 2/4** adds the "sadvipra = moralist + spiritualist + fighter against
  immorality" formula and the vikṣubdha-shūdra sociology — direct teaching; the
  "sadvipra will have no rest" vigilance is Sarkar's own framing.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** an institutional procedure for creating
  the "sadvipra society" beyond the call to build it.
