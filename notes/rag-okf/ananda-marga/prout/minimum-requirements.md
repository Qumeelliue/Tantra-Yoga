# Definition

**The minimum requirements of an age should be guaranteed to all.** Ánanda Sútram
5-9: *"Yugasya sarvanimnaprayojanaṃ sarveṣāṃ vidheyam."*

# Explanation

- **Minimum requirements (5-9):** food, clothing, medical treatment, housing and
  education must be provided to everybody. *"The entire wealth of the universe is
  the common patrimony of all"* — invoked with *Hararme pitā Gaorī mātā svadeśah
  bhuvanatrayam* ("Supreme Consciousness is my father, the Supreme Operative
  Principle is my mother, the three worlds are my homeland"). What counts as a
  minimum **changes with the age** (a bicycle in one age, an aeroplane in another)
  — i.e. with [time, space and person](/cosmology/relativity.md).
- **Surplus to the meritorious (5-10):** after meeting everyone's minimum, surplus
  wealth is distributed to meritorious people **according to the degree of their
  merit** (*guṇānupātena*), giving them greater means to serve society. "Serve
  according to your capacity and earn according to your necessity… will yield no
  results in the hard soil of the earth."
- **Raising the minimum (5-11):** increasing the minimum standard of living is the
  **indication of the vitality of society**; the effort to raise it must go on
  *endlessly* (today's special amenity becomes tomorrow's general minimum).

The author's 1989 appendix adds a three-tier frame: **minimum requirements to all**,
**special amenities for special calibre**, and **maximum amenities to all** — all
never-ending processes.

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — the minimum essentials and the gap

The 1959 chapter *The Cosmic Brotherhood* (II-11) grounds the sūtras in the
world-fraternity argument:

- **The guarantee.** "Guaranteed availability of foodstuff, clothing, medical
  assistance and housing accommodation should be arranged so that human beings may
  be able to utilize their surplus energy (energy up till now engaged in procuring
  the essentialities of life) in subtler pursuits. Side by side, there should be
  sufficient scope for providing other amenities of the progressive age. To fulfil
  the above responsibilities, enough purchasing capacity should be created."
- **No reward without work.** "If the supply of requirements be guaranteed without
  any conditions of personal skill and labour, the individual may develop the
  psychology of idleness."
- **Special amenities vs special emoluments.** "The minimum requirements of every
  person are the same, but diversity is also the nature of creation" — so special
  amenities must be provided for diversity in skill and intelligence to be fully
  utilised, financed by **special emoluments** (extra pay to the meritorious). But
  "there should be a constant effort to reduce the gap between the amount of special
  emoluments and the bare minimum requirements" — by liberalising the minimum
  (more special amenities with the age) *and* decreasing special emoluments to the
  few. "This never-ending effort of proper economic adjustment must ceaselessly
  continue at all times."
- **Liberty limited only in the physical sphere.** In the spiritual and mental
  spheres people have full liberty (the objects of aspiration are unlimited); in
  the physical sphere supply is limited, so "any effort for disproportionate or
  unrestricted acquisition of physical objects has every possibility of creating a
  vast majority of have-not's" — individual liberty in the physical sphere must not
  hamper the development of the complete personality of human beings, yet must not
  be "so drastically curtailed" that growth is hampered.

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* — co-ownership and the minimum (DNE 21)

*Social Justice* (DNE 21) grounds the minimum in the common-property principle:

- **Co-ownership is the birthright of every individual.** "for the sake of
  humanism, for the sake of social justice, equitable distribution of all the
  wealth of the universe is indispensable, and co-ownership of the world's
  resources is the birthright of every individual. Even the slightest attempt to
  deprive anyone of this right amounts to gross selfishness."
- **Natural resources are the common property of everyone.** "all the mundane
  resources of the universe are the common property of everyone, and no property
  is the personal or paternal property of anyone." "All natural resources are
  meant to be used for collective welfare. No one has a monopoly over these
  resources."
- **Equal rights to the essentials.** "every individual must have equal rights
  concerning things such as food, clothing, housing, education and medical care,
  which are absolutely essential for existence."
- **Minimum wage plus efficiency bonus.** "Is it not proper to provide every
  individual with the minimum wage necessary to maintain his or her family, plus
  twenty or twenty-five rupees extra to reward his or her efficiency and sense of
  duty? This is the way justice can be applied in relation to people's competence
  and sense of responsibility." — corroborating the 5-10 merit-surplus principle.

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1978–1979) — the physical sphere's necessities and the guarantee

NHN grounds the minimum in the physical sphere's own logic, eleven years after the
1959 II-11 statement and within the same 1961 sūtra teaching:

- **The physical necessities (nh1-2, 12 Dec 1978).** "in the physical sphere there is the
  utmost necessity of food, clothing, education, shelter, medical treatment, etc."
- **Common patrimony (nh1-2).** "common sense dictates that the wealth of the entire
  universe should be considered as common patrimony. The wealth is meant for all, so that
  all can live collectively. It is not proper that one person will snatch away the morsels
  of food from the mouths of others."
- **The Shiva/Kṛṣṇa instruction (nh1-2).** During the days of Lord Shiva and again during
  the days of Lord Kṛṣṇa people were instructed "to live and enjoy the [world's] property
  collectively – *Devābhāgaṃ yathāpūrve saṃjānānā upāsate* ["let us share our wealth
  without differentiation, like sages of the past, so that all may enjoy the universe"]" —
  "But unfortunately people have not done this, and as they have not done this, they have
  had to suffer various physical hardships, and are still suffering."
- **The lacuna (nh1-2).** "this lacuna in the physical sphere, and the consequent disparity,
  is the noumenal cause of all phenomenal troubles."
- **The guarantee (nh1-3, 2 Jan 1979).** "Each and every human being should be guaranteed
  the basic necessities of life: food, clothing, medicine, shelter and education." This is
  the spirit of proper objective adjustment: "I won't exploit anyone, and I won't allow
  others to exploit me."

# Notes — fidelity

- II-11 frames the minimum as the **economic precondition of world fraternity** (the
  cosmic-brotherhood discourse) — a social-ethical motivation distinct from the
  sūtras' terse guarantee. Keep both framings.
- The "special amenities / special emoluments" pair and the "gap-reduction" duty are
  stated in full here — the earliest prose of the 1989 appendix's three-tier scheme.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** the 1989 appendix's third tier
  ("maximum amenities to all") verbatim in II-11 — the prose gives the two-tier
  (minimum + special) account with an explicit reduction duty.
- **NHN (1978–1979) corroborates the guarantee.** The physical necessities list and the
  "common patrimony" principle (nh1-2) and the five-item guarantee "food, clothing,
  medicine, shelter and education" (nh1-3) are the same teaching in the late-1970s prose —
  kept dated alongside the 1961 sūtras and 1959 II-11.
