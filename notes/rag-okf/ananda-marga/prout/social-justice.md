# Definition

**Social justice** is the eradication of weakness and injustice from society: "no one,
through their thoughts, words or actions, should ever condone injustice," and "it is
the duty of the other members of society to eradicate that weakness with all the
sweetness of their hearts." Its economic core: "for the sake of humanism, for the sake
of social justice, equitable distribution of all the wealth of the universe is
indispensable, and co-ownership of the world's resources is the birthright of every
individual." Its method is not gift or grace but recognition of what is already owed:
"Freedom is attained through struggle. No one offers it on a platter, because freedom
is not a gift; it is one's birthright." (DNE 21, 1959.)

# Explanation

- **No condoning of injustice.** "If any physical, psychic, social, moral or spiritual
  weakness becomes apparent in a particular activity or in any sphere of individual or
  social life, it is the duty of the other members of society to eradicate that weakness
  with all the sweetness of their hearts." "The moment opportunists discover a weakness
  in somebody, they exploit that weakness and devour all the vitality of the person."
- **The status of women.** Women "have not really lost their freedom; rather, they have
  trustingly placed their destiny in the hands of men." Therefore "whatever
  responsibility there may be in this regard, it is entirely that of men. If at all a
  movement is required, it must be implemented by men themselves." "It is not a matter
  of granting rights to women, but rather a case of recognizing their rights." "It must
  always be remembered that freedom, or liberty, and license are not the same thing."
- **Making people conscious of rights.** "To make people conscious of their rights in
  every sphere of life – social, economic, psychic and spiritual – is called jiṋána
  vistára; and to exercise these rights fully is called vijiṋána sádhaná."
- **The dowry system is primarily economic.** "The problem is primarily economic,
  although there are secondary causes as well" — "the parents of the bride give a dowry
  in a particular society only when the subsistence of the woman is dependent on the
  income of her husband, or where the number of men is less than the number of women."
- **Patrilineal inheritance with protection.** "the patrilineal system is better than
  the matrilineal"; yet "after recognizing the equal rights of women to use family
  property as long as they live and to enjoy a lifelong income from such property, the
  law of inheritance should be based on the patrilineal system."
- **Educated and uneducated.** "Educated are those who have learned much, remembered
  much and made use of their knowledge in practical life." The "self-styled scholars"
  who despise the illiterate are "the living symbol of educational bankruptcy."
- **Vested interests.** "Vested interests have taken advantage of human ignorance and
  have penetrated deeply into every arena of society." "the rich… forget that, just like
  light, air and water, all the mundane resources of the universe are the common property
  of everyone, and that no property is the personal or paternal property of anyone."
- **Compensation, not confiscation.** "A monthly pension or a lump sum should be
  allocated to old people, invalids, children and destitute women. Opportunities to earn
  a decent livelihood must be provided to all those, young or middle-aged, who are
  able-bodied and healthy." An expropriated proprietor "should be employed in a similar
  profession according to his or her ability." "We must not forget for a moment that even
  those who have continuously exploited others… are human beings. The human family
  includes everyone."
- **Defective approaches.** "What are humanistic appeals, or satyágraha? They are
  simply a special means of using violence to create circumstantial pressure. We can, in
  fact, call them the intellectual's method of using violence." "Gandhism may be an
  excellent utopian model, but in the harsh reality of the world it is absurd and
  self-righteous." Communism: "If we do not equate this communism, established at the
  cost of so much suffering, with robbery, then we must consider it rather worse than
  robbery, because it suppresses through brute force the natural life-urge for
  self-expression."
- **The scale of injustice.** "seventy-five per cent of the evils in society are the
  result of the injustices that people commit against each other."
- **Justice is worldwide (DNE 17).** "For physical Pramátrikońa there must be
  sufficient food and other physical necessities for the entire world." "Side by side
  light and darkness cannot remain" (Punjab/Haryana affluent, Bihar poor). "To attack or
  think to attack another country to get sufficient necessities will cause political
  unrest."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** co-ownership of the world's resources is the birthright of every
  individual; never condone injustice; women's rights are to be recognized, not granted;
  freedom is one's birthright attained through struggle, liberty ≠ license; satyágraha
  is the intellectual's method of using violence; Gandhism/Bhudan utopian; brute-force
  communism "rather worse than robbery"; compensation and re-employment, not
  confiscation.
- **Keep dated.** The claim that "females are physically weaker than males. Because
  their nervous systems are weaker, their minds are also slightly weak" is Sarkar's own
  1959 physiology framing — do not normalize or modernize; reproduce as his teaching.
- **Could not verify:** the DNE 21 text against the identically-titled "Social Justice"
  chapters in *Human Society* 1/3 and *Prout in a Nutshell* 3/1 (both 1959); the
  exact overlap between DNE 21 and DNE 17's *Pramá – 4* education prescription was
  cross-read only at the quotes cited.
