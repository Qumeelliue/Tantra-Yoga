# Definition

**Cooperatives** are PROUT's way of organising production and distribution — built on
**coordinated cooperation** ("something done with equal rights, equal human prestige
and equal locus standi") among free human beings, in contrast to the **subordinated
cooperation** of supervision and servitude. PROUT's slogans: **"We want cooperatives,
not communes,"** and **"We are not slaves of communes."**

# Explanation

- **Coordinated vs subordinated cooperation.** Society is *"Samaānam ejati iti
  samaājah"* — the collective movement of a group toward a common goal. Where people
  work as free equals for each other's welfare, it is **coordinated cooperation** (to
  be pursued in every stratum); where they act under another's supervision, it is
  **subordinated cooperation** (to be avoided).
- **Why cooperatives, not communes.** The commune system (communism) is a
  production-distribution mechanism under regimented control: no personal ownership,
  no incentive, intelligent people forced into unsuitable work, equal wages for all —
  "fundamentally defective, exploitative and anti-human." PROUT supports cooperatives
  because their inner spirit is coordinated cooperation.
- **Farmers (agricultural) cooperatives:** land reorganised in four phases
  (uneconomic holdings → cooperative management → join → rational redistribution, till
  ownership conflict ends); shares proportional to land productivity and labour; the
  cooperative protects farmers from **distress sales** and middlemen/profiteers; taxes
  paid collectively; permanent labourers get bonuses, casual labourers wages;
  shares not transferable without permission but inheritable (to stop capitalist
  speculation). "Agriculture should have the same status as industry."
- **Producers cooperatives** (agro-, agrico- and non-agricultural industries): profit
  distributed among workers/members by capital invested and service rendered. **Essential
  commodities** (rice, pulse, salt, clothing) and **demi-essential** (oil, antiseptic
  soap) are produced cooperatively; **non-essential** (luxury) goods may stay private.
  The list of essential commodities is continually revised by the government with time,
  space and person.
- **Consumers cooperatives:** distribute consumer goods at reasonable rates, buying
  essential commodities directly from producers/agricultural cooperatives, eliminating
  hoarders, black-marketeers and middlemen.
- **Management:** each cooperative elects a board of directors (all moralists, not
  honorary); profits split between dividends, reinvestment, authorised capital and a
  **reserve fund** (to sustain dividends in lean years). Many **small satellite
  cooperatives** supply large producers cooperatives (e.g. car parts made locally).
- **Local membership:** cooperative members must be local people; "migratory birds have
  no place in cooperatives" — floating labour has no right to membership.
- **Unifying force:** cooperatives are "the best representation of the sweet nectar of
  humanity"; the physical realm's best expression of love and sympathy for humanity.

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1987) — cooperatives as the instrument of pramā

*Pramā – 1 / Dynamic Equilibrium and Equipoise* (nh2-10, February 1987) presents
cooperatives as the instrument of the physical-stratum balance:

- **The agricultural cooperative.** "Agricultural cooperatives and agricultural producers
  cooperatives will have to be started, and farmers' brigades will have to be formed.
  Agriculture should be conducted on the basis of the principle of consumption, and not the
  principle of profit."
- **Restoring pramā.** "A proper balance in the lokatrikonā or pramātrikonā of agriculture
  will help establish balance in the lokatrikonā or pramātrikonā in the physical stratum."

# Notes — fidelity

- **"We want cooperatives, not communes"** is direct teaching; the critique of the
  commune system (no incentive, no personal ownership, psychic oppression) is explicit.
- **Coordinated vs subordinated cooperation** is Sarkar's own pair — keep the
  distinction sharp.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** an exact statutory form of cooperative
  law beyond the rules stated (board election, shares, reserve fund); the four-phase
  land plan is given fully.
- **nh2-10 (1987)** grounds the agricultural cooperative as the instrument of pramā in
  the physical stratum — production "on the basis of the principle of consumption, and not
  the principle of profit" — consistent with the PiaN 14/9 and 20/2 accounts.
