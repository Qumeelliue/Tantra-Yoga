# Definition

The **four vargas** are the four aims of human life: **kāma** (fulfilment of
physical necessities), **artha** (fulfilment of physico-psychic needs),
**dharma** (fulfilment of psycho-spiritual necessities) and **mokṣa** (spiritual
emancipation). They are attained through the study of **itihāsa** (benevolent
history) — and, together, through **devotion**. (1978, PiaN 11/6.)

# Explanation

- **The four vargas:**
  1. **Kāma** — the fulfilment of physical necessities: food, clothes, shelter,
     medicine, education. "If these basic necessities are not provided, life becomes
     unbearable." It is *Varttamāneṣu vartteta* — "no one should try to move ahead
     ignoring the present" (Shiva's instruction) — the ground of PROUT itself.
  2. **Artha** — the fulfilment of physico-psychic needs, giving *temporary* relief;
     the Sanskrit word also means "money" (temporary relief in the physical sphere)
     and "meaning" (removing the psychic want of a word's sense). *Paramārtha* is the
     permanent relief (see [Artha & Paramārtha](/prout/artha-paramartha.md)).
  3. **Dharma** — the psycho-spiritual necessities; the longing for the spiritual
     that drove Caetanya and Buddha to renunciation.
  4. **Mokṣa** — spiritual emancipation; when humans attain the fullest expression
     they feel it a liability and offer it back to Him — "Your objects, O Lord, I
     offer unto You" — the last stage.
- **Itihāsa prepares the mind for all four.** Sanskrit literature has four
  categories: **kāvya** (lucid poetic verse), **purāṇa** (educative fiction — Vyāsa's
  eighteen purāṇas for mass education, with his three apologetic "mistakes"),
  **itikathā** (chronological history), and **itihāsa** — the portion of history
  "whose study leads to human welfare," giving "the moral code of conduct and an inner
  understanding of the four vargas." Itihāsa has *no English synonym* ("History of
  India" is wrongly translated); the Mahābhārata is the example.
- **The story of the four fruits.** In the Rāmāyaṇa tale (read as educative fiction),
  the boatman's wife, lost in greed for gold, is told by her husband to "carry those
  two feet" of Rāma instead. Rāma gives her four fruits — kāma, artha, dharma, mokṣa —
  while Lakṣmaṇa adds his single fruit: **devotion** — "Unless and until you hold this
  fruit in your hand, you will never be able to attain the other four." "One who is a
  genuine devotee should not worry about the attainment of the Vargas; they will come
  automatically."
- **PROUT and the vargas.** PROUT was formulated to resolve the defects of the mundane
  world — to provide the minimum necessities (kāma) first: "It is useless to preach
  the gospels of Paramārtha to a person who has no food to eat."

# Notes — fidelity

- **The four vargas are kāma/artha/dharma/mokṣa** in exactly this order and framing;
  kāma here is "physical necessities," NOT desire in general.
- **Itihāsa has no English synonym** — Sarkar's explicit point about translation.
- **The Rāmāyaṇa is explicitly read as purāṇa (educative fiction), not history** — keep
  this meta-distinction; do not report the tale as factual.
- **Devotion as the fruit that commands the other four** is direct teaching.
