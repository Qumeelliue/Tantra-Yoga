# Definition

There are **three types of jīvikā** (occupations) for human beings — **physical,
mental, and spiritual**. Animals have only one, the physical: "if animals get food
they are satisfied, but human beings are not satisfied with food or mere
physicalities." *Ādhyātmik* or *Dhārmic Jīvikā*, the spiritual occupation, is "those
which involve human beings in the field of Dharma." (1988, PiaN 16/8.)

# Explanation

- **The three occupations:** physical (supplying the body), psychic (engaging the
  mind — "mental food"), and spiritual (Dharma). Human evolution shows the trend of
  increasing psychic and spiritual engagement.
- **Prehistory of occupation** (PiaN 16/8): the first humans (evolved ~1,000,000
  years ago, in **Ráŕh**), no better than animals, had only physical occupation —
  the **Bone Age** (weapons of bone), then Stone, Bronze, Iron ages. Dance satisfied
  the primitive need for psychic occupation. Agriculture and settlement led to the
  contemplation of something higher — "Atmic or Dharmic jiivika commenced 15,000
  years ago," whence the Upanishads and Āraṇyakas ("the Vedic civilization is the
  oldest civilization in the world"). A full-fledged civilization (agriculture, the
  wheel, dress, script) began ~7,000 years ago; philosophy ~4,000 years ago.
- **Specialisation without caste.** People began to specialise by capacity and
  efficiency (the strong protect, the artistic manufacture) — "In those days there was
  no caste system." The *Mahāto* community (from *Mahātman*) are descendants of the
  talented, not a caste: "There is no logic behind the caste system."
- **The spiritual occupation in Ananda Marga.** "In Ananda Marga sádhaná, dhyana and
  japa are Atmic or Dharmic jiivika." Ananda Margis should **inspire, never compel** —
  by selfless service, sacrifice, good conduct, spiritual practices and all-round
  knowledge. "Ananda Marga is a comprehensive mode of living based on spirituality."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** "human beings are not satisfied with food or mere
  physicalities"; "the suppression of the mother tongue is a sin" is a *different*
  chapter — here the relevant contrast is animal vs human occupation.
- The **dates** (1,000,000 / 100,000 / 15,000 / 7,000 / 4,000 years) are Sarkar's
  own chronology — reproduce as stated, not as modern archaeology.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** a precise list of which occupations count
  as "psychic" beyond "engaging the mind"; the chapter is illustrative.
