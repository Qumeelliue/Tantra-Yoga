# Definition

The rotation of the [social cycle](/prout/samaja-cakra.md) can be hastened,
reversed or completed in **five named modes**, classified by **direction** and the
**magnitude of force** applied:

| Mode | Sūtra | Direction | Force | Meaning |
|------|-------|-----------|-------|---------|
| **Krānti** (evolution) | 5-3 | forward | ordinary | accelerating the cycle by force |
| **Viplava** (revolution) | 5-4 | forward | tremendous | accelerating by tremendous force |
| **Vikrānti** (counter-evolution) | 5-5 | backward | ordinary | reversing the cycle by force |
| **Prativiplava** (counter-revolution) | 5-6 | backward | tremendous | reversing by tremendous force |
| **Parikrānti** (peripheric evolution) | 5-7 | full turn | — | one complete rotation |

# Explanation

- **Krānti** (5-3): a change of ages accelerated by force — e.g. sadvipras
  establishing the Vipra Age by subduing exploiting warriors. It differs from
  *svābhāvika parivarttana* (natural change) **only** in the application of force.
- **Viplava** (5-4): when an age is replaced by the next within a short time, or
  tremendous force is needed to destroy an entrenched hegemony.
- **Vikrānti** (5-5): a forced reversion to the preceding age (e.g. Kṣatriya after
  Vipra) — **extremely short-lived**; soon replaced again.
- **Prativiplava** (5-6): a backward turn by tremendous force — **even shorter-lived**
  than counter-evolution.
- **Parikrānti** (5-7): one complete rotation of the cycle, concluding with shūdra
  revolution.

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* — the means of change (DNE 21)

*Social Justice* (DNE 21) criticizes each defective approach and specifies the
legitimate means:

- **Satyāgraha is "the intellectual's method of using violence."** "What are
  humanistic appeals, or satyāgraha? They are simply a special means of using
  violence to create circumstantial pressure. We can, in fact, call them the
  intellectual's method of using violence. They are a way to make people eager to
  move along the path of human welfare without resorting to actual violence,
  relying on legal enforcement, or becoming angry and adopting the path of
  bloodshed."
- **Gandhism and Bhudan are utopian.** "no matter how much importance was given
  to the benevolence of the human mind by the Gandhian and Bhudan movements, or
  how saintly their propounders may have been, selfish and mean-minded people
  will never accept their principles. ... Gandhism may be an excellent utopian
  model, but in the harsh reality of the world it is absurd and self-righteous."
- **Brute-force communism is "rather worse than robbery."** "If we do not equate
  this communism, established at the cost of so much suffering, with robbery,
  then we must consider it rather worse than robbery, because it suppresses
  through brute force the natural life-urge for self-expression."
- **Robin Hoods and charity fail.** "no one can survive on charity. Such an
  approach only creates a society of beggars." "plundering the wealth of the rich
  does not destroy capitalism" — "robbery may reduce the assets of the
  capitalists, but it does not destroy the seed of capitalism."
- **"Violence does not solve any problem."** "whatever poisonous tendencies of the
  individual and collective minds may be destroyed by violence, the seeds of
  those tendencies remain embedded in the mind itself."
- **The legitimate means.** "I do not consider it at all improper if exploiters
  are forced to follow the right path by circumstantial pressure in a
  totalitarian state or by legal compulsion in a democratic one. However, the
  primary objective is not simply to use any means available to create a
  vibration in the minds of exploiters. Proper moral training must also be
  imparted to keep the waves of that vibration alive." For crude minds "it is,
  and will forever remain, necessary to influence their minds by giving them a
  rude shock."

# In *Varna Vijinana* (1983) — grammar and the era

*Varna Vijinana* extends the doctrine of social change to language itself — the
progressive-reform slogan applied to grammar, and the era-bound change of
grammatical forms:

- **Grammar follows the people; grammar must be recast.** (vv-11) "Grammar must be
  formulated according to the way people speak; that is, grammar is a systemized
  form of dialogue." Since the nominative is shown to need not be the first case
  ("the relationship between case and case-ending is not a hard and fast one"),
  Sarkar concludes: "the grammar will have to be recast. When the society, economy
  and everything else have to be newly ordered, then why not the grammar as well!
  If the new cannot be newly accepted then with the passage of time it becomes
  useless; it becomes outdated."
- **All grammatical forms change as the era changes.** (vv-4) "shatr, shánac,
  átmanepadii, parasmaepadii, short, long and extended vowels, pronunciation – all
  of these change as the era changes. They have done so in the past and will
  continue to do so in the future."

# The dialectical framing (PiaN 6/2)

The lecture *Dialectical Materialism and Democracy* (PiaN 6/2) frames the same motion
in Hegelian vocabulary — while rejecting dialectical materialism:

- **Thesis → antithesis → synthesis.** "Social advancement is the triadic blending of
  thesis, antithesis and synthesis." When a theory (thesis) loses its competence for
  collective welfare, an antithesis is created; clash-and-cohesion yields a
  synthesis — which "transforms itself into the thesis of the next age." "Theoretically,
  synthesis is not the absolute factor… thesis, antithesis and synthesis take place
  within the bounds of relativity."
- **The cycle in dialectical terms.** The Kṣatriya era's neglect of the Vipras breeds
  the Vipra antithesis; the Vipra offensive breeds the bourgeois antithesis; bourgeois
  exploitation breeds the revolutionary antithesis — the four eras recur in cyclic
  order.
- **The sadvipra role.** "Sadvipras or spiritual revolutionaries will inspire and
  mobilize the crusading human spirit… and help accelerate the speed of antithetical
  social movement," then "during the stage of synthesis, they will take the leadership
  of society into their own hands." They wait for the antithesis to emerge, meanwhile
  working "to bring about the psychological background for the antithesis of the next
  phase." Under sadvipra governance the synthetic order stays intact though eras come
  and go — "the Shūdra era will come but there will be no exploitation by the Shūdras."
- **Dialectical materialism is rejected.** "The welfare of society is not possible
  through dialectical materialism" — it may fit one age but becomes "a brutal instrument
  of exploitation and destruction" in the next, because it cannot maintain adjustment
  with time, space and person; only those who have accepted the Supreme Entity as their
  goal "are alone capable of constantly maintaining proper adjustment."
