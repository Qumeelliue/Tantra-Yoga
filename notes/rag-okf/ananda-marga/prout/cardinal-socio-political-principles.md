# Definition

The **three cardinal socio-political principles** are the inviolable rules of
PROUT's social order: **"people should not be retrenched from their occupations
unless alternative employment has been arranged for them"; people should not be
forcibly converted from one religion to another; and no mother tongue should be
suppressed.** Occupation, spiritual practice and mother tongue are "very important to
human beings" — hurting their sentiments "will deeply affect" them. (1988, PiaN 16/9.)

# Explanation

- **Occupation must not be retrenched without an alternative.** Examples: rickshaw
  pullers made illegal without alternative employment die of starvation or become
  criminals; the Bagadis of Ráŕh and Cuyárs/Lodhas of Midnapur, retrenched from the
  armed forces at the end of the Mughal era, turned to crime — "If these people had
  been inducted into the military or the police, they would have earned a proper
  livelihood." Even a bad scheme, once adopted, should be withdrawn gradually (the
  princely-stipend example).
- **No forcible conversion.** People leave a religion only when it fails to guide them
  (defective teachings like the caste system or oppressing the poor); forcible
  conversion breeds counter-campaigns (Kalachand Roy → Sheik Kaluddin Khan) and
  violence. "A religion should be so strong that no one can be converted from it."
  Historical countermeasures (the Melbandhan system in Bengal, the Syed/Mallik/Mián
  titles in Bihar) show how communities protected themselves. "Hinduism will
  degenerate… as long as the caste system exists." All religions should rest "on a
  strong foundation of logic and reason."
- **No suppression of the mother tongue.** "If people's mother tongue is suppressed,
  it is equivalent to strangling them. The suppression of people's mother tongue is a
  sin." Examples: the partition of Pakistan over Urdu vs Bengali (60% Bengali); Hindi
  imposition in India (Bhojpuri, Maethilii, Angika, Magahii, Nagpuri suppressed);
  Hitler's plan to eradicate French. The model is Switzerland's four state languages;
  in India "all the languages spoken… should be recognized and encouraged."
- **Defence of the principles.** "If any group tries to violate any of these three
  cardinal socio-political principles, you should immediately oppose them with a
  thundering voice and sufficient force" — but only after raising the masses'
  consciousness of their exploitation.

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** the three principles are "cardinal" and "should never be
  violated"; "the suppression of people's mother tongue is a sin."
- The caste-system critique ("Hinduism will degenerate… as long as the caste system
  exists") is explicit — keep it; it connects to the anti-caste reading of
  [Samāja Cakra](/prout/samaja-cakra.md).
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** any extension of "occupation" to a right
  to a specific job beyond the retrenchment rule.
