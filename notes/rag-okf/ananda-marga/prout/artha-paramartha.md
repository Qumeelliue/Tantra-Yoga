# Definition

**Artha** is temporary liberation from the triple afflictions (physical, psychic and
spiritual); **Paramārtha** is the factor that brings about their **permanent
cessation** — the "instrument of permanent relief," the goal toward which one moves
while maintaining an adjustment with the relative world. (1978, PiaN 11/4.)

# Explanation

- **The jiiva lives in bondage.** "Paśhabaddho bhavejjiivah paśhamukto bhavecchivah"
  — the individual enmeshed in Māyā's snare is the jiiva; freed from the fetters one
  becomes one with Shiva. Freedom from some bondages is temporary — like a bird
  released to fly in a room but confined again to its cage.
- **Artha's three meanings.** *Artha* = (1) that which gives temporary relief from
  bondage (e.g. money removing the pangs of hunger — but "tomorrow the hunger will
  return"); (2) **money**; (3) **meaning** (knowing that "kadali" means "banana"
  removes the psychic want). "To obtain temporary liberation from pains and miseries,
  money or Artha is required but to attain permanent liberation, we need Paramārtha."
- **The triple afflictions (tāpa traya):** physical (from the material world), psychic
  (from the unmanifest world/mind — even the affluent mourn), and spiritual (the small
  mind engrossed with limited objects). Limited psychic wealth is the root cause of
  spiritual afflictions; "the state of breaking the bondages of limitations is known
  as 'spiritual liberation'"; permanent liberation is salvation.
- **Paramārtha is the permanent cessation.** "That Artha which brings permanent relief
  from the pangs of hunger, which is the instrument of permanent cessation, is called
  Paramārtha." But first things first: "It is useless to preach the gospels of
  Paramārtha to a person who has no food to eat" — hence PROUT.
- **Māyā and surrender.** Humans are under Vishva Māyā's domination; the obstacles
  (husband, wife, parents, society) are her creations. But "the blessings of Parama
  Puruṣa are always with them": *Daevii hyeṣā guṇamayii mama Māyā duratyayā; māmeva ye
  prapadyante māyāmetāṃ taranti te* (Bhagavad Gītā) — Māyā is His divine force, and
  those who take shelter in Him cross her. "One should not think 'I am a sinner'";
  *Yādṛśii bhāvanā yasya siddhirbhavati tādṛśii* — "As you think, so you become."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** Artha gives temporary relief, Paramārtha permanent cessation;
  "it is useless to preach Paramārtha to a person who has no food to eat." Keep the
  economic-spiritual sequence intact.
- **Māyā is Parama Puruṣa's own divine force** — surrender crosses her; the Gītā verse
  is quoted. Do not present Māyā as an enemy to be fought (cf. [Vidyā & Avidyā](/cosmology/vidya-avidya.md)).
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** the term *tāpa traya* itself (the three
  afflictions are described, not named with the compound).
