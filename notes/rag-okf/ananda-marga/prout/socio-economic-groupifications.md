# Definition

**Socio-economic groupification** is PROUT's plan to reorganise the world into
**self-sufficient socio-economic units** that "work to enhance the all-round welfare
of the people in their respective areas and unite humanity on a common ideological
base." As units grow strong and prosperous they **merge**, eventually forming one
integrated world unit — "universal in spirit, regional in approach." (1979, PiaN
13/4 = PE 3.)

# Explanation

- **Five criteria for forming a unit:**
  1. **same economic problems** (e.g. lack of markets, labour surplus/deficit,
     communication and transportation difficulties, lack of irrigation water);
  2. **uniform economic potentialities** (similar opportunities for prosperity;
     disparity between rich and poor progressively reduced);
  3. **ethnic similarities** (with scope for every ethnic group's expression and
     development; the "multi-coloured garland of humanity");
  4. **sentimental legacy** (language, traditions, literature, common usages and
     cultural expressions — the "common chord" of collective psychology);
  5. **similar geographical features** (topography, river systems, rainfall, irrigation).
- **Self-sufficiency:** each unit prepares and implements its own developmental
  programmes; maximum utilization of resources and **no drainage of capital** (the
  Orissa mineral-export critique). Political/linguistic state boundaries are not the
  right basis for economic planning.
- **Merging:** adjoining units with economic parity, cultural mixing, communication
  and administrative efficiency should merge; one political unit may contain several
  socio-economic units (Chotanagpur vs north Bihar; Royalseema/Shrii Kakulam/
  Telengana). PROUT does **not** favour many small states. The process culminates in
  one socio-economic unit for India, then South/Southeast Asia, then the world.
- **"Universal in spirit, regional in approach"** — the rational method of PROUT:
  differences of culture and potentiality should not divide humanity; unity is built
  on common sentiments; diversity enriches humanity.
- **Protection from exploitation** requires an **integrated ideology** (rational
  analysis + psychic/spiritual need + dynamism), an **empirical spiritual base**
  (against fissiparous tendencies), **spiritually oriented cadres** ("Self-realization
  and service to humanity"), and **proper institutions** (a world government, whose
  powers are progressively strengthened).
- **The maxim:** "Know the area, prepare the plan and serve the people."
