# Definition

**Sukha** is a **congenial mental feeling** (*anukūla-vedaniiya*). Ánanda Sútram
2-1: *"Anukūlavedaniiyaṃ sukham"* — "A congenial mental feeling is called
happiness."

# Explanation

When the mental waves of a person — whose **saṃskāra** is the quiescent form of
those waves — meet **similar waves** emanating from a crude object or another
mind-entity, those waves are *complementary and reciprocal*. The contact of these
**mutually-sympathetic waves** is happiness.

The **attachment to happiness** (*sukhānurakti*) is the **primary vṛtti
(propensity) of all living beings** (2-2,
*"Sukhānuraktih paramā jaeviivrttih"*). Every living being wants to keep itself
alive — a self-preserving mental faculty; want of happiness endangers one's very
sense of existence, so one seeks the *pervasiveness* of happiness as one's sole
refuge. This drive, carried to its limit, becomes the longing for **infinite
happiness** — [ānanda](/cosmology/ananda.md).

## Relationships

- defined as contact of sympathetic waves matching one's saṃskāra
- attachment to it (sukhānurakti) is the primary vṛtti of the [Unit Mind](/biopsychology/unit-mind.md)
- its limitless form is [Ānanda](/cosmology/ananda.md), and the longing for it is [Dharma](/philosophy/dharma.md)
