# Definition

The **sight of ghosts is created by the cittāṇu** (mind-stuff) in concentrated
thought. Ánanda Sútram 3-6: *"Abhibhāvanāt cittāṇusrṣṭapretadarshanam."*
**Spirits and ghosts do not actually exist.**

# Explanation

In a frightened, indignant or hypnotic state a person attains *temporary
concentration* of mind, and the [cittāṇu](/biopsychology/citta.md) (mind-stuff)
takes the **form of the object imagined** — which one then "sees" externally
(*pretadarśana*). Two cases:

- **Positive hallucination** — the external vision of an internal thought (one
  thinks of ghosts in solitude, then "sees" them in the open).
- **Negative hallucination** — even an actually existent object appears
  non-existent.

If the hypnotic state is thoroughly introspective, one may mistake **one's own
entity** for a ghost and behave so that others say the person is "possessed."
*Theomania* / theophanic possession is of the same variety. Those who claim to
have seen a ghost do not lie — the **delusion of the mind appears to them as
visual perception**.

# In *Yoga Psychology* (1979) — the ectoplasmic mechanism

*The Two Human Approaches* (YP 6) gives the mechanism behind the sūtra:

- **You never come in physical contact with anything** — perception is internal
  projection through nerve fibres, nerve cells and the objective mind: "whatever you
  perceive or whatever you conceive — everything is within you, nothing is outside of
  you." "You are seeing the psychic projection of the material world."
- **Extro-internal and intro-external.** *Extro-internal*: waves from the external
  world enter (flower → eye → optic nerve → brain, where "a similar flower is created
  inside the mind") — the subject of applied psychology. *Intro-external*: a mental
  creation is projected outward; with "a strong ectoplasmic structure" the projected
  image "can be seen by you and by others."
- **Positive and negative hallucination via ectoplasm.** *Positive*: an ectoplasmic
  elephant is created in the mind and projected outside, so "what appears to exist does
  not exist." *Negative*: with ectoplasmic power one withdraws the light waves of an
  external elephant, so "what actually exists appears not to exist." Both require "the
  extroversial projection of your thought-waves."

# In the Discourses on Neohumanist Education (DNE 29, 3 Jun 1987) — a glandular source

Bio-psychology adds a *physiological* route to the ectoplasmic mechanism:
under-secretion of the prostate gland "will create a fearful nature and [the
person] will suffer from psychic hallucinations. One may even see a ghost in
broad daylight." This is a parallel cause (glandular under-secretion), not a
replacement for the cittāṇu-projection account of AS 3-6.

## Relationships

- a projection of the [Citta](/biopsychology/citta.md) (cittāṇu taking a form)
- arises in a concentrated/hypnotic state of the conscious mind ([Pañcakośa](/biopsychology/pancakosa.md))
- complements the "no bodiless soul to please" teaching of [Death](/biopsychology/death.md)
- *Yoga Psychology*: the ectoplasmic intro-external projection mechanism (positive/negative hallucination); perception as internal projection — cf. [Tanmātra](/biopsychology/tanmatra.md)
- *DNE 29*: prostate under-secretion can cause "psychic hallucinations" (glandular route, cf. [Bio-Psychology](/biopsychology/bio-psychology.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- "Ghosts do not exist; they are cittāṇu projections" is a **direct teaching**
  (3-6). Keep it as a categorical claim, not a hedged opinion.
- **YP 6's "everything is within you"** is Sarkar's phenomenology of perception —
  reproduce as his framing (cf. [Tanmātra](/biopsychology/tanmatra.md)'s wave-theory).
- **DNE 29 is a parallel, not a replacement:** the prostate-gland under-secretion
  claim is a physiological cause of hallucinations, distinct from the cittāṇu
  projection of AS 3-6. Keep both without harmonizing them.
