# Definition

The **two human approaches** are the two movements of the mind — **extro-internal**
(external waves entering and creating an image within the mind) and **intro-external**
(a mental creation projected outward via the ectoplasm). The devotional approach —
surrender to Parama Puruṣa — merges the unit into Him, who has **two "imperfections":
He cannot create a second Parama Puruṣa, and He cannot hate anyone.** (1979, YP 6.)

# Explanation

- **Perception is internal projection.** "You never see anything… you are seeing the
  mental image of the flower within." Contact is through the mind, nerve fibres, nerve
  cells and the objective mind — "the entire universe is within you in miniature form."
  The human entity "is more psychic than physical"; one must maintain parallelism
  between entitative, incantative and spiritual waves.
- **Extro-internal** — waves move external → eye → optic nerve → brain, where a similar
  flower is created within; "the subject of applied psychology."
- **Intro-external** — a creation in the mind is projected outward with a strong
  ectoplasmic structure, "seen by you and by others" — a **positive hallucination**
  (what appears to exist does not exist). Withdrawing external light waves produces a
  **negative hallucination** (what exists appears not to exist). Both are extroversial
  projections of thought-waves (cf. [Ghosts & Hallucination](/biopsychology/hallucination.md)).
- **Parama Puruṣa's two imperfections.** Everything is within His mind — "nothing is
  without, nothing is external" — so He cannot create a second Parama Puruṣa (He
  remains one; whoever merges in Him becomes Him, "just like the Huang Ho" entering the
  Pacific), and He cannot hate anyone ("either you change your name or withdraw your
  order"). He sees with mental eyes — "seeing mental objects requires no eyes."
- **The devotional approach.** The intellectual (vain), the actional (bound by time/
  space/person) — neither can reach Him. The devotee's way: "O Lord, I am Your
  creation… This body is a machine. Utilize it." Because His devotees have "stolen His
  mind," He has one want — mind — and the devotee offers his/her own mind to fill it.
  "The devotees are very intelligent, they are more intelligent than intellectuals, and
  more intelligent than those engaged in actional faculties."

# In the Discourses on Neohumanist Education — the East–West subjective/objective split and the two stances of thought

**DNE 10 (Talks on Education – Excerpt C, 1969) — subjective East, objective
West.** The same polarity appears on the civilizational scale: "The East is
predominantly subjective in outlook, whereas the West has a mainly objective
bent of mind." Each alone fails: "Too much emphasis on either one of these
approaches is not conducive to the all-round growth of the society. We can build
up an ideal society only on the basis of a happy adjustment between the
subjective and the objective approaches. Here is the greatness of Ananda Marga
ideology."

- **The extreme-idealism target.** Some Indian Monists "regard the world as an
  illusion" and declare *"Brahma satyam jaganmithyā"* — "Brahma is the only
  reality; the expressed world is an illusion." Though "not logically incorrect,"
  in practice such extreme idealism "preaches that the world should be ignored"
  and "the society as a whole will not thereby progress."
- **Two one-sided failures.** "The Asian countries … have been subject to great
  humiliation during periods of foreign invasion" and "could not resist the
  foreign invaders," while the West, for all its spectacular material progress,
  "is not socially content and miserably lacks spiritual wealth."
- **The synthesis.** "Ananda Marga believes in one universal society with one
  ideology and one cosmic goal" — unlike Kipling, "East is East and West is West,
  and never the twain shall meet."

**DNE 38 (Dogma and Human Intellect, 22 Sep 1979) — the two directions for
thought.** "Human thought-waves and human knowledge may be directed towards two
objects":

- **(a) The objective psychic stance.** As a result of "that objective movement
  on the psychic level … the mind becomes bigger and bigger. And in order to
  serve the purpose of the mind, both the nerve-cells and the nerve-fibres of
  the human brain also become stronger and bigger. As a result of this
  enlargement of the nerve-cells … in the future, the human cranium will become
  bigger, and limbs and other parts of the body will become lean and thin."
- **(b) The objecto-subjective / spiritual stance.** Here "you withdraw different
  mental faculties or propensities and make them pinnacled. And when those mental
  faculties are apexed, then … you offer that pinnacled human mind at the altar
  of the supreme spirituality. This is the spiritual approach."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** the two movements, the two imperfections, and the devotee's
  "stolen mind" offering are Sarkar's own framings — keep them exact.
- **"Everything is within you"** is the phenomenological claim; the "two imperfections"
  are a devotional paradox (not ontological deficiencies of the Infinite) — preserve the
  paradox as stated, do not "correct" it.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** any systematic scheme of the "applied
  psychology" beyond the two movements and hallucination examples.
- **DNE enrichment is an extension of scope, not a conflation:** YP 6's two
  *movements* (extro-internal / intro-external) are the perceptual mechanism;
  DNE 10's subjective/objective split and DNE 38's two *stances* are the
  civilizational and epistemological applications. Keep them as distinct
  framings of "the two approaches." The DNE 38 "(a)/(b)" labels are the editor's
  shorthand for Sarkar's two enumerated stances.
- **Brahma satyam jaganmithyā** is quoted as the target of criticism (extreme
  idealism), not as Sarkar's own position — preserve that framing.
