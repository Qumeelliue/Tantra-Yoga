# Definition

A **saṃskāra** is a *distortion of the mind-stuff awaiting expression* — a
reaction in potentiality. Ánanda Sútram 3-4:
*"Manovikrtih vipākāpekṣitā saṃskārah."*

# Explanation

Every act, virtuous or non-virtuous, begets a **mental distortion** (*manovikṛti*)
of the [citta](/biopsychology/citta.md). The mind regains its normal composure
through **vipāka** — undergoing the consequences. Where an act has been done but
its vipāka is **kept in abeyance** (not yet served), that suspended vipāka is the
**saṃskāra**. This is the potential form of [karma's](/philosophy/karma.md)
requital.

At death, according to the saṃskāras held in the **causal (unconscious) mind**
(see [pañcakośa](/biopsychology/pancakosa.md)),
[Prakṛti](/cosmology/prakrti.md) — in order to get those saṃskāras served through
vipāka — effects the contact of the **bodiless mind** with a *living structure*
in a womb whose mental waves **parallel** the saṃskāra-determined waves. This is
**rebirth** (*punarjanma*): one departs after death carrying saṃskāras in
accordance with the deeds of one's lifetime.

# In the prose discourses

**Dagdhabīja — the burnt seed (liberated mind).** A *dagdhabīja* ("burnt seed") is
the sādhaka whose saṃskāras can no longer sprout. The state is reached by
**cent-per-cent devotion**, not by passively waiting out one's reactions: when the
doer regards Paramātmā — not himself — as the actor, the mind is not affected and
**no new saṃskāra accrues**. (Purely psychic action reacts exactly equal; a
physico-psychic action's psychic and physical residues "add up to a greater
reaction.") Such a sādhaka offers even the fruits of good action — "the chains of
gold" — at the Lord's feet, may voluntarily carry others' saṃskāras, and once the
*karmāshaya* is filled with Consciousness "the possibility of rebirth is destroyed
for good. Burnt seeds never sprout" — yet the inert seed-form still exists.

**Extra-cerebral memory — the substrate that carries saṃskāras across lives.**
Because the previous body's nerve cells are burnt to ashes at death, no past-life
episode is stored in present nerve cells; the saṃskāras instead **accompany the
[ātman](/cosmology/atman.md)** and seek a body whose nerve cells best express them.
Recall of past lives (*extra-cerebral* or non-cerebral memory) therefore proceeds
**without nerve-cell assistance** — one of the subconscious mind's three prongs
(cerebral memory, extra-cerebral memory, revelation), and distinct from the
unconscious mind. Those who retain it are *jātismara*; it stays vivid only to about
age twelve or thirteen, and Sarkar **discourages** cultivating it — knowing past
lives "will never help your spiritual advancement."

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — saṃskāra as momentum, infused vs acquired

The 1959 cosmology (*Life, Death and Saṃskāra*, II-7) grounds the sūtra:

- **Saṃskāra is the mind's momentum from past stages.** "The mind is an
  ever-changing functional organism and so it must have a momentum. Every stage
  results from the previous stages… The effect of action in the past stages is
  called saṃskāra, which gives momentum to the unit mind." The ultimate cause of
  every momentum is the Macrocosm, which works "through the media of microcosms";
  the attraction of the Great accelerates such momentum.
- **First the ego, then the saṃskāra-holder.** In the earliest stage of
  pratisaiñcara the microcosm evolves neither ego nor mahattattva, so it is "devoid
  of any saṃskāra"; later the ego evolves and *feels and accepts itself as a
  possessor of saṃskāra*; still later the ego starts controlling the mental
  activities — the human stage.
- **Infused vs acquired.** Because the momentum for the *negative* movement is the
  accepted momentum of the unit mind, "the saṃskāra of any living animal inferior
  to the human is 'infused' but that of the human is 'acquired'": inferior animals
  keep marching on pratisaiñcara (their ego is less developed), while humans may
  retrace by adopting mean objectives.
- **Karmāshaya across death.** At death the dissociated mind is "a state of
  inaction (or converted into *karmāshaya* — saṃskāra, or reaction in its
  potentiality)"; it "possesses the unexpressed momentum of its previous lives"
  which, "according to the universal law of Prakṛti that every force must have its
  expression," must be expressed — so the Cosmic mutative force provides a proper
  physical parallelism for it (cf. [Death](/biopsychology/death.md),
  [Ātman](/cosmology/atman.md)).

# In *Tattva Kaomudii* (1969) — the equalization of saṃskāras

*Equalization of Saṃskāras* (TK 3/5) draws the deepest consequence of the
saṃskāra-doctrine:

- **Saṃskāras individuate.** "The same saṃskāras cannot live in two separate entities –
  they want to be fused in one." Rāma and Shyāma are different because each has a
  different set of saṃskāras; "if their separate saṃskāras are removed there will
  ultimately exist one soul and one mind." "The moment the individual saṃskāras are
  removed, the mind loses its existence and only the soul remains," merging like a drop
  into the ocean.
- **Māyā and the differences.** Differences and struggles "are all due to the influence
  of Māyā"; "the moment the veil of Māyā is removed, truth manifests itself in all its
  radiance, and all the external differences merge into internal unity."
- **The divine play preserved.** "The saṃskāras of all individuals could be withdrawn
  in one moment if Parama Puruṣa so desired. But He does not because it would stop His
  entire creative flow and lead to the dissolution of this world" — so "human beings
  must strive to attain individual salvation."

# In the Discourses on Neohumanist Education (1970–1971) — the citta-store, latent symbols, and the limits of science

**DNE 30 (Cerebral and Extra-Cerebral Memory, 30 Dec 1970).** The citta stores
the accumulated saṃskāras of perception: perception "is registered in the unit
mind through the nerve cells," but once the nerve-cell impression fades, "the
perceived image is stored in the citta or ectoplasmic mind-stuff and not the
brain. Hence the mind will have to labour hard to reformulate the image … from
the accumulated saṃskāras or the mental reactive momenta of past actions." The
memory image thus lives first in the nerve cells of the brain and is then
stored in the citta — "the permanent abode of memory is the citta."

- **Extra-cerebral memory = the causal mind's revival of past-life saṃskāras.**
  The newly-born mind revives past-life experience without nerve-cell aid —
  *jātismara* remains vivid to about age twelve; the child's sleep-laughter and
  weeping is colloquially *deola káṯá*. "The revival of experiences of past lives
  is what we call 'extra-cerebral memory', and is principally the task of the
  causal mind."
- **Sādhana unveils the panorama.** By suspending the crude mind in the subtle
  and the subtle in the causal, the sādhaka "can then clearly visualize that
  panorama of sequential events in the causal mind" — one life layered after
  another — and may transcend the gaps between lives. "After a long journey of
  hundreds of years one begins to visualize the saṃskāras of one's past lives."
- **Tagore's *pashcāter āmi*.** The reactive momenta pull one back ("Ke more
  tāniche pashcāte … Pashcāte tāniche more pashcāter āmi" — "my own mental
  reactive momenta were pulling me back"), or push the sādhaka forward:
  "Sammukhe ṭheliche more pashcāter āmi [My own reactive momenta are pushing me
  forward]." "The pashcāter āmi [the 'me' behind me] of the poem refers to the
  extra-cerebral memory."
- **Forgetfulness as blessing.** "Forgetfulness is a providential decree" and "a
  blessing because human beings feel burdened by the weight of one life. It
  would be impossible for them to carry the burden of many lives together."

**DNE 37 (Expression and Symbolization, 22 May 1971) — saṃskāras as latent
symbols.** Mental feelings are first "symbolized in the mental stuff"; feelings
that find no outward expression "constitute the Saṃskāras or the reactive
momenta. They remain in the mind as symbols." These *latent symbols* must one
day be expressed as *physical symbols*: "As long as these physical symbols do
not get expressed, you will be carrying the load of those mental symbols from
one life to another. This is the reason why people take rebirths until such time
when all their mental symbols get converted into physical expressions." Sarkar
applies the term *Prārabdha* to the saṃskāras "whether they are carried over
from your previous lives or have been acquired in the present life."

**DNE 3 (Synthesis and Analysis, 27 Jul 1958) — science cannot change
saṃskāras.** No scientific manipulation of glands or brain can alter the
accumulated momenta: "However, science can never change people's saṃskāras.
Therefore, for the development of their personalities, there is no alternative
for human beings other than to perform sadhana." (The same chapter states "the
brain is the receptacle of the mind, and the mind is the receptacle of the
saṃskāras.")

# In *Varna Vijinana* (1983) — saḿskára as the invariant determinant of psycho-acoustic notes

*Varna Vijinana* 9 (*Psycho-Acoustic and Inferential Acoustic Notes*, 14 August
1983, Calcutta) applies the saṃskāra-doctrine to the differences between human
collectives:

- **Two kinds of difference in psycho-acoustic notes.** "Just as psycho-acoustic
  notes differ from person to person, a similar difference can be observed from
  collective to collective. These differences are of two kinds: due to saḿskára
  and due to inclination."
- **Saḿskára — from action, invariant.** "Saḿskára emanates from action. It does
  not differ with regards to time, place or person." Certain types of saṃskāras
  "encapsulate the differences between one collective and another" — the same
  invariant momentum developed in the sūtra-layer account.
- **Inclination — nervous structure + environment.** "Inclination depends on two
  factors: nervous structure and the influence of personal and collective
  environment." The composition of the nerves "is, in turn, based on racial
  constituents," and climate exerts its own influence (cf. *Bhinna-rucirhi lokáh*
  [people have different likings]).
- **Language and saṃskāra.** Because words are created "based on these
  psycho-acoustic notes," and the notes carry the saṃskāra-determined and
  inclination-determined differences of each people, pronunciation itself is a
  field where the two causes of difference converge — the same Indo-Aryan script
  "is pronounced differently in the throats of different races."

# Notes — fidelity

- Rebirth here is driven by **wave-parallelism between saṃskāra and womb**, a
  mechanistic account — not a soul "choosing" or a deity "assigning." Keep the
  framing. The 1962 EE footnote clarifies "living structures" renders *jaevii dehe*.
- **Infused vs acquired** (II-7) is a direct source-specific distinction; keep it
  alongside the sūtra-layer definition.
- The *karmāshaya* state ("reaction in its potentiality") corroborates the
  dagdhabīja account — the potential form persists even in the liberated state.
- **TK 3/5's equalization** is direct teaching: saṃskāras individuate, their removal
  fuses minds into one; the eyewitness account (the two embracing sādhakas, 12 May
  1969) is an editorial record — reproduce as a recorded event.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** a practical method of "withdrawing"
  saṃskāras — the chapter states the principle and the divine reason for its
  restraint.
- **DNE corroboration:** DNE 30 confirms the citta as the storehouse of
  saṃskāras and the extra-cerebral revival of past-life momenta; DNE 37
  independently frames saṃskāras as latent symbols awaiting physical expression;
  DNE 3 gives the corollary that science (glandular/brain manipulation) cannot
  alter saṃskāras. All consistent with the sūtra-layer and AV accounts — do not
  conflate DNE 37's "symbol" framing with the wave-parallelism account.
- **Could not verify in DNE:** a mechanism for how saṃskāras are "stored" in the
  citta beyond the claim that perception is registered in the nerve cells and
  the image later "stored in the citta or ectoplasmic mind-stuff"; the phrase
  "first lives in the nerve cells of the brain, then is stored in the citta"
  summarizes the passage's two-stage account, not a verbatim sentence.
- **VV 9 (1983) frames saṃskāra as one of two causes of difference** — with
  *inclination* (nervous structure + environment) as the other. This is the
  linguistics-level application of the sūtra-layer doctrine: saṃskāra "emanates
  from action" and "does not differ with regards to time, place or person."
  Keep the two causes distinct; do not collapse inclination into saṃskāra.
