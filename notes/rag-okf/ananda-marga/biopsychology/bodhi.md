# Definition

**Bodhi** (intuition) is the surplus part of the
[mahattattva](/biopsychology/mahattattva.md) over the
[ahaṃtattva](/biopsychology/ahamtattva.md). Ánanda Sútram 1-17.

# In the Discourses on Neohumanist Education (DNE 33, 26 Jan 1980) — intuition as the enlarged existential "I"

DNE 33 (*The Liberation of Intellect*) gives the parallel definition: "When the
existential 'I' feeling, or mahattattva, becomes larger in area than the
ahaṃtattva, then the enlarged area of existential 'I' feeling is called bodhi
(intuition)."

- **Intuition links the crude and subtle worlds.** "This intuition establishes
  the link between the crude world and the subtle world. And as a result of a
  closer link being established between the subtle and spiritual worlds … this
  intuition guides human beings along the path of spirituality."
- **Intuition is inborn.** "Intuition is an inborn faculty of human beings. Thus
  no effort is necessary for the liberation of intuition. The liberation of
  intuition comes automatically, without making any effort." (It is intellect
  that needs liberation; see [Buddhi](/biopsychology/buddhi.md).)
- **Intuition outshines intellect.** For those with both, "intuition being more
  colourful and more sweet, intellect becomes somewhat dimmed or lustreless" —
  "it is not that spiritual people are less intelligent, but that to them the
  intellect, as compared to the intuition, is less colourful."

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1980) — intuition as the eleventh organ

*Liberation from Staticity* (nh-1-10, 28 Jan 1980) marks the intuition off from
the intellect as a distinct, subtler faculty:

- **Intuition is subtler than the mind and special to human existence.** "Intuition
  cannot be precisely called the essence of mind. In fact, intuition is subtler than
  the mind, and a very special aspect of human existence. Intuition should be called
  the eleventh organ or the sixth sense."
- **The yardstick of the intuitional sphere.** "Discrimination between holy and
  unholy, permanent and impermanent, pure and impure in the intellectual sphere has a
  certain yardstick of its own. This yardstick should remain unstained" — the
  intuition's purity is what dogma endangers.

# In *Varna Vijinana* (1983) — the Buddha's resolve for bodhi

*Varna Vijinana* 4 (*Phonetics – 4*, 10 July 1983, Calcutta) preserves the
Buddha's resolve-shloka from "the time of the Buddha, twenty-five hundred years
ago" — cited there to demonstrate the *cal* verbal root used in *ātmanepadii*:

> Ihásane shuśyatu me shariiram
> Tvagasthimáḿsaḿ pralayaiṋca yátu
> Aprápyabodhiḿ bahukalpadurlabháḿ
> Naevásanát káyamatashcalisyate

The verse's import: the body — skin (*tvag*), bone (*asthi*), flesh (*māṃsa*) —
may wither away, yet the seeker will not move from the seat until *bodhi* (which
the sūtra-layer defines as the surplus of [mahattattva](/biopsychology/mahattattva.md)
over [ahaṃtattva](/biopsychology/ahamtattva.md)) is attained. The resolve is the
determination that bodhi — however many kalpas it costs — takes precedence over
the body's survival.

## Relationships

- surplus of [Mahattattva](/biopsychology/mahattattva.md) over [Ahaṃtattva](/biopsychology/ahamtattva.md)
- subtler than [Buddhi](/biopsychology/buddhi.md)
- *DNE 33*: intuition = the enlarged existential "I" feeling over the ahaṃtattva; it links the crude and subtle worlds, guides spirituality, and is inborn (its liberation automatic)
- *VV 4*: the Buddha's resolve — "Aprápyabodhiḿ bahukalpadurlabháḿ / Naevásanát káyamatashcalisyate" (the body may perish, but no movement from the seat until bodhi) — the determination bodhi demands, cf. [Struggle](/philosophy/struggle.md)
- *NHN 1/10*: intuition is "subtler than the mind, and a very special aspect of human existence" — "intuition should be called the eleventh organ or the sixth sense"; its "yardstick" must "remain unstained"
