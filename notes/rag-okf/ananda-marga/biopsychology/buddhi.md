# Definition

**Buddhi** (intellect) is the citta-less surplus portion of the
[ahaṃtattva](/biopsychology/ahamtattva.md) over the [citta](/biopsychology/citta.md).
Ánanda Sútram 1-16: "If the periphery of the aham be greater than that of the
citta, the citta-less surplus portion is called the buddhi."

# In the Discourses on Neohumanist Education (DNE 33, 26 Jan 1980) — the liberation of intellect

DNE 33 (*The Liberation of Intellect*) restates the sūtra in process terms:
"when the ahaṃtattva (ego), the subtler form of the citta (elementary mind),
has a greater area than that of the citta, then the enlarged area of ahaṃtattva
is called buddhi [intellect]." Where the citta merely establishes a link with
matter, the buddhi "acquires the capacity to direct matter according to its own
desire" — **"buddhi = the capacity to direct matter according to one's own
desire."** Its task is "to properly utilize all the properties of the universe,
of the expressed world."

**Intellect needs liberation.** Unlike intuition, whose liberation is automatic,
"different kinds of blow start coming from all sides on the human intellect, on
the natural intellect of human beings," so its spontaneous growth is "thwarted"
and it "starts degenerating." **Dogma stifles intellect** — the concoction of
  "so-called scriptures" and "irrational logic" (e.g., that women are not entitled
  to spiritual salvation, or that some are "destined" to poverty) "all combine to
  stifle the spontaneous growth of human intellect." Against dogma one must not
  merely keep away but "wage an uncompromising fight against it."

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1978–1980) — proper intellect and its greatest treasure

Two NHN chapters frame the intellect's sanctity and its liberation:

- **The one prayer: the right path for the intellect.** (nh-2-1, *Proper Intellect*, 24
  Aug 1978, Patna.) "If Parama Puruṣa asks you about your wish to get something, what
  will be your reply? You should say only one thing – 'I want your blessings so that my
  mind, my intellect, can move along the right path'." "Human beings meet their downfall
  due to flaws in their intellect." The Vedic prayer: *"Sa no buddhyā śubhayā
  saṃyunaktu"* — "My only prayer to Him is to always keep my buddhi (intellect) attached
  to shubha (blessedness)."
- **The Gāyatrī mantra.** (nh-2-1.) Quoted in full — *"Oṃ bhūrbhuvahsvah Oṃ
  tatsaviturvareṇyaṃ bhargo devasya dhīmahi dhiyo yo naḥ pracodayāt Oṃ"* — and glossed:
  "We meditate on" (dhīmahi) "So that He might lead our intellect along the path of
  righteousness." The upshot: "If the intellect is reformed, everything is attained. If
  the intellect goes astray, nothing is attained, even by attaining everything."
- **The greatest treasure.** (nh-1-10, *Liberation from Staticity*, 28 Jan 1980.) "The
  greatest treasure of human beings is their psychic faculty, or their intellect."
  "The essence of the human mind is known as intellect." Where dogma reigns, even "ten
  to twenty percent of the human intellect cannot be properly utilized; and the meagre
  amount of intellect used is not something worthwhile" — hence the liberating slogan,
  "Dogma – No more, no more!"
