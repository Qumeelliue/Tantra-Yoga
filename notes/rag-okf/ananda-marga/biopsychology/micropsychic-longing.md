# Definition

**Longing means "the urge that pulls forward" – it does not push.**
(DNE 29.) All micropsychic longings may be brought within **four broad
categories**: psycho-physical longing concerning inborn instinct;
psycho-physical longing concerning non-inborn instinct; psychic longing for
psychic pabulum (ābhoga); and longings concerned with **apexed** (pinnacled)
psychology.

# Explanation

**Ābhoga — "an object of enjoyment."** Pabulum is a Latin word whose meaning is
not comprehensive; "ābhoga in Sanskrit is the proper term. Ābhoga: ā – bhunj +
ghain. Ā means 'towards'; bhunj means 'to enjoy'; and ghain makes it a verbal
noun. So ābhoga means 'an object of enjoyment'." Objects of enjoyment may be
physical or psychic: "A noble idea is psychic pabulum. If the psychic object is
a sweet, psychic movement is towards something crude; if it is something
subtle, the mind moves towards the subtle and will become subtle." The
**culminating point** determines the outcome: "if the culminating point is
crude the mind gets crudified, and if the culminating point is subtle the mind
becomes subtle."

**The four broad categories** (DNE 29; DNE 31):

1. **Psycho-physical longing for physical pabulum — inborn.** A primordial
   longing common to all living beings, even to non-living elements (which
   express themselves by increasing or decreasing their inter-atomic or
   inter-molecular spaces). Its self-controlling faculty cannot move against
   the characteristics of the vital force; otherwise "disintegration and
   dissociation start both within and without the framework."
2. **Psycho-physical longing for physical pabulum — non-inborn.** Longings
   created after the development of the plexi and the change in the hormones
   secreted from those newly-developed plexi. In the individual, "one's
   physical structure may be saved if one applies or rather ascribes the
   Macrocosmic grandeur to physical pabulum." In a collective body, change of
   structure is possible only "if such a change is supported by Macrocosmic
   conation, otherwise not."
3. **Psychic longing for psychic pabulum (ābhoga).** The aspiration that
   psychic emanations move "from crude to subtle and cover all spheres of
   existence and non-existence." The aspirant may move forward only "till the
   psychic body maintains a close cooperation or parallelism with the physical
   waves." Otherwise the mind may dissociate from the physical structure, "for
   which a psychic diversion, a properly-measured diversion, is necessary or
   rather indispensable. And that diversion should always maintain proper
   equipoise and proper equilibrium with the fundamental principles of prama
   [dynamic adjustment]."
4. **Longing for apexed (pinnacled) psychology.** "Here the mental faculties
   along with the controlling faculties will move upwards and finally get
   pointed, and that point is lost in the Supra-Cognitive Entity. This is the
   goal of all living beings." The movement is not in many lateral directions
   but towards the Supreme Entity; the pointed mind "either merges in the
   Macrocosm, or gives up its individual existence in the Supreme Cognitive
   Faculty."

**Parallelism and prama.** "The mind will have to maintain parallelism with the
physical structure while moving towards a certain object. Association by proper
adjustment and parallelism between the psychic and physical bodies causes life,
and dissociation under adverse conditions results in death." If parallelism is
lost there is death, physical or psychic. "If balance is maintained and
propriety is properly measured, then the world will be an abode of prama
[dynamic adjustment] – heaven will be established on the earth." Collectively,
"a constant longing for all-round expression of micropsychic potentialities
will create a sort of balanced world, an earth of prama, an earth well-balanced
by prama," a bright future "for all humanity, and for the entire animate and
inanimate world." Such a collective practice helps the saincara and
pratisaincara movement of Macropsychic conation.

## Relationships

- ābhoga (psychic pabulum) is the third of the four categories, distinct from the two psycho-physical pabula and from apexed psychology
- the fourth category (apexed psychology) is the psychic dimension of [Sādhana](/practice/sadhana.md)
- every longing must maintain [parallelism with the physical structure](/biopsychology/prana.md) or death results
- governed by [Prama](/philosophy/prama.md) (dynamic adjustment); properly-measured psychic diversion keeps equipoise
- collective longings restructure the collective body only when "supported by Macrocosmic conation" — a [Cosmic Mind](/cosmology/cosmic-mind.md) involvement
- each unit longing is carried by the [Unit Mind](/biopsychology/unit-mind.md) in the [Citta](/biopsychology/citta.md)
- longings are conditioned by glands and plexi, the domain of [Bio-Psychology](/biopsychology/bio-psychology.md) and its [Cakra](/biopsychology/cakra.md) model
- micropsychic longings are the vehicle through which [Microvita](/cosmology/microvita.md) influence psychic structure
- apexed psychology helps the [Saincara and Pratisaincara](/cosmology/saincara.md) movement of Macropsychic conation

# Notes — fidelity

- The four-category taxonomy and the exact wording of the fourth category are
  direct teaching preserved verbatim from DNE 29 and DNE 31.
- **Ābhoga etymology** (ā + bhunj + ghain) is kept exactly as given; Sarkar
  notes pabulum is the common but less comprehensive substitute term.
- **"Culminating point"** doctrine is direct: subtle/crude outcome is fixed by
  the endpoint of psychic movement, not the starting point.
- The term **"an earth of prama"** and the "heaven on this earth of dust" motif
  appear in DNE 31 and are retained verbatim.
- DNE 31 is an RU discourse (30 May 1987, Anandanagar); DNE 29 is from
  Calcutta (3 June 1987). Dates are from source metadata.
- Could not verify in the cited passages: a catalogue of the "innumerable
  waves" of inborn longing, or an exhaustive enumeration of the four
  categories' quantitative scope beyond the doctrine given.
