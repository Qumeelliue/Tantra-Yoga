# Definition

An **indriya** (organ) is **not** the visible body part alone but a **composite of
four**. Ánanda Sútram 2-24:
*"Dvārah nāḍiirasah piiṭhātmakāni indriyāṇi"* — the indriyas are the composite of
(1) the **gateway** (*dvāra*), (2) the **nerve fibres** (*nāḍī*), (3) the **nerve
fluid / secretion** (*nāḍīrasa*), and (4) the appropriative **seat** (*piiṭha*) in
the brain.

# Explanation

The gate is where [tanmātras](/biopsychology/tanmatra.md) first bring objectivity;
the nerve fibres react to the tanmātric waves; the nerve secretions are vibrated
by them; and at the nerve-cell points the tanmātric waves are **conjoined with the
[citta](/biopsychology/citta.md)**. (Thus the optic nerve, optic fluid and optic
nerve-cell point behind the eye are *collectively* the "eye indriya.")

Functionally (2-23) the organs divide into:

- **Sensory organs** (*jñānendriya*) — eyes, ears, nose, tongue, skin — **receive**
  tanmātras from external objects.
- **Motor organs** (*karmendriya*) — vocal cord, hands, legs, anus, generative
  organ — **transmit** inherent tanmātras outward (via *saṃjñā*, internal sense).
- **Vital force** (*prāṇendriya*) — conjoins objectivity with the mind-stuff and
  creates in citta the senses of lightness, heaviness, warmth or coldness.

## In the Discourses on Neohumanist Education (DNE 4, 15 Jun 1970) — the three ways of study and organ compensation

DNE 4 (*Talks on Education – Excerpt D*) gives the pedagogic corollary of the
indriya-theory:

- **The three ways of studying.** "There are three ways of studying. First,
  there is studying using the eyes. Then there is studying by hearing. … Thirdly,
  there is studying by mudrā or movement. A blind person can know about something
  by touching it. This is called 'the tactual method.'"
- **Loss of one organ sharpens the others.** "If one indriya (sense organ) ceases
  to work, then the others become more sensitive. For example, there are five
  sense organs. Suppose that every sense organ has a power of 20 degrees. If any
  one of the sense organs ceases to function, then the power of that organ is
  shared between the remaining organs. That is, the remaining sense organs will
  get 25 degrees of power. A deaf man can understand everything with the science
  of the eyes and facial expressions and a blind man by hearing, smelling or
  touching." (cf. [Mudrā](/practice/mudra.md).)

## Relationships

- receives / transmits [Tanmātra](/biopsychology/tanmatra.md) waves
- conjoins tanmātric waves with [Citta](/biopsychology/citta.md)
- the prāṇendriya is an expression of [Prāṇa](/biopsychology/prana.md)
- *DNE 4*: three ways of study — eyes, hearing, and mudrā/tactual method; the power of remaining indriyas increases when one ceases (20° → 25°)

# Notes — fidelity

- An indriya is a **four-part composite** (gate + nerve + fluid + brain-seat), not
  merely the external sense organ. Preserve this definition.
