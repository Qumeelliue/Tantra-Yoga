# Definition

**Death is a long sleep in the causal mind.** Ánanda Sútram 3-3:
*"Kāraṇamanasi diirghanidrā maraṇam."* It is the cessation of activity across the
three minds of the [pañcakośa](/biopsychology/pancakosa.md).

# Explanation

The states of mind differ by which of the **three minds** (conscious /
subconscious / unconscious) remain active:

| State | Conscious | Subconscious | Unconscious |
|-------|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| **Wakefulness** | active | active | active |
| **Dream** | asleep | active | active |
| **Sleep** | inactive | inactive | awake (does the others' work) |
| **Death** | inactive | inactive | inactive |

Death sets in when a **vibrational disparity** arises in the *psycho-physical
parallelism* and even the unconscious mind becomes inactive.

After death, the **bodiless mind** (*videhī mānasa*) has **no doership and no
feeling of pleasure or pain** (3-5,
*"Videhiimānase na kartrtvaṃ na sukhāni na duhkhāni"*) — because perception of
weal and woe requires cerebral nerve cells and fibres (see
[indriya](/biopsychology/indriya.md)), which the bodiless mind lacks. Therefore
popular beliefs that a departed soul can be *pleased*, *made miserable*, or have
its *revenge* satisfied by rituals are, in Sarkar's words, "utterly and completely
wrong."

# In the prose discourses: Shrāddha

The discourse *Shrāddha* (AV 3, Patna, 18 Oct 1978) applies this teaching to the
funerary rite. *Shrāddha* is the universal name for a society's observance toward
its dead; etymologically it is "that which is offered with *shraddhā*" — and
*shraddhā* itself (from *shrat* + *dha*) is the mind moving, with its propensities,
toward an idea accepted as supreme. So the rite's own name means an offering of
**faith**, not of food.

Sarkar gives two independent reasons the traditional offerings (rice, sesame, ghee,
clothes, barley) cannot reach the departed. First, citing Maharshi **Charvaka**: if
a man in the courtyard cannot receive rice offered inside the house, a man in
*Aparaloka* certainly cannot — the goods are simply consumed or sold by the
*purohita*. Second, and more fundamentally, "the microcosm, or unit spirit, does not
require any food or clothes" at all. He brands the five-thousand-year practice
"scoundrels' philosophy" and a "cheating business," skewering it with the tale of
Ḍoman Sāhu haggling a paṇḍita down to ten rupees to buy his dacoit father a place in
heaven.

Positively, Sarkar holds that **social responsibility ends at cremation** —
thereafter the departed are "beyond our jurisdiction." The only legitimate shrāddha
is to offer *shraddhā*: a request to Parama Puruṣa to take care of them. The Ananda
Marga shrāddha accordingly "require[s] no edibles, no clothes or anything else."

# The prescribed shrāddha ceremony (Caryācarya 1/32)

Caryācarya 1/32 (1956 — predating the 1978 *Shrāddha* discourse) opens with the same
refutation as a standing note: *"The departed soul does not get any advantage from the
shrāddha ceremony … It is only meant for the psychic satisfaction of the person performing
it."* It then prescribes the **Ananda Marga shrāddha** itself: with an *ācārya*, at least
five healthy persons and the chief performer (the nearest relation), the assembly recites
the *Madhu Vātā* mantra and prayers to Parama Puruṣa — that the departed's *"immortal soul
attain higher and higher manifestation,"* attains *"liberation … from the bondages of all
worldly duties,"* and that *"You have freed us today from all the social responsibility we
bore"* — closing with *Sarve'tra sukhinah bhavantu* and *"Oṇṃ shāntih."* **No food or goods
are offered to the dead**; the rite is faith offered to Parama Puruṣa, consistent with the
doctrine that social responsibility ends at cremation.

Directives: the **mourning period must not exceed twelve days** and must avoid *"unnecessary
mortification or ostentatious penance."* Optionally a **male domestic animal of good breed
may be given in charity for public welfare** (never obligatory; minimal branding only for
the animal's safety; the giver maintains it to maturity, then the village collectively) —
and *"the butchering of an animal offered in charity will be considered a serious crime
against the society."*

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — the wave-mechanics of life and death

The 1959 cosmology (*Life, Death and Saṃskāra*, II-7) gives the physiological
mechanism behind the sūtra:

- **Life = psycho-physical parallelism; death = its loss.** "Association by proper
  adjustment and parallelism between the psychic and physical bodies causes life,
  and dissociation under adverse conditions results in death." An object, mental or
  physical, always emanates waves; when the physical and mental waves lose their
  parallelism, the composite factors dissociate. Elevation of the mental wavelength
  (contact with higher thought) or its degradation (meaner thought) can both break
  the parallelism.
- **The physical cause of death.** The nine vāyus (all but *dhanaṃjaya*) pass into
  eternal space (see [Prāṇa](/biopsychology/prana.md)); the psychic waves, finding
  no proper adjustment, also dissociate and pass out. The **detached mental body**
  carries the unexpressed momentum of previous lives ([saṃskāra](/biopsychology/samskara.md)).
- **Rebirth.** "It is now the duty of the Cosmic mutative force to provide it a
  proper physical parallelism for due expression" — the dissociated mind penetrates
  a subtle physical structure (a womb) whose waves match its momentum; life "comes
  into physical creation" when the spermatozoa-ovum structure (built up through
  lymph → blood → flesh → … → shukra) is entered by the dissociated mind.
- **Illustration (negative pratisaincara):** the Rāmāyaṇa story of Ahalyā turned to
  stone is read as the process of *negative pratisaiñcara* — the mind imbibing
  cruder waves takes a cruder body; conversely higher wavelengths can rarefy a lower
  being's psychic body for human association.
- **Doctrinal continuity:** the ātman "remains associated with the jīvātman,"
  witnessing the dissociated mind in *karmāshaya* (reaction in potentiality) — no
  food, clothes or ritual can reach it (cf. the [shrāddha refutation](#the-prescribed-shrāddha-ceremony-caryācarya-1-32) above).

## Relationships

- cessation across the three minds of the [Pañcakośa](/biopsychology/pancakosa.md)
- the bodiless mind has no [doership](/biopsychology/doership.md), no pleasure/pain
- it carries forward [saṃskāra](/biopsychology/samskara.md) toward rebirth
- the *shrāddha* rite cannot reach the dead — the unit spirit "does not require food or clothes," and the bodiless mind has no [indriya](/biopsychology/indriya.md) to receive offerings (cf. AS 3-5)
- social responsibility ends at cremation; the only valid shrāddha is to offer *shraddhā* — a request to Parama Puruṣa for the departed, now beyond our jurisdiction
- the prescribed AM shrāddha (*Caryācarya* 1/32): no offerings to the dead, only prayers/faith to Parama Puruṣa; mourning ≤ 12 days, no ostentation; optional male-animal charity for public welfare (butchering it = a serious social crime)
- *Idea and Ideology*: death = loss of **psycho-physical parallelism**; nine vāyus leave (dhanaṃjaya lingers); the Cosmic mutative force re-embodies the dissociated mind by wave-adjustment (cf. [Prāṇa](/biopsychology/prana.md), [Saṃskāra](/biopsychology/samskara.md))
- *DNE 30*: "Kāraṇamanasi dīrghanidrā maraṇam" — "Long sleep in the causal mind is death"; the disembodied mind floats in space with unexpressed [saṃskāras](/biopsychology/samskara.md), then with the mutative principle's cooperation finds a new physical base

# In the Discourses on Neohumanist Education (DNE 30, 30 Dec 1970) — long sleep in the causal mind

DNE 30 (*Cerebral and Extra-Cerebral Memory*) reaffirms the sūtra-layer
definition in the same words: **"Kāraṇamanasi dīrghanidrā maraṇam"** —
"Long sleep in the causal mind is death." "The crude mind remains active during
the wakeful state and the causal mind remains active during sleep. The causal
mind is the repository of infinite knowledge. Whatever saṃskāras we recreate in
the wakeful and dream states remain stored in the causal mind. When the causal
mind sleeps we call it 'death'."

The sequel echoes the II-7 mechanics of rebirth:

- **After death the disembodied mind floats with its unexpressed saṃskāras.**
  "After death the disembodied mind floats in the vast space with its unexpressed
  saṃskāras. Later on, with the cooperation of the mutative principle, the
  disembodied mind finds a suitable physical base."
- **Death = loss of psycho-physical parallelism.** If extra-cerebral memory
  persists beyond ~age twelve, "two minds will try to function in one body … A
  single body cannot tolerate the clashes of two minds, hence psycho-physical
  parallelism is lost leading to eventual death." (The loss of parallelism is
  the same mechanism II-7 gives for ordinary death.)
- **Two causes of death.** DNE 29 (*Bio-Psychology*, 3 Jun 1987) states them
  explicitly: "There are two causes of death: physical and psychic. Physical
  death occurs when the waves of the physical structure become crudified due to
  old age or any other physical deficiency. Psychic death occurs when psychic
  clash with either subtler or cruder thought-waves results in increasing or
  decreasing the wavelength of the mental waves respectively. In this case, also,
  loss of parallelism will occur, and the physical and psychic bodies shall
  dissociate."

# Notes — fidelity

- "No doership / no pleasure-pain after death" is a **direct teaching** (3-5),
  explicitly refuting ancestor-appeasement beliefs. Do not soften it.
- **Shrāddha (prose):** Sarkar gives two independent grounds the offerings fail —
  Charvaka's courtyard analogy and, more fundamentally, "the unit spirit does not
  require any food or clothes." He brands the rite "scoundrels' philosophy" / a
  "cheating business." Direct teaching — do not soften.
- **Positive reinterpretation:** social responsibility ends at cremation; the only
  legitimate shrāddha is to offer *shraddhā* (faith) — a request to Parama Puruṣa to
  care for the departed. The Ananda Marga shrāddha needs "no edibles, no clothes."
- **Scope / could not verify:** the "no nerve cells" mechanism is from AS 3-5
  (already cited), not the Shrāddha discourse; "tarpaṇa" / "piṇḍa" do not appear in
  its text (so "tarpana" was deliberately dropped from the aliases).
- **Caryācarya corroboration (1956):** the prescribed AM shrāddha (Caryācarya 1/32)
  repeats the refutation ("the departed soul does not get any advantage … only … psychic
  satisfaction of the person performing it") two decades *before* the 1978 discourse, and
  supplies the actual rite (Madhu mantra + prayers, ≤ 12-day mourning, optional male-animal
  charity for public welfare). Direct teaching, consistent across both sources.
- **Idea and Ideology (1959)** frames death **mechanistically** (wave-parallelism,
  the nine vāyus, re-embodiment by the Cosmic mutative force) — compatible with the
  sūtra-layer "long sleep in the causal mind," do not conflate the two framings.
- **DNE 30 corroboration:** repeats the sūtra verbatim ("Kāraṇamanasi
  dīrghanidrā maraṇam") and the II-7 mechanics (floating disembodied mind,
  mutative principle, new physical base); the "two causes of death" statement is
  from DNE 29, not DNE 30 — keep the attribution exact.
