# Definition

**Prāṇa** (vital energy) is the resultant force, arising from internal and
external friction among the [five fundamental factors](/cosmology/five-fundamental-factors.md),
that finds a **nucleus** within a physical structure and maintains its
solidarity. Ánanda Sútram 1-10 (as *bala*, energy) and 1-11.

# Explanation

The clash of factors under [guṇa](/cosmology/guna.md) influence produces *bala*
(energy), present in all objects in varying degree. When this resultant force
forms a nucleus in a body, the active energies are collectively **prāṇāh** —
plural in Sanskrit because it denotes the **ten vāyus** (vital forces).

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — the ten vāyus & prāṇendriya

The 1959 cosmology grounds and extends the sūtra:

- **Prāṇa vs prāṇāh.** *Prāṇa* ("energy") is "the eternal game between the Cosmic
  cause and its crudest effect" — the two opposing forces (interial, centre-seeking;
  exterial, fissiparous) developed under *bala*. *Prāṇāh* ("vital energy") is the
  *collection* of ten vāyus — five interial and five exterial — "the cooperative
  activity" that maintains structural solidarity. Life expresses only where the
  resultant is interial and the environment congenial (else *jadāshpoṭa*).
- **The ten vāyus** (II-7): five internal — *prāṇa* (navel–vocal cord;
  inhalation/exhalation), *apāna* (anus–navel; urine/stool), *samāna* (navel;
  adjustment between prāṇa and apāna), *udāna* (throat; voice), *vyāna* (blood
  circulation, afferent/efferent nerves); five external — *nāga* (jumping/extension),
  *kūrma* (contraction), *kṛkara* (yawning), *devadatta* (thirst/hunger),
  *dhanaṃjaya* (sleep/drowsiness, and the deep sleep of death). At death the nine
  (all but dhanaṃjaya) pass out; dhanaṃjaya remains until cremation/decay.
- **Prāṇendriya — the sixth "organ".** Its position is the *yogic* heart-centre —
  the middle point of the *anāhata* cakra (not the palpitating heart). It is *not*
  counted among the ten indriyas: it belongs to the tattvas (being a vāyutattva)
  and is "more or less a correlated activity of ten analysed sub-factors of
  vāyutattva." Its activity is pulsative (*saṃkoca-vikāśī*); in the state of pause
  citta receives the in-coming tanmātras and perception is correct — the
  psycho-philosophy behind **prāṇāyāma**: the sādhaka lets prāṇendriya remain in
  the pause, merging the paused unit mind "into the ocean of consciousness." It
  feels the subtler experiences (soft/hard, melodious/harsh, hot/cold, kindness)
  that fall outside the five crude perceptions; Sarkar prefers the term
  **bodha vivikti** (the other organs he would call *bodhendriyas*).
