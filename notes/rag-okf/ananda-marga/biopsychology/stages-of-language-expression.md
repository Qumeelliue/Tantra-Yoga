# Definition

**"There are six stages to the expression of language: Parā, Pashyantii,
Madhyamā, Dyotamānā, Vaekharii and Shrutigocarā."** The seed of the idea lies in
the Mūlādhāra Cakra; expression passes through Pashyantī (sprouting,
Svādhiṣṭhāna), Madhyamā (consolidated picture, Maṇipura) and Dyotamānā
(vibrational) until Vaekharī (vocal-cord) and Śrutigocarā (articulated word).
"In all languages Parā, Pashyantii, Madhyamā and Dyotamānā are uniform. Only in
the fifth and sixth stages is the expression different." Therefore "it is absurd
to divide humanity on the basis of language." (17 June 1979, Calcutta; NH 2/6.)

# Explanation

- **Parā — the seed.** "Parā Shakti, the seed of all expression, lies in the
  Muladhara Cakra, but the seed is not transformed into language. It is just the
  seed of the idea. This is called Parā in Saṃskṛta."
- **Pashyantī — the sprout.** "Pashyantii, the stage where the seed of the idea
  sprouts, lies in the next higher Cakra, that is the Svadhiṣṭhāna."
- **Madhyamā — the consolidated picture.** "In the next stage, Madhyamā, the idea
  is consolidated to give a picture to it. That is to say, people visualise the
  form of the idea they want to convey. In the Maṇipura Cakra there is an urge to
  transform the idea into sound form." The familiar experience of "such and such
  word is in my mind but I cannot articulate it" means "the flow of the expression
  has reached Maṇipura Cakra but it is not transmitted through spoken words."
- **Dyotamānā — vibrational.** "In the next stage, that is Dyotamānā, the idea
  acquires the form of language. Dyotamānā means vibrational. In this stage there
  is some vibrational expression but the exact word is not formed."
- **Vaekharī — the vocal cord.** "In the fifth stage, when the vibration reaches
  the vocal cord, the idea gets transformed into language. This is Vaekharii
  Shakti or the transformation of ideas into language."
- **Śrutigocarā — the articulated word.** "The last stage is called Shrutigocarā
  and occurs when the word gets vocalized with the help of the tongue. These are
  the six main stages of the expression of language."
- **Uniformity and difference.** "In all languages Parā, Pashyantii, Madhyamā and
  Dyotamānā are uniform. Only in the fifth and sixth stages is the expression
  different."
- **Language egalitarianism.** "All languages are the languages of Parama
  Puruṣa." No language should be suppressed: "If anyone tries to suppress any
  language the result will be disastrous, because human beings will not tolerate
  any undue pressure on their Vaekharii and Shrutigocarā." "Hence, it is absurd to
  divide humanity on the basis of language."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Six-stage scheme is Sarkar's acoustic-cosmological linguistics.** Keep the
  exact Sanskrit names, their order, and the cakra mapping (Mūlādhāra → Parā,
  Svādhiṣṭhāna → Pashyantī, Maṇipura → Madhyamā) as stated.
- **The same doctrine may also be found in *Varna Vijinana* (1983)** — this could
  not be verified in the present pass against the vv chapters; only the NH 2/6
  discourse is cited here.
- **Anti-syncretism:** do not equate these six stages with Western
  speech-production models (articulation/phonation/…); Sarkar grounds them in the
  cakras and Shabdabrahma.
- **"Language is only a vehicle of expression"** — the discourse's frame; the
  six stages are offered precisely to show language cannot divide humanity.
