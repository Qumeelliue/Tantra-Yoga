# Definition

**Citta** is the objective or "done-I" mind — the coordinated totality of
**cittāṇu** (ectoplasmic particles, "mind-stuff") that evolve when crude matter
is pulverized by excessive clash. Ánanda Sútram 1-12 ("mind is born out of
matter") and 1-13.

# Explanation

A cittāṇu is subtler than ether; their aggregation around a unit body's totality
constitutes its citta — the **crudest stratum** of the [unit mind](/biopsychology/unit-mind.md),
corresponding to tamoguṇa dominance. Perceptions remain unperceived unless citta
identifies with their reflections on itself.

## In the Discourses on Neohumanist Education (DNE 30, 30 Dec 1970) — the permanent abode of memory

DNE 30 (*Cerebral and Extra-Cerebral Memory*) confirms the citta as the
**memory-stuff**: "Thus the brain is nothing more than a worldly machine for
mental recollection. Its various parts assist the mind in various ways. But the
permanent abode of memory is the citta." Perception is first "registered in the
unit mind through the nerve cells," but once the nerve-cell impression fades,
"the perceived image is stored in the citta or ectoplasmic mind-stuff and not
the brain." The citta is thus the ectoplasmic store that holds memory and
[saṃskāras](/biopsychology/samskara.md) — "the accumulated saṃskāras or the
mental reactive momenta of past actions" — out of which the mind reformulates
recollected images.
