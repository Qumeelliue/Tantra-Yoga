# Definition

**Praṇava** (also *oṃkāra*) is the **primordial cosmic sound** produced by
[Brahma's](/cosmology/brahma.md) act of thinking creation — the collective average
of all vibrations within Him — heard inwardly as the "sound of silence." It is
Brahma expressed as sound (**Shabda Brahma**). Etymology (*pra + nu + al*): "that
entity which guides one unto the supreme status."

# Explanation

- Oṃkāra emanates from the **Supreme Nucleus**: every creative act of the
  Macrocosmic Self produces vibration, hence a sound during the phase of creation.
- Oṃ = **a + u + ma**, the acoustic roots of the three phases of the cosmic cycle:
  *a* = creation, *u* = preservation, *ma* = destruction (by sandhi a+u → o). It
  "represents the entire system of creation." (From these, Sarkar derives
  Generator–Operator–Destructor → **G-O-D**.)
- It is the **collective sound of all sounds** of the universe, within and without;
  it is "**to be heard, and not pronounced**" — a never-ending sound the tongue
  cannot form — and so is known as **Shabda Brahma**.
- The **praṇava dhvani** is the actual inner divine sound experienced in
  [sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md): concentrating at the trikuṭi / vishuddha
  [cakra](/biopsychology/cakra.md), one hears it in a graded sequence as the inner
  senses develop — cricket → ankle-bells (*nupur*) → flute → ocean → bells → finally
  pure **"oṃ".**
- **After this sound the realm of [Saguṇa](/cosmology/saguna-nirguna-brahma.md)
  ends**; in Nirguṇa Brahma there is no expression, hence no sound.

> Two senses, both preserved: cosmically *oṃkāra is praṇava* (the universal sound);
> but for the individual, **"his or her [Iṣṭa](/practice/ishta.md)-mantra is
> praṇava"** — the personal practice-mantra functions as the bow guiding one to
> Brahma. The mantra *practice* lives in [Mantra](/practice/mantra.md).

# In *Varna Vijinana* (1983) — shabdabrahma and the glyph of a-u-ma

*Varna Vijinana* grounds the praṇava in the Vedic teaching of *shabdabrahma* and
traces the *a-u-ma* glyph to the Brāhmī script:

- **Shabdabrahma and praṇava.** (vv-19) "In the New Testament it is said: 'In the
  beginning was the word.' In the Veda it is also said – shabdabrahma.
  Prańavátmakaḿ brahma." — the praṇava as the sonic essence of brahma, the
  word-form (shabda) as brahma's own expression.
- **Creation as a-u-ma and the origin of the letters.** (vv-20) "When script was
  invented the sages thought that the creation was a composite of a-u-ma, that is,
  creation, preservation and destruction." The same three-phase composite is here
  tied to the glyph-genesis of the Brāhmī letters: the manifest universe
  (káryabrahma) is symbolized by the cross of [Puruṣa](/cosmology/purusa.md) and
  [Prakṛti](/cosmology/prakrti.md), "so this cross became ka in the Bráhmii
  script." The *a-u-ma* composite thus underlies both the cosmic cycle and the
  written script.

## Relationships

- [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md) expressed as sound (Shabda Brahma)
- arises in the creative phase ([saiñcara](/cosmology/saincara.md)); a-u-ma = the cosmic cycle phases
- bounded by [Saguṇa & Nirguṇa Brahma](/cosmology/saguna-nirguna-brahma.md) (no sound in Nirguṇa)
- its seed-sounds are acoustic roots of the [Sṛṣṭimātṛkā](/cosmology/srstimatrka.md)
- in the personal frame the [Iṣṭa](/practice/ishta.md)-[mantra](/practice/mantra.md) functions as praṇava; heard at the [Cakras](/biopsychology/cakra.md) via [sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md)
- *VV 19*: "In the Veda it is also said – shabdabrahma. Prańavátmakaḿ brahma" (the praṇava as the sonic essence of brahma)
- *VV 20*: the creation is "a composite of a-u-ma, that is, creation, preservation and destruction"; the cross (Puruṣa × Prakṛti) "became ka in the Bráhmii script" — the glyph-genesis of the letters (cf. [Sṛṣṭimātṛkā](/cosmology/srstimatrka.md), [Acoustic Roots](/cosmology/acoustic-roots.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- The **G-O-D** acronym and the Brahmā/Viṣṇu/Maheshvara derivation are Sarkar's own
  etymological-devotional framing (direct teaching in AV 34/8), not independent
  linguistics. The "In the beginning there was sound…" line is his deliberate echo
  of the Johannine prologue.
- The meaning of *praṇava* **shifts by frame** (cosmic = oṃkāra; personal =
  iṣṭa-mantra) — AV 23/20 states both; preserve both, do not flatten.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** a direct Tāraka-Brahma link (Parama
  Puruṣa/Brahma is named as source/goal, not Tāraka Brahma specifically).
- **VV corroboration (1983):** VV 19 cites the Vedic "shabdabrahma.
  Prańavátmakaḿ brahma" — the praṇava-as-sonic-essence claim is direct teaching;
  VV 20 ties the a-u-ma composite to the glyph-genesis of the Brāhmī script
  (creation/preservation/destruction, and the cross → ka). The VV and AV passages
  are independent discourses — kept as distinct sources.
