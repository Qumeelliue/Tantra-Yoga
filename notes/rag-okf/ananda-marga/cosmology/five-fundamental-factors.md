# Definition

The **five fundamental factors** (bhūtas) are the stages into which Consciousness
crudifies under increasing [guṇa](/cosmology/guna.md) bondage, from subtlest to
crudest: **ethereal** (ākāśa / vyoma), **aerial** (marut), **luminous** (tejas),
**liquid** (ap), **solid** (kṣiti). Ánanda Sútram 1-8…1-10.

# Explanation

As tamoguṇa intensifies, the ethereal factor of the Cosmic Mind grows cruder in
order vyoma → marut → tejas → ap → kṣiti, each step adding an attribute (sound,
touch, …) while contracting in dimension and raising internal friction. If the
binding pressure exceeds the solid factor's balance (*bhūtasāmya*), equipoise is
lost and **jaḍasphoṭa** (explosion of matter) pulverizes it back toward subtler
factors (negative [saiñcara](/cosmology/saincara.md)). Where the tensional
disparity is moderate, **living organisms** arise instead of explosion.

# In *Varna Vijinana* (1983) — the five factors as the ādityas

*Varna Vijinana* 17 (*Proper Names – 2*, 9 October 1983, Calcutta) gives the
five-factor doctrine its name-theory:

- **The word āditya.** "In old Sanskrit the word áditya means 'one who collects
  what is due to them'."
- **The five factors collect what is due to them at death.** "After the death of a
  living being their quinquelemental existence – that is, solid, liquid, luminous,
  aerial, ethereal – these five factors collect what is due to them."
- **The twelve ādityas.** "The stars, planets, atoms and molecules, these
  luminaries and particles are an aggregate of twelve factors. Since each one of
  them collects what is due to them they are called 'the twelve ádityas'."

## Relationships

- produced-by increasing [Guṇa](/cosmology/guna.md) bondage (along [Saiñcara](/cosmology/saincara.md))
- their clash yields [Prāṇa](/biopsychology/prana.md)
- imbalance → *jaḍasphoṭa* (explosion of matter)
- *VV 17*: at death the quinquelemental factors (solid, liquid, luminous, aerial, ethereal) "collect what is due to them"; stars/planets/atoms are "an aggregate of twelve factors" — the "twelve ádityas" (*áditya* = "one who collects what is due to them")
