# Definition

**Ātman** is the Soul — in spiritual parlance, **one**. Its reflection on a unit
mind is the **jīvātman** (unit soul); the Reflector-Soul is the **Paramātman**
(Supreme Soul). Ánanda Sútram 2-8: *"Viṣaye puruṣāvabhāsah jiivātmā"* — "The
reflection of Puruṣa in a unit object is called the jiivātmā."

# Explanation

The [Ātman](/cosmology/brahma.md) (the witnessing entity) goes on reflecting
itself on the mind in whatever condition it be — manifest (animate beings,
plants) or unmanifest (inanimate earth, iron). **The reflection of the Soul on
the mind is the jīvātman** (also *aṇucaetanya*, microcosmic consciousness); the
Reflector-Soul is then **Paramātman** / *Pratyagātman* (also *Bhūmā Caetanya*,
Macrocosmic Consciousness). The totality of microcosms is the Macrocosm, since
every mind or crude entity holds the Supreme Spirit according to its capacity.

2-9 (*"Ātmani sattāsaṃsthitih"*) establishes the Ātman as the **final
substratum**: an object finds substantiation in [citta](/biopsychology/citta.md);
citta in the [Doer-"I" / Ahaṃtattva](/biopsychology/ahamtattva.md); the Doer-"I"
in the sense of existence ([Mahattattva](/biopsychology/mahattattva.md), "I am").
But "I am" itself depends on **"I know"** — and this "I" of "I know" is the Soul.
Hence the sense of all entities depends on the Ātman.

# In *Elementary Philosophy* (1955)

Chapters 4–5 approach the Soul by **elimination**: a human possesses both ātman and a body
and is therefore *"the owner of ātman and the body."* The familiar feeling of **"I" is not
the ātman** — it is **buddhitattva**, *"only a part of the mind,"* dependent on unit
consciousness *"as a plank of wood is dependent on a tree."* (Note the terminology: these
chapters call the pure "I"-feeling *buddhitattva*, whereas the Ánanda Sútram layer above
names the sense-of-existence *Mahattattva* — the two framings are kept **distinct, not
equated**.) Ahaṃtattva and citta are its *"cruder functional forms,"* and the body *"merely
forms a shelter (ādhāra) for it."*

The ātman itself is the **non-doer witness**: it neither acts nor reaps results but *"only
witnesses the actions and also the results thereof"* and *"only remains a spectator in the
human body,"* being **beyond the scope of mind**. It is **immortal and "always unchanged,"**
and, being a multiple of consciousness within Saguṇa Brahma, *"unit consciousness is also
Bhagavān."* The "I"-feeling is *"a changed or assumed form of unit consciousness"* (an actor
playing a role) and is precisely what blocks the supreme rank — its **cessation is mukti**.
Because Puruṣa and Prakṛti are *"inseparable counterparts,"* ātman carries unit prakṛti
(hence mind, and at death the saṃskāra that drives rebirth) until **final and complete
merger in the subtlest Cosmic Consciousness**.

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — the witness of the mind

The 1959 chapters (*Ātman, Paramātman and Sādhanā*, II-6; *Life, Death and
Saṃskāra*, II-7) develop the sūtra's witness-teaching:

- **The ātman is the witness *of the mind itself*.** "I exist," "I do," and done
  "I" are functional forms; the entity witnessing the *mahattattva* is the ātman —
  "the knower of the mind itself is the ātman." It is **not active**: "the ātman
  itself does not work, but its existence is the ultimate cause of knowing and
  doing." The ultimate knowership, doership and existence (*jñātrtva, kartrtva,
  sattā*) are all in the ātman — the three collectively named "witnessing entity."
- **The mind is an ever-changing functional organism, the ātman the absolute
  witness** of it "in all the aspects of time, in all the dimensions of space, and
  in all forms." Since the mind is only a never-ending clash of the three guṇas,
  an absolute entity must witness it.
- **Kūṭastha.** The Nucleus Consciousness — Puruṣottama in Saguṇa Brahma — is
  also named **Kūṭastha**; the ātman in the unit structure is *kūṭastha caetanya*,
  whose "controlling station is the middle point between the eyebrows," where the
  pituitary functions (cf. [Cakra](/biopsychology/cakra.md)).
- **Reflection with association.** As witness, Puruṣottama reflects on the unit
  mental plate via His *ota yoga* — "not like a reflection in a mirror but a
  reflection involving association like the rays of the sun." The reflection in
  each mental plate is the **jīvātman**; the Reflector-Soul, associated with the
  Cosmic Mind collectively via *prota yoga*, is Paramātman (see
  [Ota & Prota Yoga](/cosmology/ota-prota-yoga.md)).
- **Carried across death.** Because the mind exists, the mental plate exists and
  the reflection (ātman) "must remain"; the ātman therefore remains associated
  with the jīvātman, witnessing the dissociated mind "in a state of inaction (or
  converted into *karmāshaya*)" — the base that carries
  [saṃskāra](/biopsychology/samskara.md) to the next body.

## Relationships

- one Soul reflected on the [Unit Mind](/biopsychology/unit-mind.md) → jīvātman
- Reflector-Soul = Paramātman, the Macrocosmic witness (cf. [Paramaśiva](/cosmology/paramashiva.md))
- reflection of [Puruṣa](/cosmology/purusa.md) in a unit object (2-8)
- final substratum of [Citta](/biopsychology/citta.md) → [Ahaṃtattva](/biopsychology/ahamtattva.md) → [Mahattattva](/biopsychology/mahattattva.md) (2-9)
- *Elementary Philosophy*: the **non-doer spectator** beyond mind; the "I"-feeling (buddhitattva, cf. [Doership](/biopsychology/doership.md) / [Ahaṃtattva](/biopsychology/ahamtattva.md)) is what binds, and its cessation is [mukti](/practice/moksa.md)
- *Idea and Ideology*: the witness **of the mind itself** (jñātrtva/kartrtva/sattā all in the ātman); jīvātman's reflection is via [Ota yoga](/cosmology/ota-prota-yoga.md); the unit's "controlling station" is the mid-eyebrow (cf. [Cakra](/biopsychology/cakra.md)); the ātman persists across [Death](/biopsychology/death.md) to carry [Saṃskāra](/biopsychology/samskara.md)

# Notes — fidelity

- Jīvātman and Paramātman are **not two souls** but the same Soul under the
  relation of reflection (reflected-on-mind vs Reflector). Keep this non-dual
  framing; do not read jīvātman as an independent substance.
- *Elementary Philosophy* names the pure "I"-feeling **buddhitattva**; the Ánanda Sútram
  layer names the sense-of-existence **Mahattattva**. Preserve both as the source states
  them — do **not** silently equate the two terms.
- *Idea and Ideology* (II-7) adds a **compatible, more explicit terminology**: the
  ātman as witness *of the mind itself*, with knowership/doership/existence all
  located in the ātman, and the unit's "controlling station" at the mid-eyebrow.
  Keep it alongside the other two framings.
