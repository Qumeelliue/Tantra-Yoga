# Definition

Parama Puruṣa is **one** entity, but human beings call Him by **countless names**,
each name expressing a quality or relationship and carrying its own ideation
(*bhāva*). Sarkar teaches: *"A name is not just a word. The bhāva, or ideation, for
an object goes together with its name"* (SS 21). On the nāma–nāmī question he gives a
definite verdict: the **name (nāma) is "more powerful than the nāmī [the named]"** —
because the power latent (*stambhita*) in the named is *activated* and *expressed*
through the act of naming, just as Hanumān crossed the ocean "with your power" by
taking Rāma's name (SS 21).

# Explanation

**One entity, countless names.** In *Parama Puruṣa Is One — Countless Are His Names*
(AV 9) Sarkar opens with the mango (āmra, rasāla, ām, āmbā — one fruit, many names)
and applies it to God: "Parama Puruṣa is called by different names. Due to ignorance
human beings fight over the names." He etymologises names back to the one entity:
**Rāma** (ram + ghaiṇ) = "the one through whose ideation the yogiis get bliss," also
*rāti mahiidharah* (most luminous) and *Rāvaṇasya maraṇam* (death of depraved
propensities); **Nārāyaṇa** = the shelter (*ayana*) of *nāra* / Paramā Prakṛti;
**Hari** = "one who steals" (*harati pāpān*) — He steals the devotee's sins.
"Whatever meaning … we accept, it refers to the one and the same Parama Puruṣa."

**Each name unfolds a quality.** In *The Meaning of a Few Names* (AV 9) he derives
names by Saṃskṛta suffixes — **Krṣṇa** (kṛṣ + na) = "He who attracts everyone," also
"the nucleus of the universe" and "the colour black"; Sumantra = the best mantra;
Manomaya = "formed of mind" (Parama Puruṣa is *manomaya*, not *pāiñcabhūtamaya*).

**Nāma–nāmī: the name's power.** The dedicated discourse *Nāma and Nāmī* (SS 21)
addresses the "old problem — which is more powerful, Parama Puruṣa or His name?"
Verdict: the **name**, because its latent power is activated by the namer (figured by
the oṃkāra dot = potential, crescent = expression). One cannot name oneself; the
namer does the naming. The most activating name for each person is one's own **Iṣṭa
mantra** — "other names of Parama Puruṣa are no name at all" for that person.
Reaching the named *through* the name, the mind transcends relativity into samādhi;
ultimately "the name and the namer will no longer remain, only **the named** will
remain."

## Relationships

- the countless names all denote one [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md); the doctrine grounds why a sādhaka takes only ONE name in [kiirtana](/practice/kiirtana.md)
- the most activating, proper name for a person is that person's own [Iṣṭa mantra](/practice/ishta.md) (SS 21), tying nāma–nāmī to [mantra](/practice/mantra.md) practice
- *Bābā Nāma Kevalam* is the eight-syllabled siddha mantra repeated in [kiirtana](/practice/kiirtana.md) (AV 30); "bābā" etymologically = "dear" (bapra → bappa → bābā), the name expressing the love-relationship
- the name's latent vs expressed power is figured by the dot and crescent of [Oṃkāra](/cosmology/omkara.md) (SS 21)
- Krṣṇa as "He who attracts everyone / the nucleus of the universe" (AV 9) connects to [Krṣṇa](/people/krsna.md)
- taking the Name to reach the Named is the path of the devotee, grounding [bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md)

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching (etymologies):** all name-meanings are reproduced exactly as
  Sarkar gives them (Rāma = ram+ghaiṇ / rāti mahiidharah / Rāvaṇasya maraṇam; Hari =
  harati pāpān; Krṣṇa = kṛṣ+na; Nārāyaṇa = nāra+ayana; bābā = bapra→bappa→bābā). No
  dictionary etymologies were imported.
- **Nuance — NOT a flat "nāma = nāmī" identity.** The corpus does not teach that name
  and named are simply identical/inseparable. SS 21 ranks the **name as MORE powerful
  than the named** (the name *activates* latent power) and says that ultimately "only
  the named will remain." The *abheda* (inseparability) language applies to the
  equivalence of DIFFERENT NAMES of one Parama Puruṣa (Hanumān's shloka: "Shriinātha
  and Jānakiinātha are one and inseparable"), not to a strict name = named equation.
- "Taking the Name brings the Named" IS grounded, but as a **processual** teaching
  (through the name one reaches the named, then transcends into samādhi), not a
  metaphysical equation.
- **Could not verify:** an explicit "nāma and nāmī are one and the same" doctrinal
  statement (absent — flag any such claim as interpretation); a single verbatim gloss
  translating "Bābā Nāma Kevalam" (the gloss is a synthesis of "bābā = dear" + the
  siddha-mantra identification).
