# Definition

Ánanda Sútram distinguishes **two modes of transformation** (*pariṇāma*) in the
flow of creation:

- **Svarūpa pariṇāma** — *homomorphic evolution* (4-2);
- **Sadṛśa pariṇāma** — *homogenesis* or similitude (4-5).

# Explanation

**Svarūpa pariṇāma** is the **pre-manifest churning of the guṇas** within the
[triangle of forces](/cosmology/srstimatrka.md): sattva ⇄ rajah ⇄ tamah
transforming back and forth endlessly, and polygons resolving into triangles. It
is *qualitative self-transformation* with no curvilinear, sequential offspring —
the theoretical stage.

**Sadṛśa pariṇāma** is the **sequential flow of kalās** (curvatures) in the
*manifest, vibrational world*: each *kalā* is **similar but not identical** to the
one before (*kalā pravāha*). Successive forms therefore resemble their source —
"human begets human, tree begets tree" — yet across long spans the accumulated
small differences become vast. This homogenetic flow evolves the mental and
physical worlds (the [Bhavānī phase](/cosmology/stages-of-prakrti.md)).

## Relationships

- svarūpa pariṇāma drives the [Sṛṣṭimātṛkā triangle](/cosmology/srstimatrka.md) (guṇa-churning)
- sadṛśa pariṇāma drives the manifest [Bhavānī phase](/cosmology/stages-of-prakrti.md) and [saiñcara](/cosmology/saincara.md)
- both are transformations of the three [Guṇa](/cosmology/guna.md)

# Notes — fidelity

- The pair is **structurally distinct**: svarūpa = qualitative, pre-manifest;
  sadṛśa = sequential, manifest, hereditary. Do not conflate them or read either
  as Darwinian "random variation" — these are guṇa-driven transformations.
