# Definition

[Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md) is **Satya** — Absolute Truth, the unchangeable;
the universe is **also** truth, but **relative** (*āpekṣika*). Ánanda Sútram 2-14:
*"Brahma Satyaṃ jagadapi satyamāpekṣikam"* — "Brahma is Absolute Truth; the
universe is also truth, but relative."

# Explanation

The apparent changes perceived on the "body of Brahma" under the influence of
[Prakṛti](/cosmology/prakrti.md) are governed by **three fundamental relative
factors — time, space and person** (*deśa-kāla-pātra*). These changes are:

- **not false** — they are genuinely perceived; yet
- **not eternal truths** — they depend on the relativity of the three factors.

Therefore the world is a **relative truth**. The unit-entity or
[unit mind](/biopsychology/unit-mind.md), in its progressive bearing, is itself
involved with these three factors, so its existence too is relative. One relative
entity appears as a "spiritual truth" to another — and so the changeable world
appears true to the changeable living unit.

# In the Discourses on Neohumanist Education — a small island and a passing phenomenon

**DNE 37 (Expression and Symbolization, 22 May 1971) — the island of the
expressed.** Relative symbolization has three properties: *first*, "that which
gets symbolized is never complete" — "between an idea and its expression a gap
remains"; *second*, "symbolization is not of the same character" — "feelings and
ideas never find[] identical expression and identical symbolization" across
individuals; *third*, symbolization "cannot remain static" — it changes with
time and space ("Hence there is change according with time and space"). Because
the organs can grasp so little, "the expressed universe is a very small island
in the ocean of the unexpressed universe."

**DNE 39 (Prápta Vákya and Ápta Vákya, 25 Feb 1970) — a changing reality on
three pillars.** "This quinquelemental universe is a relative truth, a changing
reality, a passing phenomenon – a passing flow of constantly changing events. It
rests on the three pillars of the relative factors – time, space and person."
Only Parama Puruṣa is exempt: "The only eternal truth is Parama Puruṣa. He is
anadi, beginningless, endless, all-pervasive; an entity beyond the scope of
time, place and person."

# In *Varna Vijinana* (1983) — the three bases of establishment and the science of time, place and person

*Varna Vijinana* restates the same three factors as the bases on which anything
is established:

- **Three bases of establishment.** (vv-15) "In order for anything to be
  established, it has to be established on the basis of place or land, idea or
  time." The grammarian Pāṇini "accepted three bases of things – time, idea and
  base," from which the three locatives were created — the locative of time, the
  locative of idea and the locative of base. (The *deśa-kāla-pātra* triad here
  reads as *place, idea, time* — the same three relative factors under a
  grammatical framing.)
- **Science changes with time, place and person.** (vv-2) "Here it will have to be
  borne in mind that the application of science changes according to the changes
  in time, place and person." The Vedic *kśa* (k-śa) becomes *kśa* (k-cha) "with
  a change in person"; pronunciation "takes place in accordance with differences
  in individual body structure, changes in blood and climatic changes."

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1979) — time, causal chains, and the mind as measure

*Dogma – No More* (nh1-7, 23 September 1979) frames the relative factors from the
mind's side:

- **Time.** "What is time? Time is psychic measurement of the motivity of action. So
  everywhere the psychic body must be present."
- **One and infinite as projections.** "Parama Puruṣa is one" and "He is infinite" — "both
  are different projections of the same mind."
- **Everything is causal.** "Everything in our universe is causal. Everything is guided by,
  controlled by, the theory of cause and effect. This theory of cause and effect was
  propounded by Maharshi Kanada long ago." As per the theory, "an effect is the cause of the
  following stage, a cause is the effect of the previous stage"; and "the cause is generally
  subtler than the effect."
- **The limit of the mind.** "if one tries to go beyond the seed of the mind, one cannot go,
  cannot move, because all these measurements are done by the mind."

## Relationships

- contrasts Absolute Truth ([Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md)) with the relative world
- relativity arises under [Prakṛti](/cosmology/prakrti.md) via time, space, person
- binds the [Unit Mind](/biopsychology/unit-mind.md) in its progressive bearing
- *DNE 37*: three properties of relative symbolization (never complete; not identical across individuals; changes with time and space); "the expressed universe is a very small island in the ocean of the unexpressed universe"
- *DNE 39*: the quinquelemental universe is "a relative truth, a changing reality, a passing phenomenon" on the three pillars of time, space and person; only Parama Puruṣa is eternal (*anādi*)
- *VV 15*: "In order for anything to be established, it has to be established on the basis of place or land, idea or time" — Pāṇini's three bases (locatives of time, idea, base), the grammatical form of the same relative factors
- *VV 2*: "the application of science changes according to the changes in time, place and person"
- *nh1-7 (1979)*: "Time is psychic measurement of the motivity of action"; "Parama Puruṣa is one" and "He is infinite" are "different projections of the same mind"; everything causal per Maharṣi Kaṇāda ("an effect is the cause of the following stage, a cause is the effect of the previous stage"; "the cause is generally subtler than the effect"); one cannot go "beyond the seed of the mind" (cf. [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- "Relative" (*āpekṣika*) is a precise middle term: the world is **neither
  illusory (mithyā) nor absolute**. Do not collapse this into a bare māyā-as-illusion
  reading.
- **DNE 39's "passing phenomenon"** is Sarkar's own wording for the same doctrine;
  the sūtra-layer "relative truth" is retained as primary. DNE 37's "very small
  island in the ocean of the unexpressed universe" echoes the same relativity —
  keep both framings.
- **VV (1983) corroborates the three factors:** VV 15's "place or land, idea or
  time" is the same triad in Pāṇini's grammatical framing (locatives of time, idea
  and base); VV 2 explicitly states "the application of science changes according
  to the changes in time, place and person." Direct teaching — keep alongside the
  sūtra triad without forcing the terms into identity (VV 15 speaks of "idea,"
  DNE 39 of "person").
- **nh1-7 (1979) grounds time as "psychic measurement of the motivity of action"** — an
  explicit psychical definition of time, distinct from but compatible with the
  *deśa-kāla-pātra* triad; the Kaṇāda cause-effect theory is attributed by name and quoted
  verbatim.
