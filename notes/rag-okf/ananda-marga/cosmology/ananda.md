# Definition

**Ānanda** is *infinite* happiness — limitless [happiness](/biopsychology/sukha.md)
(*sukham anantam ānandam*). Ánanda Sútram 2-3: *"Sukhamanantamānandam"* — "Infinite
happiness is ānanda (bliss)."

# Explanation

No living being is content with a little; one wants *endless* happiness. Such
endless happiness is **a condition beyond the precincts of weal and woe**: the
sense of happiness perceivable through the senses oversteps the limit of the
sense organs when it is established in limitlessness. This limitless happiness is
ānanda.

The limitless object is **one, not many** — "many-ness can have no quarter in
endlessness." That self-same blissful entity is therefore [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md)
(2-4: *"Ānandaṃ Brahma ityāhuh"*), the composite of Shiva and Shakti. Because the
living being thirsts for limitlessness and no limited object can quench that
thirst, **only establishment in Brahma ends all craving** (2-5:
*"Tasminnupalabdhe paramā trṣṇānivrttih"*).

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — Saccidānanda corrected

The 1959 cosmology (*Life, Death and Saṃskāra*, II-7) refines the term
*Saccidānanda*, arguing that the stock translation "existence, knowledge and bliss"
is "not only loose terminology but incorrect":

- **Sat means "that which undergoes no change"** — a Transcendental Entity.
  "Existence" is a relative term (existence under conditions of time, place,
  person); *Sat* implies "unchangeable." The Transcendental Entity may *appear* to
  undergo metamorphosis but "actually undergoes no qualitative change" — so Sat can
  only mean the unchanging Transcendental Entity.
- **Ānanda is "divine bliss"** — "the bliss experienced subjectively and not in an
  objective manner" (consistent with the sūtra framing above).
- **Cit means "consciousness," not "knowledge."** It is by the power of *cit*
  (Citishakti) that the Cosmos is created and that the unit mind experiences or
  activates created substance — "This subjectivation is only due to the
  Consciousness or Citishakti in Brahma."
- In Saguṇa Brahma the whole Cosmos is "the thought-projection of the Macrocosm";
  the analysis of perception (organs → citta → ahaṃtattva → mahattattva → the
  witnessing [ātman](/cosmology/atman.md)) shows that the *real knower* is the
  ātman, "the knower of the mind itself" — ultimate knowership lies there, not in
  the mind.

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1978) — Dharmameghānanda

*Self-Realization Is the Real Knowledge* (DNE 40, 13 Dec 1978) names a specific
grade of ānanda, the bliss that attends constant memory of God:

- **Dharmameghānanda.** "Such people are called Dhruvāsmṛti – they can always
  remember God and thus feel a special kind of Ānanda or bliss in their minds.
  That Ānanda is called Dharmameghānanda." (Cf. [Dhruvāsmṛti](/biopsychology/memory.md),
  [Dharmamegha Samādhi](/practice/grades-of-samadhi.md).)
- **"Know all – know one."** At the pinnacle of intellect the whole world becomes
  the recollector's single point (vindu); "when one attains God, one becomes
  omniscient. Brahmavid sarvavid ātmavid brahmavid. ... If you want to know all –
  know one. Then only everything will be known to you."

# Notes — fidelity

- Ānanda here is **ontological**, not merely a pleasant emotion: it is identified
  with Brahma, the cosmic substratum — distinct from ordinary `sukha` which is a
  finite, wave-contact feeling.
- The **Saccidānanda correction** (II-7: sat = unchanging, not "existence"; cit =
  consciousness, not "knowledge"; ānanda = subjective divine bliss) is direct
  teaching — reproduce it exactly, do not fall back on the loose gloss.
