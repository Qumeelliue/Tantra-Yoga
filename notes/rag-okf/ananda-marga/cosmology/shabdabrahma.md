# Definition

**Śabda-Brahma** — "Sound as Brahman" — is Sarkar's name for the Supreme Cognitive
Principle expressing itself through sound. He grounds it in the Vedic line
(*Varna Vijinana* 19): *"Sound is the subtlest of the five inferences – sound,
touch, form, taste and smell. In the New Testament it is said: 'In the beginning
was the word.' In the Veda it is also said – shabdabrahma. Prańavátmakaḿ
brahma."*

# Explanation

**Sound, the subtlest inference (vv-19).** Sound stands first among the five
inferences (*tanmātras*) — sound, touch, form, taste, smell — and is therefore the
finest channel through which the Cognitive Principle first expresses. Sarkar sets
the Johannine prologue ("In the beginning was the word") beside the Vedic
*sabdabrahma, praṇavātmakam brahma* — Brahma whose essence is the *praṇava*
(oṃkāra).

**From external sounds to the first verbal roots (vv-19).** Language is born
acoustically: "In the distant past people imitated the different sounds they
heard, and with their help they tried to express the ideas that arose in their
minds." This sound-imitative word-formation Sarkar calls "acoustic derivation":
"This process of word formation is called 'acoustic derivation' and the original
verbal roots in all the world's languages were created in this way. The first word
forms in all the world's languages were the original verbal roots."

**Psycho-acoustic notes and the seven-note scale (vv-9).** Every percept creates an
inner vibration before it becomes a word: "First a vibration arises in the nerves
when an object is seen, then a mental sound is created and finally a word is made
based on that sound." The claim is totalising: "all sounds in this universe have
their origin in these psycho-acoustic notes." These sounds resolve into seven
partial sounds: "These specific sounds are chiefly divided into seven partial
sounds which we call the seven note scale. The composition of various sounds, the
creation of all languages and dialects, is produced from permutations and
combinations of this seven note scale. Shiva took the help of these fundamental
sounds to create the six ragas and thirty-six raginis of Indian classical music."

**a — the acoustic root of creation (vv-13).** The alphabet encodes cosmology:
"The reason is that a is the acoustic root of creation. Because a is the first
sound in the creation it has been placed at the beginning of the alphabet."

**ka — the first consonant, kāryya-brahma (vv-13).** The consonant series opens
with *ka* because it is the seed of the expressed universe: "The letter ka
prepresents saḿvrttibodhicitta, that is, ka is a physical transplantation of
psychic idea, thus it occupies the first position in the list of consonants. Ka is
káryyabrahma." (See [Saṃvṛtti-Bodhicitta](/cosmology/samvrttibodhicitta.md).)

**The akṣamālā — fifty letters on Parama Puruṣa's breast (vv-8).** The fifty
Saṃskṛta letters are a cosmic garland: "There are fifty letters in Sanskrit
beginning with a and ending with kśa. Since they are held together like a garland
they are collectively called the akśamálá, the garland of letters. Because this
garland of letters is held together in this universe on the breast of Parama
Puruśa through the dance of Paramá Prakrti, it is also called in philosophical
language the rudrákśa." (The Atharvaveda's image of Bhadrakálii with skulls is, per
Sarkar, a popular misreading: "It is, in fact, the garland of letters.")

**The ceaseless reverberation (vv-8).** Because sound is Brahma, no utterance is
lost: "Whatever sound has been produced on this earth, whatever song has been
sung, will continue to reverberate in the cosmic hub for infinite time. It has no
end. No one will be able to check it."

## Relationships

- the acoustic *substrate* whose individual seed-sounds are the [Acoustic Roots (Bīja Mantra)](/cosmology/acoustic-roots.md), and whose fifty-sound flow is the [Sṛṣṭimātṛkā](/cosmology/srstimatrka.md)
- its collective sound is aum — [Praṇava (Oṃkāra)](/cosmology/omkara.md); vv-19's "praṇavātmakam brahma" is the Vedic line behind the praṇava doctrine
- sound as Brahma: cf. [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md) and [Saguṇa & Nirguṇa Brahma](/cosmology/saguna-nirguna-brahma.md) — sound belongs to the expressive, Saguṇa side
- distinct from [Mantra](/practice/mantra.md): shabdabrahma is the cosmic sonic substrate; a mantra is the personal practice-sound (iṣṭa-mantra) carrying an acoustic root
- the seven-note scale and Shiva's six rāgas / thirty-six rāgiṇīs are the musical derivation from these sounds — cf. [Sāṃgīta](/practice/samgiita.md) (vv-9)
- the ceaseless reverberation of sound in the cosmic hub (vv-8) is the acoustic face of the subtle-vibration cosmos of [Microvita](/cosmology/microvita.md) (which move through sound)

# Notes — fidelity

- **Anti-syncretism.** This is Sarkar's **acoustic cosmology**, not a generic "Word
  of God" theology. Sarkar himself invokes John 1:1 as a *parallel* ("In the New
  Testament it is said:…"); it is his framing device, not an endorsement of Logos
  doctrine. Keep shabdabrahma inside the Praṇava / Vedic frame.
- **Distinct from mantra.** Do not collapse shabdabrahma into mantra practice: the
  personal iṣṭa-mantra lives in [Mantra](/practice/mantra.md) and
  [Dīkṣā](/practice/diksa.md); shabdabrahma is the cosmic acoustic substrate
  (vv-19, 9, 13, 8).
- **Separate discourses.** The five-senses statement and the first-verbal-roots
  statement are from vv-19; the seven-note scale / rāga material from vv-9; the
  akṣamālā / rudrākṣa and reverberation material from vv-8; the *a*- and *ka*-
  etymology from vv-13. These are complementary but distinct arguments — do not
  conflate their contexts.
- **Printed transliteration preserved.** Direct quotes keep the electronic
  edition's forms: "Prańavátmakaḿ", "saḿvrttibodhicitta", "káryyabrahma",
  "akśamálá", "rudrákśa".
- **"sonority" / "veda-logos"** are editorial aliases (indexing aids), not Sarkar's
  terms.
