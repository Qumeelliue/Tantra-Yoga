# Definition

**Tārakabrahma** is the bridge (*setu*) between **Nirguṇa Brahma** (the
metempirical, objectless state) and **Saguṇa Brahma** (the empirical, qualified
state). Ánanda Sútram 1-25: *"Bhāvah bhāvātiitayoh setuh Tārakabrahma."*

# Explanation

It is the common point linking the saguṇa and nirguṇa aspects of
[Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md) — the **Liberating (Tāraka)** entity. Tārakabrahma
appears in saguṇa (embodied) form as **Mahāsambhūti**. (The saguṇa/nirguṇa states
are the poles reached in [samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md).)

# In the prose discourses

Sarkar develops Tārakabrahma chiefly through the **sambhūti / mahāsambhūti**
distinction. In *Sambhūti and Mahāsambhūti* (SS 9) every created entity — "from the
mighty sun to the tiny ant" — is a *sambhūti* of Parama Puruṣa; ordinary great
beings, even the highest evolved *Iishvarakoṭi*, remain graded *avatāras* (*kalā-*,
*aṃsha-*, *khaṇḍāvatāra* — all incomplete). When collective degradation exceeds any
single sambhūti's capacity, Parama Puruṣa expresses Himself through "a special
structure" — **Mahāsambhūti** — to discipline and re-harmonise all sambhūtis.
Pointedly, "Shiva and Krṣṇa are **not** mentioned as avatāras"; "when the Supreme
Entity appears as Mahāsambhūti He is not regarded as an avatāra, rather He is called
Tāraka Brahma." *Krṣṇa Unparalleled* (AV 17) defines Mahāsambhūti as Parama Puruṣa
building a body from the five fundamental factors to launch "an ideological tidal
wave."

In social-leadership terms the ordinary guide is a *sadvipra*; the initiator of
revolutionary change is a *mahasadvipra* — "in the scriptures called Tāraka Brahma"
(AV 8). *Our Concept of Tāraka Brahma* (AV 33) locates Him at the "tangential touch"
of the Brahmacakra, within **both** Nirguṇa and Saguṇa — "Tāraka Brahma is a concept
of Tantra" — and gives His four marks: a **born guru with no guru of His own**; a
dharma-restoring mission; an advent that polarises society into moralists and
immoralists in a fight where dharma triumphs; and no need of sādhanā, which He yet
performs alongside the masses to set an example. He descends, Sarkar adds, because
"the human heart is not satisfied with the Impersonal Entity" and to guide a society
its own intellect cannot save (AV 12) — becoming "the Bābā of the created world."

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — Tāraka Brahma in the cosmology

The 1959 chapter *Ātman, Paramātman and Sādhanā* (II-6) gives the first (1959)
prose statement of the tangential-touch doctrine, consistent with the later
discourses:

- **The tangential touch.** Because the speeds of the guṇas differ, the unit mind's
  movement becomes elliptical, "the motion changes to oval from circular." Those who
  aspire to Puruṣottama merge in Saguṇa Brahma; "those who aim at mokṣa, where
  sādhana is the complete surrender of self into That (Nirguṇa Brahma, the
  Objectless Consciousness), get out of this Brahma Cakra by a tangential touch. At
  this point of tangential touch is the abode of Tāraka Brahma (who resides within
  the scope of both Nirguṇa and Saguṇa Brahma). **Tāraka Brahma is a concept of
  Tantra.**"
- **Sambhūti & Mahāsambhūti.** "In Tantra the whole creation is known as *sambhūti*."
  When Tāraka Brahma by His own will takes the help of the five fundamental factors
  (the *pañcabhūtas*), His physical entity comes within Saguṇa Brahma — in Tantra
  this is His **Mahāsambhūti**; otherwise He is Nirguṇa Brahma.
- **The third sādhana.** Only in Tantra is the sādhana of Tāraka Brahma "specially
  defined distinct from the sādhana of Nirguṇa and Saguṇa and has its own
  peculiarity": Saguṇa Brahma has infinite saṃskāras (and will long enjoy their
  fruits), Nirguṇa is the Objectless Entity with no action, but Tāraka Brahma "is
  the middle point and can fulfil the function of both," guiding, loving and
  favouring His children. The chapter closes with the devotee's surrender to Him —
  "the summum bonum of all spiritual sādhana, which only can lead to Him from where
  decline is not even imaginable" — noting "Tāraka Brahma is not a figure of
  philosophy — it is a creation of devotional sentiment."

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1978) — the advent is for jīva welfare

*The Physical, Psychic and Spiritual Strata* (nh1-2, 12 December 1978) states the
purpose of the advent in its very opening:

- **The advent is for jīva welfare.** "the very existence of Parama Puruṣa is meant for
  promoting the well-being of the jiivas [microcosms, living beings]. Had the question of
  the microcosmic well-being not existed, Parama Puruṣa would have remained as a nirguṇa
  [transcendental] entity. He would not have come down to the level of Saguṇa Brahma or
  Tāraka Brahma." His main purpose "is public welfare – the welfare of human beings."
- **The Gītā śloka.** To this end He has declared: *"Paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṃ vināśāya ca
  duṣkṛtām; Dharmasaṃsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge."* [I incarnate Myself in this
  world from age to age for the protection of the virtuous, the destruction of the wicked,
  and the restoration of dharma.] — and again: *"sambhavāmi yuge yuge"* ["I incarnate
  myself in every age"].
- **All three spheres.** "Human welfare" means "primarily, welfare in the spiritual sphere,
  and secondarily, in the mental and physical spheres"; "He will promote welfare in all
  three spheres of life."

## Relationships

- bridges the Saguṇa and Nirguṇa aspects of [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md)
- embodied form = Mahāsambhūti
- the poles it bridges are realised in [Samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md)
- embodied historically as [Sadāśiva](/cosmology/paramashiva.md) (~7,000 years ago) and [Krṣṇa](/people/krsna.md) (~3,500 years ago) — the two Mahāsambhūtis named in the prose
- "Tāraka Brahma is a concept of [Tantra](/practice/tantra.md)" — the Liberating Entity who gives the cult and devotion
- contrasted with ordinary *sambhūti* / *Iishvarakoṭi* / graded *avatāra* (kalā / aṃsha / khaṇḍāvatāra) — He is the unique, complete Mahāsambhūti
- as *muktidātā* He alone liberates (not time, fate, the five factors, nor even Saguṇa Brahma), staying linked to the expressed world via *ota-prota yoga*
- *Idea and Ideology* (II-6): the **tangential touch** at which His abode lies; creation = *sambhūti*, His embodied descent = **Mahāsambhūti** (a body from the pañcabhūtas); His sādhana as the distinct third path (Saguṇa / Nirguṇa / Tāraka)
- *nh1-2 (1978)*: "the very existence of Parama Puruṣa is meant for promoting the well-being of the jiivas" — "He would not have come down to the level of Saguṇa Brahma or Tāraka Brahma" had microcosmic well-being not existed; the Gītā's *"sambhavāmi yuge yuge"* (cf. [Saguṇa & Nirguṇa Brahma](/cosmology/saguna-nirguna-brahma.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- **Mahāsambhūti is NOT a generic avatāra.** Sarkar explicitly excludes Shiva and
  Krṣṇa from the graded avatāra sequence (kalā/aṃsha/khaṇḍāvatāra); do not normalise
  toward popular avatāra theology.
- The **~7,000 / ~3,500-year** datings (Sadāśiva, Krṣṇa) are Sarkar's own teaching;
  a minor internal variation (3,500 vs 3,300) appears across discourses — reproduced
  as stated.
- **Idea and Ideology (1959)** is the earliest prose of the tangential-touch and
  sambhūti/mahāsambhūti doctrine; it also flags that "Tāraka Brahma is not a figure
  of philosophy — it is a creation of devotional sentiment," a nuance to keep.
- **Could not verify:** no read passage names any Tāraka Brahma other than Sadāśiva
  and Krṣṇa. "Cosmic preceptor" does not occur verbatim — the grounded terms are
  Mahāsambhūti, Tāraka Brahma, mahasadvipra, and Liberating Entity (*muktidātā*).
- **NHN 1/2 (1978)** states the advent's purpose (jīva welfare, the descent to Saguṇa
  Brahma/Tāraka Brahma, *"sambhavāmi yuge yuge"*) — an independent 1978 statement
  consistent with, and dated before, the sambhūti prose. The Gītā śloka is quoted
  verbatim with the editors' translation.
