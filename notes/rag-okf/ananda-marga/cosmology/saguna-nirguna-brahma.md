# Definition

[Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md) is viewed in two states with respect to the
[guṇas](/cosmology/guna.md): **Saguṇa Brahma** — Brahma within the influence of
the binding principles — and **Nirguṇa Brahma** — Brahma beyond them. Ánanda
Sútram 2-12: *"Saguṇāt srṣṭirutpattih"* — "The creation originates from Saguṇa
Brahma."

# Explanation

Since the created world is **concerned with the guṇas**, it evolves in Saguṇa,
not in Nirguṇa. Nirguṇa Brahma — being beyond the guṇas — is not the locus of
creation; there, no creative flow operates. The "seed of the universe" lies in a
state the mind cannot comprehend (2-11, *"Mānasātiite anavasthāyāṃ jagadbiijam"*),
so asking *when* or *why* creation began is, in Sarkar's framing, "redundant and
untenable" — to posit mind where mind does not exist is the fallacy of infinite
regress.

[Puruṣa](/cosmology/purusa.md), established at the **nucleus of Saguṇa Brahma**
(2-15), witnesses creation and controls the guṇas through which energy emanates,
yet **is not subject** to them — He is their governor. The transitional point
between the two states is the **bridge** of
[Tārakabrahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md).

# In *Varna Vijinana* (1983) — sakala/niskala and the manifest universe

*Varna Vijinana* supplies the same distinction under the philological pair
*sakala / niskala* and grounds the manifest universe in the guṇas:

- **Sakala brahma — the manifest.** (vv-18) *Sakala* means "that from which the
  sprout has emerged", that is, "that which is manifest or expressed. Everything
  in this universe is a manifest entity. For example, in philosophy it is called
  sakala brahma, that is, manifest brahma. When unmanifest it is niśkala brahma
  or nirguńa brahma." (Written with *sha*, *shakala* means "piece" or "part" — a
  mere letter changes the meaning.) Note that *sakala brahma* is the manifest
  counterpart of *saguṇa brahma*, and *niṣkala* that of *nirguṇa* — the two
  name-pairs are the same distinction.
- **The manifest universe and the guṇas.** (vv-20) "the manifest universe is a
  result of the influence of the guńas of prakrti over consciousness – the
  supremacy of the binding faculty over the cognitive faculty. In other words,
  Parama Puruśa comes within the jurisdiction of the binding power of prakrti.
  This manifest universe is called saḿvrttibodhicitta in the Buddhist
  nomenclature and káryabrahma (expressed universe) in Aryan nomenclature."
  Creation, preservation and destruction are the three phases the sages assigned
  to the composite *a-u-ma* — and the manifest universe is *káryabrahma*.

## Relationships

- two states of [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md) with respect to [Guṇa](/cosmology/guna.md)
- creation (saiñcara) originates from Saguṇa (2-12)
- [Puruṣa](/cosmology/purusa.md) is the nucleus and controller of Saguṇa Brahma (2-15)
- bridged by [Tārakabrahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md)
- *VV 18*: *sakala brahma* (manifest) vs *niskala brahma* / *nirguṇa brahma* (unmanifest) — "that from which the sprout has emerged"
- *VV 20*: the manifest universe = "saḿvrttibodhicitta" (Buddhist) / "káryabrahma" (Aryan) — "a result of the influence of the guńas of prakrti over consciousness" (cf. [Sṛṣṭimātṛkā](/cosmology/srstimatrka.md), [Five Fundamental Factors](/cosmology/five-fundamental-factors.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- Saguṇa / Nirguṇa are **not two Brahmas** but one Brahma described relative to
  the guṇas. Keep the non-dual reading.
- **VV (1983) names the same pair in the sakala/niskala vocabulary** — *sakala
  brahma* (manifest) / *niskala brahma* or *nirguṇa brahma* (unmanifest); VV 20
  grounds the manifest universe in the guṇas ("káryabrahma"). These are Sarkar's
  own philological framings of the same doctrine — keep alongside the sūtra pair,
  without claiming identity of the name-pairs beyond what the source states.
