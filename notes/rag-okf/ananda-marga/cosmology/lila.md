# Definition

**Liilā** is the **divine play** of [Parama Puruṣa](/cosmology/purusa.md) — His act
of creating the manifold universe out of His own imaginative flow (*saṃkalpa*),
where the **purpose of the game is not known** to the created beings. **Kriidā** is
the structurally identical "game" of imagination performed by a unit being, **whose
causal factor IS known**. The term *liilā* is reserved for the divine game alone.

# Explanation

- The **defining distinction** (Sarkar's own): "When the causal factor is known to
  you, it is *kriidā*; when the causal factor is not known to you, it is *liilā*."
  What the Lord creates in His imaginative flow is liilā for humans (they do not
  know its cause); what jīvas create by their own imaginative power is kriidā.
- It is **voluntary/playful, not mechanical** — "the universe is but the play of
  vibrations." The jīva's kriidā only *reflects* His liilā: "you cannot create
  anything original."
- The devotee's answer to **"why creation?"**: a **compulsion of love** — "My Lord
  was utterly alone… restless before creation. He could neither love nor feel
  angry with anyone. So creation was a compulsion. He created all these forms by
  multiplying Himself." "He is playing hide-and-seek with Himself" — and the name of
  this search is [sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md). The "why" is unanswerable by
  intellect; only the [devotee](/practice/bhakti.md), merging mind with His, knows
  some of its secret.
- **Liilābhāva vs nityabhāva:** while Brahma continues creation through
  saiñcara/pratisaiñcara, that is the *liilābhāva* — His **expressed**
  ([Saguṇa](/cosmology/saguna-nirguna-brahma.md)) stance, "One Puruṣa becomes many";
  in *nityabhāva* He remains in His original, unexpressed rank (Nirguṇa).
- **Liilānanda vs Nityānanda:** engrossed in Himself, His bliss is *Nityānanda*;
  "when He plays the game of joy and sorrow with His created beings, that is His
  *Liilānanda*." "Everything originates from nityānanda, remains in liilānanda, and
  returns to nityānanda" (see [Ānanda](/cosmology/ananda.md)).
- The jīva is a **partner, not a victim**: the bliss the devotee gains approaching
  Him is itself Liilānanda — "a billion times greater" than what Parama Puruṣa
  derives — so the sādhaka "always gains, never loses."

# In *Varna Vijinana* (1983) — rhythm as the natural characteristic of movement

*Varna Vijinana* 16 (*Proper Names – 1*, 2 October 1983, Calcutta) supplies the
rhythm-ground of the divine play:

- **Human life itself is rhythmic.** "Human beings try to find rhythm in
  everything because human life itself is rhythmic. Movement is the natural
  characteristic of life so rhythm must exist within human movement." "So in this
  universe all movement flows in the waves of systaltic order."
- **No rhythm, no happiness.** "If life's movement were continuous or linear then
  there would be no scope for rhythm, and without rhythm there can be no
  happiness either." The playful, wave-like motion of the play is thus the very
  condition of the bliss ([ānanda](/cosmology/ananda.md)) native to it.

## Relationships

- the playful aspect of creation — the "why-as-play" beside the lawful "how-as-cycle" of the [Brahmacakra](/cosmology/brahmacakra.md) ([saiñcara](/cosmology/saincara.md) / [pratisaiñcara](/cosmology/pratisaincara.md))
- *liilābhāva* = expressed [Saguṇa Brahma](/cosmology/saguna-nirguna-brahma.md); Liilānanda & Nityānanda are the two modes of [Ānanda](/cosmology/ananda.md)
- creation by His saṃkalpa — relates [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md) / [Sṛṣṭimātṛkā](/cosmology/srstimatrka.md) ("play of vibrations")
- the "search" within the play is [Sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md), known only to the [devotee](/practice/bhakti.md)
- *VV 16*: rhythm is the natural characteristic of movement — "all movement flows in the waves of systaltic order"; "without rhythm there can be no happiness either" (cf. [Existential Flow](/philosophy/existential-flow.md), [Ānanda](/cosmology/ananda.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- *Kriidā* is double-edged in the corpus: anchor on the **jīva's known-purpose
  game** (the de-confliction axis), not the looser scriptural verb "*kriidate*"
  (sports).
- No doctrinal drift across the 1969–1978 discourses; the Liilānanda/Nityānanda
  bliss-pair (1978) extends the 1969 liilā/kriidā distinction.
- **Could not verify / corrected:** "Tadātmānaṃ Srjāmyaham" (a likely-looking
  source) actually concerns *avatāra/saṃskāra*, **not** liilā — excluded (it
  belongs to [Tāraka Brahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md)). The classical Vedānta
  "purposeless sport" gloss was **not** found — Sarkar's framing is the opposite:
  the play has an internal cause (His love), merely **unknown** to created beings.
- **VV 16 (1983) corroborates the rhythmic character of the play** — "all movement
  flows in the waves of systaltic order" and "without rhythm there can be no
  happiness" are direct teaching; they ground the play's wave-like motion (and
  its bliss) without naming liilā explicitly — flagged as corroborating, not a
  liilā-specific quote.
