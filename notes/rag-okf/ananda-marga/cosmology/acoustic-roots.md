# Definition

An **acoustic root** is the supporting sound that constitutes "the rudimental
portion of an action" — in Saṃskṛta, the **bīja mantra**. As Sarkar teaches in *An
Expression Is Never Alone* (AV 12): *"That particular sound, sound representing an
action, is called 'acoustic root' — in Sanskrit, biija mantra. There is a sound just
to represent the spirit of that being, of that deed."* In *Acoustic Roots* (AV 14)
the principle is universal: *"For each and every action there is a supporting sound
in this universe. That supporting sound is called the acoustic root of that
action."* Thus moving generates *khaṭ-khaṭ*, laughing *hā-hā-hā*, and the spiritual
fight against the mind generates *hum* — each the acoustic root of that action.

# Explanation

**From innumerable sounds to fifty main sounds.** In *Acoustic Roots* (AV 14,
Taipei 1979) Sarkar reasons saying → word → syllable → letter → "a sound having a
particular wavelength." Sounds are innumerable, but "as a result of human endeavour,
those expressions have been brought within the range of **fifty main sounds**. Those
fifty main sounds are controlled by fifty main glands and sub-glands of the human
body." He illustrates with the pancreas, which controls ten of the fifty —
*ḍa, ḍha, ṇa, ta, tha, da, dha, na, pa, pha* — and "these ten sounds control ten
propensities of the human mind — shyness, fear, etc."

**Each sound controls a propensity (vṛtti).** *The Subtlest Propensity* (AV 30)
corroborates the count from the propensity side: "In the human mind there are fifty
such pabula … the human propensities, vṛttis," distributed across the
[cakras](/biopsychology/cakra.md) (mūlādhāra 4, svādhiṣṭhāna 6, maṇipura 10, anāhata
12, vishuddha 16, ājiṇā 2 = 50). Several vishuddha entries are themselves seed-sounds
(*oṇm, hum, phaṭ, vaoṣaṭ, vaṣaṭ, svāhā, namah*), tying propensity to acoustic root.

**Aum as the collective sound.** All fifty sounds together "create a collective sound
in the universe. That collective sound is **aum**" — *a*, *u*, *ma* representing
creation, retention and destruction (see [oṃkāra](/cosmology/omkara.md)).

**Mantrāghāta in sādhanā.** *Diikṣā and Initiation* (AV 6) gives the mechanism: "each
and every mantra has the support of an acoustic root: and when that acoustic root
strikes at the root of the coiled serpentine, it is aroused. This is called
*mantrāghāta*." The aroused [kuṇḍalinī](/practice/kundalini.md) then moves toward
supreme positivity (mantra caetanya).

# In *Varna Vijinana* (1983) — the empirical core of the doctrine

The *Varna Vijinana* discourses (Calcutta, August–October 1983) supply the
empirical core of the acoustic-root doctrine: the psycho-acoustic origin of
sounds and words, and the derivation of the seed-letter *ka*.

- **Psycho-acoustic notes — sound from the nerves.** (vv-9) "First a vibration
  arises in the nerves when an object is seen, then a mental sound is created and
  finally a word is made based on that sound." Seeing white gives *dhapdhap*
  (→ *dhava* → *dhavala*, "white"), seeing red gives *t́akt́ak*. "All sounds in
  this universe have their origin in these psycho-acoustic notes." The notes are
  "chiefly divided into seven partial sounds which we call the seven note scale";
  "the creation of all languages and dialects, is produced from permutations and
  combinations of this seven note scale." "Shiva took the help of these
  fundamental sounds to create the six ragas and thirty-six raginis of Indian
  classical music."
- **Inferential acoustic notes — "the hidden wealth of the human mind."** (vv-9)
  These are "the linguistic expression of the vibration arising in consciousness
  due to contact of the nerve fibres with sounds produced in the external world" —
  e.g. *mishkálo* (black as *mishi*), *kuckuce kálo* (black as pomegranate seed),
  *tultule naram* (soft as cotton). "Language cannot move forward without these
  inferential acoustic notes," yet "they have still been overlooked" — "they are
  the hidden wealth of the human mind."
- **The sound-imitative genesis of language.** (vv-19) In the distant past "people
  imitated the different sounds they heard" — *t́hak-t́hak*, *jhanjhan*, *gaŕgaŕ*,
  *turtur* → *turaga* ("horse"), *kurkur* → *kuraga* ("deer"). "The first word
  forms in all the world's languages were the original verbal roots," and words
  were then derived "by adding prefixes before these verbal roots and suffixes
  after them."
- **a — the first sound of creation.** (vv-13) "a is the acoustic root of
  creation. Because a is the first sound in the creation it has been placed at the
  beginning of the alphabet."
- **ka — a "physical transplantation of psychic idea."** (vv-13) "The letter ka
  prepresents saḿvrttibodhicitta, that is, ka is a physical transplantation of
  psychic idea, thus it occupies the first position in the list of consonants.
  Ka is káryyabrahma." (vv-20) "The biija [seed] mantra of káryabrahma is ka" —
  the cross of vertical [Puruṣa](/cosmology/purusa.md) × horizontal
  [Prakṛti](/cosmology/prakrti.md) "became ka in the Bráhmii script" (see
  [Sṛṣṭimātṛkā](/cosmology/srstimatrka.md)).
- **Meaning is universal.** (vv-15) "Every sound on earth is meaningful, whether or
  not we are aware of that meaning." (vv-16) "no word on this earth is
  meaningless."

## Relationships

- the collective sound of all fifty acoustic roots is **aum / [oṃkāra](/cosmology/omkara.md)** (*a-u-ma* = creation, preservation, dissolution) (AV 14)
- each acoustic root controls a **propensity (vṛtti)** seated on a [cakra](/biopsychology/cakra.md); the fifty sounds map to the fifty propensities enumerated in AV 30
- in sādhanā a [mantra](/practice/mantra.md) carries an acoustic root that, via *mantrāghāta*, strikes and arouses the [kuṇḍalinī](/practice/kundalini.md) (AV 6, AV 14)
- *mantrāghāta* is a sub-phase of [dīkṣā](/practice/diksa.md) (AV 6)
- distinct from [Sṛṣṭimātṛkā](/cosmology/srstimatrka.md): that node is the primordial creative sound-*matrix* / triangle-of-forces flow; this node is the individual bīja sounds and their propensity-controlling function
- *VV 9*: the psycho-acoustic notes are the empirical origin of sounds and words — the seven-note scale → all languages and the six rāgas / thirty-six rāgiṇīs; the inferential acoustic notes are "the hidden wealth of the human mind"
- *VV 13/20*: the letters *a* ("the acoustic root of creation") and *ka* ("a physical transplantation of psychic idea… Ka is káryyabrahma") are themselves acoustic roots; the *ka* glyph derives from the [Puruṣa](/cosmology/purusa.md)×[Prakṛti](/cosmology/prakrti.md) cross → [Sṛṣṭimātṛkā](/cosmology/srstimatrka.md)
- *VV 19*: "The first word forms in all the world's languages were the original verbal roots" — acoustic derivation from imitated natural sounds (*turaga*, *kuraga*)

# Notes — fidelity

- **"Petals" is NOT verbatim.** AV 30 lists the fifty propensities BY cakra
  (4/6/10/12/16/2) but never says "petals." The standard "fifty letters on the petals
  of the cakras" is the conventional Tantric scheme — present it as interpretation,
  not a direct quote. AV 30's per-cakra headings are themselves editor-added
  ("Headings regarding the cakras added by us … —Eds.").
- **"Fifty main sounds"** is Sarkar's exact phrase (AV 14, grounded in "fifty glands
  and sub-glands"; the pineal controls 50 × 2 × 10 = 1,000 propensities). Identifying
  these with the fifty Saṃskṛta *varṇas* (mātṛkā) is the conventional reading — AV 14
  gives only ten consonant examples, not the full alphabet.
- The two sources are NOT one continuous teaching: AV 14 grounds sounds in *glands*;
  the cakra mapping comes from the SEPARATE AV 30 propensity list. Do not conflate.
- **VV corroboration (1983):** *Varna Vijinana* 9 independently grounds words in
  psycho-acoustic notes ("First a vibration arises in the nerves…") and names the
  inferential acoustic notes "the hidden wealth of the human mind"; VV 13/20
  ground the letters *a* and *ka* as acoustic roots (*ka* = "a physical
  transplantation of psychic idea," the bīja of *kāryyabrahma*, glyph from the
  Puruṣa×Prakṛti cross). VV 15/16 state the universal meaningfulness of sound.
  The AV "fifty main sounds" and the VV "seven note scale" remain distinct
  frameworks — do not conflate their counts.
- **Could not verify:** "Shabda Brahma" (Sound-Brahma) does not appear in the read
  discourses; the full petal-by-petal seating of all fifty varṇas on six cakras; and
  "mātṛkā-varṇa" applied to the sound-letters (AV 12 uses *varṇa matrkā* only for the
  COLOUR/ocular root, "colour matrix").
