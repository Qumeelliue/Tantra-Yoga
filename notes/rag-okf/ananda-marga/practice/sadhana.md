# Definition

**Sādhanā** is spiritual practice — the **effort to attain Paramārtha** (that which
completely alleviates worldly affliction): the [jīvātman](/cosmology/atman.md)
becomes Paramātman the moment it **eliminates all its
[saṃskāras](/biopsychology/samskara.md)** through sādhanā. It is essentially the
disciplined control of the mind's infinite potentialities and the redirection of
every propensity toward [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md).

# Explanation

- **Mental discipline:** "controlling the infinite potentialities of the mind is
  sādhanā." Since the mind holds one object at a time, worldly action and ideation
  on Brahma cannot occur together — the mind must be sanctified for His worship.
- **Practicable in household life:** one need not forsake home or take sannyāsa;
  "the only thing necessary… is an ardent desire for it." *Yama–niyama* is sādhanā
  through worldly deeds; the **differentiating feelings** (caste, creed, high/low)
  are the chief obstacles.
- **Madhuvidyā** ("honey knowledge") is the technique for engaging worldly objects
  (*preya*) without bondage: take each object with **cosmic feeling** (feeding
  one's child = serving Brahma in the form of a child). It keeps one free of the
  shackles of action, pervades within and without with Brahmānanda, and **guards
  against [Avidyā](/cosmology/vidya-avidya.md)**.
- **Three blended stages** (Ananda Marga): *Shākta* (ceaseless struggle to master
  [Prakṛti](/cosmology/prakrti.md), via jñāna+karma) → *Vaeṣṇava* (floating in the
  flow of Cosmic bliss, offering the mind, via action+devotion) → *Shaeva* (the
  non-attributional stance, nirvikalpa). Each must be developed in turn.
- **Surrender of original actions** (*pratyayamūlaka*) to Brahma is "the most
  important aspect" — so no new reactions are incurred; "the more one suffers from
  reactions, the more one is progressing."
- **Mechanism:** *pratyāhāra* (withdrawal) in four stages — *yatamāna, vyatireka,
  ekendriya, vashīkāra* — then *dhāraṇā → dhyāna →* [samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md);
  *vashīkāra siddhi* is attained **only by devotees**. In *abhidhyāna* the organs
  and mind are withdrawn from the vṛttis and directed straight to Parama Puruṣa
  (hence no idol-worship); [kiirtana](/practice/kiirtana.md) is a parallel path.

# In *Elementary Philosophy* (1955)

Chapters 8–9 define sādhanā as *"an all-round effort for emancipation from the bondage of
Prakrti,"* proved possible by precedent (the bound Prajāpati *"became free (mukta) … by
carrying out intuitional practices"* — Hiraṇyagarbha). Consciousness retracts gradually —
**citta → ahaṃtattva → mahattattva**, then mahattattva dissolves into unit consciousness;
when only the "pure I" of mahattattva remains it is **savikalpa samādhi**, and when that too
dissolves, **nirvikalpa samādhi**. The fight is against undivided **Infinite Prakṛti** (She
cannot be split into units), so sādhanā is *"no easy task"*; *"concentration of mind is
nothing else but sādhanā,"* yielding clearer reflection of Consciousness (greater jñāna).

**Fidelity — the supernatural powers won in this "war" must NOT be used.** Wishing to use
power is itself Prakṛti's qualifying influence; using it brings one under Avidyāmāyā and
**degradation** — even curing disease or relieving suffering is *"greater disservice than
service"* (robbing the sufferer of the karmaphala that could awaken their own sādhanā), and
nullifying nature's laws would be *"no greater blasphemy."* A **preceptor is indispensable**
(the shackled cannot self-free) and must be a *muktapuruṣa* — *"the preceptor (guru) is
Bhagavān incarnate"*; one who displays powers *"should be avoided like a venomous serpent."*
Yet *"idle dependence on the preceptor cannot obtain emancipation"*: only humans
(fully-reflected consciousness) can practise, *"everyone has an equal right,"* but one must
**earn** it by earnest desire — it is *"our permanent duty."*

**Why people avoid it (ch.9) — four baseless fears:** (1) that one must renounce the world
— but *"Avidyāmāyā will go with you to the jungles,"* whereas the householder has *greater*
conveniences plus *"the opportunity to serve humanity, an important aspect of intuitional
practice"* (no place is better; to discriminate places *"is to divide Brahma"*); (2) that
Brahmacarya means celibacy — it means *"to introvert the extroversial tendencies of the
mind and to devote it completely to Brahma,"* freedom from all fourteen (ṣaḍripu +
aṣṭapāsha), attained by practice *"not [by] taking a vow of celibacy"*; (3) postponing to
old age — *"only a young bamboo can be bent"*; (4) fear of losing pleasures — sādhanā makes
them lose their attraction, so *"the question of giving them up does not arise."* And
*"there is no compulsion in intuitional practice."*

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — sādhana as accelerating pratisaincara

The 1959 cosmology (*Ātman, Paramātman and Sādhanā*, II-6) states sādhana's
cosmological meaning most compactly:

- **Sādhana is the attempt to accelerate the motion of
  [pratisaiñcara](/cosmology/pratisaincara.md)** — to "cover up the painful hours
  of divine separation as early as possible." The unit mind *can* accelerate by
  realizing His blessing more and more and "thereby acquiring a psycho-spiritual
  parallelism with greater speed."
- **Only human beings can do sādhana** — only they possess the ego to perform it.
- **The touchstone:** "The touchstone of spiritual sādhana is therefore the feeling
  that 'I am tending towards Him.' Every delayed moment of life is only a moment of
  pain. That alone which brings His realization is His spiritual practice, all
  other actions being ritualistic ostentations."
- **The goal ranks** (cf. [Mokṣa](/practice/moksa.md)): the sādhaka whose goal is
  Puruṣottama merges in Saguṇa Brahma; one who aims at Nirguṇa Brahma "becomes
  surrendered into the Objectless Entity"; only in Tantra is the sādhana of
  **Tāraka Brahma** specially defined (see [Tāraka Brahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md)).
- **The integrated personality** (II-9, *Psycho-Spiritual Parallelism*): complete
  development of the integrated personality is "an essentiality for perfect
  spiritual realization" — the physical body (*jadādhāra*), mental body
  (*bhāvādhāra*) and spiritual body (*cetanādhāra*). The physical needs proper
  nourishment and physical labour (see [Caryācarya](/works/caryacarya.md)); the
  mental needs physical-and-mental struggle plus mental food — ideas
  (psycho-spiritual parallelism, see [Idea & Ideology](/philosophy/ideology.md));
  the spiritual is strengthened by *longing for the Great* — "an intense desire to
  be for Him. This is the real bhakti or devotion. This is the ultimate and only
  way to final liberation."
- The same chapter closes with Śaṅkarācārya: of all means to mokṣa, devotion alone
  is the best — cf. [Bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md).

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1958–1987) — memory, science, and the guidance of practice

The *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* add three framings of sādhanā:

- **Sādhanā as the visualization of the samskāra-panorama.** (DNE 30, *Cerebral
  and Extra-Cerebral Memory*, 30 Dec 1970.) "Human beings, if they so want, may
  try to relive those experiences in their memories. This endeavour is called
  sādhanā or spiritual practice. Sādhakas or spiritual aspirants, by dint of
  sādhanā, suspend their crude mind in the subtle mind, and the subtle mind in
  the causal mind. They can then clearly visualize that panorama of sequential
  events in the causal mind. As they have full control over the time factor they
  can easily transcend the intervening gaps between two lives and establish a
  link between them." The samskāras of one's own past lives are harder to
  visualize than those of others (providence shields the sādhaka from a past
  life of sin).
- **"No alternative other than to perform sadhana."** (DNE 3, *Synthesis and
  Analysis*, 27 Jul 1958.) Scientific change of the glands and even the brain is
  possible — "a dishonest person will be able to become an honest one" — "But
  science can never change people's samskāras. Therefore, for the development of
  their personalities, there is no alternative for human beings other than to
  perform sadhana."
- **Guidance for beginning and the Guru's grace.** (DNE 29, *Bio-Psychology*,
  3 Jun 1987.) "Preferably, one should start bio-psycho-spiritual practice as
  early as possible after the age of five. If that is not possible, one should
  definitely start sādhanā by the age of thirteen after the sex glands develop
  and the sense of responsibility and dutifulness arises in the mind." The
  pinnacled mind is attained by "dhyāna of the Guru in the Guru cakra," "the
  internal side of the sahasrāra cakra"; hence "Guru krpāhi kevalam ['The grace
  of the Guru is everything']." And "those who say that women are not entitled to
  spiritual salvation are wrong. Men and women are equally entitled to spiritual
  salvation" (cf. [Guru](/practice/guru.md), [Dhyāna](/practice/dhyana.md),
  [Women's Rights](/philosophy/womens-rights.md)).

## Relationships

- the practice whose goal is [Samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md) and [Mokṣa](/practice/moksa.md)
- a form of [Yoga](/practice/yoga.md) (saṃyoga; pratyāhāra); the introversial [pratisaiñcara](/cosmology/pratisaincara.md) return
- eliminates [Saṃskāra](/biopsychology/samskara.md); depends on [Bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md) (vashīkāra only by devotees)
- realized via [Tantra](/practice/tantra.md), [Mantra](/practice/mantra.md), [Kiirtana](/practice/kiirtana.md), under a [Guru](/practice/guru.md), toward the [Iṣṭa](/practice/ishta.md)
- operates on the [Unit Mind](/biopsychology/unit-mind.md) / [Citta](/biopsychology/citta.md) / [indriyas](/biopsychology/indriya.md); Madhuvidyā counters [Avidyā](/cosmology/vidya-avidya.md)
- *Elementary Philosophy*: effort to free unit consciousness from **Infinite [Prakṛti](/cosmology/prakrti.md)**; powers won must **not** be used; the [Guru](/practice/guru.md) (a *muktapuruṣa*, "Bhagavān incarnate") is indispensable; the fears that deter practice are baseless (cf. [Obstacles](/practice/obstacles.md))
- *Idea and Ideology*: the **acceleration of [Pratisaiñcara](/cosmology/pratisaincara.md)** ("I am tending towards Him"); only humans can practise; its goal may be Saguṇa merger, Nirguṇa surrender, or [Tāraka Brahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md) sādhana; it builds the integrated personality (cf. [Idea & Ideology](/philosophy/ideology.md))
- *DNE 30*: sādhanā suspends the crude mind in the subtle and the subtle in the causal mind, visualizing the sequential [saṃskāras](/biopsychology/samskara.md) of past lives ("extra-cerebral memory")
- *DNE 3*: science can change glands and even the brain but "can never change people's samskāras" — hence "no alternative ... other than to perform sadhana" (cf. [Samskāra](/biopsychology/samskara.md))
- *DNE 29*: start after age five, at latest thirteen; dhyāna of the Guru in the Guru cakra ("Guru krpāhi kevalam"); men and women "equally entitled to spiritual salvation" (cf. [Guru](/practice/guru.md), [Dhyāna](/practice/dhyana.md), [Women's Rights](/philosophy/womens-rights.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- The three-stage Shākta/Vaeṣṇava/Shaeva scheme and the four pratyāhāra sub-stages
  appear in the **1958** discourse; the **1955** discourses present sādhanā more
  simply (mind-control, madhuvidyā, samādhi). Note the elaboration over time
  (1955 → 1958 → 1979).
- Madhuvidyā (cosmic feeling on objects) is **distinct** from abhidhyāna (withdrawal
  toward Parama Puruṣa) — related but not the same.
- **Idea and Ideology** (1959) frames sādhanā **cosmologically** (acceleration of
  pratisaiñcara) — a framing compatible with, but earlier than, the SS/AV prose.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** the specific numbered "lessons" of
  Ananda Marga meditation — only abhidhyāna, dhyāna, pratyāhāra, kiirtana and
  yama-niyama are named here; do not assert a lesson-count system.
