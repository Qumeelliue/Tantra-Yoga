# Definition

**Vidyā Tantra and Avidyā Tantra** are the two orientations of Tantric practice,
distinguished by their six actions (*ṣaṭkarma*). The **ṣaṭkarma of Vidyā Tantra**
are applied "in order to elevate human beings in the world of spirituality by goading
them towards auspicious thoughts"; the **ṣaṭkarma of Avidyā Tantra** are applied "in
order to exploit human beings and utilize them for one's own self interest." (1990,
DT 2/39.)

# Explanation

- **The six actions of Vidyā Tantra:**
  1. **awakening the benevolent intellect** — training the crude intellect to be
     utilized in the practical sphere;
  2. **awakening the nobler vṛttis** — among the ~1000 propensities (controlled from
     the sahasrāra / guru cakra), arousing the benevolent ones (compassion, love,
     sense of righteousness, service, helping others, consoling the bereaved,
     arousing hope) against the malevolent (the eight fetters, six enemies, harming
     others, hypocrisy, etc.) — while some propensities are neutral (survival,
     multiplication, the senses);
  3. **awakening humility** — *"Vidyā dadāti vinayam…"* (education leads to modesty,
     modesty to respect); arrogance disturbs peace;
  4. **awakening the sense of dharma** — *"Dharma hi teṣām adhiko viśeṣah…"* (humans
     possess an especial Bhāgavata Dharma, in whose absence they are no better than
     animals);
  5. **arousing self-surrender** to the Supreme Entity — *"Tvameva mātā ca pitā
     tvameva…"* ("Thou art my father and mother, friend and companion, learning and
     wealth");
  6. **śāntikarma** — propitiatory rites to remove the defects caused by bad stars.
- **The six branches of Avidyā Tantra:**
  1. **māraṇa** — killing/destroying someone through mantra or technique;
  2. **vaśīkaraṇa** — bringing someone under one's control (mantra, dravyaguṇa, the
     squirrel-tail ring, etc.);
  3. **uccāṭana** — uprooting someone from residence (e.g. the brick-and-mantra
     practice — the mantra deliberately withheld);
  4. **sammohana** — hypnotism (Mesmer's "mesmerism");
  5. **stambhana** — stopping movement/flow (e.g. mūtrastambha, vākstambha);
  6. **śāntikarma** (of Avidyā) — saving one person from danger by redirecting it to
     another.
- **śāntikarma is common to both** — the shared sixth element.
- **Guhya.** The secret processes of both are called *guhya vidyā*. The motivation of
  Avidyā Tantra is *"Māraya māraya nāshaya nāshaya uccāṭaya uccāṭaya mama śatruṇām"* —
  "Kill my enemy, destroy, uproot" — "this sort of mentality should not be allowed to
  develop."
- **Historical note:** the six actions of both fell out of use when Kālacakrayāna and
  Vajrayāna vanished from Bengal. "What is desirable is that people move towards
  spiritual elevation along the paths of jñāna, karma and bhakti with a healthy and
  rational outlook."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** the six/seven named actions with their Sanskrit terms; the
  Avidyā ṣaṭkarma are "to exploit human beings and utilize them for one's own self
  interest." Keep the vocabulary exact.
- **The mantra techniques** (vaśīkaraṇa's squirrel-tail, uccāṭana's brick) are
  described by Sarkar — who states he deliberately withholds the uccāṭana mantra.
  Reproduce as his descriptions, not as instructions.
- **Historical claims** (Kālacakrayāna/Vajrayāna in Bengal) are Sarkar's period
  statements — keep dated.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** a correlation of the vidyā/avidyā ṣaṭkarma
  with a named school beyond the Buddhist examples given.
