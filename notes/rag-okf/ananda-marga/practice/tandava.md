# Definition

**Tāṇḍava** and **Kaoṣikī** are the two devised dance-exercises of Ananda Marga,
valued by Sarkar "more as physical exercises, useful to the body, than … forms of
dance." **Tāṇḍava** — from the Saṃskṛta *taṇḍu*, "of jumping habit / upward jump" —
was "first introduced" by **Sadāśiva** "about seven thousand years ago"; it is a
vigorous, jumping, masculine dance enacting the eternal fight against death.
**Kaoṣikī** (*kaoṣikii nṛtya*) is the dance Sarkar states he himself introduced "on
6 September 1978" at Patna — a dance of all-round health "beneficial for both men
and women" and "a medicine for twenty-two diseases."

# Explanation

**Two dances, one purpose.** In *Tāṇḍava and Kaoṣikii* (AV 22) Sarkar frames both as
serving the trifarious (physical, psychic, spiritual) development that must be kept
in "perfect balance." Tāṇḍava "is prescribed for you [men]" and "is conducive for
physical and spiritual progress"; Kaoṣikī, devised because of "certain
physiological constraints," is "as important as tāṇḍava."

**Tāṇḍava — the dance of fighting death.** Per *Tāṇḍava Dance — What and Why?*
(AV 10) and *Dance, Mudrā and Tantra* (AV 12), tāṇḍava is *ullamphan* (high jumping
that separates from the earth) and "represents life … vitality … the principle of
survival." The dancer holds in the left hand a symbol of death (a skull, a venomous
snake, or fire at night) and in the right a dagger or trishula, "the functional
symbol of struggle." The symbolism is the life–death **ideation**, not the macabre:
"you must not develop the psychology of fear or defeatism." Physiologically it
stimulates the lymphatic and endocrine glands (generating "manliness and courage"),
activates the pineal (*sahasrāra*) and pituitary (*ājiṇā*) glands, and is "the only
exercise for the brain." Sarkar grades Brahma / Viṣṇu / Rudra tāṇḍava by how high
the knees cross (navel / anāhata / throat).

**Kaoṣikī — the flower wishing to please the Lord.** *Dances and the Path of Vidyā*
(AV 12) gives its eight-part ideation — hands upraised/folded ("establishing a link
with Parama Puruṣa"), rightward and leftward bends ("I know the right way to request
You" / "to fulfil Your demands"), forward (surrender), backward (readiness to face
all troubles), and the closing rhythm ("O Lord, I repeat Your rhythm"), the body
bending at 45°. Per AV 6 it assures safe deliveries and "checks the advent of old
age."

**Tantra and Shiva.** Both flow from Sadāśiva, the **Naṭarāja**, who "brought …
music and those dances within the scope of a system" (AV 12). Tāṇḍava "is the
starting phase of Tantra," for "Tantra is a cult of life, it is not a cult of
death." It is paired with Pārvatī's devotional *lalita mārmika* (used in kiirtana);
their union — Shiva's *tāṇḍava* (jumping) + Pārvatī's *lalita* (mudrā-dominated) —
gave Indian *tāla* (tā + la) (AV 23).

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1970) — tāṇḍava, lalita and the origin of *tāla*

*Talks on Education – Excerpt D* (DNE 4, 15 Jun 1970) adds the classical Indian
framing that corroborates and extends the AV 23 etymology:

- **Tāṇḍava is rhythm-dominant; Pārvatī's *lalita* is mudrā-dominant.** "In the
  tāṇḍava nrtya of Lord Shiva, there is more rhythm, and in Parvatii's lalita
  dance there is the dominance of mudrā, sweet and refined."
- **The word *tāla* = *tā* + *la*.** "In the word 'tāl', 'tā' is derived from
  tāṇḍava and 'la' from lalita, so it has become tāl. Tāl is the adjustment of
  both, that is why it has become more popular."
- **Two Indian dance styles from Lord Shiva.** "Today we find two types of dance
  in India. First, there is Aryavarta Nrtya or North Indian style. Secondly,
  there is Dāksinātya Nrtya or Carnatic or the South Indian style. Both styles
  were given by Lord Shiva, but credit for bringing them to the public goes to
  Maharshi Bharata."
- **Shiva, creator of the fine arts.** "The fine arts came into existence through
  the medium of mudrā. Lord Shiva is the creator of the fine arts. Mudrā is 'the
  externalization of internal feeling' and means 'to express the desires of the
  mind.'" (Cf. [Mudrā](/practice/mudra.md), [Saṃgiita](/practice/samgiita.md).)

## Relationships

- both dances derive from [Sadāśiva / Paramaśiva](/cosmology/paramashiva.md), the Naṭarāja who systematised them (tāṇḍava "first introduced" ~7,000 years ago)
- *DNE 4*: tāṇḍava has more rhythm, Pārvatī's *lalita* the dominance of mudrā; *tāla* = *tā* (tāṇḍava) + *la* (lalita); the two Indian styles given by Lord Shiva — Aryavarta Nrtya (North Indian) and Dāksinātya Nrtya (Carnatic), popularized by Maharshi Bharata; "Lord Shiva is the creator of the fine arts" (cf. [Mudrā](/practice/mudra.md), [Saṃgiita](/practice/samgiita.md), [Paramaśiva](/cosmology/paramashiva.md))
- tāṇḍava is "the starting phase of [Tantra](/practice/tantra.md)," which Sarkar calls "a cult of life, not a cult of death"
- tāṇḍava is a dance of *vidyā* — centripetal movement toward the Nucleus — set against avidyā ([vidyā–avidyā](/cosmology/vidya-avidya.md)); cursing-gestures belong to Avidyā Tantra
- both are prescribed alongside [sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md) (japa, dhyāna, āsana, prāṇāyāma) for trifarious development
- Pārvatī's companion *lalita mārmika* is the dance used during [kiirtana](/practice/kiirtana.md)
- tāṇḍava activates the pineal (*sahasrāra*) and pituitary (*ājiṇā*) glands / [cakras](/biopsychology/cakra.md), strengthening the brain

# Notes — fidelity

- **Garment:** the prescribed dress for tāṇḍava is an **orange** garment; the
  "tiger-skin / red garment" imagery appears only as exegesis of a shloka (AV 10
  footnote) and must NOT be presented as the costume.
- **Mudrā tension (preserve, do not flatten):** AV 12/22 calls all three dances
  "mudraic," yet AV 6 says plainly "In tāṇḍava there is no mudrā" and AV 12/8 calls
  tāṇḍava itself a mudrā (Naṭarāja *in* tāṇḍava mudrā). Best reading (AV 23):
  tāṇḍava is rhythmic/non-mudra-dominated, *lalita*–*kaoṣikī* are mudra-dominated.
- **Anti-syncretism:** the skull/snake/fire/dagger are explicitly the life–death
  survival *ideation* ("you must not develop the psychology of fear"), NOT macabre
  or performance symbolism.
- **Date:** Kaoṣikī's introduction is given as "6 September 1978" (AV 15, AV 6);
  AV 5 (27 Nov 1978) hedges "very recently, perhaps on 6th September." Place: Patna.
- ***Lalita mārmika*** is a third, related dance (Pārvatī's, used in kiirtana) —
  NOT one of the two scoped dances; linked via kiirtana, not conflated with Kaoṣikī.
- **Could not verify:** an itemised list of which "twenty-two diseases" Kaoṣikī
  cures — the sources give only the number plus categories (female / younger-male /
  liver), never an enumerated list. The "panacea" claim is reproduced as Sarkar's
  teaching, not a verified medical assertion.
