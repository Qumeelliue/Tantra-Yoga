# Definition

**Tāntrika / Tanmātrika samādhi** is samādhi attained by achieving "full control over
objects (an object is nothing but a collection of inferential vibrations)" — the
mind concentrated on "the smallest diminutive of this vast universe," the point that
is "the silver line between the relative world and the absolute world." (1969, AMPiN
4/5.)

# Explanation

- **The universe is a point.** The visible world came into being "through positive
  projection" of tanmātric vibrations from the invisible; if the liquid factor were
  withdrawn the universe would shrink, and if the ethereal factor were withdrawn only
  "a small point would remain having a certain position but no magnitude" — "the vast
  universe is in reality nothing but a point." The Supreme Entity attaches the five
  factors to this tiny point in His imaginative flow.
- **Tāntrika samādhi = control of the tanmātras.** In sādhanā the mind must be
  one-pointed; controlling the objects (collections of tanmātric vibrations) is
  tāntrika samādhi. Creation itself is "the positive tāntrika samādhi of Brahma";
  withdrawing all the tanmātras — the negative tāntrika samādhi of Parama Brahma —
  is the dissolution of creation.
- **The world is relative, not unreal.** "This world cannot be dismissed as unreal
  because it provides vitality required from its food, drink and air to attain the
  absolute" — Ananda Marga differs from the illusion-schools (Vedānta).
- **The mind merged with the Macrocosmic mind** can know whatever the Macrocosmic
  mind thinks or does — "the seed of omniscience lies inherent in the human mind
  itself."
- **Negative tāntrika samādhi — prakṛtiliina avasthā.** When the mind concentrates on
  mean and crude thoughts it enters *prakṛtiliina avasthā*, "a kind of negative
  tāntrik samādhi" — the mind absorbed into matter. Those who die before reaching it
  remain in the *videhaliina* stage and, per their saṃskāras, become gandharva,
  kinnara or vidyādhāra, regaining the human frame once their saṃskāras are requited.
- **Parama Puruṣa as the "stealer of vanity"** (*Darpahārī*, *Manachora*): if the unit
  being, puffed with vanity, misuses God-given power, He may snatch away whatever one
  considers one's own.

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** tāntrika samādhi = full control of the tanmātras at the
  infinitesimal point; "the vast universe is in reality nothing but a point"; the
  negative form (prakṛtiliina) is absorption into matter. Keep the vocabulary.
- **Ananda Marga's non-illusionism** is explicit ("this world cannot be dismissed as
  unreal") — do not normalise toward the Vedantic illusion-thesis.
- The **gandharva/kinnara/vidyādhāra** intermediate states are Sarkar's own
  intermediate-entity doctrine — reproduce as stated.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** a ranking of tāntrika samādhi against the
  sūtra-level savikalpa/nirvikalpa — the chapter presents it as its own attainment
  (AMPiN 4/1-5 is the same series).
