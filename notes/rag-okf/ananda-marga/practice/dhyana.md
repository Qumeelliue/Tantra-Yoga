# Definition

**Dhyāna** is the meditative/concentration process by which the aspirant withdraws
the mind from physical and mental objects and directs it, point by point, toward
the Supreme Consciousness — a movement that begins **physico-psychic** and
culminates **psycho-spiritual**. In the ranking of practices it is the
"second best" (after direct ideation on Brahma), paired with *dhāraṇā*
(concentration).

# Explanation

- **Two phases:** first withdraw the mind from physical objects to one point and
  direct it toward the mental realm; then withdraw from mental pabula and direct it
  toward the Supreme Subjectivity. "This process of movement is the process of
  concentration."
- Concentration is itself **a major [sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md)**; *āsana* and
  *prāṇāyāma* are not sādhanā but only helping items (*sādhanāuṅga*). The mind is
  freed of impurities through selfless [service](/practice/seva.md) and of
  propensive ideas at the mental level.
- **Seven (effectively six) factors of success** (Shiva to Pārvatī): firm
  determination, *shraddhā* for the goal, *gurupūjana* (devotion to the
  [guru](/practice/guru.md)), *samatābhāva* (equipoise), *indriyanigraha* (restraint
  of the [organs](/biopsychology/indriya.md)), *pramitāhāra* (regulated food) — the
  seventh is "nil."
- **Four stages of meditation**, by the mental layer addressed: (1) *toward
  [citta](/biopsychology/citta.md)* — difficulty, thoughts like wild horses; (2)
  *toward [ahaṃ](/biopsychology/ahamtattva.md)* — beginning success, a taste of
  ecstasy; (3) *toward [mahat](/biopsychology/mahattattva.md)* — where powers
  develop, a **great but dangerous** stage (one should pray only for Him, never for
  power); (4) *from mahat to the Supreme within* — realizing the true self and
  merging the mind into [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md).
- **Abhidhyāna** is the *relentless ideative pursuit* of Parama Puruṣa as the
  Sportive One (the [Liilāmaya](/cosmology/lila.md)) — "I will run after Him… I will
  surely catch Him"; through it (with *tattvabhāva*, half-meditation amid action)
  one overcomes the spell of Vishvamāyā (*Tasyābhidhyānāt yojanāt tattvabhāvāt…*).
- **Ārādhanā** is the further stage of **complete self-forgetting absorption**;
  **abhidhyāna samādhi** is reached as objects are perceived ever more subtly until
  their touch becomes the blissful touch of Supreme Consciousness.

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1987, 1990) — dhyāna of the Guru and the glandular science

The *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* connect dhyāna to the bio-psychological
model of [Bio-Psychology](/biopsychology/bio-psychology.md):

- **Dhyāna of the Guru in the Guru cakra.** (DNE 29, *Bio-Psychology*, 3 Jun
  1987.) "To make the mind pinnacled, one should do dhyāna of the Guru in the
  Guru cakra. The Guru cakra is slightly below the pineal gland, though the
  sahasrāra cakra and the Guru cakra are virtually the same." "Spiritual
  aspirants concentrate on the Guru cakra and not on the pineal gland for dhyāna
  ... Because the Guru cakra is the internal side of the sahasrāra cakra" (cf.
  [Guru](/practice/guru.md), [Cakras](/biopsychology/cakra.md)).
- **Meditation as "a still subtler science."** (DNE 35, *The Mind Grows in
  Magnitude* — the quote is from DNE 35, not DNE 29, though both belong to the
  same bio-psychological line of teaching.) "Regarding thinking with
  concentration, that is, meditation – it is another, still subtler, science.
  Meditation should be performed knowing fully well the location of different
  glands and sub-glands and also the different cells in the human brain," and one
  must know "their respective systems of hormone secretion, otherwise their
  meditation will not produce any fruit. So for this system of meditation,
  knowledge of biology is essential."
- **Four dimensions of Yoga psychology ↔ four phases of meditation.** (DNE 29.)
  "There are four dimensions of Yoga psychology – from the mūlādhāra cakra to the
  maṇipura cakra; from the maṇipura cakra to the vishuddha cakra; from the
  vishuddha cakra to the ājinā cakra; and above the ājinā cakra. These dimensions
  of psychology correspond to the four phases of meditation."

## Relationships

- a major form of [Sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md); culminates in [Samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md) (abhidhyāna samādhi)
- *DNE 29*: dhyāna of the Guru in the Guru cakra ("the internal side of the sahasrāra cakra"); the four dimensions of Yoga psychology correspond to the four phases of meditation (cf. [Guru](/practice/guru.md), [Cakra](/biopsychology/cakra.md), [Bio-Psychology](/biopsychology/bio-psychology.md))
- *DNE 35*: meditation is "a still subtler science" requiring knowledge of the glands and sub-glands and their systems of hormone secretion (cf. [Bio-Psychology](/biopsychology/bio-psychology.md))
- requires the [Guru](/practice/guru.md) (gurupūjana, shraddhā); expresses [Bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md) (abhidhyāna/ārādhanā as devotional pursuit of the [Liilā](/cosmology/lila.md)maya)
- its four stages traverse [Citta](/biopsychology/citta.md) → [Ahaṃtattva](/biopsychology/ahamtattva.md) → [Mahattattva](/biopsychology/mahattattva.md) → [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md)
- restrains the [indriyas](/biopsychology/indriya.md); overcomes Vishvamāyā ([Vidyā & Avidyā](/cosmology/vidya-avidya.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- The seven-factors verse is attributed by Sarkar to the *Shiva Saṃhitā* (his
  framing). The fine gradation of subtle perception in "Abhidhyāna Samādhi"
  (solid → dust → subatomic → Consciousness) is an **editorial footnote**, not
  Sarkar's spoken words.
- Two source discourses are "date not known," so chronology is partial.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** the yoga-sūtra chain
  *dhāraṇā → dhyāna → samādhi* as a formal numbered ladder (the relation is implied
  — concentration "as a process" leading to samādhi — but not laid out as such).
- **Attribution flag (DNE):** the "still subtler science" quote on meditation and
  the glands appears in DNE 35 (*The Mind Grows in Magnitude*), not in DNE 29;
  DNE 29 supplies the Guru-cakra dhyāna and the four-dimensions/four-phases
  correspondence. Both chapters are cited accordingly; do not relocate the quote.
