# Definition

**Tantra** is the practical spiritual cult formulated and introduced by
**Sadāśiva** to liberate human beings from inertness/staticity — **"Taṃ jāḍyāt
tārayet yastu sah tantrah parikiirtitah"** ("that which redeems and emancipates
from the bondages of staticity is Tantra"). It is struggle- and practice-based,
not theoretical.

# Explanation

- **Twofold etymology, one spirit:** *ta* (acoustic root of dullness/staticity) +
  *tra* ("that which liberates"); and root *tan* ("to expand"). Tantra **expands**
  the entity and thereby **liberates** it from crudity and lethargy.
- It is the cult Sadāśiva gave to remove inertness in the physical, psychic and
  spiritual strata — the **first sādhanā** given in the world.
- **Essentially practical:** ~90% of its intuitional science is practical, its
  philosophy evolved out of practice; **"struggle is the essence of life,"** and
  those who shun struggle make no progress (cf. [obstacles](/practice/obstacles.md)).
- **Tantra = sādhanā:** "Without sādhanā there is no Tantra and without Tantra
  there is no sādhanā." Sādhanā ideates on the greatest Entity, expanding the mind
  until [Prakṛti's](/cosmology/prakrti.md) bondages snap.
- **Yoga ↔ Tantra are practically the same:** yoga = *"Saṃyoga yoga ityukto
  jiivātmā Paramātmanah"* (union of unit soul with Supreme Soul), reached by the
  constant practice of Tantra; jñānīs, karmīs and bhaktas are all Tantrics, and
  devotion is "the best Tantra."
- **Differs from the Veda:** practical and easily followed, ridding the mind of
  fear from the outset; its theory is evolved from tested practice, so it is
  "infallible in applied life."
- **Graded progression:** at first humans are *pashvācāra* ("as good as animals");
  conquering fear makes one a *viirācārī* (hero); rising above the six *ripus* and
  eight *pāshas* makes one a *divyācārī*, one with Parama Puruṣa — *"Pāshabaddha
  bhavet jiivah pāshamuktah bhavet Shivah."*
- It is **rigorous yet not joyless:** it accepts [kiirtana](/practice/kiirtana.md),
  song, dance and music; the *kaola* raises the [kuṇḍalinī](/practice/kundalini.md)
  to the highest point, and the *Mahākaola* (Shiva, Krṣṇa) can raise it in others.

# In the prose discourses: Āgama & Nigama

Sarkar describes Tantra as "a bird with two wings" — **nigama** and **āgama**
(AV 4). *Nigama* is the theoretical, philosophical side — the **questions** on
spiritual practice ("what to do, how to do, why to do"); *āgama* is the practical,
applied side — the **answers** (AV 23, *Glossary*). The two together *are* the
Tantra shāstra.

Their literary vehicle is the **Shiva–Pārvatī dialogue**: Pārvatī poses the
questions and Sadāśiva answers — "Pārvatii posed questions, and Shiva answered
them" (AV 23, *The Dialogues of Shiva and Pārvatii*). This was deliberate
*lokashikṣā*: Pārvatī already knew the answers, but since no sādhaka was subtle
enough to ask, she posed as questioner so the exchange could be recorded for
humanity. A genuine spiritual question is a *pariprashna* — asked to understand and
follow, never to test the guru's scholarship. The same method, Sarkar notes,
systematised every branch of knowledge, music (*saṃgiita*) and dance (*mudrā*,
whence *tāṇḍava* + *lalita* → *tāla*) included.

The word *āgama* is decoded from the Rudrayāmala couplet as Shiva's words *coming
from* his mouth (ā-gatam), *going to* Pārvatī's ears (ga-tam), with the approval
(ma-tam) of Vāsudeva. Sarkar **rejects** the "approved by Vāsudeva/Krṣṇa" clause as
a later interpolation by "the protagonists of the Vedas" — Krṣṇa came some 3,500
years after Shiva, and Shiva's authority alone validates a teaching (SS 8). Because
the Vedas are purely theoretical, some are inclined to classify them as *nigama*;
only Tantra supplies the *āgama*. Sadāśiva never wished Tantra written down — its
sixty-four texts exist only to save it from extinction.

# In the *Discourses on Tantra* (1979–1990) — the ācāras, vidyā/avidyā, and schools

The *Discourses on Tantra* volumes add the systematic Tantric vocabulary:

- **The three species and three ācāras** (DT 2/29): among humans there are *pashu*
  (animal-minded), *viira* (human in mind and body) and *deva* (god in human form);
  they are elevated by **pashvācāra** (animality → humanity), **vīrācāra** (south of
  the Himalayas; the avadhūtas' practice) / **ciinācāra** (north — Tibet, China,
  Siberia), and **divyācāra** (union with Mahādeva). The definition of yoga here is
  *"Saṃyoga yoga ityukto jīvātmā Paramātmanah"* — unit consciousness unified with
  Supreme Consciousness — against Patañjali's "cittavṛttinirodhah" (see
  [Three Species](/practice/three-species.md)).
- **Vidyā Tantra and Avidyā Tantra** (DT 2/39): the ṣaṭkarma of Vidyā Tantra — awaken
  benevolent intellect, noble vṛttis, humility, dharma, self-surrender, and
  śāntikarma — elevate toward spirituality; the ṣaṭkarma of Avidyā Tantra — māraṇa,
  vaśīkaraṇa, uccāṭana, sammohana, stambhana, śāntikarma — exploit and harm. The
  Avidyā motivation *"māraya māraya… mama śatruṇām"* ("kill my enemy") "should not be
  allowed to develop in a person." (See [Vidyā & Avidyā Tantra](/practice/vidya-avidya-tantra.md).)
- **The schools and Rājādhirāja** (DT 1/6): the Siddhāntācāra, Vāmācāra and Kulācāra
  of Shaeva Tantra are recognized by Hindu and Buddhist schools; the purest part,
  beyond Dakṣiṇācāra and Vāmācāra, formerly in *sāmrājya dīkṣā* from a *kaola guru*,
  "is now included in Rājādhirāja Yoga," to which some of Ananda Marga sādhanā's
  subtler processes bear a close relation. The later Shaeva school "altogether lost
  its high-grade spiritual cult," leaving only distorted Vāmācāra remnants
  (Aghorpanthā).

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1958–1979) — Tantra as spiritual science, vidyā/avidyā, and mudrā

The *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* add three further framings:

- **"Spiritual practice comes within the scope of Science."** (DNE 41, *Art and
  Science*, 16 Aug 1979.) "The first scientist who invented this spiritual
  science was Lord Shiva who was born about 7000 years ago. The name of His
  spouse was Pārvatii. In Old China, Pārvatii was known as Tārá." His spiritual
  science "was divided into two portions – two compartments just like two wings
  of a bird. One portion was known as Nigama, the other portion, Āgama. The
  questions of Pārvatii were known as Nigama, and the replies of Shiva were known
  as Āgama." This corroborates the prose āgama/nigama structure above, adding
  Shiva's ~7000-year-old founding date and the old-Chinese name Tārá for Pārvatī.
  **NOTE — two senses of "āgama":** DNE 39 (*Prápta Vákya and Ápta Vákya*, 25 Feb
  1970) uses *āgama* in a different sense — "the third source of knowledge, āgama
  or authoritative documents" (scripture), one of the *relative* sources of
  knowledge that "can provide real knowledge ... only if it is based on spiritual
  realization." Do NOT conflate: in DNE 39 *āgama* = scripture-as-relative-knowledge
  (a pramāṇa), in DNE 41 *āgama* = Shiva's replies to Pārvatī (the practical wing
  of the Tantra shāstra). The two senses are flagged, not merged.
- **Vidyā vs Avidyā "going on magnificently."** (DNE 3, *Synthesis and Analysis*,
  27 Jul 1958.) "The conflict between Vidyā [the introversial force] and Avidyā
  [the extroversial force] is going on magnificently now" — the world-force
  struggle in which Tantra's science of introversion is the sentient side (cf.
  [Vidyā & Avidyā](/cosmology/vidya-avidya.md)).
- **"In Tantra mudrā is dominant."** (DNE 4, *Talks on Education – Excerpt D*,
  15 Jun 1970.) "Rhythm is guided by mudrā." Yet "in a few places, the Vedas also
  use mudrā. For example, in shrāddha (last rites) there is piṇḍadān mudrā which
  is also called 'aunkush mudrā'. In pitr yajina, there are also mudrās, which are
  called namah mudrā, abhaya mudrā, varada mudrā, etc." — the Indian-made portion
  of the Veda (Atharva Veda) is "responsible for these mudrās" (cf.
  [Mudrā](/practice/mudra.md)).

## Relationships

- founded by [Paramaśiva / Sadāśiva](/cosmology/paramashiva.md)
- *DNE 41*: "Spiritual practice comes within the scope of Science"; the first scientist was Lord Shiva (~7000 years ago), whose spiritual science has two wings — nigama (Pārvatī's questions) and āgama (Shiva's replies); in old China Pārvatī = Tārá (cf. [Paramaśiva](/cosmology/paramashiva.md))
- *DNE 3*: the conflict between [Vidyā & Avidyā](/cosmology/vidya-avidya.md) "is going on magnificently now"
- *DNE 4*: "In Tantra mudrā is dominant"; the Indian-made portion of the Vedas also uses some mudrās (piṇḍadān/aunkush, namah, abhaya, varada) (cf. [Mudrā](/practice/mudra.md))
- identical to genuine sādhanā; its highest stage is nirvikalpa [Samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md)
- culminates in [Bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md) ("the best Tantra," kevalā bhakti)
- requires a [Guru](/practice/guru.md) (Mahākaola) and raises the [Kuṇḍalinī](/practice/kundalini.md)
- overcomes the ripus/pāshas — the [obstacles](/practice/obstacles.md) — and unspent [Saṃskāra](/biopsychology/samskara.md)
- structured as two wings — *nigama* (questions/theory) and *āgama* (answers/practice) — in the [Sadāśiva](/cosmology/paramashiva.md)–Pārvatī dialogue
- the same Q&A method systematised music and dance (*tāṇḍava*, *lalita* → *tāla*) — cf. [Kiirtana](/practice/kiirtana.md)
- *Discourses on Tantra*: the *pashu/viira/deva* gradation with the ācāras (pashvācāra → vīrācāra/ciinācāra → divyācāra); the vidyā/avidyā ṣaṭkarma; the Shaeva schools and Rājādhirāja Yoga (cf. [Three Species](/practice/three-species.md), [Vidyā & Avidyā Tantra](/practice/vidya-avidya-tantra.md), [Paramaśiva](/cosmology/paramashiva.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- **Anti-syncretism:** Sarkar's Tantra is the science of **struggle, expansion and
  liberation from staticity** — the *viirācāra* / burial-ground imagery is about
  **conquering fear**, NOT sexual or "left-hand" ritual. Do not normalise toward
  popular neo-tantra/sexuality readings (absent from all cited passages).
- His is a **Puruṣa-primary** Tantra ("Shaktih Sā Shivasya Shaktih"), contrasted
  with Sāṃkhya. "Yoga and Tantra are almost the same" is his own emphatic claim.
- **Vidyā/avidyā ṣaṭkarma (DT 2/39) is now grounded** — this fills the earlier
  could-not-verify on a named vidyā-tantra vs avidyā-tantra pair; the Avidyā practices
  are described by Sarkar (who withholds the uccāṭana mantra).
- **Rājādhirāja and the Shaeva schools** (DT 1/6) are Sarkar's own historical framing
  (1990) — keep dated.
- **Āgama & nigama (prose):** *nigama* = the questions (theory), *āgama* = the
  answers (practice); the dialogue runs Pārvatī asks → Sadāśiva answers — confirmed
  across all read passages, never reversed. "Some are inclined" to classify the
  Vedas as *nigama* — Sarkar reports this as attributed, not his own classification.
- Sarkar's **rejection of the "approved by Krṣṇa" clause** of the Rudrayāmala couplet
  (SS 8) is a direct teaching — a deliberate Vedic interpolation, since Krṣṇa
  post-dates Shiva by ~3,500 years.
- **Could not verify (āgama/nigama):** no named grouping of which tantras form a
  "nigama portion" vs an "āgama portion" (only the count "sixty-four texts");
  "Gaorii" as a name for Pārvatī was not found in the read passages.
- **Two senses of "āgama" flagged (DNE):** DNE 41 uses *āgama* for Shiva's
  replies to Pārvatī (the practical wing of Tantra, corroborating the prose AV
  framing); DNE 39 uses *āgama* for "authoritative documents" / scripture as a
  third *relative* source of knowledge. The two are distinct and are kept
  separate here — do not conflate them.
