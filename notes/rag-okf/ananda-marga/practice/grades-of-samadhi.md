# Definition

The **grades of samādhi** of the *Tattva Kaomudii* (c. 1969) name the successive
absorptive states of sādhanā by their devotional character: **bhāva samādhi** —
attained "with such a devotional sentiment" that only one propensity remains, the
intense desire for Parama Puruṣa — and **dharmamegha samādhi** — the "deep
dharmamegha… around the anāhata cakra, bringing intense bliss" in which the sādhaka
feels Parama Puruṣa pervading everywhere.

# Explanation

- **Bhāva samādhi** (TK 3/7): the sādhaka directs all psychic energies to Parama
  Puruṣa so that "only one thought was dominant… only Parama Puruṣa exists and no
  other entity"; absorbed in limitless bliss, "waves of bliss constantly flowed through
  the glands, nerve cells and fibres," the body shivering continuously. It "can be
  experienced in any of the four lower cakras"; "as soon as the mind rises above the
  anāhata cakra a higher samādhi is experienced." During it "the vibration created in
  different parts of the sādhaka's body are not controlled by the unit mind but by the
  Cosmic Mind" — the smaller "I" surrenders to the bigger "I."
- **Dharmamegha samādhi** (TK 3/8): the human heart is "the abode of higher psychic
  propensities"; as the sādhaka "superimpose[s] cosmic ideation on every action and
  thought," the devotional sentiment strengthens, and the sādhaka "feel[s] the existence
  of Parama Puruṣa deep in their hearts and pervading throughout the entire world." A
  "deep dharmamegha" (cloud of dharma) forms around the anāhata cakra; "usually in this
  samādhi sādhakas cannot remain still in meditation but fall on the ground."
- **The series.** The *Tattva Kaomudii* part 3 also names the *samādhi of inferential
  and non-inferential bliss*, *anindyananda-rasa samādhi*, *kaṅkālamālinii samādhi*,
  *tanmātrika samādhi*, *rāganugā and rāgātmikā samādhi*, and *abhedajñāna and
  nirvikalpa samādhi* — the graded cakra-feelings of the devotional path (cf.
  [Mokṣa](/practice/moksa.md)'s sālokya…sārṣṭhi as cakra-feelings, and
  [Tāntrika Samādhi](/practice/tantrika-samadhi.md)).

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1978) — Dharmameghānanda

*Self-Realization Is the Real Knowledge* (DNE 40, 13 Dec 1978) names the bliss of
dhruvāsmṛti — corroborating and extending the dharmamegha series above:

- **Dharmameghānanda — the bliss of constant memory of God.** "They are called
  Dhruvāsmṛti – they can always remember God and thus feel a special kind of
  Ānanda or bliss in their minds. That Ānanda is called Dharmameghānanda."
- **The world as one's point of recollection.** "In the sphere of jina
  when people make their minds singularly pointed – when they reach the pinnacle
  of intellect then they can, at will, place and establish the mind on any
  particular point or vindu. ... the whole world then becomes their special point
  or vindu for concentration and recollection by becoming one and the same with
  the singular minded individual." This is the fruit of self-realization:
  "Brahmavid sarvavid ātmavid brahmavid" — and "If you want to know all – know
  one. Then only everything will be known to you" (cf.
  [Dhruvāsmṛti](/biopsychology/memory.md), [Ānanda](/cosmology/ananda.md)).

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** bhāva samādhi at the four lower cakras, dharmamegha at the
  anāhata; the sādhaka "cannot remain still… but fall[s] on the ground." Keep the
  cakra-anchoring exact.
- The **eyewitness accounts** (10 May 1969 Ranchi, the embracing sādhakas in TK 3/5)
  are editorial records of Sarkar-era experiences — reproduce as recorded events, not
  doctrine.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** a single ranked ladder ordering all the
  named grades against one another — each is described independently.
