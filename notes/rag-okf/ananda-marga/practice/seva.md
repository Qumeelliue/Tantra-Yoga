# Definition

**Sevā** is service performed as part of spiritual activity — using one's physical
body and mind for social welfare with the inner attitude of **satisfying Parama
Puruṣa**: since the created world is created out of Him, serving created beings is
"as good as serving Him." It is one of the three precious prerequisites
(*praṇipāta–pariprashna–sevā*) and the form [devotion](/practice/bhakti.md) takes
in action.

# Explanation

- **Four kinds** of service, named after the social classes: *shūdrocita* sevā
  (physical/medical), *kṣatriyocita* sevā (security — protecting the weak),
  *vaeshyocita* sevā (economic — relief, feeding the poor), *viprocita* sevā
  (intellectual — teaching, dharma pracāra). All four are of **equal standard**,
  but the first three yield temporary results while viprocita is permanent (so
  called "the best") — *yet* for a hungry, dying person the best service is to feed
  him, not to tell him to meditate.
- Sevā is the **second half of the human mission** — *Ātmamokṣārthaṃ jagaddhitāya
  ca* ("for self-liberation **and** the welfare of the world"); without serving
  others one is merely selfish.
- Because the world "is created out of Him" and is dear to Him, **serving creation
  serves Him**, and through it He becomes pleased.
- **Only physical work done as spiritual activity** counts as sevā — a farmer
  ploughing is mere work, but "satisfying Parama Puruṣa by using your body for
  social service" is sevā, with a dual benefit (world-welfare + the doer's
  development). **This is [karma yoga](/philosophy/karma.md).**
- Sevā is the **third** of *praṇipāta* (surrender, identified with devotion),
  *pariprashna* (earnest questioning leading to action) and *sevā* — together the
  path culminating in devotion (the verse *Bhaktirbhagavato sevā* — "service to God
  is devotion").
- The **ideal life** combines meditation/[kiirtana](/practice/kiirtana.md) with
  maximum social service — "the supreme ideology for an ideal human being."

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1979) — service as the exclusive speciality of human beings

*Plants, Animals and Human Beings* (DNE 22, 15 Oct 1979) grounds service in human
dharma (one of the four aspects of Bhāgavata Dharma — *vistāra, rasa, sevā,
tadsthiti*), distinguishing it sharply from animal commodity-transaction:

- **Service is one-sided — give, not take.** "Human beings alone have the
  speciality of service. At the time of doing service, the motive must be that I
  shall give but not take. Service is one-sided." In animal life "there is only
  scope for mutual transaction, or what we call commodity transaction" (the
  crocodile-and-bird example — "This is a commercial transaction").
- **Offer yourself cent per cent.** "You have to offer yourself without expecting
  anything in return. If you offer yourself completely at that time, you will
  attain the highest benefit. You will become one with Parama Puruṣa, you will
  become Parama Puruṣa. But if you keep one paise out of a rupee, you cannot get
  oneness with Parama Puruṣa because of that one paise. Hence if you want to
  offer yourself, offer yourself cent per cent. This alone is known as service."
  A donation should be forgotten immediately ("A donation is one-way").
- **Service as the third aspect of Bhāgavata Dharma**, the eternal human dharma
  that "makes no distinctions based on isms — it is for the entire humanity" (cf.
  [Dharma](/philosophy/dharma.md)).

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1987) — sevayā and the unilateral nature of service

*Physical Propulsion, Psychic Propensities and Spiritual Attainment* (nh-2-11, 1 Jan
1987, Tiljala, Calcutta) restates the threefold learning (*praṇipātena pariprashnena
sevayā*) in the psychic sphere:

- **Service is unilateral, not mutual.** "Sevā means rendering selfless service to the
  universe, not to a particular group of people but to anybody and everybody, to all
  living creatures – animals, plants or human beings. Service is unilateral, not
  mutual. Where it is mutual, it is not service – it is commercial transaction. You are
  giving salt to somebody and he pays you in return. This is not service. People should
  carry on commercial transactions to earn their bread and render service in order to
  attain their spiritual progress." Deviating from "this practical dharma" stops
  progress: "the pramātrikona in the physical stratum is also destroyed" (cf.
  [Pramā](/philosophy/prama.md)).
- **The three ways of learning.** *"Praṇipātena pariprashnena sevayā"* — (1)
  **praṇipāta** = "surrendering oneself before one's goal," represented by
  *ṣāṣṭāṅga-praṇāma*, "to lie flat in a straight line like a rod. The idea is, however
  crooked I may be to others, to my ideology I am as simple and straight as a rod"; (2)
  **paripraśna** = "to get the detailed answers to all the questions which will
  facilitate one's individual progress as well as the collective progress of the
  cosmos"; (3) **sevayā** = "rendering selfless service to the universe… to all living
  creatures – animals, plants or human beings."

## Relationships

- expresses [Bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md) ("Bhaktir Bhagavato sevā") and is a form of [Karma](/philosophy/karma.md) yoga
- *DNE 22*: "Human beings alone have the speciality of service"; "the motive must be that I shall give but not take. Service is one-sided"; "offer yourself cent per cent. This alone is known as service" — the *sevā* aspect of Bhāgavata/eternal [Dharma](/philosophy/dharma.md)
- serves [Parama Puruṣa](/cosmology/purusa.md) (serving creation serves Him); the third of the [Yoga](/practice/yoga.md) prerequisites (praṇipāta–pariprashna–sevā)
- fulfils the mission of [Dharma](/philosophy/dharma.md) (*Ātmamokṣārthaṃ jagaddhitāya ca*); prepares the mind for [Sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md)
- complements the universal-service ethos of [Neohumanism](/philosophy/neohumanism.md)
- *NHN 2/11*: "Service is unilateral, not mutual. Where it is mutual, it is not service – it is commercial transaction"; "People should carry on commercial transactions to earn their bread and render service in order to attain their spiritual progress"; the three ways of learning — praṇipāta (self-surrender, ṣāṣṭāṅga-praṇāma), paripraśna (detailed questioning), sevayā (service) (cf. [Pramā](/philosophy/prama.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- The four kinds are named by *varṇa* adjectives (shūdrocita…viprocita) — Sarkar's
  own categories tied to his social theory; preserve the Sanskrit names.
- Evolution by date (1971 → 1984): the four-kinds taxonomy and the
  ātmamokṣa/jagaddhita mission come first; sevā-as-karma-yoga and as the third
  prerequisite are developed later — a deepening, not a shift.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** an explicit verbatim condemnation of
  "service as self-aggrandizement" (the texts affirm service is not for selfish
  ends, but no such exact phrase was found).
