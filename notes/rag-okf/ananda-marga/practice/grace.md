# Definition

**Grace (kṛpā)** is the **descending action of Parama Puruṣa** by which the aspirant
becomes one with the Nucleus — the counterpart to the aspirant's own ascending effort and
devotion. In *Shortening the Radius* (AV 12) Sarkar asks: *"Now what is His Grace? The
moving entity becomes one with the Nucleus, how? It depends on His Grace. It does not
depend on human logic, or human intellect; it depends on His Grace."* Grace is universal
and constant — *"The Grace is everywhere … just like rainfall"* — and its non-attainment is
never God's withholding but the aspirant's own obstruction.

# Explanation

**The rainfall and the umbrella of vanity.** Sarkar's signature image recurs almost
verbatim across discourses. *Shortening the Radius* (AV 12): *"The Grace is everywhere, but
you know, just like rainfall … there is an umbrella of vanity upon your head, that's why
you are not drenched. Remove the umbrella of vanity and you will be drenched; then you will
enjoy His Grace."* *Brahma Cakra* (AV 33) repeats it — "that water of compassion is being
showered on each and every creature equally" — and widens the obstruction to the "umbrella
of hatred … the umbrella of shyness," defining sādhanā itself as **"the action of removing
this umbrella"** (also *Rādhikā Shakti*, AV 23).

**Equal to all, yet received by surrender.** Grace is impartial: *"God cannot have any
partiality … His krpā … is being equally showered upon each and every individual … equally
on pāpiis [sinners] and puṇyavānas [the virtuous]"* (AV 33); in *Brahma Krpāhi Kevalam*
(SS 1) He "constantly showers His grace without reservation even on those persons who make
no effort for salvation." Yet it is *received* only by removing pride and surrendering:
*"It is for You, O Lord, to grace me or not … If you are proud of your meritorious actions,
this pride will remain in the end and not the grace of God"* (AV 23). (Note a real tension
in the text — see fidelity.)

**Grace and self-effort, reconciled.** In *Rādhikā Shakti* (AV 23) Sarkar endorses
Ramakrishna's etymology: *"krpā is made of two syllables, kr and pā. Kr means … 'Do your
sādhana' … and pā means … 'Get.' … Get it by doing sādhana."* Effort and grace are thus not
rivals — effort (removing the umbrella) is the very condition under which ever-present grace
is *felt*. The summit of devotion is the cry **"Brahma krpāhi kevalam"** — "by Brahma's
  grace alone" (SS 1) — uttered as complete self-surrender. Supreme bliss is nonetheless the
  aspirant's **birthright, "not as a mark of compassion"**: the Father gives help out of love,
  not condescending pity (AV 12, *The Birthright of All*).

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1978–1979) — a wee bit of Cosmic grace, and the birthright

Two NHN chapters corroborate and specify the grace doctrine:

- **Effort + mahat's blessings + "a wee bit" of Cosmic grace.** (nh-1-2, *The Physical,
  Psychic and Spiritual Strata*, 12 Dec 1978, Calcutta.) In the spiritual sphere the
  afflictions are removed "by dint of their personal efforts; combined with that, there
  is also the blessing of great personalities and the grace of the Almighty Lord – *Mahat
  kṛpayaeva Bhagavatkṛpāleṣādvā* ['By the blessings of realized personalities and a wee
  bit of the divine grace']." "Endeavour is a must; then the blessings or grace of the
  realized personalities, and only a wee bit of the omniscient grace, will be enough."
  "Mahat means a great personality, a highly-evolved personality." Human effort alone
  cannot prevail against *"Aghaṭana ghaṭana paṭīyasī Māyā"* — hence "the blessings of
  realized personalities and just a wee bit of Cosmic grace are indispensable."
- **Parama Puruṣa's advent is for jīva welfare.** (nh-1-2.) "Had the question of the
  microcosmic well-being not existed, Parama Puruṣa would have remained as a nirguṇa
  [transcendental] entity. He would not have come down to the level of Saguṇa Brahma or
  Tāraka Brahma." He declares the Gītā śloka: *"Paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṃ vināśāya ca
  duṣkṛtām; Dharmasaṃsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge"* — "I incarnate Myself in this
  world from age to age for the protection of the virtuous, the destruction of the
  wicked, and the restoration of dharma." "This is the Cosmic grace, the divine grace,
  that He has already showered on humanity."
- **The birthright, corroborated.** (nh-2-4, *The Birthright of All*, 8 May 1979, Fiesch.)
  "It is your right, it is your birthright. You get help from the Supreme Father. To
  attain the supreme state of bliss is your birthright. You will get it from your Father
  as a birthright, and not as a mark of compassion." Grace here is framed through the two
  things Parama Puruṣa "cannot do": He "cannot create a second Parama Puruṣa"; and "He
  cannot hate anybody" (because "everybody is within His mind"). "Your only qualification
  is that you are the affectionate progeny of the Supreme Progenitor" — the birthright
  belongs "not only [to] human beings, but… all living creatures. Not only living
  creatures, but… all animate and inanimate objects."

## Relationships

- the **descending** counterpart to the **ascending** side of [Bhakti (devotion)](/practice/bhakti.md); the highest *kevalā* bhakti "is not attained by baths, exercises or austerity" but by grace (*Bhaktitattva*, SS 1)
- felt only through [sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md), which Sarkar defines as "just the action of removing this umbrella from upon my head"
- becoming one with the Nucleus through grace IS the attainment of [Mokṣa / liberation](/practice/moksa.md) ("the radius will be shortened, and in this very life you will become one with your Goal")
- the grace-bearing Nucleus is [Parama Puruṣa / Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md), described as *Krpāmaya*, "that which is formed of grace"
- grace is bestowed even on sinners and non-strivers, appearing to operate *alongside* the law of [karma / requital](/philosophy/karma.md) (relation not directly adjudicated in the read passages — see could-not-verify)
- *NHN 1/2*: grace is only "a wee bit" (*Mahat kṛpayaeva Bhagavatkṛpāleṣādvā*) on top of "personal efforts" plus the blessings of *mahat* ("realized personalities"); the Gītā śloka (*"Paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṃ…"*) grounds His advent in jīva welfare (cf. [Saguṇa & Nirguṇa Brahma](/cosmology/saguna-nirguna-brahma.md), [Tāraka Brahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md))
- *NHN 2/4*: "To attain the supreme state of bliss is your birthright… not as a mark of compassion"; Parama Puruṣa "cannot create a second Parama Puruṣa" and "cannot hate anybody"; "Your only qualification is that you are the affectionate progeny of the Supreme Progenitor" — corroborates the AV 12 birthright-nuance

# Notes — fidelity

- The **rainfall / umbrella-of-vanity** image is reproduced verbatim and confirmed across
  **four** independent discourses (AV 12, AV 33, AV 23 ×2) — high confidence.
- **Real tension in the text (preserve, don't smooth):** SS 9 (*The Lord of the Devotees*)
  says *"the grace of Parama Puruṣa is only showered on devotees,"* whereas SS 1 / AV 33 /
  AV 23 say grace showers **equally on all**, sinners included. The coherent reading is
  *bestowed equally / felt only by devotees* (the umbrella distinguishes them) — but SS 9's
  wording is stronger than that reconciliation; both are kept.
- Grace as **"birthright, not a mark of compassion"** (AV 12) is a deliberate nuance — do
  not flatten kṛpā into mere "mercy/pity."
- The kr + pā etymology (AV 23) is reported BY Sarkar approvingly ("And I say…") — it is
  Sarkar-endorsed, not a neutral quotation.
- **Could not verify:** the exact term *ahaetukii / ahetukii kṛpā* ("causeless grace") does
  NOT appear — "causeless" (akāraṇa) is used only of Brahma's own nature, not as a qualifier
  of grace (the unconditional sense is strongly *implied* by SS 1, not named). A discrete
  *guru-kṛpā* doctrine, and any text linking grace to microvita / ota-prota yoga, were not
  found in the read passages; the grace↔karma relation is inferential only.
- **Corroboration (AMPiN 4/15, *Bhakti and Kṛpā*, c. 1969):** the kr+pā etymology is
  reported again via "Thākur Rāmakṛṣṇa used to say" — "kr means 'do something' and pā means
  'get something'" — and grace is tied to the **birthright**: "It is the birth right of unit
  beings to attain cosmic grace"; Parama Puruṣa "judges everything by studying the inner
  sentiments of individuals," so even the most sinful are equally entitled to salvation. This
  corroborates the birthright-nuance (AV 12) and the equal-bestowal reading (SS 1/AV 33).
