# Definition

**Mokṣa** (mukti, salvation) is the **permanent liberation** of the unit being
(*jīva*) from all bondages — temporal, spatial and personal — attained when the
[unit mind](/biopsychology/unit-mind.md) dissolves and the reflected Consciousness
returns to and merges in [Puruṣottama](/cosmology/purusa.md). Etymology:
*Bandhanam Muchyate Mukti* (root *much*, "to be freed from bondage").

# Explanation

- Liberation belongs only to the **spiritual realm**: physical and psychic
  bondages admit no permanent freedom; freed from spiritual bondage, "all miseries
  and pains vanish."
- The five classical grades are presented **not** as a ranked salvation taxonomy
  but as the **feelings accompanying the piercing of successive
  [cakras](/biopsychology/cakra.md)** in [sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md):
  **sālokya** (the Lord is with me, mūlādhāra), **sāmīpya** (nearness,
  svādhiṣṭhāna), **sāyujya** (close touch, maṇipura), **sārūpya** (sameness,
  anāhata), **sārṣṭhi** ("I am He", vishuddha); beyond them, at ājñā, **kaevalya**
  ("Thou art" / oneness), culminating in *nirbīja nirvikalpa*
  ([samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md)).
- Liberation requires **exhausting reactive momenta**
  ([saṃskāra](/biopsychology/samskara.md)): good and bad actions equally bind — "a
  chain of gold" and "a chain of iron" — so the actional momenta of
  [karma](/philosophy/karma.md) must be spent through sādhanā (crude → subtle).
- **Salvation is subordinate to devotion:** "salvation loses its lustre before
  devotion"; devotion is the philosopher's stone, salvation the gold it produces.
  In the parable, the four fruits *kāma–artha–dharma–mokṣa* cannot be digested
  without the fifth — [bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md). Jñāna and karma are only
  secondary aides; without love for Brahma they become obstacles.
- **Māyā is transcended, not fought:** suppression rebounds and fleeing deceives;
  mukti is won by **channelizing** Māyā toward the Absolute (the sattvic force
  carrying the sādhaka to the edge of the *Bhāvasāgar*, which only devotion
  crosses). The doctrine of Māyā itself lives in
  [Vidyā & Avidyā](/cosmology/vidya-avidya.md).
- The highest devotee **does not even desire mukti**, preferring to remain near Him
  and surrendering even that choice ("I am at your disposal").

# In *Elementary Philosophy* (1955)

Chapter 7 ("What Is the Aim of Humanity") makes mokṣa the explicit **goal of human life**
and — unlike the discourses above, which use *mukti* and *mokṣa* interchangeably — draws a
**two-rank distinction** between them (a source-specific framing; see the fidelity note):

- **Mukti** = merger with the **Qualified** Supreme Entity (Saguṇa Brahma), i.e. *"freedom
  from the movement of the thought-waves of the Supreme."* But this *"is not emancipation in
  reality"*: the merged unit re-emerges in His thought-waves and *"re-enters the Srṣṭicakra,
  or Brahma Cakra."* Mukti can come *"either through sādhanā or in the natural course of the
  flow of the thought-waves."*
- **Mokṣa** (= *kaevalya mukti*, *"higher than that"*) = **freedom from the bondage of
  Prakṛti**: after merger with the **Non-Qualified** (Nirguṇa) Brahma, Prakṛti can no longer
  drag the unit back into creation.

The aim is grounded in the **purpose of creation itself** — *"the wish of the Qualified
Supreme Entity … for every one of Its units"* is that each attain the non-qualified rank, so
premature merger in Saguṇa Brahma *"defeats the very purpose of the Qualified Supreme
Entity."* Humanity, the *"tail-end of this thought-wave,"* is the turning point of the
return. On final merger the unit **loses all individual identity** — *"a drop of water
which, on mixing … loses its identity"* — for *"there cannot be two similar identities when
one of them is infinite."*

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — the tangential touch

The 1959 cosmology (*Ātman, Paramātman and Sādhanā*, II-6) gives a third,
cosmological framing of the mukti/mokṣa distinction (cf. the *Elementary
Philosophy* rank above):

- **Mukti = merger in Saguṇa Brahma.** "In Tantra sādhana or in Ananda Marga
  sādhana one whose goal is Puruṣottama merges in Saguṇa Brahma." Because the speeds
  of the three guṇas differ, the unit mind's movement becomes elliptical (oval); the
  aspirant aiming at Puruṣottama follows this oval path into the qualified
  Supreme — the state the chapter calls *mukti*.
- **Mokṣa = exit by a tangential touch.** "Those who aim at mokṣa, where sādhana is
  the complete surrender of self into That (Nirguṇa Brahma, the Objectless
  Consciousness), get out of this Brahma Cakra by a tangential touch." Only at that
  tangential point does the unit leave the [Brahmacakra](/cosmology/brahmacakra.md)
  altogether — the abode of [Tāraka Brahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md).
- This corroborates *Elementary Philosophy*'s rank (merger-with-Saguṇa re-enters the
  cycle; freedom from Prakṛti is final), here expressed in the geometry of the
  Brahmacakra.

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1971, 1979) — the end of symbolization and the Ātman-tīrtha

The *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* add two framings of liberation:

- **Mokṣa as the end of symbolization, achievable in this very life.** (DNE 37,
  *Expression and Symbolization*, 22 May 1971.) The sādhaka should "direct [his]
  mental symbolization so that it finds shelter in the spiritual flow," converting
  psychic symbols into "psycho-spiritual waves." "Where the change has been made
  to the psycho-spiritual plane it has to be converted into the spiritual, and
  since the Ātman, the soul, is beyond the sphere of symbolization, the problem
  of symbolization ends. It will mean final liberation, Moksa." And: "In this
  very life convert your psychic symbolization into psycho-spiritual
  symbolization and become an emancipated being" (cf.
  [Symbolization](/philosophy/symbolization.md), [Ātman](/cosmology/atman.md)).
- **"Aatman is the Supreme Tiirtha ... Aatman is the only tiirtha."** (DNE 41,
  *Art and Science*, 16 Aug 1979.) Shiva answers Pārvatī's question on holy
  places: *"Idam Tiirtham idam Tiirtham bhramanti tāmasajānāh; Ātmatiirtham na
  jānanti katham Moksō Vārāṇāne."* — "so many people are moving throughout the
  world, they do not know that the highest Tiirtha ... lies [coverted] within
  their very existence." Self-knowledge is the means to mokṣa:
  *"Aatmajinānamidam Devi param mokṣaekasādhanam; sukrtaer mānavaḥ bhūtvā
  jināniicen mokṣamāpnyāt"* — self-knowledge is the greatest means to salvation
  (cf. [Ātma Tīrtha](/practice/atma-tirtha.md), [Subjective Approach](/practice/subjective-approach.md)).

## Relationships

- attained through [Sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md); culminates in [Bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md)
- *DNE 37*: mokṣa as the end of symbolization — convert psychic symbolization into psycho-spiritual symbolization "in this very life," since the Ātman is beyond the sphere of symbolization (cf. [Symbolization](/philosophy/symbolization.md), [Ātman](/cosmology/atman.md))
- *DNE 41*: "Aatman is the Supreme Tiirtha ... Aatman is the only tiirtha"; self-knowledge the means to mokṣa (*Aatmajinānamidam Devi param mokṣaekasādhanam*) (cf. [Ātma Tīrtha](/practice/atma-tirtha.md), [Subjective Approach](/practice/subjective-approach.md))
- requires exhaustion of [Saṃskāra](/biopsychology/samskara.md) / [Karma](/philosophy/karma.md)
- transcends Māyā — see [Vidyā & Avidyā](/cosmology/vidya-avidya.md)
- final merger into [Puruṣa / Puruṣottama](/cosmology/purusa.md) ([Ātman](/cosmology/atman.md) ↔ [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md))
- *Elementary Philosophy*: distinguishes **mukti** (merger with Saguṇa — re-enters the [Brahmacakra](/cosmology/brahmacakra.md)) from **mokṣa/kaevalya** (freedom from [Prakṛti](/cosmology/prakrti.md)); the aim of creation is every unit's nirguṇa rank
- the grades ascend the [Cakras](/biopsychology/cakra.md) via the [Kuṇḍalinī](/practice/kundalini.md), ending in [Samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md)
- *Idea and Ideology* (II-6): **mukti = merger with Saguṇa** along the oval path; **mokṣa = exit by a tangential touch** into Nirguṇa — the abode of [Tāraka Brahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md)

# Notes — fidelity

- The terms sālokya…sārṣṭhi are traditionally the Vaiṣṇava "grades of mukti";
  Sarkar (AV 33/19) presents them **strictly as cakra-feelings in sādhanā**, not as
  a ranked ladder. Keep his framing.
- Evolution by date: the 1956 discourse frames liberation cosmologically (unit mind
  dissolving into Puruṣottama); 1966–68 foreground **devotion-over-salvation** and
  "Māyā-as-help" — a deepening toward bhakti-primacy, not a contradiction.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** any jīvanmukti vs videhamukti
  distinction, or a formal ranked salvation taxonomy under the word "mukti."
- **mukti vs mokṣa:** *Elementary Philosophy* (1955) draws a two-rank distinction — *mukti*
  = merger with Saguṇa (not final, re-enters the cycle) vs *mokṣa/kaevalya* = freedom from
  Prakṛti (final). The other cited discourses use the two words interchangeably; treat this
  as a source-specific (early-work) sharpening, not a contradiction. **Idea and Ideology
  (1959)** independently corroborates the two ranks via the Brahmacakra geometry (merger in
  Saguṇa vs tangential exit into Nirguṇa, at [Tāraka Brahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md)'s
  abode). The could-not-verify on a ranked taxonomy still holds for the cakra grades — the
  ranking is the mukti/mokṣa pair, not the sālokya…sārṣṭhi series.
