# Definition

**Upavāsa** (voluntary fasting on certain days) takes its derivative meaning from
*upa* + *vāsa* — *"to remain in proximity to Iishvara; that is to say, to keep one's mind
absorbed in the thought of Iishvara"* (Caryācarya 3/3). It is thus a spiritual discipline,
not mere abstention from food.

# Explanation

**When.** Those initiated into Ananda Marga [Iishvara Praṇidhāna](/practice/ishta.md) must
fast **compulsorily on every ekādashii** (the eleventh day after the new or full moon);
*sannyāsiis* and renunciants also fast on every *pūrṇimā* and *amāvasyā*. On a fast day **no
food or water** is taken from one sunrise to the next; if unavoidable circumstances prevent
it, one fasts the preceding or following day (illness exempts, with written permission of
the Dharma Pracāra Secretary).

**Why.** Sarkar gives a psycho-physiological rationale: near the new and full moon the
**gaseous and aqueous factors rise into the head and chest**, causing discomfort; fasting
draws them back down. More fundamentally, food is transformed into its final essence,
**shukra**, *"the food of the brain"* from which the ectoplasmic particles of the unit mind
are produced — *"if one fasts according to the system, no excess shukra will excite the
lower vrttis … and the mind will be led toward the higher vrttis."* Fasting also expels
bodily waste and frees the energy otherwise spent on digestion, *"therefore a fast day is an
excellent time for sādhanā."*

## Relationships

- a support for [sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md) — frees energy and keeps the mind in the higher [vṛttis](/biopsychology/citta.md); mandated by [Sixteen Points](/practice/sixteen-points.md) (point 8: "observe fast as prescribed")
- *upavāsa* = remaining in proximity to [Iishvara](/practice/ishta.md); compulsory on ekādashii for initiates
- shukra as the brain's food links to the [pañcakośa](/biopsychology/pancakosa.md) / mind-body relation (cf. [Brahmacarya](/practice/yama-niyama.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- Grounded in **Caryācarya 3/3** (1965, Jamalpur); the etymology, the ekādashii rule, the
  no-water rule and the shukra rationale are reproduced faithfully.
- The shukra/lower-vṛtti rationale is Sarkar's stated reason — keep it; do not reduce
  fasting to a generic health or willpower practice.
