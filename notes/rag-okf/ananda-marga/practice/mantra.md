# Definition

A **mantra** is a particular collection of sounds (*shabda*) whose meditation
leads to liberation — **"Mananāt tārayet yastu sah mantrah parikiirttitah"**
(*man* = to meditate/suggest + *trae* = to liberate). Not any word qualifies:
only one whose inner repetition (*manana*) effects permanent liberation
(*trāṇa*).

# Explanation

- **Sonic cosmology:** everything is vibrational; *shabda* (sound) is the first and
  subtlest expression of the vibrational flow of [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md)
  (*Shabda Brahma*). Every mantra therefore vibrates simultaneously in the Cosmic
  system and in the unit body.
- **Mantra caetanya** ("awakening" the mantra): the condition where, via a
  particular *shabda*, one establishes parallelism between external physical
  vibration and internal ectoplasmic ([citta](/biopsychology/citta.md)) vibration
  and raises it to the spiritual level; its culmination is **mantra siddhi**. An
  un-awakened mantra is "mere letters" (Sadāśiva: *"Caetanyarahitāh mantrāh… phalaṃ
  naeva prayachanti lakṣakoṭijapaerapi"* — even millions of *japa* bear no fruit).
- **Siddha mantra:** one a **Mahākaola** (a Tantric guru able to raise others'
  [kuṇḍalinī](/practice/kundalini.md)) has charged with Cosmic vibration in rhythm
  with the Macrocosm; an aspirant cannot awaken it alone — hence only such an
  entity is worthy of [guru](/practice/guru.md) status. The act of charging sound
  with power is *purashcaraṇa*.
- **Two meanings required:** a true mantra has a mundane meaning **and** a
  supporting acoustic root (e.g. *Krṣṇa* from *krṣ* = "to attract," acoustic root
  *klrṃ*; *Hara/Shiva* = *ha* + *ra*). Lacking either, the word is not a mantra —
  which is why "incantation" is an inadequate translation.
- **Iṣṭa mantra** (the personal mantra of the [Iṣṭa](/practice/ishta.md)) is the
  *praṇava / oṃkāra* — the single most meaningful word for that aspirant, kept
  secret and repeated **mentally**, the mind moving toward the Ātman. The **guru
  mantra** sanctifies one's mission/service, while the iṣṭa mantra serves personal
  salvation (*ātmamokṣa*).

## Relationships

- requires a [Guru](/practice/guru.md) (only a Mahākaola creates siddha mantras)
- works through the ectoplasmic vibration of [Citta](/biopsychology/citta.md); suited to one's [Saṃskāra](/biopsychology/samskara.md)
- awakens the [Kuṇḍalinī](/practice/kundalini.md) (purashcaraṇa, parābindu → sahasrāra)
- the chant of [Kiirtana](/practice/kiirtana.md) is a siddha mantra; the personal form is the [Iṣṭa](/practice/ishta.md) mantra
- leads to [Samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md) (mantra siddhi) and arouses [Bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md)

# Notes — fidelity

- The sound-stage sequence (parā → pashyantī → madhyamā → … → vaikharī) is only
  *listed* in the cited discourse (it refers the reader elsewhere for discussion).
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** a fixed number of *japa* repetitions;
  cakra-by-cakra mantra mappings beyond parābindu → sahasrāra; the initiation
  (*dīkṣā*) procedure.
