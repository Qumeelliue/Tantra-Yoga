# Definition

Among human beings there are **three species**: *pashu* (animals in human form),
*viira* (human in mind and body), and *deva* (gods in human form — "god" with a small
"g"). They are elevated through three Tantric styles taught by Shiva: **pashvācāra**
(animality → humanity), **vīrācāra** (south of the Himalayas) / **ciinācāra** (north
of the Himalayas; humanity → godhead), and **divyācāra** (union with **Mahādeva**).
(1979, DT 2/29.)

# Explanation

- **Pashu** — human structure, animal mind: "always moving in order to eat, drink, or
  sleep." Their practice, **pashvācāra** (taught by Shiva ~7000 years ago), elevates
  them "to become human both in mind and body."
- **Viira** ("brave") — human both in mind and body, "fighting against the fetters of
  mind, against the satanic fetters." Their style is **vīrācāra** (the avadhūtas and
  avadhūtikās practise it); north of the Himalayas the same practice is **ciinācāra**
  ("the Chinese style") — Vashistha went to China ~5000 years ago to learn it, and Pa
  Fa Hu Le came to Bengal ~1500 years ago to revive it.
- **Deva** — gods in human structure; established in vīrācāra, elevated to the
  standard of deva. "Everything in this universe emanates from the Supreme God" — all
  are different waves of the Supreme Entity; these expressions of Parama Puruṣa are
  devas, and "the factor just above these devas is **Mahādeva**" (capital "g").
  Their goal — **divyācāra** — is "attainment of the status of Mahādeva, to be one with
  the Supreme Entity."
- **The correct definition of yoga.** Against *"Sarvacintā parityāgo niścinto yoga
  ucyate"* (empty mind ≠ yoga) and Patañjali's *"Yogashcittavṛttinirodhah"* (which
  only controls the thousand propensities, "nothing to do with the proper import of
  the term"), Ananda Marga's definition is ***"Saṃyoga yoga ityukto jīvātmā
  Paramātmanah"*** — "the unification of unit consciousness with Supreme Consciousness
  is yoga"; in that unification the unit loses its identity (river becomes the sea).
- The chapter derives the **thousand propensities**: the fifty main propensities × 2
  (internal/external) × 10 (directions) = 1000, "controlled by the pineal gland… the
  pineal gland is called sahasrāra."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** the three species and three ācāras with their geography
  (south/north of the Himalayas); the Ananda Marga yoga definition is "the best
  interpretation"; the pineal-pituitary point (pineal = sahasrāra) is stated.
- **The historical claims** (Vashistha in China ~5000 yrs ago, Pa Fa Hu Le ~1500 yrs
  ago) are Sarkar's own — reproduce as his chronology, not modern history.
- **Patañjali is engaged critically** — Sarkar rejects "cittavṛttinirodhah" as the
  definition of yoga while using its propensity-count; keep the critical framing.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** an exact description of divyācāra's
  practice beyond its goal.
