# Definition

**Kiirtana** is the singing/chanting of the siddha [mantra](/practice/mantra.md)
**"Bábá Náma Kevalam"** (often to varied tunes, accompanied by the *lalita
mármika* dance), which Sarkar frames as **sádhanásaháyakam** — the most essential
**auxiliary aid to sādhanā**.

# Explanation

- Kiirtana is the aspirant's **best positive/auxiliary force**: it accelerates the
  devotee toward the hub of the universe and helps "in all circumstances, in all
  possible ways."
- It should be done, like food and sleep, **whenever there is opportunity** — even
  for two or five minutes — with **"no restriction regarding time, place or
  person,"** by educated and uneducated alike.
- It is the **panacea** for the three afflictions (physical, psychic, spiritual):
  done with distinct expression of the mantra it purifies mouth, tongue, ears and
  the whole body, so the *ātman* too becomes pure — "a holy dip in the Ganges."
- **Collective kiirtana dispels calamities**: assembled devotees' physical and
  psychic energies flow in one channel under the inspiration of Parama Puruṣa,
  bringing relief in natural calamities (flood, famine, drought, epidemic) and
  man-made ones. During kiirtana Parama Puruṣa is "compelled" to sit on the throne
  the devotees place in their hearts; even those who merely *overhear* benefit
  ("shraddhayá helayá vá" — with veneration or without).
- "Bábá Náma Kevalam" is **aṣṭākṣarī** (eight-syllabled) and must be uttered fully
  in each phrase; kiirtana carries the four stages of progress — *yatamāna,
  vyatireka, ekendriya, vashīkāra* — culminating in surrender (vashīkāra).
- It is performed with the **lalita mármika** dance: touching the earth with the
  toe-tip (minimum pressure), and in the psycho-spiritual approach raising the
  hands above 90° — "now at the mercy of Parama Puruṣa."

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1980) — the gharāṇā schools and rhythm

The *Faculty of Knowledge* discourses add a musicological note (brief):

- **The four *gharāṇā* schools of Bengali kiirtana** (DNE 43, 25 May 1980):
  "In Bengal, there are different schools of kiirtana such as Manoharshahii
  Gharānā, Reneti Gharānā, Garanāhāta Gharānā, and Māndāran Gharānā. Bengali
  kiirtana has been classified into four schools." Each has its own local style
  and history (Reneti is a distortion of "Ranihati"; Māndāran refers to
  Garhmāndāran in Hooghly District).
- **Folk to classical** (DNE 44, 2 Jun 1980): "When kiirtana first began to
  develop, it did not have any hard and fast rules, just as folk songs do not
  conform to hard and fast rules. ... as soon as kiirtana was brought within the
  framework of fixed rules, it came to be elevated from folk music to classical
  music."
- **Rhythm as memory technology** (DNE 43): "In the absence of rhythm it is
  difficult to memorize. That is why since ancient times ... the common practice
  has been to bring every valuable branch of knowledge within the scope of
  rhythm. Human beings do not easily forget rhythm" (cf.
  [Memory](/biopsychology/memory.md), [Saṃgiita](/practice/samgiita.md)).
