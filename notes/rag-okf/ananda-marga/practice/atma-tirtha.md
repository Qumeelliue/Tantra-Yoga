# Definition

**The Inner Pilgrimage.** Wandering to external holy places is the wandering of the
tamasic (ignorant): "Idaḿ Tiirthaḿidaḿ Tiirthaḿ Bhramanti Támasájanáh /
Átmatiirthaḿ na Jánanti kat́haḿ Mokśo Varánáne" — people wander calling each place
a tīrtha, not knowing the Ātma-tīrtha, so how can mokṣa come? "Átmá is the best
tiirtha. Átmá is the Supreme Tiirtha. Rather Átmá is the only tiirtha." The highest
tīrtha lies within one's own "I exist" feeling, behind which "lies the Supreme
Consciousness, the Átmá." The related shloka gives the means: "Átmajiṋánamidaḿ
Devi paraḿ mokśaekasádhanam; Sukrtaermánavo bhútvá jiṋániicenmokśamápnuyát" —
self-knowledge is the greatest means to attain salvation.

# Explanation

**The shloka on the tīrthas.** Shiva answers Pārvatiī's question about people who say
"this is a holy place, that place is an unholy place" and wander "in search of so many
Tiirthas, places of pilgrimage":

> "Idaḿ Tiirthaḿidaḿ Tiirthaḿ Bhramanti Támasájanáh
> Átmatiirthaḿ na Jánanti kat́haḿ Mokśo Varánáne."

The ignorant do not know that the highest tīrtha "lies within their very existence.
One needn't go elsewhere in search of Tiirtha."

**The tīrtha behind "I exist."** "Each and every man has the feeling that 'I exist'.
This feeling of 'I exist' is the final form of the expressed world. Behind that final
form of the expressed world, there lies the Supreme Consciousness, the Átmá." Hence:
"átmá is the best tiirtha. Átmá is the Supreme Tiirtha. Rather Átmá is the only
tiirtha."

**Self-knowledge as the only means to mokṣa.** The "minimum qualification" in the
science of spirituality, as Shiva told Pārvatiī: "Átmajiṋánamidaḿ Devi paraḿ
mokśaekasádhanam; Sukrtaermánavo bhútvá jiṋániicenmokśamápnuyát" — "Self-knowledge
is the greatest means to attain salvation. People are born as human beings due to
their past good saḿskáras, but to attain non-qualified liberation they will have to
attain self-knowledge."

**Crude to subtle, not away from the world.** "Your movement is not from subtle to
crude, but from crude to subtle" — yet "you must not neglect this crude world, because
your existence is being nourished by this crude world." The approach: "ours is a
subjective approach through objective adjustment."

## Relationships

- the inner tīrtha is the means to [moksa](/practice/moksa.md) ("param mokśaekasádhanam")
- the pilgrimage within is a [sādhana](/practice/sadhana.md) of the [subjective approach](/practice/subjective-approach.md) (subjective approach through objective adjustment)
- self-knowledge (*ātmajñāna*) is the work of the [faculty of knowledge](/philosophy/faculty-of-knowledge.md)
- reaching the Ātma behind "I exist" is the goal of [dhyāna](/practice/dhyana.md) and is sustained by [bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md)

# Notes — fidelity

- **Shloka framing kept exact:** the tīrtha doctrine is given in Shiva's shloka and
  its immediate prose gloss; the "Átmá is the only tiirtha" conclusion is quoted
  verbatim, not smoothed into a generic "inner pilgrimage."
- **Source context:** this teaching occurs within *Art and Science* (DNE 41) on
  Shiva's spiritual science (Nigama/Āgama); the tīrtha passage is preserved as a
  distinct teaching.
- **"I exist" is Sarkar's term for the final form of the expressed world** — kept as
  printed, including the source's transliteration (Tiirtha, Átma, jiṋána).
- **Could not verify:** an independent scriptural source for the two shlokas; they
  are cited as spoken by Shiva within the discourse.
