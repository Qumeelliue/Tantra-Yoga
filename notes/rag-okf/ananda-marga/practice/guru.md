# Definition

Out of the **intense desire for mukti (liberation)** one attains one's **sadguru**
(true spiritual preceptor). Ánanda Sútram 3-8:
*"Muktyākāunkṣayā sadguruprāptih."* And ultimately **Brahma alone is the guru**
(3-9, *"Brahmaeva gururekah nāparah"*).

# Explanation

When a *vehement desire for emancipation* awakens in a person, that very desire
draws the sadguru to him or her. Yet in the deepest sense **only
[Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md) is the guru**: Brahma alone directs the units to
the path of emancipation, working **through the media of different receptacles or
bodies**. No one except Brahma conforms to the real significance of the word
"guru."

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1967, 1987) — the guru's house and the grace of the Guru

The *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* add two framings:

- **The guru's-house system and *chatra*.** (DNE 9, *The Education System of the
  Mahābhārata Age*, 10 Sep 1967.) "Generally students would go to the guru's
  house, at the age of five, and, completing their studies by the age of
  twenty-four, would return home." The pupils "were under the control of, within
  the jurisdiction of, one pandit with one school of thought." "'Chatra' means one
  who is under the control and jurisdiction (chatra) of a school of thought of
  one guru" — "Chatra" has "now wrongly been used to mean any student. Present
  students are not chatra" (cf. [Gurukul](/philosophy/gurukul.md),
  [Education](/philosophy/education.md)).
- **"Guru krpāhi kevalam" and the microvital grace.** (DNE 29, *Bio-Psychology*,
  3 Jun 1987.) A spiritual aspirant practises "to satisfy Parama Puruṣa in the
  form of Parama Guru. That is why it has been rightly said: Guru krpāhi kevalam
  ['The grace of the Guru is everything']." "When the Guru is pleased with the
  disciples' efforts, the Guru graces them by encouraging and enhancing their
  good propensities and by reducing their bad ones by microvita. The good
  propensities are enhanced by positive microvita and the bad propensities are
  reduced by positive microvita. Through microvita the Guru does the needful to
  increase or decrease the secretion of the glands" (cf.
  [Microvita](/cosmology/microvita.md)).

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1987) — the sevenfold verse of the psycho-physical urge

*Physical Propulsion, Psychic Propensities and Spiritual Attainment* (nh-2-11, 1 Jan
1987, Tiljala, Calcutta) supplies the seven *sādhanā* requisites in the physical
stratum, verse and gloss:

- **The verse.** *"Phaliṣyatiiti viśvāsah siddherprathamā lakṣaṇam / Dvitīyaṃ śraddhayā
  yuktaṃ tṛtīyaṃ gurupūjanam / Caturtho samatābhāvo pañcamendriyanigrahaḥ / Ṣaṣṭhaiṃca
  pramitāhāro saptamam naeva vidyate."*
- **(1) Firm determination.** "The first item of the psycho-physical urge is the firm
  determination that 'I must be successful in my mission.'"
- **(2) Śraddhā.** "The second item is that one should have respect for one's goal.
  'Śrat + dhā = śraddhā.' 'Śrat' means satyam, the Supreme Veracity, and 'dhā' means
  movement towards that. So 'śraddhā' means movement towards the Supreme Veracity.
  Sometimes people wrongly understand the word 'śraddhā'." — *"Śrat satyaṃ Tasmin
  dhīyate yā sā śraddhā."*
- **(3) Guru-pūjā.** "Tṛtīyaṃ gurupūjanam: one should do as per the gospels of the
  preceptor."
- **(4) Balanced mind.** "Caturtho samatābhāvaḥ: one must have balanced mind, that is,
  one must establish Pramā within and without i.e., in the physical, psychic and
  spiritual spheres. If Pramā is lost, it must be restored immediately" (cf.
  [Pramā](/philosophy/prama.md)).
- **(5) Self-restraint.** "Pañcamendriya nigrahaḥ: one must have self-restraint, one
  must not be goaded by crude propensities."
- **(6) Balanced diet.** "Ṣaṣṭhaiṃca pramitāhāraḥ: one must have a balanced diet.…
  This human body is a composite of protoplasmic cells, and these protoplasmic cells
  are made of the food we take in. If the food and drink is defective, then the
  protoplasmic cells will also become defective, and the human mind, which is the
  collective mind of so many protoplasmic minds, will also become defective. That is
  why tāmasik or static food has a negative influence on the human mind."
- **(7) The seventh is not found.** "saptamam naeva vidyate" — no seventh requisite is
  given.

## Relationships

- attained through the longing for liberation (the aim of [Dharma](/philosophy/dharma.md))
- *DNE 9*: the guru's-house system of the Vedic/Mahābhārata age — students went at five and "completing their studies by the age of twenty-four, would return home"; "Chatra" = one under the control and jurisdiction (chatra) of one guru's school of thought (cf. [Gurukul](/philosophy/gurukul.md), [Education](/philosophy/education.md))
- *DNE 29*: "Guru krpāhi kevalam" — the Guru graces the disciple by enhancing good propensities and reducing bad ones through [Microvita](/cosmology/microvita.md)
- ultimately, the one guru is [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md), acting through bodies
- guides the aspirant past [obstacles](/practice/obstacles.md) toward [Samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md)
- *NHN 2/11*: the sevenfold verse of the psycho-physical urge — (1) *viśvāsaḥ* (firm determination), (2) *śraddhā* ("Śrat" = satyam/the Supreme Veracity + "dhā" = movement — "movement towards the Supreme Veracity"; "Śrat satyaṃ Tasmin dhīyate yā sā śraddhā"), (3) *gurupūjanam*, (4) *samatābhāvaḥ* (balanced mind — "If Prama is lost, it must be restored immediately"), (5) *pañcamendriya nigrahaḥ*, (6) *pramitāhāraḥ* (tāmasik food affects protoplasmic cells and the mind), (7) *saptamam naeva vidyate* (cf. [Pramā](/philosophy/prama.md), [Sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md), [Sevā](/practice/seva.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- "Brahma alone is the guru" reframes the human preceptor as a *receptacle*
  through which Brahma acts — not as an independent object of worship. Preserve
  this non-dual framing.
