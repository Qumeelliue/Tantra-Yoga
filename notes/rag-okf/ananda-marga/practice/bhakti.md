# Definition

**Bhakti (devotion) is ideation on God** (*bhagavad-bhāvanā*) — being merged in
the constant thought of God — **not** flattery of God or ritualistic worship.
Ánanda Sútram 3-12: *"Bhaktirbhagavadbhāvanā na stutirnārcanā."*

# Explanation

Devotion is **not** related to the chanting of hymns or ritualistic worship with
paraphernalia; a devotee *may* perform these, but they are **not an indispensable
part** of devotional sādhanā. Indeed, *prayer and ritualistic worship are a source
of confusion* (3-11, *"Prārthanārcanā mātraeva bhramamūlam"*): it is useless to
pray for something, "for He is sure to give what is necessary," and solicitation
in the name of worship is "nothing but toadyism and flattery."

# In the prose discourses

**Grades of devotion; kevalā bhakti as the goal.** Because people often relate to
God selfishly, devotion is graded. Along the guṇas: *tāmasikī bhakti* (static —
praying that harm befall enemies), *rājasikī bhakti* (mutative — praying for
personal gain), *sāttvikī bhakti* (sentient — wanting only the end of one's own
afflictions); **none attains Parama Puruṣa**, since none truly wants Him alone. The
highest is **kevalā bhakti** — exclusive, non-attributional devotion wanting Him
and nothing else — itself graded into *rāganugā bhakti* (a faint tinge of
selfishness, worshipping to enjoy bliss) and the wholly selfless *rāgātmikā bhakti*,
whose devotee is a *gopa* ("one who gives pleasure to Parama Puruṣa"). Yoga and
Tantra are the practical cult (*puṣṭhi mārga*) nourishing this faculty; Parama
Puruṣa is attained "by this devotion alone, not by the vanity of jñāna or the
restlessness of karma." A fuller definition is also given: *Bhaktir Bhagavato sevā
bhaktih prema svarūpinii; bhaktirānanda rupā ca bhakti bhaktasya jiivanam* —
devotion is service to God, the form of divine love, the embodiment of bliss, and
the very life of the devotee.

**Cardinal vs non-cardinal prayer.** The sūtra's blanket caution is refined:
*cardinal* prayer concerns only spiritual advancement — "guide my intellect onto
the path of bliss" (the Savitṛ Ṛk / Gāyatrī, asking for *shubha-buddhi*) — and is
acceptable, even necessary; *non-cardinal* prayer ("give me this or that") is
futile, since one's real needs are better known to Him. Attributional worship is
impossible; only adorative and ideative worship remain.

**The trio — jñāna, karma, bhakti.** Knowledge (subjectivization), action (the
actional faculty) and devotion (pointedness) are three avenues; the first two are
defective — knowledge breeds pride, action is bound by time/space/relativity —
while **devotion alone liberates**. Among the three the devotee ranks first
("A-grade"), above the karmī and jñānī.

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — bhakti as longing for the Great

The 1959 chapter *Psycho-Spiritual Parallelism* (II-9) ties bhakti to the
integrated-personality scheme (see [Idea & Ideology](/philosophy/ideology.md)):

- **"The real bhakti or devotion" is longing for the Great.** "The spiritual body
  is the higher mind, subtler taste or higher taste. When the microcosm keeps
  itself absorbed in the Macrocosm, the meaner and cruder thoughts wave away. The
  spiritual body gets strengthened by longing for the Great — an intense desire to
  be for Him. This is the real bhakti or devotion. This is the ultimate and only
  way to final liberation or final union."
- **It crowns the sūtra's ideal:** devotion — not jñāna or karma — is the
  "ultimate and only way," corroborating the kevalā-bhakti teaching above.
- **Śaṅkarācārya is cited** for the supremacy of devotion over every other means:
  *"Mokṣakāraṇa samagryāṃ bhaktireva gariyasiī"* — "of all the means to mokṣa,
  devotion alone is the best." (The same chapter is the source of the bhakti-as-idea
  parallelism.)

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1982) — the devotional sentiment as humanity's greatest treasure

*Devotional Sentiment and Neohumanism (Discourse 1)* (DNE 24, 21 Feb 1982) frames
bhakti as the crowning human treasure and links its preservation to the
subjective-approach method and Neohumanism:

- **The devotional sentiment is the highest and most valuable treasure of
  humanity.** "This element of devotion, the most precious treasure of humanity,
  must be preserved most carefully." Because it is "such a tender inner asset, to
  preserve it from the onslaughts of materialism, one must build a protective
  fence around it, just as people put up a guard-rail around a small tender
  plant." The fence is "a proper philosophy which will establish the correct
  harmony between the spiritual and material worlds, and be a perennial source of
  inspiration for the onward movement of society."
- **Kṛṣṇa as the *vaeduryamaṇi* of the heart.** "I have said in *Námámi
  Kṛṣṇasundaram* that Kṛṣṇa is the vaeduryamaṇi [brightest jewel] of the human
  heart."
- **Geo-sentiment and socio-sentiment pollute the devotional sentiment.**
  Geo-sentiment "attempts to keep humanity confined within a limited part of this
  world"; it "pollutes the devotional sentiment; it degrades human beings and
  undermines human excellence." Socio-sentiment, though "a bit better," is "not
  altogether ideal" — it has "caused much bloodshed and created enormous division
  and mutual distrust among human groups."
- **The real bhaktas.** "All molecules, atoms, electrons, protons, positrons and
  neutrons are the veritable expressions of the same Supreme Consciousness. Those
  who remember this reality, who keep this realization ever alive in their
  hearts, are said to have attained perfection in life. They are the real bhaktas
  [devotees]." When this devotional cult is elevated into "a devotional
  sentiment, a devotional mission," with the underlying spirit of humanism
  extended to everything animate and inanimate, "I have designated this as
  'Neohumanism'" (cf. [Neohumanism](/philosophy/neohumanism.md)).
- **Food and rational adjustment.** "living creatures are the food for other
  living beings (jiivah jiivasya bhojanam); and indeed, the vegetables that we
  eat every day also have living cells in them" — human beings "will have to
  behave rationally; they must maintain their existence while adjusting with the
  external environment."
- **The subjective approach recap.** "the actual task of human beings is to
  maintain a subjective approach – that is, they will advance psycho-spiritually
  towards the Supreme Consciousness, inspired by Neohumanistic ideals – while at
  the same time they must strive for the expansion of humanistic principles and
  thus establish a social structure based on universalism" (cf.
  [Subjective Approach through Objective Adjustment](/practice/subjective-approach.md)).
- **Prayer to preserve the treasure.** When "the pressure of external
  circumstances threatens this life essence, we should appeal to the Supreme: 'O
  Lord, please be kind enough to protect our inner vitality from total
  destruction. Save us from the agony of total loss.'"

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1978–1979) — devotion as the only path, and the only friend

Three NHN chapters supply the devotional doctrine in its most direct form:

- **The bhakta reaches right to the goal.** (nh-2-2, *Devotion – the Only Path*, 18 Nov
  1978, Patna.) "A jñānī (person of knowledge) reaches near the goal but yet a little
  distance remains in between. A karmī (person of action) reaches nearer the goal but
  even then a little gap remains. A bhakta (person of devotion) reaches right to the
  goal." The jñānī and the karmī "repent ultimately"; "There is no repentance in the
  minds of the bhaktas, however, because their minds are always full of bliss… the
  unbroken flow of happiness (ānanda)."
- **The gopa — the supreme height of devotion.** (nh-2-2.) "Those who work for the
  happiness of Parama Puruṣa are called 'gopa'. This is the supreme height of devotion.
  It is the only true path." The goal of this path "is not to ask Parama Puruṣa for
  something or to get something from Him, but to serve Him with the purpose of making Him
  happy."
- **The devotee-and-dog anecdote.** (nh-2-2.) While a devotee ate with a hungry dog, an
  intellectual protested; the devotee replied, "I am Viṣṇu, this dog is Viṣṇu. Why then
  do you laugh at me? The whole universe is pervaded by Viṣṇu."
- **Upavāsa and devotionless minds.** (nh-2-2.) "Upavāsa (fasting) means living nearer to
  God. 'Upa' means near and 'vasa' means living. During ekādaśī upavāsa the mind of a
  devotionless person will be in the stomach and not with Parama Puruṣa."
- **Yoga and real knowledge.** (nh-2-2.) "Where the microcosm becomes one with the
  Macrocosm it is called 'yoga'." "Self-realization is the real knowledge and nothing
  else can be classed as such. All other knowledge is a mess of knowledge."
- **Bhakti mārga as the most intelligent phase.** (nh-2-3, *The Process of Human
  Progress*, 31 Dec 1978, Patna.) The intellectual approach of bhakti was discovered
  "nine to ten thousand years ago.… The people of this phase of human history were the
  most intelligent people." Karma and jñāna yoga, though "less intelligent," are the
  "bright side": "these two yogas will strengthen the path of devotion, will make it
  more solid."
- **Only Parama Puruṣa is your friend.** (nh-2-5, *Social Animals and Friends*, 25 May
  1979, Rotterdam.) "Nobody is your friend. Only Parama Puruṣa is your friend, and nobody
  else is your friend." The four Sanskrit words for "friend" are NOT synonyms:
  **bandhu** — "those within the bondage of love… when one cannot tolerate the idea of
  separation"; **suhrd** — "with whom there is never any clash, clash of ideology";
  **sakhā** — "one entity in two different structures"; **mitram** — "colleague… a
  similar duty and profession." Only Parama Puruṣa is bandhu, suhrd and sakhā; humans
  may at best be mitram ("two physicians are mitram… but they are at best colleagues
  because there is competition between them").

# In *Ananda Marga Philosophy in a Nutshell* (1969) — Bhakti and Kṛpā

*Bhakti and Kṛpā* (AMPiN 4/15) gives the fullest definition and grade-scheme:

- **Definitions.** *"Bhaktirbhagavato sevā bhaktih premasvarūpinii; bhaktirānanda rūpā
  ca bhakti bhaktasya jiivanam"* — devotion is service to Parama Puruṣa, love and
  bliss personified, the very breath of the devotee. Etymologically *bhaj + ktin*; per
  Ánanda Sútram, ideation on the Supreme, *na stuti nārcanā* (neither eulogy nor
  ritual worship). *"Sā parānuraktirīshvare"* — *rakti* (attraction) for the Infinite
  Entity; love for the finite is *āsakti*. "Attraction is the law of nature."
- **Grace and the birthright.** "Without total surrender, Macrocosmic grace is not
  attained, and without Macrocosmic grace nothing is attained" (*Nānyah panthā
  vidyate'yanāya*). "It is the birth right of unit beings to attain cosmic grace";
  Parama Puruṣa "judges everything by studying the inner sentiments of individuals" —
  even the most sinful are equally entitled to salvation. The bandit Rātnakar's
  one-day intense longing sufficed. *Kṛpā* (grace): "kr means 'do something' and pā
  means 'get something'" — to merit grace one must ideate on Him.
- **The grade-scheme** (with *Nārada Bhaktisūtrāṇi*): (1) **nirguṇā bhakti** — running
  toward the goal without motive, out of intense love; (2) **vaedhī bhakti** —
  ostensible vows and ritual paraphernalia, "lack of expression of the simplicity of
  the devotee's heart"; (3) **jñānamishrā bhakti** — self-knowledge unfolding, but
  with "a slight vanity of occult power and knowledge"; (4) **kevalā bhakti** — one
  identified with the goal, "not attainable by one's individual effort… by the grace of
  great personalities or Parama Puruṣa"; (5) **rāgātmikā bhakti** — "I love Him because
  I cannot remain without loving Him" (prior stage: rāganugā — to *get* pleasure is the
  main thing; rāgātmikā — to *give* pleasure). "In Ananda Marga, there is no place for
  vaedhī bhakti or jñānamishrā bhakti… every sādhaka is a follower of rāgātmikā," moving
  on to kevalā bhakti.
- **Haribhakti.** *"Ananyāmamatā Viṣṇormamatā premasaṃgatā"* — intense love and
  attraction for Viṣṇu is Haribhakti: *"Cāṇḍālopi dvijashreṣṭha haribhaktiparāyaṇa;
  haribhaktivihiinaśca vipro'pi śvapācādhamah"* — a pariah with Haribhakti is superior
  to a brāhmaṇ; a vipra without it is inferior to a pariah.

# Notes — fidelity

- 3-11's rejection of petitionary prayer and ritual is a **direct teaching**, not
  an interpretation. Keep "prayer/worship as confusion" intact; do not soften to
  "ritual is optional."
- **Idea and Ideology (1959)** defines bhakti independently as *longing for the
  Great* and cites Śaṅkarācārya for devotion's supremacy — a source-specific
  framing, consistent with (not identical to) the SS/AV grade-scheme above.
- **AMPiN 4/15 (c. 1969)** supplies the five-grade scheme (nirguṇā/vaedhī/
  jñānamishrā/kevalā/rāgātmikā) and the *sā parānuraktīrīshvare* definition — a
  source-specific, more detailed grade-scheme than the SS/AV tāmasikī→kevalā ladder;
  keep both distinct. The "no place for vaedhī/jñānamishrā" is direct teaching.
