# Definition

**Iṣṭa** is one's **chosen, dearest object of ideation and adoration** — the
**personal God** ([Parama Puruṣa / Tāraka Brahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md))
whose realization fills the mind and ends all hunger and thirst. Sarkar binds it
to dharma: **"Yato dharma tato iṣṭah, yato iṣṭa tato jayah"** — where there is
dharma there is iṣṭa, and where there is iṣṭa there is victory.

# Explanation

- Iṣṭa has **two convergent meanings**: the *dearest/most-favourite* object, and
  the one whose realization *fills the mind* (removing all dissatisfaction). In the
  final analysis the two are one.
- The iṣṭa is the **personal God** to whom the devotee can reveal joys and sorrows
  and take shelter — **not** the formless, impersonal
  ([Nirguṇa](/cosmology/saguna-nirguna-brahma.md)) God of philosophy, for the human
  heart cannot love an abstraction.
- *Ādarsha* (ideal/ideology, the goal all entities move toward) and *iṣṭa* (the
  cherished personal goal) are theoretically distinct but **practically the same**;
  the aspirant becomes one with the iṣṭa through Bhāgavata [dharma](/philosophy/dharma.md).
- **A psychic law:** "any object takes the form of its goal" — the
  [microcosm](/biopsychology/unit-mind.md) is transmuted into its object of
  ideation — therefore the object of ideation must be chosen **very carefully**.
- Sarkar **rejects false objects** of ideation (time, nature, fate, accident, the
  five quinquelemental factors, blind Cosmic Energy, the limited unit spirit): each
  is bound by time/space/person or is cruder than the Cognitive Force. Only Parama
  Puruṣa qualifies.
- Each microcosm has **two "I"s** — the small finite "I" (finite happiness) and the
  greater "I," Parama Puruṣa (infinite happiness); the iṣṭa is this greater "I."
- **Victory** belongs to dharma/iṣṭa, not the weak human: by constant ideation
  (like a lake joining the ocean) and *sharaṇāgati* (taking shelter) one succeeds.

## Relationships

- realized through [Bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md) and held by the [Iṣṭa mantra](/practice/mantra.md)
- identified with [Tāraka Brahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md) and [Puruṣa](/cosmology/purusa.md) (the greater "I")
- inseparable from [Dharma](/philosophy/dharma.md); contrasted with impersonal [Nirguṇa Brahma](/cosmology/saguna-nirguna-brahma.md)
- the goal toward which the [Unit Mind](/biopsychology/unit-mind.md) is transmuted; "fate" reframed as [Saṃskāra](/biopsychology/samskara.md) and rejected as a goal

# Notes — fidelity

- Iṣṭa is the **personal, loving God** — keep this distinction from the impersonal
  Absolute; it is the affective core of devotion, not a metaphysical abstraction.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** the Sanskrit *bhāvanā* as the word
  for "ideation" (the English "ideation" is pervasive, but *bhāvanā* itself does
  not appear); a formal etymology of *iṣṭa*; the technical *iṣṭa-mantra* sense
  (defined in a separate discourse — see [Mantra](/practice/mantra.md)).
