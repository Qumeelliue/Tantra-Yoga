# Definition

**Be a First-Class Person.** There are "three categories of human beings," classified
by the consistency of thought, word and deed. **A-class people** — "whatever they
think, they speak, and whatever they speak, they do." **B-class people** — "they
think one thing and speak another, but they do whatever they speak." **C-class
people** — "They think one thing, speak another, and do something altogether
different." The exhortation: "You should try to become A-class people. You should
speak what you think, and you should do what you speak."

# Explanation

**The three categories.** The classification is Sarkar's own, by the alignment of the
three expressions of the human personality:

- **A-class:** "their thoughts, words and deeds are the same – which means whatever
  they think, they speak, and whatever they speak, they do."
- **B-class:** "their thoughts and words are different, but whatever they speak, they
  do – which means they think one thing and speak another, but they do whatever they
  speak."
- **C-class:** "their thoughts, words and deeds are different from each other and
  never the same. Such people think one thing, speak another, and do something
  altogether different. Such people are C-class people."

**The indictment of the present-day leaders.** "Most of the leaders of the present-day
world fall in this category" — the C-class.

**The ideal.** "You should try to become A-class people. You should speak what you
think, and you should do what you speak."

## Relationships

- the A-class ideal — thought, word and deed in agreement — is the practical heart of [moralism](/philosophy/moralism.md)
- the B-class gap (word ≠ thought, deed = word) is a milder lapse of [social values](/philosophy/social-values.md); the C-class gap is their negation
- the ideal of personal consistency serves [neohumanism](/philosophy/neohumanism.md)'s universal conduct
- the discipline to align word and deed is cultivated in [sādhana](/practice/sadhana.md) and the [yama-niyama](/practice/yama-niyama.md)
- condemning the C-class "leaders of the present-day world" echoes the criticism of "human chameleons" and their [metamorphosed sentimental strategy](/practice/proto-psycho-spirituality.md)
