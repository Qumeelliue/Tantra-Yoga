# Definition

In its supreme spiritual sense, **yoga** is **saṃyoga** — the *unification* of the
unit consciousness ([jīvātman](/cosmology/atman.md)) with the Cosmic Consciousness
([Paramātman / Parama Puruṣa](/cosmology/brahma.md)): *"Saṃyoga yoga ityukto
jiivātmā Paramātmanah."* It is not physical exercise but union "like sugar and
water."

# Explanation

- **Two roots:** *yuj* ("to add" — identities preserved, like counted mangoes) vs
  *yuiñj* ("to unify" — identities lost, like sugar dissolving in water). Spiritual
  yoga derives from **yuiñj** = unification, not addition.
- Sadāśiva's (Tantra's) definition above is "the most scientific." **Patanjali's**
  *"Yogashcittavṛttinirodhah"* (suspension of propensities) is judged inadequate:
  mere suspension names no desideratum, and withdrawn propensities, lacking a goal,
  disturb the mind internally — Tantra "moves a step further" by **goading the
  withdrawn propensities toward the Supreme**.
- Yoga operates across **three strata**: physical (all action within the Creator's
  scope; via Guru Mantra), psychic/intellectual (*Karma Yoga*, *Jñāna Yoga*) and
  spiritual (*Bhakti Yoga*); imbalance loses "human equipoise."
- Its **three branches** are *jñāna* (knowledge), *karma* (selfless action) and
  *bhakti* (devotion) — all leading to oneness with Parama Puruṣa, with **devotion
  supreme** and most efficient given a short human life.
- **Yoga ≈ Tantra:** unification is reached only through gradual psycho-spiritual
  expansion, which *is* the practice of [Tantra](/practice/tantra.md) /
  [sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md); jñānīs, karmīs and bhaktas are all Tantrics.
- "**Yoga is dharma**" — the path of progress every human must tread, the panacea
  for physical, psychic and spiritual ailments; emphatically **not** mere exercise.
- Its three guiding factors (Krṣṇa's *"Praṇipātena pariprashnena sevayā"*): *sevā*
  (unilateral service), *pariprashna* (questioning only for spiritual practice) and
  *praṇipāta* (complete surrender / ego-dissolution).

## Relationships

- equates with [Tantra](/practice/tantra.md); realized through [Sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md)
- defined as union of [Ātman](/cosmology/atman.md) with [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md); is [Dharma](/philosophy/dharma.md)
- three branches via [Bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md) (supreme), jñāna and karma; leads to [Mokṣa](/practice/moksa.md)
- goads the propensities of [Citta](/biopsychology/citta.md) toward the Supreme; uses the [Mantra](/practice/mantra.md); ego is the chief [obstacle](/practice/obstacles.md)

# Notes — fidelity

- "Yoga ≈ Tantra" is Sarkar's **own explicit claim**, not a comparativist gloss.
- Doctrine progressively enriched across 1978–79 (union-definition → three-branches
  synthesis → three-strata + mysticism framing); consistent throughout.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** the full *aṣṭāṅga* eight-limb list.
  Only *āsana, prāṇāyāma, dhāraṇā, dhyāna* are named in the body; "aṣṭāṅga" itself
  appears only in an editor's footnote. Do not assert a complete eight-limb scheme
  from this corpus.
