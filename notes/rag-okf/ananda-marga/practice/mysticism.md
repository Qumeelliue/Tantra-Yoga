# Definition

Sarkar gives an explicit, repeated formula: **"Mysticism is a never-ending
endeavour to find a link between finite and infinite. It is the first phase of
non-attributional devotion"** (AV 23). In the Istanbul discourse he expands it:
"Mysticism is the never-ending endeavour to find out the link between the finite and
the infinite … between the self and the Super-Self, *khud* and *Khudā*" (AV 14). He
also frames it devotionally as **"the search for the Great by the little,"** adding
that "the fundamental point of spirituality depends on this mysticism" (AV 30).

# Explanation

**The longing of the finite for the Infinite.** In *Mysticism* (AV 30) Sarkar speaks
in the first person: "I exist, my Lord exists, and my search for His glamour … is
never-ending … This search for the Great by the little is called mysticism." It is
the very basis of spiritual life — "The fundamental point of spirituality depends on
this mysticism" — and its goal is reached "when this … outward existence of
mysticism coincides with the inner spirit of mysticism … the unit becomes Cosmic."

**Relation to devotion and yoga.** *Mysticism and Spirituality* (AV 23) names it
"the first phase of non-attributional devotion," distinguishing *bhakti* (attraction
to the unlimited) from *āsakti* (attraction to finite objects). *Mysticism and Yoga*
(AV 14) stages it — aesthetic science → mysticism → spirituality — "when this
mysticism reaches the pinnacle of human glory … it is called spirituality"; here
yoga is "the finite moving towards the Infinite in a mystic style," a unification
"like sugar and water," not arithmetic addition.

**Relation to aesthetics — and how it exceeds it.** Mysticism arises "when the
aesthetic sense, based on the subtle aesthetic science, comes to touch a certain
standard" (AV 14). In *Aesthetics and Mysticism* (AV 34) Sarkar places "the world of
mystics" *beyond* "the world of aesthetics," rooting all art, architecture,
literature and music "just at the common point of aesthetics and mystics" — so
mysticism transcends and goads aesthetic charm toward Parama Puruṣa.

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1979) — the way of mysticism

*The Ever-Expanding Domain of the Microcosm* (DNE 13, 16 Jun 1979) frames
mysticism as the only way beyond the relative factors:

- **Transcending time, space and person.** "The human entity exists within the
  boundaries of time, space and person. While remaining within the scope of time,
  space and person, people should endeavour to go beyond, to transcend these
  limitations and march on towards the Infinite."
- **"There is no alternative."** "There is no other alternative to reach the goal
  of the Infinite than following the path of synthesis, than evolving a synthetic
  consciousness. Thus one will have to adopt the way of mysticism, to break free
  from the bondages of limitation and merge with the Infinite. There is no
  alternative." (Cf. [Relativity](/cosmology/relativity.md),
  [Synthesis & Analysis](/philosophy/synthesis-and-analysis.md).)
- **"Caraeveti, caraeveti."** "They must march ahead vigorously with firm steps,
  keeping a synthetic goal ever before them. 'Caraeveti, caraeveti' – 'March
  forward, march forward'. This should be the slogan of today's humanity."

## Relationships

- mysticism is "the first phase of non-attributional [devotion](/practice/bhakti.md)"; Sarkar contrasts *bhakti* (attraction to the unlimited) with *āsakti* (attraction to finite objects) (AV 23)
- *DNE 13*: mysticism is the way to transcend [time, space and person](/cosmology/relativity.md) — "march on towards the Infinite"; "There is no alternative"; "Caraeveti, caraeveti" ("March forward, march forward") (cf. [Relativity](/cosmology/relativity.md), [Synthesis & Analysis](/philosophy/synthesis-and-analysis.md))
- it is the basis of [yoga](/practice/yoga.md): yoga is "the finite moving towards the Infinite in a mystic style," a unification (sugar-and-water) rather than mere addition (AV 14)
- it expresses the longing of the unit toward [Brahma](/cosmology/brahma.md) / Parama Puruṣa — "the search for the Great by the little" — culminating in the unit becoming Cosmic (AV 30)
- its goal is the inexplicable Supreme Bliss, linking it to [ānanda](/cosmology/ananda.md) as the end of the never-ending search (AV 14, AV 23)

# Notes — fidelity

- **Exact wording:** the primary formula in AV 23 is "find *a* link between finite
  and infinite" (no articles); AV 14 reads "find *out the* link between *the* finite
  and *the* infinite" — both verbatim. Use AV 23 as the canonical phrasing.
- *Mysticism and Spirituality* appears reprinted across three places (AV 23/18 and
  two SS gloss+discourse prints of the same 20 Oct 1967 DMC); AV 23/18 cited as
  canonical, with a near-identical restatement in *The Supreme Cognition* (SS 24).
- Sarkar treats mysticism as BOTH an urge ("never-ending endeavour") AND a stage
  ("the first phase of non-attributional devotion"; the band between aesthetic
  science and spirituality) — direct teaching, not interpretation.
- **No `sanskrit` field:** Sarkar gives no single Saṃskṛta equivalent for
  "mysticism" — he uses the English term and glosses it with the Persian/Urdu pair
  *khud / Khudā* (self / Super-Self).
- **Could not verify:** no read passage frames the mysticism definition via the words
  "microcosm / Macrocosm" (he uses finite/infinite, unit/Cosmic, the little/the
  Great); and no samādhi-specific linkage was found, so none is asserted.
