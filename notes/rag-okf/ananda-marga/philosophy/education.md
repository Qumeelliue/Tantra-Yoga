# Definition

**Education** is, in Sarkar's scheme, not literacy but the awakening of "a thirst for
knowledge": "real education means proper, adequate knowledge and the power of
understanding… an awareness of who I am and what I ought to do." Teachers are
**samāja gurus** — the guides of society. (1959, Human Society 1/2.)

# Explanation

- **The awakening of a thirst for knowledge.** "It will not do to impart education
  through intimidation. A thirst for knowledge must be awakened, and, to quench that
  thirst, proper education must be given." Fear-based learning fades when the fear
  disappears. Children are inclined to play and stories, so "children should be
  educated through play methods" and stories (history, geography, universalism);
  adolescents through idealism ("the dream of the future first crystallizes in the mind
  of the adolescent"); young adults through a harmonious blend of idealism and realism.
- **Teachers are samāja gurus.** Teachers must be selected carefully — academic
  qualifications alone do not qualify; they need integrity, strength of character,
  righteousness, social-service feeling, unselfishness, inspiring personality and
  leadership ability. Their salaries should be at least on a par with public servants;
  and they must have the right to formulate educational policy, "instead of being mere
  teaching machines." A teacher's conduct must set the example (no smoking after
  lecturing on intoxicants; no factions; no political exploitation of students).
- **The responsibilities of parents.** Parents bear most of the responsibility for the
  moral and spiritual education of the child; society must see that children of immoral
  parents are brought up as virtuous citizens (removing them from the unwholesome
  environment if possible). Parents need only "two virtues: self-restraint and good
  judgement"; they must not lie to, terrorise (goblins/ghosts), or deceive their
  children — children learn deception at home.
- **Sāhityikas — the teachers of society.** Writers who hold the welfare of all
  humanity in mind are *sāhityikas*, a kind of teacher; literature, radio, pictures,
  dramas and cinema can educate — provided they are not controlled by parties or
  commercial interests. "Cheap detective novels, adventure stories and nationalistic or
  communalistic stories… gradually deprive [youngsters] of sound judgement."
- **Nationalism and communalism in education are detrimental.** "Like provincialism and
  communalism, nationalism is highly detrimental to the minds of children." Education
  should strengthen the bonds of human unity (English once forged the unifying link of
  India); students should not be burdened with a heavy load of imposed languages.
- **A world language (vishva bhāṣā).** Communication across humanity needs a world
  language; on the three tests — widely spoken, easily understood, powerful expression —
  **English** alone qualifies today (but may be replaced in the future); adopting it
  "will in no way harm any mother tongue."

# In *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1980–1987) — the liberating curriculum

The DNE "Talks on Education" chapters and *Pramā – 4* add the curricular and method
core that the 1959 chapter left open — the goal, the three fundamentals, and the
worldwide kindergarten-first programme.

- **The liberating goal (dne-5, Apr 1981).** *"Sá vidyá yá vimuktaye"* — "Education is
  that which liberates." "The real meaning of education is trilateral development –
  simultaneous development in the physical, mental and spiritual realms of human
  existence." The E-D-U-C-A-T-I-O-N acronym: E – Enlargement of mind; D – DESMEP (D
  Discipline, E Etiquette, S Smartness, M Memory, E English, P Pronunciation); U –
  Universal Outlook; C – Character; A – Active habits; T – Trustworthiness; I –
  Ideation of the Great; O – Omniscient grace; N – Nice temperament. "The practice of
  morality should be the most important subject in the syllabus at all levels"; from
  the beginning children learn three languages — the mother tongue, basic Saṃskṛta (or
  the appropriate classical language), and the world language. Education "should be
  free at all levels" and "free from all political interference," controlled by "a
  board comprised of efficient teachers."
- **The three fundamentals (dne-34, Aug 1980).** "Education is just remoulding the old
  structure of the mind and goading it unto the highest state of realization, the
  exalted status of Supreme Veracity." The three fundamentals: (1) **factuality** —
  "There must not be the injection of any dogma or fanaticism or any type of
  geographical or racial chauvinism"; (2) **awakening the thirst for knowledge** — the
  Arabic *tālib-ul-ilm* means "a genuine seeker of knowledge"; (3) a **balanced mind**,
  "unaffected, unassailed by unimportant entities." Education is "a must… for all
  living beings," not only humans. Kindergarten is "the first phase of making human
  beings" — the bamboo metaphor: "A bamboo, when green, can be shaped or bent in any
  way you like. Once it ripens, any attempt to reshape it will break it." "The psychic
  environment is more powerful in human life than the physical environment."
- **The teachers' mission (dne-45).** "The noblest form of social service is to educate
  the public and create a sense of consciousness in them." "Education which leads to
  the acquisition of knowledge plus morality makes for a peaceful society." "You are
  to create a new social order."
- **Primary schools worldwide, not colleges (dne-17, Feb 1987).** "I am not in favour
  of starting colleges; I am in favour of starting primary schools" — "thousands of
  kindergarten and primary schools must be started with this new system of education,
  to create a spiritual urge amongst children throughout the entire world," with the
  practices of *Aṣṭāuṅga Yoga* incorporated "into the curriculum in all grades."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** "a thirst for knowledge must be awakened"; teachers are
  samāja gurus and must shape policy; nationalism/communalism in education is
  "highly detrimental"; English is the present world language (replaceable).
- **Mother-tongue tension preserved:** the chapter recommends English as the world
  language *alongside* the mother tongue, while [Cardinal Socio-Political
  Principles](/prout/cardinal-socio-political-principles.md) forbids suppressing any
  mother tongue — both are grounded; keep them as complementary.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** any full curriculum proposal; the chapter
  is on method and responsibility, not syllabus.
- **DNE now grounds the curriculum** (dne-5/34/17/45): *Sa vidya ya vimuktaye*,
  trilateral development, the E-D-U-C-A-T-I-O-N acronym, the three fundamentals
  (factuality, thirst for knowledge, balanced mind), morality as "the most important
  subject," the three-language programme, education free at all levels and free from
  political interference under a board of efficient teachers, and the primary-schools-
  worldwide (not colleges) programme with Aṣṭāuṅga Yoga. Keep the 1959 chapter's
  method emphasis distinct from these DNE curricular statements.
