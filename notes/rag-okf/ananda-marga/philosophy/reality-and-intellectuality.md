# Definition

**"So that which we call 'reality' is not reality"** — and intellectuality is
equally baseless, for "the very movement of the ectoplasm is baseless." Neither
reality nor intellectuality exists. What remains: **spirituality**, whose
movement "is never pulsative in character, but rather linear: it flows in a
straight line" — and "Spirituality alone is the absolute stance; and to search
for it is the real intellectuality." (17 May 1980, Varanasi; NH 1/12.)

# Explanation

- **Why reality fails — systalsis.** Perception is built on vibrations: "the
  tanmátras of sound, touch, form, taste and smell of an object reach the
  gateways of the sense organs as vibrations of different wavelengths. These
  vibrational expressions, these movements, are not linear, but are of systaltic
  order." In every phase "there is pulsation, there is systalsis." The mind with a
  **positive tendency** integrates the expressive phases into a whole object; with
  a **negative tendency** it integrates the pause phases and "we will say, even
  after seeing an elephant or a camel, that we did not see any elephant or camel
  at all."
- **Duality kills absoluteness.** "So that which we call 'reality' has two
  subdivisions – positive and negative. Duality always exists in reality. Because
  of the existence of duality, reality cannot be an absolute entity."
- **Sensory perception is defective.** Optical illusions (sand appearing as water
  at night), no recognized standard of reality, limited jurisdiction of each
  sense organ, objects only perceptible through instruments — therefore "what we
  call reality or crude fact, on which the whole concept of materialism stands,
  is not at all real." Materialism has no foothold: "Where there is duality, how
  can there be truth?"
- **Intellectuality is equally baseless.** Ectoplasmic thought-waves are also
  systaltic (crests and troughs, speed and pause). Positive/negative
  hallucinations afflict the inner arena of thought; "the mind's subjectivated
  compartment has no control over its objectivated mental chamber," so the mind
  may take the non-existent to exist and vice versa — "This kind of intellectuality
  is affected by ambiguity." Hence "intellectuality is also baseless, because the
  very movement of the ectoplasm is baseless." "If the sum is taken of all those
  psychic pauses together, then intellectuality amounts to nothing."
- **The conclusion and the linear escape.** "We can say that in this world
  neither intellectuality nor reality exists." The wise "should search for
  something beyond this pulsative order of contraction and expansion, speed and
  pause. And what is that something? It is nothing else but spirituality."
- **The linear movement and its two ends.** "The movement of spirituality is
  never pulsative in character, but rather linear: it flows in a straight line.
  Due to this straightness, it has no positive curvature or negative curvature.
  Yet for the sake of convenience we can call it the supreme positivity; and its
  farthest extremity, the point where resides all human existence, the
  starting-point of all intellectuals and non-intellectuals, can be termed the
  supreme negativity. So the movement of a living being starts from the supreme
  negativity, and as it gradually flows towards the supreme positivity, that is,
  towards spirituality, its line of movement becomes straight. This is the real
  path."
- **The absolute stance.** "At the end nothing exists – neither intellectuality
  nor reality – only spirituality pervades everything. No entity in the external
  world can claim the status of an absolute entity. Spirituality alone is the
  absolute stance; and to search for it is the real intellectuality. Whatever
  people do apart from this is nothing but the expression of their distorted
  propensities."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** keep the anti-materialism and anti-intellectualism sharp —
  Sarkar is not proposing humble scepticism but declaring both reality and
  intellectuality *baseless*. Do not soften into generic philosophical doubt.
- **"Supreme positivity" / "supreme negativity" differ from the
  [Psychological Complexes](/biopsychology/psychological-complexes.md) sense.** In
  NH 1/12 the supreme negativity is the *starting point* (crude existence) and
  the supreme positivity the *spiritual goal*; in the psychological-complexes
  chapter the ideological flow *begins* from the supreme positivity and returns
  to it. The same terms, reversed poles — keep both senses distinct.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** any fuller doctrine of "real
  intellectuality" as an independent faculty beyond the closing statement.
- **Source note:** the discourse text uses the diacritic form *tanmátras* and
  italicized "reality" in quotation; preserved here in Sarkar's exact wording.
