# Definition

**Neohumanism** is the approach in which — the mind purified of dogma and ego —
one feels the "charm of living" of, and renders service to, **the entire
universe**: not only human beings but all animate *and* inanimate entities,
because everything moves to the rhythm of [Parama Puruṣa](/cosmology/brahma.md).
Sarkar calls it **"the last word for attaining Him"** and **"the only way."**

# Explanation

- Neohumanism extends help and feeling **beyond humanity** — "the entire world,
  not only the living world but both the animate and inanimate worlds" (gold,
  iron, water and everything) — because everything is "dancing to the rhythm of
  Parama Puruṣa" (the [Brahmacakra](/cosmology/brahmacakra.md)).
- It is **bound to purity of mind**: a "dogma-amalgamated" approach is
  *non-Neohumanistic*, so that even one who does sādhanā twenty hours a day is on
  a defective path "if it is not the path of Neohumanism."
- It maps onto the four phases of merging the mind into the Supreme: the approach
  "must be Neohumanistic" especially from the second phase, when one feels the
  pains and joys of all beings.
- It is the **prerequisite of final emancipation**: one must accept it at least
  "on the eve of one's final merger"; accepting it only then, however, deprives
  the world of one's service — a collective loss — so it should be accepted "from
  the very first step."
- It resists **narrow geo-sentiment** and aims at the "permanent liberation of
  human beings, animals and plants," applied dynamically as the age changes.
- It is the **gateway of spiritual love into the objective world**: it is aroused
  by sādhanā and [kiirtana](/practice/kiirtana.md), "when our minds develop love
  for the Supreme Consciousness." "The solution to all problems lies in
  Neohumanism."

# In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) — the Cosmic Brotherhood

The 1959 chapter *The Cosmic Brotherhood* (II-11) — the earliest text in the bundle
on world fraternity — states the universalist impulse behind Neohumanism:

- **Spirituality is practical, not utopian.** "Spirituality is not a utopian ideal
  but a practical philosophy which can be practised and realized in day-to-day
  life, however mundane it be." It "stands for evolution and elevation, and not for
  superstition in action or pessimism."
- **Against fissiparous tendencies.** "All fissiparous tendencies and group or clan
  philosophies which tend to create the shackles of narrow-mindedness are in no way
  connected with spirituality and should be discouraged. That which leads to
  broadness of unison alone should be accepted. Spiritual philosophy does not
  recognize any distinctions and differentiations unnaturally made between one human
  being and another, and stands for universal fraternity."
- **The Cosmic ideal.** Humanity's ultimate and absolute ideal is the **Cosmic
  ideal** — "beyond the scope of time, place and person… the Absolute, without and
  beyond relativity," the unifying force that "shall strengthen humanity to smash
  the bondages and abolish all narrow domestic walls of fissiparous tendencies."
- **Cosmic sentiment, not uniformity.** Sentiment-provoking ideas are to be firmly
  opposed, but innate sentiments and habits that do not hamper Cosmic development
  are preserved — e.g. the movement for uniform dress is "ridiculous and
  irrational"; zonal and regional differences in traditions and customs "should be
  appreciated and encouraged." "Under no circumstances should there be a compromise
  in principle or yielding to tendencies detrimental to the inculcation of Cosmic
  sentiment."
- **Its objective programme** (the "fundamental problems" to be solved on a
  collective humanitarian basis): a **common philosophy of life**; a **common
  constitutional structure** (a world government with one world militia and
  autonomous, non-national units); a **common penal code** (with progressive
  legislation; the sense of crime parallels the local concept of virtue and vice);
  and the **availability of the minimum essentialities of life** (cf.
  [Minimum Requirements](/prout/minimum-requirements.md)). The social philosophy of
  Ananda Marga thus advocates "the development of the integrated personality of the
  individual, and also the establishment of world fraternity, inculcating in human
  psychology a Cosmic sentiment."
- **Nationalism is out of date.** "Nationalism is fast getting out of date" — the
  world wars and the social-cultural blending of the age show "the domination of
  cosmopolitanism in world affairs"; vested interests fearing loss of domination
  cause the retrograde fissiparous reactions.

# In *The Liberation of Intellect: Neohumanism* (1982) — the sentiments and the conscience

The 1982 discourse series — the standalone book named for the doctrine — develops
Neohumanism's practical edge: against **geo-sentiment** and **pseudo-humanism**, and
through the **awakened conscience**.

- **Rationality vs sentiment.** Human existence "is more psychic than physical"
  (*manu* = "mind-predominant being"; *mānava* = *manu + śna*). The mind works by
  **discrimination/rationality** (*viveka*, choosing the proper path — slow but safe),
  **sentiment** (running blindly after a liking "like an unbridled horse" — "a hundred
  percent probability of great danger"), and **instinct** (undeveloped creatures only).
  Human beings must follow the path of rationality; those who do "no harm to society."
- **Geo-sentiment is the greatest threat to humanity's inner asset.** "It is to let
  one's sentiment flow towards a particular territory," substituting sentiment for
  logic and then superstition for logic. Its forms: **geo-economic sentiment**
  ("we must have a steel plant in our area" without iron ore; the Dundee-jute
  non-utilization of Bengal's resources), **geo-religious sentiment** (dying only in
  Kashi; "I am the slave of Vrindavana"), **geo-socialism/geo-politics**. "Dharma is
  the ultimate goal of the path of conscience — it is not related to geo-sentiment."
  The geo's "alienate one particular social group from another… and different groups
  become violent towards each other."
- **Humanism is socio-sentiment maximitis — and pseudo-humanism is its strategy.**
  Ordinary/general humanism "is nothing more than an enlarged form of nationalism";
  it fails because of **intra-humanistic clash** (feeding the starving to make them
  suppliers/purchasers; the "literacy drive" that paralyses minds to govern colonized
  people) and because humans are not the only living beings — destroying the plant/
  animal world upsets the balance and ruins human life. **Pseudo-humanistic strategy**:
  "outwardly we preach the gospels of humanistic idealism, but in actuality we stab
  others in the back" (cf. [Pseudo-Humanism](/philosophy/pseudo-humanism.md)). Every
  creature has **utility value and existential value** — "no one has the moral right"
  to say only humans may live.
- **The awakened conscience (viveka).** To fight the sentiments: (1) **study** —
  intensive intellectual analysis (literal and non-literal), despite its two defects —
  *defect due to ignorance* and *defect due to change in time*; (2) **rationalistic
  mentality** — verify everything, come to a logical decision (*siddhānta*); (3) the
  final decision for or against an idea rests on whether it conduces "to the benefit
  and happiness of all beings, the spiritual well-being of all" — this final
  desiderative point of discrimination is **viveka** (conscience). "Study is the first
  step, and rationalistic mentality is one step higher… towards the establishment of
  Neohumanism."

# In *A Few Problems Solved* (1983) — the status of inanimation

*The Status of Inanimation in the Philosophy of Neohumanism* (AFPS 5/14, 1983)
carries the scope down to sub-atomic matter:

- **"Neohumanism is newly-explained humanism."** Humanism/humanity have for a century
  covered only human beings; "this explanation is not sufficient… Why should the love
  and affection of developed human minds be restricted to human beings only? Why should
  it not include all living beings, including plant life?"
- **Inanimation is included.** "Neohumanism includes within its scope not only human
  beings and animate creatures… but all inanimate entities as well, for the scope of
  Neohumanism extends down to the smallest particles of sub-atomic matter."
- **Extensive AND intensive.** Movement must be not only *extensive* (the whole animate
  world) but *intensive* — "a never-ending movement from the imperfect world of
  humanism to the perfect world of the unit assembling body, to the original primordial
  phase of perfection." Even inanimate atoms' particles "maintain their structural
  unity and struggle against their internal and external fissiparous tendencies"; the
  "golden day" comes when "human intuition will realize that the essence in the
  sub-atomic world is pure Consciousness."

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1979) — Universal Fraternity

*Universal Fraternity* (NH 2/7, Athens 1979) grounds the fraternity in the common
origin:

- **Everything comes from Parama Puruṣa.** "Nothing comes from nothing, everything
  comes from something"; human society, the animal world, the plant world "come from a
  particular source, and that source is Parama Puruṣa, the Supreme Progenitor, the
  great Father." "Ya āgacchati sah gacchati" — whoever has come will go; movement is
  the order of the universe. "He is the starting point… and the culminating point of
  all creatures."
- **"There lies an inherent tie of fraternity."** Because all beings share the same
  origin and the same goal, "there lies an inherent tie of fraternity among human
  beings, among the living creatures." "You are all spiritual aspirants… all going to be
  one with the Creator. You should remember this inherent tie of humanity… and, if you
  move along the path of righteousness with the spirit of Universal Fraternity, victory
  is yours."

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1978–1984) — dogma, pseudo-spirituality and the one-indivisible society

Four further NHN chapters ground the doctrine: dogma defined and dissolved through the
**agryābuddhi** (nh-1-11); **Neohumanism as the cult, perfect spirituality as the goal**
(nh-1-13); the new era and the singular humanity (nh-1-16); and the one-indivisible
society against caste, religion, nationalism and language-division (nh-2-6).

- **Dogma = imposition without logic; genuine dharma = logic + intellectuality.**
  (nh-1-11, *What Is Dogma*, 16 May 1980, Varanasi.) "You all know that the most
  detrimental thing for human society and human progress is dogma. What is dogma? Where
  there is no logic, where there is no support of intellectuality, where there is no
  debate and free discussion, but there is only a severe imposition forcing people to
  accept something, there is dogma." Its preachers are "the blind followers of
  religion," whereas "genuine dharma is completely based on logic and supported by
  intellectuality," accepted because "people are convinced by logic; and people analyse
  and accept it after free and frank discussion."
- **The agryābuddhi (pinnacled intellect) resolves the karma-requital vs Kṛṣṇa's-shelter
  tension.** (nh-1-11.) The tension between *"Nābhuktam kṣīyate karma
  kalpakotiśataerapi; Avashyameva bhoktavyam kṛtam karma śubhāśubham"* (requital must be
  experienced) and Kṛṣṇa's promise of shelter for the *durācārī* is dissolved in the
  mind: *"Mana eva manuṣyāṇāṃ kāraṇam bandhamokṣayoḥ"* — "It is the human mind which is
  the cause of bondage or liberation." If "the mind is kept engrossed in the ideation of
  Parama Puruṣa," the mind "becomes pointed. This is called *agryābuddhi* [pinnacled
  intellect] in Sanskrit." "The moment the mind is centred on Parama Puruṣa, then that
  mind will naturally become pointed," and "there is no scope for the rise and fall of
  any wave – neither the wave of virtue nor the wave of vice." Thus Kṛṣṇa's statement
  "is neither a philosophical assertion nor any dogma" but simple truth: "human beings
  should not be anxious under any circumstances, Parama Puruṣa is always with everyone."
- **Perfect spirituality is the goal, Neohumanism is the cult.** (nh-1-13,
  *Pseudo-Spirituality and Neohumanism*, 31 Dec 1982, Anandanagar.) Pseudo-spirituality
  is "spirituality that is not in perfect order"; "wherever there is pseudo-spirituality,
  there is the seed of dogma," and "the seed of welfare cannot exist where the seed of
  dogma exists." In all four strata of life (physical, physico-psychic, psychic,
  psycho-spiritual) pseudo-spirituality fails; "what we really want is perfect
  spirituality, not pseudo-spirituality." "So that perfect spirituality cannot be the
  cult – it is the goal, it is the desideratum, it is the culminating point of all
  movements.… And what is the cult? The cult is the movement of Neohumanism. That is,
  the cult is the movement of all human expressions, all human manifestations – and not
  only human expressions and manifestations, but all expressions, all manifestations, of
  all living beings, including flora and fauna. This is the cult, this is Neohumanism."
  "Our approach, our cult, is Neohumanistic, and our goal is perfect spirituality."
- **The threshold of a new era.** (nh-1-16, *Humanity Is at the Threshold of a New Era*,
  26 May 1984, Ranchi.) "We do not want any dogma. The age of dogma is gone. What we want
  is an idea based on Neohumanism. We are for the entire created world; and not only for
  human beings or living beings, but for the entire animate and inanimate universe."
  "Is humanity the summum bonum of existence? No, no, no, certainly not. The universe
  does not consist only of humans; other animals, other creatures, other plants, also
  have the right to live." "We want no more gospels. We want something practical,
  something practical for the elevation, or for the exaltation, of the entire human
  race." The closing refrain: "All humanity is a singular entity, it is one and
  indivisible.… humanity is a singular entity, humanity is one and indivisible. And for
  this purpose we should always maintain an equilibrium amongst different humans, and
  there must be one equipoise for the development of all, irrespective of caste, creed,
  nationality and clan isms."
- **Human society is one and indivisible.** (nh-2-6, *Human Society Is One and
  Indivisible – 3*, 17 Jun 1979, Calcutta.) "I have been saying for a long time that
  human society is a singular entity, that human society is indivisible." None of the
  alleged grounds of division withstand scrutiny: caste, religion, race, nationality,
  language, wealth, education. **Caste** — the puruṣa-sūkta (*Rg-Veda* X.90:
  "Brāhmaṇosya mukhamaāsīt vāhurājanyo'bhavat; Madhyatadasya yadvaeshyah padbhyāṃśūdrah")
  is inverted: the śūdras are "born out of the holy feet of Parama Puruṣa [and] certainly
  deserve to be revered by all. Hence, the system of casteism is totally baseless. The
  so-called scriptures which are based on casteism are equally baseless." **Religion** —
  "Perhaps religion has done the most damage to humanity"; "Majhabme akkalkā dakhl nehi
  hae" — "In religion there is no room for logical argument." Scripture is defined by
  function: *"Śāsanāt tārayet yastu sah śāstra parikīrtitah"* — "That which disciplines
  human beings, that which inspires human beings to follow the path of spirituality, the
  path of supreme benevolence, that is scripture." **Nationality** — the Jessore/Bongaon
  Partition example (overnight, "they were Pakistanis" / "citizens of India");
  "the question of nationality is completely meaningless and something devoid of rational
  judgement"; via Tagore, the exploited masses are "just like the lamp stand" while
  "those at the helm of society are like the lamp" — "Everything is illuminated by the
  lamp except the lamp stand." **Language** — "all languages are the languages of Parama
  Puruṣa"; "no language should ever be suppressed," and "it is absurd to divide humanity
  on the basis of language."

# In the prose discourses: Dogma and its dissolution

Across a cluster of late discourses Sarkar makes the critique of **dogma** the
practical edge of Neohumanism. In *What Is Dogma* (AV 19) he defines it as the
condition "where there is no logic… no debate and free discussion, but there is only
a severe imposition forcing people to accept something" — "the most detrimental
thing for human society and human progress." Genuine *dharma*, by contrast, "is
completely based on logic and supported by intellectuality," accepted only "after
free and frank discussion"; so the spiritual path is rational, never a dogmatic
imposition.

In *Dogma — No More* (AV 14) an idea becomes dogma the moment it is "surrounded by
boundary lines" beyond which the mind may not pass: thus "dogma seals the future of
the human intellect," and the aspirant enlarges the mind only "by a constant fight
against… all psychic dogmas." *Beware of Dogma* (AV 30) calls dogma "a psychic
structure" whose fault is "rigidity of the boundary line" — those controlled by it
lose their stir and "become lifeless… worse than dead bodies" (communism/Marxism is
named as one such dogma, "It will not allow you to think beyond its periphery").
*Revolt against Dogma* (AV 34) extends the critique to ritual itself: fixed gestures
and postures are "the abode of dogma," "not spirituality," and even the devotional
faculty can carry "colours of dogma" — the first historical revolt against dogma
being the Bhāgavata insight that "no physical show is required for the devotional
approach."

# In *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1982–1987) — the universal formula

The *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* series (DNE) supplies the doctrine's compact
universalist formula verbatim — "elevation of humanism to universalism," grounded in
devotion — plus the two-fold educational/spiritual remedy.

- **The devotional base.** "The devotional sentiment is the highest and most valuable
  treasure of humanity" — a "tender inner asset" to be preserved "from the onslaughts
  of materialism" behind a protective fence, i.e. "a proper philosophy which will
  establish the correct harmony between the spiritual and material worlds."
- **The Neohumanism definition — now verbatim.** "When this devotional cult does not
  remain confined to a mere practice, but instead is elevated to a devotional
  sentiment, a devotional mission, to the realm of devotional ideation – when the
  underlying spirit of humanism is extended to everything, animate and inanimate, in
  this universe – I have designated this as 'Neohumanism'. This Neohumanism will
  elevate humanism to universalism, the cult of love for all created beings of this
  universe."
- **Diversity, not identity.** *"Vaecitryaḿ prákrtadharmah samánaḿ na bhaviśyati"*
  ("Diversity, not identity, is the law of nature"). Theories that spoke "glibly of
  human equality" but ignored this diversity failed; "dynamism is indeed the first and
  last word of human existence."
- **The three narrow sentiments.** Geo-sentiment ("the sentiment that grows out of love
  for the indigenous soil of a country") and the "more expanded" socio-sentiment
  (well-being of one group "even to the exclusion of all other groups") are narrowing;
  even **human sentiment** violates non-human creatures — "living creatures are the
  food for other living beings (*jiivah jiivasya bhojanam*)" — so extending the same
  sentiment to all creatures alone is the "final consummation" of human existence.
- **The Viṣṇupurāṇa ground.** *"Vistárah sarvabhútasya Viśńorvishvamidaḿ jagat;
  Draśt́avyamátmavattasmádabhedena vicakśańaeh"* — the manifested universe is
  Viṣṇu's expression; "All molecules, atoms, electrons, protons, positrons and
  neutrons are the veritable expressions of the same Supreme Consciousness."
- **The two-fold approach (dne-25).** Human psychic exploitation "is even more
  dangerous than on the physical one"; the remedy is "a two-fold approach" — "We will
  have to educate the mind properly, along the lines of Neohumanism… and at the same
  time there should be spiritual practice for proper psychic transformation. This is
  what we require most. There is no alternative."
- **The controlling philosophy (dne-17).** "The guiding philosophy, the controlling
  philosophy should be: 'This universe is ours' – and 'we' means humans, animals and
  plants."

# In *Varna Vijinana* (1983) — Neohumanist linguistics

In the 1983 philology series Sarkar draws the egalitarian, anti-dogmatic corollaries
of the doctrine into linguistics itself:

- **Philology serves Neohumanism.** (vv-5) "The study of philology and phonetics
  will do a lot to close the distances and eliminate inequalities between people,
  and it will also help to establish Neohumanism."
- **No dialect may be despised.** (vv-7) "under no circumstances do we have the
  right to turn up our nose at a dialect, considering it to be unrefined or
  deprived of a glorious lineage. Even if its lineage has no glory or has
  disappeared, from the existential point of view it is worthy enough." Bhojpuri,
  Angika, Maethilii, Magahii and Chatrisgarii "deserve recognition and should be
  recognized" as full-fledged languages (they possess the eight conditions of a
  language). Kabir's maxim is cited in this same spirit: "Saḿskrta kúpodaka
  bhákhá bahatá niir" — the Sanskrit language is like stagnant well-water while
  the people's language is "like the flowing water of a river, that is, full of
  vitality."
- **Free speech is the first idea of humanism.** (vv-8) "Therefore the first idea
  of humanism is that people must be allowed to speak freely. Unfortunately
  nowadays those anti-Neohumanistic theories which give indulgence to dogma are
  flourishing and thereby depriving people of their fundamental right of free
  speech. They are not fulfilling the first condition of human expression."
- **"Hindu" is a geographical, inclusive word.** (vv-19) "Hindu is a Farsi word
  whose fundamental meaning is 'Indus River-basin' or the community of people
  living in the nearby areas. All of them, regardless of what religion they
  followed, were Hindus. In other words, the word hindu is a completely
  geographical word." In the religious sense those called Hindu today are followers
  of the Árśa doctrine — while followers of "the various other so-called sects in
  India are Hindus in the geographical sense but not in the religious sense."

- *NHN* (nh-1-11): dogma as "severe imposition" without "logic," "support of intellectuality" or "debate and free discussion" — the opposite of genuine dharma; the *agryābuddhi* (pinnacled intellect) resolves the karma-requital/Kṛṣṇa's-shelter tension via the mind (*Mana eva manuṣyāṇāṃ kāraṇam bandhamokṣayoḥ*)
- *NHN* (nh-1-13): "perfect spirituality cannot be the cult – it is the goal"; "the cult is the movement of Neohumanism… of all living beings, including flora and fauna"; "wherever there is pseudo-spirituality, there is the seed of dogma"
- *NHN* (nh-1-16): "The age of dogma is gone"; "Is humanity the summum bonum of existence? No, no, no, certainly not"; "All humanity is a singular entity, it is one and indivisible"
- *NHN* (nh-2-6): the one-indivisible society against caste (puruṣa-sūkta reinterpreted), religion ("Perhaps religion has done the most damage to humanity"; "Majhabme akkalkā dakhl nehi hae"), nationality (Jessore/Bongaon; Tagore's lamp/lamp-stand) and language-division ("all languages are the languages of Parama Puruṣa"); scripture defined as "Śāsanāt tārayet yastu sah śāstra parikīrtitah"

# Notes — fidelity

- Sarkar's Neohumanism is an explicitly **spiritual, devotional** path — extension
  to *all* beings on a cosmological basis and freedom from dogma — **not** secular
  or Western humanism. Do not normalise toward generic humanism (anti-syncretism).
- **Dogma-critique (prose) is direct teaching:** dogma = "no logic… only a severe
  imposition," "a psychic structure" with a rigid boundary line that "seals the
  future of the human intellect"; even ritual gesture/posture and the "so-called
  devotional" faculty can carry "colours of dogma" (AV 19, 14, 30, 34). Keep it sharp.
- **Cosmic Brotherhood (II-11, 1959)** is the earliest universalist text: it uses
  "Cosmic ideal / Cosmic sentiment / world fraternity" rather than the later term
  "Neohumanism" — do not retro-write "Neohumanism" into the 1959 chapter; treat it
  as the seed of the later, named doctrine.
- **VERIFIED (dne-24, 21 Feb 1982):** the compact formula is now verbatim — "This
  Neohumanism will elevate humanism to universalism, the cult of love for all created
  beings of this universe." The earlier "could not verify" caveat on the one-line
  slogan is resolved; *socio-sentiment*, *pseudo-humanism*, and *geo-sentiment* are
  grounded in both LOIN (1982) and the DNE series (dne-23, 24). DNE 25 gives the
  two-fold remedy (Neohumanist education + spiritual practice, "There is no
  alternative"); DNE 17 gives the controlling philosophy "This universe is ours"
  (humans, animals and plants).
- **The 1982 series is the doctrine's own book** — geo-sentiment as "the greatest
  threat to humanity's inner asset" and the awakened-conscience method are its core;
  keep the 1959 seed separate from the 1982 named doctrine.
- **VV (1983) extends the doctrine into linguistics:** dialect-respect and
  language-recognition are framed as Neohumanist duties; free speech is called "the
  first idea of humanism" in explicit opposition to "anti-Neohumanistic theories
  which give indulgence to dogma"; the etymological claim that "hindu" is "a
  completely geographical word" is direct teaching — keep it in Sarkar's own
  framing (inclusive geography, not sectarian religion).
- **AFPS 5/14 (1983)** extends the scope to inanimation: "the scope of Neohumanism
  extends down to the smallest particles of sub-atomic matter"; the movement is
  extensive AND intensive, ending in the realization that "the essence in the
  sub-atomic world is pure Consciousness." Direct teaching — keep the inanimate
  inclusion (this grounds the "animate and inanimate" of SS 16/1 more fully).
