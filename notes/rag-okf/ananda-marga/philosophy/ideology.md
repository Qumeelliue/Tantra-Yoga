# Definition

In *Idea and Ideology* (1959) Sarkar redefines two everyday words from their
psycho-spiritual root: **"idea"** (*bhāva*) is the **psycho-spiritual
parallelism** — the state in which the unit mind's waves have attained a
parallelism with the spiritual waves of the [ātman](/cosmology/atman.md);
**"ideology"** is *the conception of idea and nothing else*. Hence the term
"ideology" carries a **spiritual sense** — it is an *inspiration which has a
parallelism with the Spiritual Entity* — so calling a materialistic or political
programme an "ideology" is, in Sarkar's words, **a wrong use of the term**.

# Explanation

- **Mechanism of the parallelisms.** When the mind takes the ātman as its object
  — attainable only by **complete surrender**, since actually the mind is the
  object of the ātman — its mental waves straighten gradually until, with infinite
  wavelength, they flow in a straight line and the mind is transformed into the
  ātman: this state is [samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md). The parallelism so
  attained is **psycho-spiritual parallelism** = *bhāva* = **idea**. When this idea
  is *conceived on the psychic level*, it is **ideology**.
- **Artha (meaning) is the psycho-physical parallelism.** A word is initially a
  psychic wave (*shabda*); its *artha* is fixed only when the mental waves are
  brought into contact with the physical concept — e.g. the child associates the
  word "cat" with the seen cat, "establishing a parallelism between the mental
  waves created by uttering the word and the physical wave coming from the form."
  So: *psycho-spiritual parallelism is idea, psycho-physical parallelism is
  artha* (meaning). The latter may be indirect — built through an internal
  saṃskāra or another's description when the object is not directly perceived.
- **Psycho-physical parallelism tends to crudify** the mental waves (the path of
  negative [pratisaiñcara](/cosmology/pratisaincara.md)); the remedy is to keep the
  spiritual wave supreme and perceive the physical as "one of the manifestations of
  the spiritual infinite one."
- **Brahmacarya redefined.** *Brahmacarya does not mean celibacy* but "the
  assimilation of the psychic wave of the unit mind into the eternal waves of the
  Macrocosm." Ananda Marga supplies a practical method — **bhāva sādhanā**
  (spiritual practice of auto-suggestion) at the time of action — by which the mind
  tends toward Brahmacarya and toward psycho-spiritual parallelism, "the only way to
  ultimate liberation."
- **Integrated personality.** Complete development of the integrated personality is
  essential for perfect realization; personality comprises the physical body
  (*jadādhāra*), the mental body (*bhāvādhāra*) and the spiritual / supramental
  body (*cetanādhāra*). Each needs its own nourishment: physical labour and proper
  food purify the physical; **mental struggle** (adopting newer, progressive
  thoughts rather than blind faith in the ancients) and mental food — ideas
  (psycho-spiritual parallelism) — build the mental; longing for the Great, "an
  intense desire to be for Him," the *real bhakti*, strengthens the spiritual.
- The chapter closes on [bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md) as the best way to mokṣa,
  citing Śaṅkarācārya: *"Mokṣakāraṇa samagryāṃ bhaktireva gariyasiī"* — "of all
  means of liberation, devotion alone is the best."

## Relationships

- defines the book's title: "ideology" = conception of *bhāva* — the psycho-spiritual parallelism that is the goal of [Sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md)
- the unity achieved is [Samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md) / [Yoga](/practice/yoga.md) (straightened waves, merger with the [Ātman](/cosmology/atman.md))
- *artha* grounds [Tanmātra](/biopsychology/tanmatra.md) perception and the working of the [Indriyas](/biopsychology/indriya.md)
- Brahmacarya here = assimilation into the Macrocosmic waves — the [Yama](/practice/yama-niyama.md) read psychically, not as celibacy
- the real devotion = longing for the Great — cf. [Bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md), [Sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md)

# Notes — fidelity

- **"Ideology" here is a spiritual term.** Sarkar explicitly calls the political
  usage "a wrong use of the term." Do not soften this redefinition into ordinary
  political ideology.
- **Bhāva has two senses across the bundle** — here "psycho-spiritual parallelism
  (idea)" in *Idea and Ideology*; elsewhere the graded devotional ecstasy
  (dashā → bhāva → mahābhāva) in [Bhāva & Mahābhāva](/practice/bhava.md). Keep
  them distinct; do not conflate.
- **Brahmacarya-not-celibacy** corroborates [Yama & Niyama](/practice/yama-niyama.md)
  and *Elementary Philosophy* — direct teaching, keep it sharp.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** any numbering of meditation "lessons"
  behind "bhāva sādhanā" (the auto-suggestion practice is named but not enumerated).
