# Definition

**Women and men are the progeny of the same Supreme Consciousness** — "equally divine
and [inherit] similar and equal rights to life, liberty and expression." Life means
"living for a great cause… the freedom to express one's potentialities in the
physical, economic, psychic and spiritual realms — real liberty and not license."
(1981, PiaN 13/9.)

# Explanation

- **Equal potentiality, coordinated cooperation.** Women stand second to none in
  philosophy, spirituality, social reform, education, science and technology. "The
  difference in natural and biological characteristics between men and women speaks
  only of coordinated cooperation, not of subordinated cooperation" (cf.
  [Cooperatives](/prout/cooperatives.md)).
- **Women's exploitation is psycho-economic.** To fulfil the sinister design of
  exploitation, "dogmas were created which led to psycho-economic exploitation…
  women were degenerated to slavery." Psychic exploitation was infused into women's
  minds; "many symbols which are religiously observed are nothing but symbols of
  slavery" (e.g. women barred from the priesthood in many religions; franchise rights
  not granted equally). "Women's exploitation is more or less the same everywhere."
- **The programme:**
  1. **free education for all women** in all countries of the world;
  2. **no discrimination** in the social, educational and religious realms;
  3. the provision of **economic and social security** to all women.
- **The call.** "Let women be the vanguard of a new revolution which humanity must
  achieve for a glorious tomorrow" — a powerful, dynamic, upsurging social
  consciousness among women to abolish dogma and annihilate all symbols of slavery,
  ushering in "a new era of coordinated cooperation and glorious achievement."

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* — Social Justice (DNE 21)

*Social Justice* (DNE 21) is Sarkar's most extended discourse on women's status;
it treats the weakness-difference without derogating women's value:

- **Physical and mental weakness, equal value.** "As in the societies of most
  other species, in human society also females are physically weaker than males.
  Because their nervous systems are weaker, their minds are also slightly weak.
  Nevertheless women have no less value in human society than men. Selfish men,
  however, have disregarded the value of women ... Although men have publicly
  declared that women should be respected as the mothers of society, they have
  actually relegated them to the status of domestic cattle and sheep."
- **Purity impossible while injustice reigns.** "In individual life purity is
  equally necessary for both men and women, and to make this possible real
  spiritual vision is essential. As long as injustices against either sex exist,
  purity is impossible."
- **Women "trustingly placed their destiny in the hands of men."** "women have
  not really lost their freedom; rather, they have trustingly placed their
  destiny in the hands of men. This is the plain truth." Hence "whatever
  responsibility there may be in this regard, it is entirely that of men. If at
  all a movement is required, it must be implemented by men themselves. Today men
  should consider the needs of women and gradually restore to women the rights
  which women once entrusted to men."
- **Recognition, not granting.** "As children of nature, women should have the
  same access as men to light, air, earth and water. In fact, it is not a matter
  of granting rights to women, but rather a case of recognizing their rights."
- **Inheritance.** The patrilineal system "has some advantage over the
  matrilineal one" (the father's responsibility for his children; the mother is
  not left alone), but "after recognizing the equal rights of women to use family
  property as long as they live and to enjoy a lifelong income from such
  property, the law of inheritance should be based on the patrilineal system" —
  "nothing is framed in the name of the patrilineal system that will make women
  slaves fighting for their very survival in the houses of their brothers or
  brothers-in-law."
- **Dowry is "primarily economic."** "The problem is primarily economic, although
  there are secondary causes as well" — dowry flows to whichever family must
  support the wife; "it is completely unrealistic to expect that the parents of
  the groom will relinquish their claim to a dowry."
- **Purdah/burka are "entirely farcical."** "their efforts to protect their
  daughters and themselves by following the custom of purdah and making women
  wear burkas, are entirely farcical."
- **Freedom, not license.** "freedom, or liberty, and license are not the same
  thing. The concept of women's freedom is good, but license should not be
  encouraged in the name of liberty" — echoing the PiaN qualifier.

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** women and men are "equally divine" with equal rights; women's
  exploitation is psycho-economic and dogma-driven; "symbols which are religiously
  observed are nothing but symbols of slavery." Keep it sharp.
- **DNE 21** (Social Justice) adds the equality-in-value + weakness-difference
  framing, the "trustingly placed their destiny in the hands of men" diagnosis,
  the recognition-not-granting principle, and the inheritance/dowry/purdah
  positions. The chapter carries no printed date; the year "1959" is taken from
  the task attribution and could not be verified from the source file itself.
- **"Real liberty, not license"** — Sarkar's qualifier on freedom; preserve it.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** any detailed affirmative-action scheme
  beyond the three-point programme.
