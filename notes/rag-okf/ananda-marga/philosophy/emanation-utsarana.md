# Definition

*"If something is produced from something and gradually moves forward towards
greater manifestation it is called utsárańa."* — Varna Vijinana 20. Its corrupt or
arrested counterpart is *vivartana* (distortion): *"we call this natural change
'emanation' instead of 'distortion' because distortion refers to a special form of
change or a corrupted form of change. In Sanskrit grammar it is called
vivarttana."* — Varna Vijinana 21.

# Explanation

**From unmanifest to manifest (vv-20).** The seed holds the potentiality: "We know
that the sprout comes from the seed. The potential for vastness or extensiveness
lies in a dormant condition within the seed." The movement out is named twice:
"This sprouting – that is, this movement from the unmanifest to the manifest state
– is vyaktiikarańa [manifestation] or utsárańa."

**Systaltic, contractive-expansive flow (vv-20).** Emanation is not linear:
"This manifestation or emanation is vibrational, and this vibration moves in a
contractive-expansive flow, a systaltic order which moves in crests and troughs.
For example, the flow of river-water is not straight and continuous; it moves in
waves of crest and trough."

**The universal scope (vv-20).** The law applies "equally to language" — which
"changes in the process of maintaining adjustment with the changes in time, place
and person, and this change is irresistible. It cannot be held in check" — and to
the human body (the tail and the appendix receded as their need disappeared:
"Since there is no need for a tail now, there is no tail"), to dress ("human dress
and clothing also shows the clear influence of emanation"), to custom and cuisine,
and to script ("Emanation also affects script and letters").

**The law of providence as system, not entity (vv-21).** Change is universal and
institutionalised: "Everything in this cosmos is moving. Nothing stops, so
whatever moves also has to undergo change. This is the law of providence
[vidhátá]. The name of the system by which the Supreme Consciousness carries out
the various activities of the universe is known as vidhátá; that is, vidhátá is
not a specific entity but rather a specific system. So vidhátá is required to
follow its own rules of creation. It does not have the right to do anything in a
haphazard way."

**Distortion (vivartana) (vv-21).** Natural change is emanation; the arrested or
corrupted form is distortion: "we call this natural change 'emanation' instead of
'distortion' because distortion refers to a special form of change or a corrupted
form of change. In Sanskrit grammar it is called vivarttana." Sarkar gives three
causes: "Linguistic vivarttana or distortion happens primarily due to three
reasons. First, it occurs when a certain number of people cannot pronounce a word
correctly" — after which "the impetus of the word after it gets corrupted is
checked. It does not undergo further distortion." The following sections treat
distortion "through abbreviation" ("Often distortion of a word takes place through
abbreviation") and the fabrication of pseudo-etymologies and ghost-words — words
with no Saṃskṛta ground such as *stimita*, *anushiilana* and *rúpakathá* ("There
is no such word as rúpakathá in Sanskrit… It is standing on quicksand"). Only
where the distortion is natural does it resume the path of emanation (prastara →
patthara → páthar).

**Rhythm as the existential warrant (vv-16).** Human life itself is systaltic:
"Human beings try to find rhythm in everything because human life itself is
rhythmic. Movement is the natural characteristic of life so rhythm must exist
within human movement… So in this universe all movement flows in the waves of
systaltic order."

## Relationships

- utsāraṇa is Varna Vijinana's name for the natural expansive drift — related to, but not identical with, [Saiñcara](/cosmology/saincara.md) (the Cosmic Cycle's guṇa-driven extroversion)
- the seed → sprout → flower sequence is a [Parināma](/cosmology/parinama.md) (evolutionary transformation)
- the systaltic crest-and-trough movement is the movement of the [Existential Flow](/philosophy/existential-flow.md)
- the spontaneous creativity of emanation aligns with [Līlā](/cosmology/lila.md)
- change is always relative to time, place and person — cf. [Relativity](/cosmology/relativity.md)
- its social face is progressive, planned reform, not random drift — cf. [Social Change](/prout/social-change.md)
- applied to language and script it governs the life of words — cf. [Acoustic Roots](/cosmology/acoustic-roots.md)
- era-based change (language ~1000 years, script ~2000 years) links to [Yuga](/cosmology/yuga.md); the transform-as-discipline link is [Yoga](/practice/yoga.md)

# Notes — fidelity

- **Keep utsāraṇa distinct from sāiñcara.** VV's utsāraṇa is the specific term for
  natural expansive change, paired with its corrupt counterpart vivartana;
  sāiñcara is the Cosmic Cycle's extroversional phase. They are related, not
  synonyms.
- **vidhāta as system, not personified deity.** Sarkar is explicit: "vidhátá is
  not a specific entity but rather a specific system." Do not normalise the "law of
  providence" into a personal god-of-order.
- **The "three reasons" for distortion.** The printed text names the first
  explicitly ("First, it occurs when a certain number of people cannot pronounce a
  word correctly") and then presents abbreviation and ghost-word / pseudo-etymology
  fabrication as the further causes; it does not print the words "Second" and
  "Third". The three-cause framing follows the discourse flow.
- **"vivartana" vs "vivarttana".** vv-21 prints "vivarttana" (double *tt*) and
  "utsárańa"; the title uses the standard IAST forms utsāraṇa / vivartana.
- **Translation choices preserved** ("vyaktiikarańa" = manifestation; "vidhátá" =
  the law of providence) are the electronic edition's renderings.
