# Definition

*"All the languages of the world are bound together on a single thread – sútre
mańigańá iva. No language is beyond this law."* — Varna Vijinana 1. From this
underlying unity Sarkar derives both the dignity of every mother tongue (no
dialect is contemptible) and the practical need for a common world language,
*vishva-bhāṣā*, for communication across humanity (DNE 1).

# Explanation

**The single thread (vv-1).** All languages share one law — the verse *sūtre
maṇigaṇā iva*, "like jewels strung on a single thread." The method of derivation
is common to the four root languages: "Among the established languages of the
world, mainly four can be considered as the root languages. They are Vedic, Latin,
Hebrew and old Chinese. If one examines the derivation of the vocabulary of these
languages one will find that their method or process is identical" (vv-19).

**No dialect is contemptible (vv-7).** "under no circumstances do we have the right
to turn up our nose at a dialect, considering it to be unrefined or deprived of a
glorious lineage. Even if its lineage has no glory or has disappeared, from the
existential point of view it is worthy enough." Calling a dialect *bhākhā* "out of
contempt" is the wrong attitude: "Many persons call or used to call dialects
bhákhá … out of contempt." Kabir's verse is the emblem of the teaching: "Kabir has
said: Saḿskrta kúpodaka bhákhá bahatá niir – in other words, the Sanskrit
language is like stagnant well water and the people's language is like the flowing
water of a river, that is, full of vitality."

**Recognition of the mother tongues (vv-7, vv-8).** Languages such as Angika,
Maethilii, Magahii, Bhojpuri and Chhattisgarhi are full languages, not dialects:
"Although these languages are not recognized in India by the constitution, they
deserve recognition and should be recognized. From the standpoint of linguistics
they should be accepted as languages" (vv-7). vv-8 adds: "Bhojpuri, Magahii,
Chatrishgaŕii and other languages deserve to be accepted as full-fledged
languages." DNE 8 states the historical attitude behind the prejudice: "Saḿskrta
was called 'Bháśa' and the peoples' language was called 'Bhákha' to give it a
lower status… Vernacular means 'the language of the slaves.' You should do
something for the recognition of peoples' languages."

**The mother tongue as living river (vv-12).** A living language is bound only by
its own vital energy: "Bengali is a flowing river which moves forward by virtue of
its own vital energy, breaking the dams, overflowing the banks, devastating and
recreating the countryside. Grammar is unable to bind it with its hard and fast
rules."

**Sāhitya and the first idea of humanism (vv-8).** Language serves welfare and free
expression: "Sáhitya means hitena saha [with welfare]… all sáhitya is literature
but not all literature is sáhitya." And: "Therefore the first idea of humanism is
that people must be allowed to speak freely." For every language an accepted
standard spoken form is needed: "there is a need for an easy, well-understood
spoken language with a high quality of intonation to be accepted as the standard
spoken and written language for each and every language."

**Philology as a Neohumanist discipline (vv-5).** "The study of philology and
phonetics will do a lot to close the distances and eliminate inequalities between
people, and it will also help to establish Neohumanism." Bengali stands on
Saṃskṛta: "The Bengali language stands upon the firm foundation of Sanskrit."

**The world language (dne-1, 1959).** For global communication a *vishva-bhāṣā* is
needed: "In order for people around the world to be able to communicate, a vishva
bháśá [universal or world language] is needed, and the teaching and study of that
language should be given equal importance in every country. If we consider the
following three qualities of a language – that it should be widely spoken, be
easily understood and be capable of powerful expression – English alone is
qualified to become the world language." But English is the candidate *of today*,
not a permanent claim: "Of course in the distant future people may select another
language to replace English as the world language, in accordance with the needs of
their age; English cannot keep its position as the world language forever."

**Mother tongue, Saṃskṛta and world language together (dne-5, 1981).** The
educational policy pairs them, never sacrificing the mother tongue: "From the
beginning, children in India can be taught three languages – their mother tongue
or natural language, basic Saḿskrta or the appropriate classical language, and the
world language." In the practical field — "government and non-government work and
court work – the mother tongue should be used."

**Suppression is nastiness (dne-8, 1989).** "I once said that all the languages of
this universe are mine, but you know only 5,7 or 10 of them. Suppression of the
mother tongue is suppression of human sentiment and suppression of human
expression. Such a thing is not only bad, it is nasty!" (This is DNE 8's
statement — the same principle as the PROUT cardinal rule, see below.)

**The inner voice and the longevity of languages (dne-8).** Language is grounded
in cognition itself: "The language of the 'inner voice' is always one and
indivisible. Only in the outer manifestation do we get so many languages." And the
timescale of change: "The average longevity of a script is 2000 years and the
average longevity of a language is 1000 years."

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1979) — the six stages of language expression

*Human Society Is One and Indivisible – 3* (nh2-6, 17 June 1979) gives the
six-stage account of language expression (cross-link to the new
[Stages of Language Expression](/biopsychology/stages-of-language-expression.md) node):

- **The six stages.** "There are six stages to the expression of language: Parā,
  Pashyantī, Madhyamā, Dyotamānā, Vaekharī and Śrutigocarā." "Parā Shakti, the seed of all
  expression, lies in the Muladhara Cakra" — "It is just the seed of the idea." Pashyantī,
  "the stage where the seed of the idea sprouts," lies "in the next higher Cakra, that is
  the Svādhiṣṭhāna"; in Madhyamā "the idea is consolidated to give a picture to it" — "In
  the Maṇipura Cakra there is an urge to transform the idea into sound form." Dyotamānā
  "means vibrational"; in the fifth stage "when the vibration reaches the vocal cord, the
  idea gets transformed into language. This is Vaekharī Shakti"; the last stage, Śrutigocarā,
  "occurs when the word gets vocalized with the help of the tongue."
- **Only the last two differ.** "In all languages Parā, Pashyantī, Madhyamā and Dyotamānā
  are uniform. Only in the fifth and sixth stages is the expression different."
- **No suppression.** "no language should ever be suppressed"; "all languages are the
  languages of Parama Puruṣa"; "it is absurd to divide humanity on the basis of language."

# Notes — fidelity

- **World language and mother tongue, not either/or.** DNE 1 and DNE 5 hold both: a
  *vishva-bhāṣā* for global communication AND the mother tongue as the language of
  practical life and education. Do not read the world-language passages as a
  licence to suppress local languages.
- **English is "today's" candidate.** DNE 1 (1959) says English *alone* now
  qualifies (widely spoken, easily understood, powerful expression) but may be
  replaced "in the distant future" — "English cannot keep its position as the
  world language forever." Present it as time-bound, not as a permanent claim.
- **The "suppression" quote is from DNE 8 (1989), not DNE 5.** The three-language
  policy is DNE 5; the "suppression of the mother tongue is suppression of human
  sentiment" statement is DNE 8. Keep the two chapters distinct.
- **Kabir's verse is quoted in both vv-7 and vv-13** with slightly different
  Bengali: "Saḿskrta kúpodaka bhákhá bahatá niir" (vv-7) and "Saḿskrta
  kúpodaka, bhákhá vahatá niira" (vv-13). Each printing preserved where quoted.
- **The four root languages** (Vedic, Latin, Hebrew, old Chinese) with an
  "identical" method is vv-19's list — Sarkar's own philological claim; keep it
  exact and do not graft on an Indo-Europeanist scheme.
- **Could not verify in the cited chapters:** a single consolidated
  "vishva-bhāṣā proposal" beyond DNE 1's statement; and DNE 1's exact place of
  delivery (the source prints only "1959").
- **nh2-6 (1979) grounds the six stages verbatim** — Parā/Pashyantī/Madhyamā at the three
  lower cakras, Dyotamānā as "vibrational," Vaekharī at the vocal cord, Śrutigocarā as the
  articulated word; only the last two differ across languages (cf.
  [Stages of Language Expression](/biopsychology/stages-of-language-expression.md), [Cakra](/biopsychology/cakra.md)).
