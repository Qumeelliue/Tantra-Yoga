# Definition

**Art** is "movement towards finer sentiments"; **Science** is "a rational expression,
with each and every stage having cause and effect." "Nothing in this world is
non-causal" — *"Kárańábhávátkáryábhávah"* ("Where there is no causal factor, there
cannot be any effect," said Maharśi Kańáda ~2000 years ago). "Spiritual practice
comes within the scope of Science. The first scientist who invented this spiritual
science was Lord Shiva who was born about 7000 years ago," whose science has two wings,
**Nigama** (Párvatii's questions) and **Ágama** (Shiva's replies). (DNE 41, 16 August
1979, Taipei.)

# Explanation

- **Art as finer sentiment.** "If you do these things in a fine way, in a subtle style,
  then within that style, they come within the scope of Art, as in the art of saying,
  and writing languages."
- **Science as rational cause-and-effect.** "When you are doing something along a
  rational line or on the basis of logic, and at the same time, you are drawing
  attention to cause and effect, it is Science." "In this world, everything comes within
  the scope of causality. Nothing in this world is non-causal." Sugar's cause is sugar
  cane or sugar beet; the seed is the cause of the cane — "So, in all spheres of life,
  there is a cause."
- **Spiritual practice is Science.** "Spiritual practice comes within the scope of
  Science. The first scientist who invented this spiritual science was Lord Shiva who
  was born about 7000 years ago." His science "was divided into two portions – two
  compartments just like two wings of a bird. One portion was known as Nigama, the other
  portion, Ágama. The questions of Párvatii were known as Nigama, and the replies of
  Shiva were known as Ágama."
- **Self-knowledge is the greatest means.** Shiva's reply on the minimum qualification:
  *"Átmajiṋ́ánamidaḿ Devi paraḿ mokśaekasádhanam; Sukrtaermánavo bhútvá jiṋániicenmokśamápnuyát."*
  [Self-knowledge is the greatest means to attain salvation. People are born as human
  beings due to their past good saḿskáras, but to attain non-qualified liberation they
  will have to attain self-knowledge.]
- **The Ātma is the only tiirtha.** "Átmá is the best tiirtha. Átmá is the Supreme
  Tiirtha. Rather Átmá is the only tiirtha." Objective knowledge is only "the umbra and
  penumbra of shadows"; the proper knowledge is "the subjectivization of objectivities,"
  where "the very subjective mind is metamorphosed into Consciousness."
- **Subjective approach through objective adjustment.** "your movement is not from
  subtle to crude, but from crude to subtle… you should pay your proper respect to the
  crude world also… ours is a subjective approach through objective adjustment."
- **Vijiṋána gloss (DNE 3).** "in Sanskrit vijiṋána stands for Brahma vijiṋána
  [intuitional science], or adhyátma vijiṋána [the science of spirituality]" — yet
  "science can never change people's saḿskáras," hence sādhana remains the
  alternative.

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** Art = finer sentiments; Science = rational cause-and-effect;
  nothing is non-causal; spiritual practice is Science; Shiva the first scientist;
  Nigama/Ágama = Párvatii's questions / Shiva's replies.
- **Two senses of ágama.** Here (DNE 41) Ágama is Shiva's replies; in DNE 39
  (*Prapta Vákya and Apta Vákya*) ágama carries the sense of scripture as relative
  knowledge. The user's cross-reference flags the contrast; DNE 39 was not consulted for
  this node, so the second sense is flagged rather than quoted.
- The slokas are reproduced exactly as printed in DNE 41 (including the source's
  "Átmajiṋ́ánamidaḿ" with its mixed diacritics) — do not normalize the
  transliteration.
