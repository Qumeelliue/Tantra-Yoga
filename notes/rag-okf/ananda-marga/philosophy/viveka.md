# Definition

Sarkar defines **viveka** as a special kind of deliberation: *"Viveka is that kind of
deliberation where there is a conscious endeavour to decide in favour of shreya
(benevolence) when confronted with the two opposing ideas of shreya and preya
(malevolence)"* (SS 6). It is narrower than ordinary deliberation (*vicāra*): "The
denotation of vicāra is broader than that of viveka" — every viveka is a vicāra, but
only deliberation aimed at the good is viveka. The bundle glossary gives the compact
form: *"Viveka — conscience, power of discrimination between good and evil."* It is
the defining human faculty: "A human is different from an animal only because he or
she possesses **viveka** … and **buddhi** [intellect]" (AV 23).

# Explanation

**The fivefold conscience.** In *The Five Kinds of Conscience (Viveka)* (SS 6, 1957)
Sarkar enumerates *viveka paiñcaka*: (1) **nityānitya viveka** (permanent vs
impermanent), (2) **dvaetādvaeta viveka** (whether the Eternal is one or many),
(3) **ātmānātma viveka** (Consciousness vs non-consciousness), (4) **paiñcakoṣa
viveka** (discerning the five layers from Consciousness), and (5) **mahāvākya
viveka** — "the resultant of the other four" — which teaches that Brahma is attained
not by knowledge alone but through "jiṇāna yoga, karma yoga and bhakta yoga." The
five "begin with nityānitya viveka and terminate in devotion."

**Conscience as the mind's eye.** Sarkar's recurring Gītā allegory makes viveka the
guide of an otherwise blind mind. In *Blind Mind and Conscience* (AV 34) and *The
Field of Dharma* (AV 31) he reads Dhṛtarāṣṭra as the mind ("born blind") and Sañjaya
as viveka: "the mind cannot see without the help of the conscience … The power of
discrimination between good and bad is called viveka." *Dharmakṣetra — the Human
Body* (AV 17) states it as a rule: "for a clear vision of anything … the guidance of
the conscience is a must."

**Role in dharma and the choice of shreya.** Viveka is "an inseparable part of the
practice of dharma" (SS 6); *nityānitya viveka* makes one "realise the necessity of
observing dharma" and, accepting "only the eternal," issues in detachment (SS 3,
*Desire and Detachment*). In AV 30 viveka appears as one of the fifty propensities
(vṛttis), seated in the anāhata cakra, glossed "conscience, discrimination."

# In *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1982) — the awakened conscience

*Awakened Conscience (Discourse 9)* (dne-7, 22 Mar 1982) is the DNE companion of LOIN 9
and carries the conscience's definition to its final point:

- **Viveka as the final discrimination.** "This final discrimination for promoting
  human welfare which is the final outcome or desiderative point of discrimination, is
  called viveka – 'conscience'."
- **Siddhānta = "logical decision".** After weighing pros and cons: "this clear
  conclusion, this logical decision – not a mere decision only – in favour of either
  the positive or the negative is termed siddhánta in Sanskrit. So siddhánta means
  'logical decision'. Not just 'decision', 'logical decision.'"
- **The two defects of study.** Knowledge through study is defective two ways:
  "Defect due to ignorance" (the teacher's/author's own defect, passed on) and "defect
  due to change in tempor [time]" (knowledge valid at one time loses validity later —
  e.g. Allahabad vs Lucknow as UP's capital).
- **The two steps to Neohumanism.** "The first step towards the establishment of
  Neohumanism is study; the second step is rationalistic mentality." Study
  (intensive intellectual analysis) → rationalistic mentality (verify everything →
  logical decision, *siddhānta*) → the final desiderative point of discrimination =
  **viveka** (conscience).
- **Keep the conscience ever-vigilant.** "You must keep your conscience ever-vigilant"
  to combat "geo-sentiment, socio-sentiment, or ordinary humanistic sentiment," and
  "pseudo-humanistic strategy"; the awakened conscience is the "rationalistic
  mentality" reached after study, analysis of positive/negative, and the
  blissful/non-blissful verdict.

## Relationships

- viveka is the faculty that operates the choice between [shreya and preya](/philosophy/shreya-preya.md) — "a conscious endeavour to decide in favour of shreya" (SS 6)
- Sarkar pairs viveka with the intellect: a human differs from an animal only by possessing viveka and [buddhi](/biopsychology/buddhi.md) (AV 23); both are destroyed by vanity (abhimāna)
- *nityānitya viveka* discriminates the permanent from the transient — the same axis as [vidyā and avidyā](/cosmology/vidya-avidya.md) (shreya guided by vidyā, preya by avidyā)
- it is "an inseparable part of the practice of [dharma](/philosophy/dharma.md)" (SS 6) and a prerequisite of [sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md) ("not possible to follow the spiritual cult without … paiñcakoṣa viveka")
- the fivefold conscience culminates not in knowledge but in devotion — the path toward [mokṣa](/practice/moksa.md)
- dne-7: viveka = the "final desiderative point of discrimination" after study → rationalistic mentality (*siddhānta*); "the first step towards the establishment of Neohumanism is study; the second step is rationalistic mentality" — the same method as [Neohumanism](/philosophy/neohumanism.md) and the counter to [Pseudo-Humanism](/philosophy/pseudo-humanism.md)

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** the SS 6 definition, the *viveka paiñcaka* enumeration, and the
  Sañjaya = viveka / blind-mind allegory (AV 34, AV 31, AV 17) are Sarkar's own words.
- The shreya/preya material (SS 3) frames the *choice* viveka makes; SS 6 itself
  defines viveka *by* that choice, so the link is direct, not interpretive.
- **No "viveka vs vairāgya" contrast** is presented in the read passages; the grounded
  relation is that *nityānitya viveka issues in* detachment (SS 3). Treat any
  side-by-side contrast as light interpretation.
- **Could not verify:** *vivekakhyāti* (Patañjali's term) appears NOWHERE in the
  corpus (grep returned no hits) — do not attribute it to Sarkar; he pairs viveka with
  *buddhi*, not *vivekakhyāti*. The discourse "Discrimination in Rights" (AV 9/10) is a
  confirmed MISFIT (it concerns the social right to liberation, not the viveka faculty).
