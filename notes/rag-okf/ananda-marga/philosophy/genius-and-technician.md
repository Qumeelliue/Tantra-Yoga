# Definition

**"A genius is not made – only a technician is. A genius is born, and a technician is
made by constant practice."** A genius possesses "an inborn faculty; it cannot be
created"; a technician possesses "an ordinary qualification, developed to an
extraordinary degree of efficiency." The first factor of success in any endeavour is
firm determination: *"Phaliṣyatiiti viśvāsaḥ siddher prathamā lakṣaṇam"* — "'I shall
surely succeed' – this sort of firm conviction is required for success in life."
(19 June 1979, DNE 12.)

# Explanation

- **Inborn vs. acquired.** "Genius is an inborn faculty; all people do not possess it."
  Technicians, by contrast, "have ordinary qualities, but by dint of application
  (*Sādhanā*) and effort they have converted these into outstanding skills" (DNE 37).
  Sarkar's test: Rabindranath Tagore was "an inborn poet"; those who merely rhyme one
  line "after much difficulty" and can find no original thought are "technicians, not
  geniuses."
- **Firm determination — the first factor.** "according to Lord Shiva, of all the factors
  for success in life, the first and most important fact is firm determination:
  *Phaliṣyatiiti viśvāsaḥ siddher prathamā lakṣaṇam.*" Doubt and hesitation ("Can I
  really do it?") is exactly what must be overcome; "one should think, 'Since Parama
  Puruṣa is inseparably associated with me, the flow of capability and energy will
  emanate from Him and operate through me.'"
- **The tiny fraction of innate ability used.** "human beings use only a small fraction
  of their innate ability; perhaps only 1% or 2% of their capability, and 98% remains
  unutilized." Even Rabindranath "hardly utilized 10% or 15 % of his entire capacity."
- **Civilisations fail by lack of coordination.** "The reason for their failure to
  survive was their lack of coordination in various aspects of life: they could not
  bring about a proper adjustment among the different spheres of existence." The Mayans
  "could not invent the wheel"; the Egyptians "began to lag behind due to the lack of
  *kṣatriyan* spirit which is essential for the preservation of civilization." "dharma
  means the balanced state of all aspects of human life."
- **Society must protect, not cripple, its geniuses.** "We never appreciate them during
  their life times; rather we treat them with hostility and create obstructions in their
  activities. As a result, the entire society is harmed." "One of the main defects of
  people is jealousy" — "if we encourage a genius to unfold his or her potentialities,
  then the whole human society will be benefited."
- **The collective garden.** "human life is not like a single flower; it is like a
  bouquet or a garden of flowers blooming with many varieties of flowers" — "We must
  discover unity in the midst of colourful diversity."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** the one-line contrast "a genius is not made – only a technician
  is"; the shloka *Phaliṣyatiiti viśvāsaḥ siddher prathamā lakṣaṇam* is kept exact.
- **Sanskrit nuance:** *pratibhā* is the closest single term for the inborn faculty;
  Sarkar himself never equates the two as synonyms, so the pairing is kept as a
  translation aid, not as his equation.
- **Specific claims (Mayans, Egyptians, Rabindranath's %) are his own** historical and
  biographical assertions; reproduced as claims, not verified history.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** a full list of "genii"; the chapter names
  Tagore and Madhusudhan Dutt as examples but offers no exhaustive canon.
