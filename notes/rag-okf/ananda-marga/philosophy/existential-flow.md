# Definition

The **existential flow** is the mind's essential movement — "movement or mobility is a
must for psychic existence." Its **culminating point** (*desideratum*) is **Parama
Puruṣa — "love, eternal love, universal love, personified."** The human mind's
speciality: unlike a river (which flows only mountain → sea), it may move "from the
mountain towards the sea or from the sea towards the mountain" — crude → subtle or
subtle → crude. (1986, NH 1/19.)

# Explanation

- **Mind in all existence.** In inanimate objects the mind "is in its stagnant point, in
  cimmerian slumber"; among animated beings the mind plays an important role; in humans
  the most dominant — "human beings are more psychic beings than physical ones."
- **Pravrtti and nivrtti.** The mind's movement is called *vṛtti* (not exactly
  "propensity"): toward the crude = **pravrtti**, toward the subtle = **nivrtti** —
  *"Pravrttireṣā bhūtānāṃ sthūlā nivrttistu mahāphalā."* The speciality: pravrtti may
  be **redirected** into nivrtti at any moment — "your longing for money, your longing
  for name or fame, may be converted into a longing for Parama Puruṣa." "The surging
  waves of pravrtti today will tomorrow be converted into subtle blissful vibrations."
- **The mind is the cause of bondage and liberation.** *"Mano karoti karmāṇi mana
  lipyate pātakaeh; manashca tanmanā bhūtvā na puṇyaeh na ca pātakaeh"* — the mind that
  does the action is not stained if immersed in the thought of God; *"Mana eva
  manuṣyāṇāṃ kāraṇaṃ bandhamokṣayoḥ"* — "The mind is the cause of bondage, and this
  mind, this very mind, is the cause of liberation."
- **The two culminations.** Encouraging movement toward the subtle yields either the
  **qualified stance (saguṇāsthiti)** — the metamorphosis of unit mind into Cosmic
  Mind — or, if the "I"-feeling is surrendered at the altar of Supreme Existence,
  **nirguṇāsthiti** — the existential factor becomes one with the Cognitive Principle,
  "one will become omniscient."
- **The two-fold approach of the march.** (1) Parama Puruṣa is the goal and one must
  get Him; (2) while moving, one fights internal and external bondages: the **eight
  pāshas** (imposed bondages — hatred, doubt, fear, shyness, dissemblance, vanity of
  lineage, cultural superiority complex, egotism) and the **six ripus** (inborn
  enemies). "This fight is with the left hand, and with the right hand what is one to
  do? One is to serve the entire universe… with the feeling of Neohumanism: 'The
  universe is mine; all living beings are mine.'"
- **The birthright.** "Whoever is endowed with a human mind and a human body is
  entitled to follow this path… everybody has the birthright to move along this path of
  righteousness."

# In *Varna Vijinana* (1983) — rhythm, systaltic flow, and the law of change

*Varna Vijinana* gives the flow-doctrine its acoustic and cosmological shape:

- **Rhythm is native to life's movement.** (vv-16) "Human beings try to find rhythm
  in everything because human life itself is rhythmic. Movement is the natural
  characteristic of life so rhythm must exist within human movement. If life's
  movement were continuous or linear then there would be no scope for rhythm, and
  without rhythm there can be no happiness either. So in this universe all movement
  flows in the waves of systaltic order."
- **Emanation as systaltic flow.** (vv-20) "This manifestation or emanation is
  vibrational, and this vibration moves in a contractive-expansive flow, a
  systaltic order which moves in crests and troughs." The initial wave impulse of
  emanation "wanes in the subsequent phase due to the different blows and
  counterblows."
- **The law of providence (vidhátá).** (vv-21) "Everything in this cosmos is
  moving. Nothing stops, so whatever moves also has to undergo change. This is the
  law of providence [vidhátá]." *Vidhátá* is not a specific entity "but rather a
  specific system" — the system "by which the Supreme Consciousness carries out the
  various activities of the universe."

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1979) — the ideological flow and mercy toward animals

*An Ideal Life* (nh1-3, 2 January 1979) states the flow-doctrine in the context of
animal life and human responsibility — seven years before the 1986 NH 1/19 discourse:

- **The ideological flow.** "never forgetting that human life is an ideological flow, human
  beings should always move towards the supreme ideology. They have been moving in this
  direction since time immemorial and, through this process of movement, have reached the
  present stage of humanity. Passing through many lives as worms, insects, birds, and
  animals, they have undergone countless pains, miseries, tortures and humiliations."
- **The goat parable.** The goat "spends its life eating grass, until the day comes when the
  butcher grabs it, kills it, skins it and sells its flesh piece by piece in the market."
  Animals "fully trust human beings; they trust them even up to one minute before their
  death."
- **Mercy toward animals.** "we should have a merciful attitude towards them"; "human beings
  should carry out their duties properly in the external world and should always ensure that
  no being suffers unnecessarily from any undue mistreatment. In the physical sphere, the
  rights of all should be protected."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** "the mind is the cause of bondage, and this mind… is the cause of
  liberation"; pravrtti converts into nivrtti; the two-fold approach (fight + serve).
  Keep the shlokas exact.
- **Vṛtti ≠ "propensity"** — Sarkar prefers the Sanskrit term for the mind's movement;
  keep his preference.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** a fuller scheme of "saguṇāsthiti/
  nirguṇāsthiti" beyond the two-endpoint statement (cf. [Samādhi](/practice/samadhi.md)'s
  savikalpa/nirvikalpa, which is the same pair under the sūtra names).
- **VV (1983) corroborates the flow/rhythm framing:** VV 16 grounds rhythm in the
  "systaltic order" of life's movement; VV 20 describes emanation as "a
  contractive-expansive flow, a systaltic order which moves in crests and
  troughs"; VV 21 states the law of change ("the law of providence [vidhátá]")
  and defines *vidhátá* as a system, not an entity. All direct teaching — kept
  with source wording.
- **NHN 1/3 (1979) uses the flow-titles in the sādhaka's ideal life:** the "ideological
  flow" of human life and the goat parable on animal suffering are grounded verbatim;
  the "culminating point" (Paramātmā, "the supreme fulfilment in life") is there stated
  as the end of the withdrawal chain (cf. [Subjective Approach](/practice/subjective-approach.md)).
