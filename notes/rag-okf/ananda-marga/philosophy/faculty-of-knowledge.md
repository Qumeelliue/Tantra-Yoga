# Definition

**The faculty of knowledge** (*jñāna*) is knowing as the **subjectivization of
objectivity** — taking an external object into oneself and "assimilat[ing] it in [one's]
psychic existential 'I' feeling" — distinct from the merely mechanical knowledge of
reflections and refractions. The *jñāna mārga* (Shaeva cult) is the path that asks
"What is this? What is its source?" until the mind reaches the **Supreme Point** where
reflection and refraction end. (1980, *The Faculty of Knowledge* 1.)

# Explanation

- **Two aspects of knowledge.** In the mechanical stratum, knowing occurs "with the
  perception of special types of reflections and refractions" (echo, vibration passing
  through a plate); in the psychic stratum, it is "the subjectivization of any external
  objectivity" — the elephant, horse, word or touch is brought inside and assimilated.
- **Etymology.** *Jñā* (to know) was a later Aryan invention — the Rgvedic language
  used *vid*, which "did not carry the full import" (it could not distinguish the
  farmer's knowing when to reap from the scholar's research-discovery); *jñā* meant
  "subjectivization of objectivity in a mechanical way through reflections and
  refractions" (→ Old Latin *keno*, → English "know"). Language grows with a community's
  advancement (monkeys near humans have 700–900 words vs 30–40 in the wild).
- **The Shaeva cult of knowledge.** The jñāna mārgī poses "What is this?" then "What is
  its source or origin?" and proceeds "to another source of knowledge where both
  reflection and refraction end" — the point where the mind loses its capacity to
  analyse is the **Supreme Point**. "Judged in this light, Ananda Marga philosophy also
  supports the Shaeva cult," encouraging intellect and wisdom; "the constant pursuit of
  intellectuality leads one to its furthest point, the place where intuition begins."
- **The prapatti mārga (non-Shaeva) defect.** Prapattivāda holds that whatever happens
  is due to Cosmic will ("not even a single blade of grass can move"); this "hampers, at
  the very outset, the development of humanity." The jñāna mārgī's refutation: "it is a
  hundred percent true that nothing… can move without His will. This is exactly what we
  wish to discover in jñāna mārga: that He desired it, and then the blade of grass
  moved."
- **Knowledge and sādhanā.** "The path of sādhanā is not devoid of intellect or
  intuition; rather it is based on intuition. It gives no scope for superstition or
  blind faith."

# In *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1978–1980) — the same Faculty of Knowledge series

The DNE series is the same *Faculty of Knowledge* series (April–June 1980) plus its
companion discourses on intellect, memory and self-realization; it supplies the
later chapters (fok-2, fok-4, fok-5) and the withdrawal/objectivation mechanics that
fok-1 announced.

- **DNE 16 = fok-1 (cross-reference).** *The Faculty of Knowledge – 1* (dne-16, 27 Apr
  1980) is the same chapter as the node's existing fok-1 source; the DNE path is
  recorded in `sources` for cross-linking.
- **The withdrawal formula (dne-42, fok-2, 4 May 1980).** Knowing is governed by how
  one withdraws from worldly factors: *"Yacchedváuṋmanasii prájiṋastadyacchejjiṋána
  átmani; Jiṋánamátmani mahati niyacchettadyacchecchánta átmani"* — wise persons
  first merge their indriyas into citta, then citta into aham, aham into mahat, mahat
  into jīvātmā, and finally jīvātmā into Supreme Consciousness. "A major part of the
  knowing process is material, and only a small part of it is abstract." The mind has
  "two contradictory inherent tendencies: one of acquisition, the other of sacrifice" —
  the spirit of sacrifice grows with evolution. *"Anubhútaviśayásampramośah smrtih"*
  (the re-creation of things already perceived is memory).
- **Objectivated and subjectivated mind (dne-43, fok-4, 25 May 1980).** The elephant
  seen outside becomes "an object of the objectivated mind"; memory is "the re-creation
  of the same mental formation that was once created in the objectivated mind." "When
  you reconvert your mind into sound, touch, form, taste or smell, it is called smrti
  or memory." **Rhythm as ancient memory technology:** "for fifteen thousand years, the
  common practice has been to bring every valuable branch of knowledge within the scope
  of rhythm… Human beings do not easily forget rhythm" (hence the seven Vedic metres);
  the *gharáńá* schools of music and kiirtana (Manoharshahii, Reneti, Garanaháta,
  Mandárań) exemplify the same rhythm-tagged knowing.
- **The Supreme Subject (dne-44, fok-5, 2 Jun 1980).** The third chamber of knowledge
  behind the objective and subjective minds is the **Supreme Subject** — "the noumenal
  factor behind each and every action, but it does nothing by itself… His presence is
  an obligatory prerequisite for everything" (the catalyst in a scientific experiment).
  "The snake looks at the frog… But the snake itself is not aware that it is being
  watched by the peacock… Nobody looks at the subject." "The best way to meditate on
  Parama Puruśa, or remember Him, is to bear in mind that… the Supreme Subjectivity is
  maintaining a constant and close watch on me" — when this ideation "becomes clear,
  stable and permanent… you will see that you have become one with the Supreme
  Subjectivity. And this is the final stage of the faculty of knowledge."
- **The intellect's two duties (dne-38, Dogma and Human Intellect, 22 Sep 1979).** The
  intellect has "two duties: to think and to remember." "To think means, then, to speak
  internally." Knowledge is "one direct and the other taken from memory." Two stances:
  the **objective psychic stance** (the mind "becomes bigger and bigger," nerve-cells
  strengthen, "the human cranium will become bigger") versus the **objecto-subjective
  spiritual stance** — "you withdraw different mental faculties or propensities and
  make them pinnacled… you offer that pinnacled human mind at the altar of the supreme
  spirituality."
- **Smṛti vs jñāna; self-knowledge as the only knowledge (dne-40, 13 Dec 1978).**
  Memory is *anubhūtaviśayásampromośah smrti*; jñāna is understanding — "memory and
  jiṋána are not one and the same." Constant, unfailing memory is *dhruvásmrti*,
  "an essential prerequisite for spiritual samádhi." "Excepting self-realization or
  Átma-jiṋána – all other knowledge is of no value" (mundane knowledge is but "a
  shadow"). "If you want to know all – know one. Then only everything will be known to
  you."

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1979) — an earlier occurrence of the withdrawal śloka

*An Ideal Life* (nh1-3, 2 January 1979) opens with the same withdrawal śloka that
fok-2 (dne-42, 4 May 1980) later quotes:

- **The śloka.** *"Yacchedvāṅmanasi prājñastadyacchejjñāna ātmani; Jñānamātmani mahati
  niyacchettadyacchecchānta ātmani"* [Wise persons first merge their indriyas (sense
  organs) into their citta (mind-stuff), then their citta into aham (doer "I"), then aham
  into mahat ("I exist" feeling), then mahat into jīvātmā (unit spirit, soul), and finally
  their jīvātmā into Paramātmā (Supreme Consciousness).]
- **Corroboration.** This 1979 occurrence (nh1-3, Patna) predates the 1980 fok-2
  occurrence, grounding the indriya → citta → aham → mahat → jīvātmā → Paramātmā
  withdrawal in the same words Sarkar uses in the knowing-process chapters; the context
  there is the sādhaka's ideal life, not the faculty of knowledge per se.

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** jñāna = subjectivization of objectivity; the Shaeva cult is
  Ananda Marga's own; prapattivāda "hampers, at the very outset, the development of
  humanity." Keep the contrast sharp.
- **The etymology** (vid → jñā → keno → know; monkey-vocabulary examples) is Sarkar's
  own philological account — reproduce as his framing, not standard linguistics.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** the full five-chapter treatment (fok-2…5)
  beyond ch. 1's opening; the chapter is an introduction.
- **DNE resolves the gap:** the same *Faculty of Knowledge* series as DNE 16 (fok-1),
  42 (fok-2), 43 (fok-4) and 44 (fok-5) is now ingested — the withdrawal formula,
  objectivated/subjectivated mind, and the Supreme Subject are all verbatim-grounded.
  DNE 38 and 40 add the intellect's two duties and smṛti vs jñāna.
- **NHN 1/3 (1979) adds an earlier grounding of the withdrawal śloka** — *An Ideal Life*
  (2 January 1979) quotes the same śloka verbatim (with the same translation) before the
  1980 fok-2 occurrence; the two prints keep the same Sanskrit in slightly different
  transliteration (AM-style in the NHN print), reproduced faithfully per print.
