# Definition

Human existence is two-sided — individual and collective — and so has **two sets of
values: social values** (ascertained on the basis of social responsibilities) and
**human cardinal principles** — the "silver lining between the psycho-spiritual and
spiritual strata of human existence." The sadvipras' rule: **human value takes
precedence over social value.** (1970, PiaN 7/8.)

# Explanation

- **Social values vs human values.** Society has always paid tribute to social values
  (honouring kings in the Kṣatriya era, intellectuals in the Vipra era) "but never,
  not even for a moment, has anyone respected human cardinal principles." Human
  cardinal principles stand at the confluence where "where animality ends, humanity
  begins, where humanity ends, divinity begins."
- **The critique of scriptures and dogma.** Vice and virtue, codes of justice and
  scriptural texts "have been formulated by different religions to further vested
  interests"; "no scripture should gain supremacy by slighting or neglecting
  humanity." Examples of cruelty sanctioned by scripture: satī, child marriage, no
  remarriage of widows, polygamy, caste pollution. "Those who are ensnared by the
  scriptures cannot be expected to know the value of human life."
- **Vice and virtue redefined.** Vice/virtue are "the outcome of mental perversion
  under the influence of time, space and person"; "that which blocks this movement
  [toward the zenith of human progress] is vice and that which facilitates it is
  virtue." To exploit an individual, group or society is vice; to rob a person of the
  right to exist is vice.
- **Punishment is for rectification.** The penal code will be based on human values;
  punishment "should be inflicted humanely" and withdrawn on correction; offenders
  alone are punished, never their relatives; "an entire life should not have to be
  wasted over a single act of vice"; "on no account should anybody be branded forever."
  The sadvipras "will knock down the prisons and build reform schools, rectification
  camps"; those criminal from organic defects should be treated, those criminal from
  poverty — their poverty removed. (This section also printed separately as "The
  Neohumanism of Sadvipras".)
- **"Human beings are divine children."** "The human mind goaded by depraving
  propensities is satan. If their propensities are sublimated, they will no longer be
  satans; they will be transformed into gods." "To them no sinner is contemptible, no
  one is a rogue."
- **Social responsibility.** Those who shoulder social responsibility must be
  moralists (base), universalists (goal) and practitioners of rigorous sādhanā
  (means) — "Morality is the base, sādhanā is the means, and life divine is the goal."
  Irresponsible people cannot be entrusted with it; "one whose character is not in
  accordance with Yama-Niyama should not get opportunity to become a representative"
  (quoting Caryācarya Part 2).
- **The present age values money.** "A person who possesses wealth is respected…
  human beings have become the means for the rich to earn money." The judicial system
  favours those who can hire good lawyers; black-marketeers "are now occupying the
  commanding positions in society."

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1970) — the concluding part of the same discourse

*The Neohumanism of Sadvipras* (nh1-1, March 1970) is the **separate concluding part
of the same discourse** as PiaN 7/8 / DNE 27: the source's own editors' footnote says
it is "the concluding part" of the complete discourse, the earlier part having
explained "social value" as "the recognition that society pays to its leaders by
virtue of their position." It corroborates the node's content and adds:

- **The Samamantrena society definition.** "A rṣi [sage] has said: *Samamantreṇa jāyate
  iti samājaḥ* ["Society is the collective movement of a group of individuals who have
  decided to move together towards a common goal"]" — "whether people are pāpii or tāpii
  [sinners or victims]... they are so only superficially; internally they are filled with
  the potential for purity."
- **The Chaitanya–Haridas exemplum.** "The pandits puffed up with vainglory could turn
  their attention to their books instead of attending on the ailing non-Hindu Haridas, but
  Chaitanya Mahaprabhu found it impossible to remain indifferent to him. He took Haridas in
  his arms and nursed him carefully, and thus showed respect to human value."
- **Tagore on justice.** "According to Rabindranath Tagore, 'Justice and law at the cost of
  humanity is like a stone instead of bread. Maybe that stone is rare and valuable, but it
  cannot remove hunger.'"
- **Human value above social value.** "It is customary to give preference to social value
  over human value. Sadvipras want to strike at the root of this custom... human value must
  lay the foundation for the social value."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** "human value takes precedence over social value"; "punishment is
  not an end in itself"; the sadvipras "will knock down the prisons and build reform
  schools"; "human beings are divine children."
- **Vice/virtue are relative** to time, space and person — do not read as moral
  relativism, but as Sarkar's critique of fixed scriptural codes.
- The chapter's final section ("The Neohumanism of Sadvipras") is a source-specific
  cross-publication — flagged in the source itself.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** a formal list of "human cardinal
  principles" beyond the confluence-definition; the Caryācarya quotation is embedded
  in the chapter.
- **nh1-1 (1970) corroborates the whole discourse.** The source's own footnote marks it as
  the concluding part of the same complete discourse as PiaN 7/8; the Samamantrena
  society-definition, the Chaitanya–Haridas exemplum and Tagore's "stone instead of bread"
  are quoted verbatim from the source.
