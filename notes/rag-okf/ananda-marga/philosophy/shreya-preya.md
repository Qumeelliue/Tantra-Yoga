# Definition

Shreya and preya are the two paths, "completely opposite in principle," that the
Kaṭhopaniṣad sets before every human being. Sarkar glosses **shreya** as "supreme
benevolence" — that which "relieves pain forever," and is therefore called
*Paramārtha* — and **preya** as "mundane pleasure," which gives only *artha*
(temporary relief) "but keeps them away from paramārtha." The defining verse, given
by Yama to Naciketā: *"Anyacchreyo'nyadutaeva preyaste ubhe nānārthe
Puruṣaṃsiniitah; Tayoshreya ādadānasya sādhu bhavati hiiyate'rthād ya u
preyovṛṇiite"* (SS 3; the source named as the Kaṭhopaniṣad of the Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda).

# Explanation

**The criterion: temporary vs permanent cessation of pain.** In *Supreme Benevolence
and Mundane Pleasure* (SS 3) Sarkar defines *artha* as "that which causes the
cessation of pain," distinguishing *temporary* cessation (artha — pain recurs) from
*permanent* cessation (Paramārtha — pain destroyed "at the root"). Preya gives artha;
shreya is Paramārtha. He illustrates with hunger sated by sweets, money that never
ends greed, and the sick boy who refuses bitter neem (which would cure him) for sugar
(which worsens his disease): "Sugar is his preya."

**Why even the wise run after preya.** "The road to preya is an easy one"; it is fear
of the slight difficulties of shreya, and the accumulated saṃskāras of past animal
lives, that drive even knowledgeable people to it. Preya is the realm of *kāma*
(mundane longing for fragmentary units); shreya leads to *prema* (divine love).

**The choice of the wise (dhīra).** A second verse — *"Shreyashca Preyashca
manuṣyametastao sampariitya vivinaktidhiirāh…"* — teaches that the wise person
"judge[s] within themselves with cool heads which is shreya and which is preya,"
choosing shreya, while the dull (*manda*) chooses preya. This maps onto vidyā vs
avidyā: "one is guided by vidyā or the introversive principle, the other by avidyā …
one to light, the other to darkness." In *Which Is the Right Path* (AV 4) the same
shreya/preya dilemma frames the whole question of the right path, resolved by
*"Dharmasya tattvaṃ nihitaṃ guhāyām"* — dharma's essence lies in the cavity of the
heart, in Parama Puruṣa.

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1957, 1969) — shreya–preya in art and in the East–West debate

The *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* apply the pair to aesthetics and to the
one-sidedness of the East:

- **Shreya + preya evolve svabhāva.** (DNE 2, *The Practice of Art and
  Literature*, 14 Feb 1957.) "In every era literature has brought about different
  steps, different phases, of evolution of svabhāva [humanity's true nature]
  through the unique union of shreya [blissful spiritual union] and preya
  [sensory pleasure]. That which is antithetical to human nature ... can never be
  embraced by humanity." The two are united in literature's service-and-sādhana,
  not opposed as exclusive paths (cf. [Sāhitya](/philosophy/sahitya.md)).
- **The East's exclusive-shreya path is one-sided.** (DNE 10, *Talks on Education
  – Excerpt C*, 1969.) "All the religions of the East have clearly instructed
  their followers to try and accept the path of shreya or that which leads to
  supreme benevolence only, to the utter exclusion of preya or that which leads
  to the acquisition of mundane objects! It may be that this sort of philosophy is
  not logically incorrect, but in practice this type of extreme idealism preaches
  that the world should be ignored. The individual human being may develop
  conscience, morality, renunciation, etc., and may reach the height of
  spirituality, but the society as a whole will not thereby progress." The
  remedy is the golden policy of give-and-take — "expressed in Bengali as, 'Dive
  ar nive milave milive'" (cf. [Education](/philosophy/education.md),
  [Subjective Approach](/practice/subjective-approach.md)).

## Relationships

- discerning shreya from preya is the very work of [viveka](/philosophy/viveka.md): "Viveka is that … endeavour to decide in favour of shreya … when confronted with shreya and preya" (SS 6)
- *DNE 2*: the union of śreya and preya evolves svabhāva (humanity's true nature) — literature brings "different steps, different phases, of evolution ... through the unique union of shreya [blissful spiritual union] and preya [sensory pleasure]" (cf. [Sāhitya](/philosophy/sahitya.md))
- *DNE 10*: the East's exclusive-śreya path is one-sided — individual spirituality flourishes but "the society as a whole will not thereby progress"; the golden policy of give-and-take, "Dive ar nive milave milive" (cf. [Education](/philosophy/education.md), [Subjective Approach](/practice/subjective-approach.md))
- the pair is explicitly identified with [vidyā vs avidyā](/cosmology/vidya-avidya.md): shreya guided by vidyā (introversive), preya by avidyā (extroversive)
- choosing shreya is following [dharma](/philosophy/dharma.md); in AV 4 the shreya/preya dilemma is the dilemma of "the right path," answered by dharma rooted in Parama Puruṣa
- shreya leads to permanent cessation of pain and final emancipation — to [mokṣa](/practice/moksa.md)
- the discipline that lets one pursue shreya while still handling worldly objects without bondage is *madhuvidyā* within [sādhanā](/practice/sadhana.md) (SS 1)
- preya is the field of *kāma*; shreya leads to *prema*, the substance of [bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md)

# Notes — fidelity

- **Source attribution is direct:** SS 3 names "the Kāṭhopaniṣada of Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda";
  SS 1 also attributes the combined verse to the Kaṭhopaniṣad.
- **Do NOT conflate two different verses.** The "better to die" discourses (AV 4/91,
  AV 8/165, AV 8/168, AV 15/7) quote the **Gītā** verse *"Shreyān svadharmo viguṇah
  paradharmāt … Svadharme nidhanaṃ shreyah…"* (svadharma vs paradharma, where
  *shreyah* = "better/preferable"). That is a SEPARATE teaching from the
  Kaṭha-Upaniṣadic *shreya/preya* pair, though both share the word-root and the theme
  of choosing the higher path at cost. This node is the Kaṭha pair only.
- The verse appears in two transliterations across the bundle (SS 3 splits it; SS 1
  gives all four lines together) — quotes above follow SS 3.
- **Could not verify:** the exact Kaṭha chapter/verse numbers (only the work-level
  attribution appears in the passages read).
