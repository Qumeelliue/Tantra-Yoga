# Definition

**Pramā** is **equilibrium and equipoise** — "psychic equilibrium (in the arena of
psychic faculty) and equipoise (in the arena of psychic mass)." In its final stage
the equilibrium is absolute and "the triangle of forces, the triangle of
equilibrium, remains undisturbed" (*Pra-mā + ḍa + ṭa = Pramā*).

# Explanation

- Pramā is bound to the doctrine of propensities (*vṛtti*): introversial
  (*Saṃvṛtti*, toward the Subtlest) and extroversial (*Pravṛtti*, toward crude
  pabula). The thousand propensities are controlled by the multi-propensive
  [plexus](/biopsychology/cakra.md); when its point is pinnacled and touches Parama
  Puruṣa, that is **Nivṛtti** — perfect psychic equilibrium.
- **Nivṛtti** is "the silver line of demarcation" between Pravṛtti and Saṃvṛtti.
  Where there is no scope of Nivṛtti there is no equilibrium, and beings crudify
  (the state of *Prakṛtilīna*). Extroversial propensities cannot be wholly crushed,
  so the aspirant maintains adjustment with Pravṛtti while moving introversially.
- Pramā must be established **within and without** — physical, psychic and
  spiritual; "if Pramā is lost, it must be restored immediately." It is the fourth
  requisite of practice (*caturtho samatābhāvo* — "one must have a balanced mind").
- **Three kinds form a balanced triangle** (*lokatrikoṇa*): **pramā saṃvṛddhi**
  (physical), **pramā ṛddhi** (psychic), **pramā siddhi** (spiritual). When people
  deviate from practical [dharma](/philosophy/dharma.md), the *pramā-trikoṇa* in the
  physical stratum is destroyed and "material prosperity becomes an impossibility."
- **Progression:** pramā saṃvṛddhi (individual *and* collective) → pramā ṛddhi (via
  love of the Lord + psycho-spiritual action) → pramā siddhi (attaining the goal,
  "for the promotion of the interests of all"), at which the aspirant contacts the
  *kaoṣikī shakti* and, via the [kuṇḍalinī's](/practice/kundalini.md) rise, becomes
  one with the *guṇa-trikoṇa*.

# In *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1987) — Pramātrikona worldwide

*Pramā – 4* (dne-17, Feb 1987) is a question-and-answer discourse that applies the
three *Pramātrikonas* to world adjustment, dogma and education:

- **Physical Pramātrikona is worldwide or not at all.** "Even if nature is bountiful or
  human dexterity is bountiful in a particular country, there cannot be physical
  pramātrikona if there is want or shortage in neighbouring countries." The contrast:
  "Punjab and Haryana are a bit affluent; Orissa, East UP and Bihar are extremely poor.
  Side by side light and darkness cannot remain. Naturally, political peace is sure to
  be disturbed." "For physical Pramātrikona there must be sufficient food and other
  physical necessities for the entire world."
- **Psychic freedom for a radical cure.** Physico-psychic diseases (physical origin,
  psychic level) "cannot be cured radically" "If the psychic self is not properly
  balanced"; free psychic movement is the prerequisite of radical cure.
- **Spiritual Pramātrikona requires a spiritual urge created by education.** "For
  spiritual practice you are to create a spiritual urge. Therefore you are to create a
  proper system of education… We must start primary schools throughout the entire world
  to create a spiritual urge amongst the little pupils. I am not in favour of starting
  colleges."
- **Dogma blocks even physical Pramātrikona.** "What to speak of spiritual
  Pramātrikona, even physical Pramātrikona is not possible where there is dogma."
  "Religion means dogma, communism means dogma, capitalism means dogma – all the
  political 'isms' are expressions of dogma."
- **The tug-of-war of the trikonas.** When physical, psychic and spiritual
  Pramātrikonas do not coincide, "there would be a tug-of-war amongst them and finally
  they will get deranged… they will reach the stage of disruption." "When they
  coincide, the triangles of forces come in close proximity of Guńatrikona."
- **The controlling philosophy.** "The guiding philosophy, the controlling philosophy
  should be: 'This universe is ours' – and 'we' means humans, animals and plants."

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1987) — Prama 1: the guṇātrikona and the three attainments

*Prama – 1 / Dynamic Equilibrium and Equipoise* (nh-2-10, Feb 1987, Calcutta) is the
doctrine's fullest statement — etymology, the triangle of forces, the derangement
ladder, and the socio-economic application:

- **The guṇātrikona.** *"Triguṇātmikā sṛṣṭimātṛkā aśeṣātrikona dhārā"* — "In the
  primordial phase of creation, the three attributional principles [of the Cosmic
  Operative Principle] evolve countless geometric figures which all eventually get
  transformed into triangles of forces (guṇātrikona)." "Whenever there are more than
  three forces operating, the tendency is invariably to form a stable triangular
  figure." When balance is lost "due to the pressure of the static Prakṛti," creation
  begins "through one of its vertices: there ensues in the flow of creation a
  lokatrikona. Human existence is trifarious – physical, psychic and spiritual. These
  three aspects give rise to pramātrikona or lokatrikona in individual entities as well
  as in the collective body."
- **Etymology — pra + mā + ḍa/ṭa.** "The word pramā is derived as the root mā prefixed
  by pra and suffixed by ḍa and ṭa. The meaning of the root verb mā is 'to measure', 'to
  fathom'. So the etymological meaning of pramā is 'balance'." "Equilibrium" is "balance
  in physical strength" (the tug-of-war example) and "equipoise" is "balance in weight"
  (the kilo-of-brinjal scale); "Equilibrium and equipoise are collectively known as
  pramā."
- **The derangement → disruption → degeneration ladder.** In subsequent stages "due to
  the influence of time, space and person and the clash of propensities, the balance of
  lokatrikona or pramātrikona gets lost. This state of loss of balance… is called 'the
  stage of derangement'." If the lost pramā is not re-established, "the lokatrikona
  degenerates and enters the stage of disruption"; failure to counteract the downward
  trend carries it "to the stage of degeneration." (nh-2-12 names the last stage
  *Viparyasta Avasthā*.) "Human society today has reached the stage of degeneration."
- **The three attainments.** **Pramā saṃvṛddhi** — "When the balanced state of material
  development, having reached a supreme height, maintains proper adjustment with the
  psychic and spiritual elevation of individuals and the collectivity." **Pramā ṛddhi** —
  "when the balanced psychic stratum attains the peak of progress… the ectoplasmic stuff
  of the mind gets powdered down… moves forward towards the pinnacled intellect
  (agryābuddhi)." **Pramā siddhi** — "a state in which the mind, having transcended the
  psycho-spiritual stratum, attains a pinnacled state and absolute equilibrium in
  spiritual progress." When all lokatrikonas coincide, "its central point will coincide
  in a perfect balance with the central point of the supreme guṇātrikona."
- **The socio-economic application.** The Rāṛh/Orissa case: despite "enormous natural
  resources," "its people are undernourished, half naked, poorly accommodated, illiterate
  and ridden with disease" — lack of pramā-based planning. Restoration requires four
  factors (physical demand present and foreseeable; physical supply present and
  foreseeable; maximum utilization of land; the Five Fundamental Principles of PROUT as
  applied to the physical stratum) — and "Agriculture should be conducted on the basis of
  the principle of consumption, and not the principle of profit."

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (Prama – 3): Buddha's Nivṛtti path and the flower-image

*Prama – 3* (nh-2-12, date not known) applies the trikona to Buddha's path:

- **Buddha's path was Nivṛtti Mārga at its peak.** "The Path of Gaotama Buddha was the
  Path of Abnegation (Nivṛtti Mārga). To speak the truth, in Nivṛtti Mārga he was at
  the peak." Yet "there was no perfect adjustment between Saṃvṛtti and Nivṛtti, that
  leads to perfect balance in the spiritual sphere"; Varddhamāna Mahāvīra "considerably
  deviated from the part of reality."
- **The trifarious flower-image.** "The physical stratum may be compared to a sweet and
  lovely radiant bud which is about to blossom into a flower"; "The psychic stratum can
  be compared to a newly blown flower full of charm"; "the third factor, that is, the
  spirituality, is simultaneously the witnessing entity, the faculty of witnessing and
  the guiding entity. All the strata together create the Pramātrikona or Lokatrikona of
  entities."
- **The lokatrikona's origin.** "In the flow of emanation starting from the noumenal
  cause of Parama Tattva emerges the Lokatrikona… based on Pramā." Human beings "strike
  blow after blow at the triangle of forces" — derangement, then disruption, and finally
  "the stage of degeneration (Viparyasta Avasthā)" when "existence itself becomes a big
  burden."
- **Buddha's one-sidedness.** The Eightfold Path — "Samyak Darshana… Samyak Samādhi" —
  because "these teachings do not include Saṃvṛti (material acquisition) they lack charm
  and do not give a good reason for living." "Buddha wanted to carry humankind on the
  singular path of Nivṛtti. Had he prescribed an adjustment of spirituality with worldly
  (social, economic, etc.) activities, there would have come into being a beautiful
  Lokatrikona, a Mahātrikona advancing through equilibrium nurtured by Pramā."

# Notes — fidelity

- "Dynamic equilibrium" is an **interpretive synthesis** of the surrounding text;
  the discourses say "equilibrium and equipoise" against a backdrop of universal
  dynamicity ("nothing is static"). Discourse 18/3 frames pramā narrowly as
  *psychic* equilibrium; 18/1 extends it across all three spheres — both preserved.
- Many corpus hits for "prama" are **false positives** (*pramatta/pramāda* =
  carelessness; *pramāṇa* = proof/measure) and were excluded; only the genuine
  equilibrium discourses are cited.
