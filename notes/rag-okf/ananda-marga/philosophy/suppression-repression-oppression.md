# Definition

**Suppression, repression and oppression** are "the three psychic calamities" that
the human beings of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries have undergone — the
"triangle of suppression, repression and oppression" inflicted especially by
**capitalism and communism**. In Saṃskṛta: suppression = *pradamana*, repression =
*avadamana*, oppression = *damana*. (1989, PiaN 17/6.)

# Explanation

- **Suppression (*pradamana*)** — "the mind is prevented from expanding and all its
  outlets are closed, sealed and blocked." The mind's nature is to expand; even if a
  force blocks it, "still the mind must try to expand."
- **Repression (*avadamana*)** — occurs "whenever you are faced with trouble, whenever
  you want to express your feelings, or whenever you want to be in an open atmosphere":
  women prevented from sports in Islamic countries, people sent to concentration camps
  for criticising communism, punishment for free singing and dancing. "Repression
  directly affects the subconscious mind" — the psychic structure is severely damaged
  and finally changed, producing "a defeatist psychology and an inferiority complex"
  (the untouchable boy who would not fetch water: "How can you drink water touched by
  me?").
- **Oppression (*damana*)** — "when you are opposed and punished for what you have done
  or for what you want to do, and as a result your desire to do something worthwhile
  dies forever."
- **The triangle.** "In communist states, you will find the triangle of suppression,
  repression and oppression in full force. Communism is based on these three defects…
  amongst these three oppression has occurred the most, repression next and suppression
  the least."
- **Communism critique.** Capitalism makes people slaves; communism "makes them worse
  than slaves" — "the human mind is transformed into matter," because "in communism
  human values do not exist." Communism is "a collection of contradictions" (supports
  democracy outside, practices dictatorship inside; talks of classlessness, practises
  party rule by an elite; the Tevahagā/Bargadār duplicity). The slogan contrast: in
  communism "From each according to his capacity, to each according to his necessity";
  in PROUT's cooperative system "Each will get according to his or her capacity."
- **Sarkar's history claim:** PROUT (1959) "would be the death-knell of communism";
  communism "collapsed in 1989… died a premature death by at least 30 years." Marx "was
  a good man" but his theory "was not practical"; Lenin and Mao became frustrated, and
  "Stalin was a demon who killed millions."
- **The Rāmāyaṇa as an example of oppression:** Sarkar reads Rāma as "the apostle of
  capitalism and Aryan supremacy" who killed Sudroka out of Aryan supremacy and was
  therefore "not Parama Puruṣa" (not *Maryādā Puruṣottama*), while Rāvaṇa's conduct
  (separate quarters for Sītā with women attendants) shows he "was not demonic."
- **The way out:** "Now is the time for the emergence of the third psychic force where
  there will be no more suppression, repression and oppression. Let the mind develop
  according to its longings; let the mind be free. Let there be a human society of
  coordination and cooperation" — the crimson rays of PROUT.

# In *Neohumanism in a Nutshell* (1978) — the earlier triad

*The Physical, Psychic and Spiritual Strata* (nh1-2, 12 December 1978) gives the
**earlier enumeration**, eleven years before the PiaN 17/6 taxonomy, in the physical-
sphere argument for PROUT:

- **The triad.** "This exploitation has been perpetrated mainly in three ways: oppression,
  suppression and exploitation. I have not mentioned repression, because repression is
  inseparably associated with exploitation."
- **Context.** The three ways belong to the crude physical sphere: PROUT was formulated
  "with the sole purpose of removing physical misery and affliction"; mean opportunists
  would have continued "their exploitation, taking advantage of the simplicity or
  intellectual deficiency of the innocent and credulous masses."
- **Enumeration difference.** The 1978 triad names *oppression* and *suppression* as two of
  the three ways (with *exploitation* as the third), and keeps *repression* out of the list;
  the 1989 PiaN 17/6 discourse names all three as "the three psychic calamities" with the
  Saṃskṛta terms *pradamana* (suppression), *avadamana* (repression), *damana* (oppression).
  Keep the two enumerations distinct.

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** the three-term taxonomy with the Sanskrit names (*pradamana,
  avadamana, damana*) is Sarkar's own; "communism makes them worse than slaves";
  "the human mind is transformed into matter."
- **The Rāmāyaṇa reading** (Rāma = Aryan supremacy, Rāvaṇa not demonic) is Sarkar's
  **revisionist interpretation**, presented as his claim — reproduce as his framing,
  NOT as textual fact (cf. the purāṇa-as-fiction note in [Four Vargas](/prout/four-vargas.md)).
- **Sarkar's historical claims** (communism's collapse in 1989, PROUT as its
  death-knell, the Stalin/Lenin/Mao assessment) are his own period statements — keep
  dated, do not generalise.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** a full enumeration of the eight pāshas (not
  in this chapter).
- **Two dated enumerations.** The 1978 NHN triad (oppression, suppression, exploitation —
  with repression excluded) is a distinct, earlier enumeration from the 1989 PiaN 17/6
  taxonomy (pradamana/avadamana/damana). Both are kept verbatim and dated; do not merge
  them into one list or harmonise the Saṃskṛta names across the two.
