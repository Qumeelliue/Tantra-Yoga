# Definition

**Mahāpuruṣa** — *"elevated psychic being"* — is Sarkar's name for the genuinely
great: individuals far advanced in the process of [pratisaiñcara](/cosmology/pratisaincara.md)
who "set the ideal for humanity." They are the result of **gradual elevation**, not
of a divine descent; the **avatāra** theory (*avatāravāda*) that calls them
incarnations of God is, in Sarkar's words, "an illogical hypothesis" and a
"misleading misnomer."

# Explanation

- **Avatāra means "derivation," not descent.** *Ava + tr* = "descend," "derive" or
  "degenerate." Since the whole universe is created out of and by Brahma, *every
  entity* is His "incarnation"; singling out a few beings as direct descents is
  logically inconsistent — it leaves the rest of creation's origin unexplained.
- **Elevation is gradual, never abrupt.** Every stage of the elevated Mahāpuruṣa is
  "the result of saincara and then pratisaincara" — a gradual elevation, not "an
  abrupt descent or occurrence." If any unit consciousness is an incarnation, then
  *all* are; there is no special class of incarnated beings.
- **The social critique.** Behind the "superstitious belief" of avatāravāda Sarkar
  locates "a class of vested interests": intellectual parasites who lived on the
  labour of others curbed rational reaction by enforcing commands "with so-called
  divine force," called dissenters reactionaries and atheists, and even manipulated
  the writings of great philosophers — "Corruption was perpetrated in the name of
  God."
- **Mahāpuruṣas as the rightful objects of homage.** Such elevated beings desire no
  "crude earthly flowers and offerings"; unto their feet one may offer "the flowers
  of devotion and aspiration."

## Relationships

- the status reached at the end of [pratisaiñcara](/cosmology/pratisaincara.md) — cf. [Mokṣa](/practice/moksa.md), [Mahattattva](/biopsychology/mahattattva.md)
- **distinct from** the [Mahāsambhūti](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md) — Mahāpuruṣas are the *elevated units* of creation, whereas Mahāsambhūti is Parama Puruṣa Himself taking a body (the two are contrasted in the same chapter); do not collapse them
- opposed to the graded *avatāra* schema rejected in [Tāraka Brahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md) and [Krṣṇa](/people/krsna.md) — the prose refuses "avatāra" for Shiva and Krṣṇa
- the devotional homage to such beings feeds [Bhakti](/practice/bhakti.md)

# Notes — fidelity

- **The rejection of avatāravāda is direct teaching** (II-6): "Incarnation is an
  illogical hypothesis"; "the whole theory of incarnation or avatāravāda is,
  therefore, not convincing and rational." Do not soften it into a polite
  reformulation.
- **Fidelity tension preserved:** elsewhere in the bundle [Krṣṇa](/people/krsna.md)
  and [Tāraka Brahma](/cosmology/tarakabrahma.md) are called Mahāsambhūti (Parama
  Puruṣa embodied), while this chapter stresses that *no unit* is a "descent." The
  Mahāsambhūti's "body from the five fundamental factors" is Sarkar's way of
  reconciling the two; keep both framings visible rather than smoothing them.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** a fixed list or count of historical
  Mahāpuruṣas; the chapter names the class generally, not specific instances.
