# Definition

For Sarkar, **"struggle is the essence of life"** (SS 11): "every movement involves
opposition, and the resultant achieved during the fight against the obstacles is
called progress … Those who are averse to struggle will never make any progress."
The same teaching opens *Taking the Opposite Stance in Battle* (SS 10): "life means
struggle — without struggle there can be no life … Hence the very essence of life is
struggle. Life without struggle means death." Progress in every sphere — physical,
psychic, spiritual — "takes place through **clash and cohesion**" (AV 10); the
friction itself Sarkar names **saṃgharṣa** (SS 7).

# Explanation

**Struggle as the engine of evolution.** In *Struggle and Progress* (SS 7) Sarkar
traces creation's whole cycle through friction: "To describe this clash I have used
the term saṃgharṣa (friction)." As the fundamental factors crudify, internal clash
intensifies; that clash converts matter into mind and drives unit minds upward along
[pratisaiñcara](/cosmology/pratisaincara.md) — the introversial movement from crude
to subtle, toward the Macrocosmic Nucleus.

**Three strata of clash.** Sarkar distinguishes *physical clash* (the body following
[saiñcara](/cosmology/saincara.md) while the mind follows pratisaiñcara), which
develops the lower koṣas; *psychic clash* ("to produce friction or commotion in
others' minds"), which tills the psychic field and breaks "blind attachment to old
worn-out ideas"; and the *attraction of the Great*, the spiritual pull without which
"even the awakening of hiraṇmaya koṣa is not possible." Struggle against dogma —
outmoded custom, ritual, "old systems and ideas" — is explicitly a form of psychic
clash.

**Taking the opposite stance.** *SS 10* generalises a strategic principle
"applicable to all spheres — physical, psychic and spiritual": one moves *against*
the opposing force. Psychically this means conquering anger by patience, greed by
generosity, falsehood by truth.

**Obstacles as friends.** *Yatamāna* (AV 10) frames perseverance through the boatman
rowing **upstream** — "struggling against the current" — versus the effortless
downstream drift. Sarkar declares: "Obstacles need to come and are desirable … If
you struggle against obstacles, you will stand to gain and will never lose. That is
why obstacles are to be considered as friends," and one must "struggle fiercely
against such staticity" (tāmasika vṛttis). His framing is welcoming, not nihilistic:
clash sharpens the intellect and accelerates progress.

# In the *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1957–1979) — power through clash, the artist's resistance, and youth

The *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* add three framings:

- **"It is through clashes that power finds expression."** (DNE 2, *The Practice
  of Art and Literature*, 14 Feb 1957.) "In a life which is averse to fight,
  where there is little urge to fight, the expression of life also remains vague
  and indistinct. Human intellect is awakened through natural, social, psychic
  and economic struggles. Those who desire to awaken their intellects should not
  be afraid of struggle." The artist must "resist the surging current of
  destruction single-handedly," keeping in mind the refrain *"Yadi tor dāk
  shune keu nā āse; Tave ekla cala re ..."* ("If none to my call pays heed, Then
  alone must I proceed"). "Their pens may break into pieces ... yet their efforts
  must continue unceasingly. Each of their petty defeats shall be strung together
  as pearls in the garland of victory" (cf. [Sāhitya](/philosophy/sahitya.md),
  [Aesthetic Science](/philosophy/aesthetic-science.md)).
- **The microcosm grows "through clash and cohesion."** (DNE 13, *The
  Ever-Expanding Domain of the Microcosm*, 16 Jun 1979.) "Through clash and
  cohesion the area of microcosmic mind gradually began to develop and the human
  body as the vehicle of microcosm also gradually became very complex. ... all
  these fundamental developments in microcosm occurred through sheer clash and
  cohesion" (cf. [Pratisaiñcara](/cosmology/pratisaincara.md)).
- **Youth as the vanguard of change.** (DNE 6, *Talks on Education – Excerpt G*,
  date not known.) "The nature of life is to change and to grow. Where there is
  no change, it is just like the condition of death." "It is the youth, and the
  youth only, who have the vigour to bring about the necessary change. It is the
  youth who have the resilience to change and adapt and implement the vision of a
  new society" (cf. [Social Change](/prout/social-change.md)).

# In *Varna Vijinana* (1983) — the Buddha's "body may perish" determination

*Varna Vijinana* 4 (*Phonetics – 4*, 10 July 1983, Calcutta) preserves the
Buddha's resolve-shloka from "the time of the Buddha, twenty-five hundred years
ago":

> Ihásane shuśyatu me shariiram
> Tvagasthimáḿsaḿ pralayaiṋca yátu
> Aprápyabodhiḿ bahukalpadurlabháḿ
> Naevásanát káyamatashcalisyate

The verse states the utter determination struggle may demand of the aspirant:
let the body — skin (*tvag*), bone (*asthi*), flesh (*māṃsa*) — wither away,
yet one will not move from the seat until *bodhi*, "which is difficult to attain
even in many kalpas," is won. It is struggle carried to the point where the
physical frame itself is expendable — the same resolve as the boatman rowing
upstream against the current (AV 10).

## Relationships

- "struggle is the essence of life" is taught in the context of [Tantra](/practice/tantra.md), whose sādhanā is "an endless effort to move towards Puruṣottama" against all obstacles (SS 11)
- *DNE 2*: "It is through clashes that power finds expression"; the artist must "resist the surging current of destruction single-handedly" — "Yadi tor dāk shune keu nā āse; Tave ekla cala re" (cf. [Sāhitya](/philosophy/sahitya.md), [Aesthetic Science](/philosophy/aesthetic-science.md))
- *DNE 13*: the growth of the microcosm occurred "through clash and cohesion" (cf. [Pratisaiñcara](/cosmology/pratisaincara.md))
- *DNE 6*: "It is the youth, and the youth only, who have the vigour to bring about the necessary change"; "Where there is no change, it is just like the condition of death" (cf. [Social Change](/prout/social-change.md))
- the upward, introversial movement struggle serves is [pratisaiñcara](/cosmology/pratisaincara.md) (crude→subtle, toward the Source), opposed to the extroversial [saiñcara](/cosmology/saincara.md)
- the chief force struggled against — staticity (*tamoguṇa*) — is described in [guṇa](/cosmology/guna.md); the obstacles on the path are detailed in [obstacles](/practice/obstacles.md)
- struggle is carried out through [sādhana](/practice/sadhana.md), culminating in the "attraction of the Great"
- struggle against dogma and outmoded custom links to the revolt-against-dogma spirit of [Neohumanism](/philosophy/neohumanism.md)
- collective clash drives [social change](/prout/social-change.md) (group polarisation, "the clash between the two groups")
- *VV 4*: the Buddha's resolve — "Tvagasthimáḿsaḿ pralayaiṋca yátu / … Naevásanát káyamatashcalisyate" (let skin, bone and flesh perish; the body will not move from the seat) — the "body may perish" determination (cf. [Bodhi](/biopsychology/bodhi.md))

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** "struggle is the essence of life" / "the very essence of life
  is struggle" is verbatim in BOTH SS 11 and SS 10; the clash/cohesion → progress
  doctrine is direct teaching across all four sources. *Saṃgharṣa* (friction) is
  Sarkar's own term (SS 7).
- **Welcoming, not violent:** SS 11 condemns those "who consider struggle to be
  violence" as hypocrites only for denying the universal fact of struggle — NOT as an
  endorsement of aggression. Keep this framing.
- **Could not verify — pratisrota / anusrota:** these terms do NOT appear anywhere in
  the ingested corpus (grep returned zero). The "against the current" image is present
  only as the boatman rowing upstream (AV 10), not under that label. The
  conceptually-equivalent verified pairs are **pratisaiñcara vs saiñcara**
  (introversial crude→subtle vs extroversial subtle→crude) and **vidyā vs avidyā**
  (centripetal vs centrifugal). Hence the title is "Struggle (Clash & Cohesion)," not
  "Struggle / Pratisrota," and the Saṃskṛta *srota* terms are not attributed to Sarkar.
- **Could not verify — ripus/pāshas** as named targets of struggle were not found in
  these four discourses (they name "static propensities / tāmasika vṛttis," "blind
  attachment to old ideas," and generic "enemies"). See [obstacles](/practice/obstacles.md)
  for the ripu/pāsha framing.
- **VV 4 (1983) — the Buddha's resolve-shloka** is cited verbatim in the source
  (transliteration as printed); the verse's application to the struggle-doctrine
  (the "body may perish" determination) is the reading made here — flagged as
  interpretation of the shloka's import, while the verse itself is verbatim.
