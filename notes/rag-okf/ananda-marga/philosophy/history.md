# Definition

**"True history should be a faithful record of the entire human life."** Its standard:
history must be "rewritten in the interest of humanity and the universal well-being of
the human race," reflecting "the weal and the woe, the hopes and aspirations of the
masses" — not the chronicles of kings and vested interests. The etymological definition
is *"Iti hasati ityarthe itihāsah"*: "history is a resplendent reflection of collective
life." "Human beings should not be compared to individual flowers, but to an entire
flower garden." (28 February 1980, DNE 15.)

# Explanation

- **Etymology — Iti hasati.** "The recognized definition of history is, *Iti hasati
  ityarthe itihāsah*… that is, history is a resplendent reflection of collective life,
  whose study will be of immense inspiration for future generations. 'Iti hasati'
  literally means, 'the glowing example of glorious human dignity.'"
- **Against king-centred chronicles.** "The history written so far is a history of kings
  and monarchs. It should be thoroughly overhauled and completely rewritten." If the
  annals "merely describe who succeeded to the throne and when, who plundered the
  neighbouring countries or kingdom, and who became a minister, they will be of no
  importance whatsoever, nor, indeed, of any interest to the common masses."
- **What genuine history must record.** "which communities brought about which amount of
  progress and prosperity in which area of social life and in which part of the world";
  "the trials and tribulations which confronted human beings, how those trials and
  tribulations were overcome." It "should focus upon the prevalent educational system,
  cultural standards, thoughts and ideas, dress, the social condition of women, the role
  of women in the social and economic spheres, and the socio-economic conditions of the
  weak and backward communities."
- **Biased history injects dogma.** "In most cases history is written in the interest of
  a particular vested interest." Per era: "the heroic tales of the chivalry of the
  Kṣatriyas era," "the history of the Vipra era is full of praises for the glorious
  deeds of the Vipra leaders, and the history of the Vaeshya era contains an abundance
  of stories about the glories of the Vaeshyas." Such "so-called history injects some
  kind of dogma in the readers mind, and consequently chokes the development of a
  spontaneous human intellect."
- **The whole garden, not single flowers.** "A flower garden is made up of innumerable
  varieties of flowers, each with its own fragrance and colours. Human history is like
  this: the beauty of history lies in its kaleidoscopic variations in spatial, temporal
  and personal factors."
- **One dharma for all.** The question of separate histories for householders and
  sannyasins is answered with an emphatic "NO": "the quadri-dimensional manava dharma
  (human dharma) is one and the same. It includes: *vistara* (expansion), *rasa* (flow),
  *sevā* (service) and *mokṣa* (spiritual emancipation)." The only difference:
  "householders have to care for two families whereas the renunciates have to care for
  only one" — the smaller family and the "larger human family, that is, the entire
  universe."
- **The task of the present.** "You should write a new history of the human race by your
  collective endeavour… unequivocally reflect such important factors as how human society
  has evolved through trials and tribulations."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** "true history should be a faithful record of the entire human
  life"; the *Iti hasati* etymology; "human beings should not be compared to individual
  flowers, but to an entire flower garden." Keep the shloka exact.
- **The quadri-dimensional mānava dharma** (*vistara/rasa/sevā/mokṣa*) is stated as one
  and the same for householders and sannyasins; preserved without harmonising to any
  other formulation of dharma.
- **Era-names kept as his:** Kṣatriya, Vipra, Vaeshya — his historical sociology, not
  generalised "classes."
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** a specific methodology for "rewriting
  history"; the two chapters give the standard and the target, not the procedure.
