# Definition

**Gurukul** is the ancient Indian educational unit in which students lived in the
guru's house — "students would go to the guru's house, at the age of five, and,
completing their studies by the age of twenty-four, would return home" — studying
under the curriculum of one pandit and supported "by begging from the public" rather
than by government. Its sizes are fixed by name: a *Pathshála* (~100 students), a
*Catuspáthii* (100–1000), a *Mahá-catuspáthii* / *Vidyapeetha* (1000–5000), and a
*Gurukul* (5000–500,000). "Is it proper to use the term 'University'? It has been
loosely translated in the vernacular as 'Vishvavidalaya'. This is a misnomer. We
already have a proper term for a university and it is 'Gurukul'." (DNE 18, 29
September 1990.)

# Explanation

- **Chátra — under the guru's canopy.** "'Chátra' means one who is under the control
  and jurisdiction (chátra) of a school of thought of one guru" — first applied in the
  Mahábhárata period to pupils staying under the canopy (*chatra*) of a pandit.
  "'Chátra' has now wrongly been used to mean any student." DNE 19 adds: "those who
  would sit under the chatra or umbrella of the kulapati were specially known as
  chatra"; primary-stage pupils who did not sit under the umbrella were *vidyarthi*.
- **The Ácárya teaches by exemplary conduct.** "The meaning of the word acárya is: one
  who imparts lessons to others by their exemplary conduct – Ácarańát páthayati yah
  sah ácaryah." Only then "will the education imparted by them be firmly established
  and properly assimilated by the pupils."
- **Kulapati strictly defined.** "Of those great scholars or Mahámahopádhyáyas, those
  who would provide ten thousand students of his institution with free food, clothes,
  education and accommodation were called Kulapati." Present chancellors do not feed and
  house ten thousand students, so calling them Kulapati is "defective use of
  terminology"; the proper terms are *Prajiṋa-dhiipaka* or *Vidyádhiipaka*.
- **Public help, not governmental help.** "Hence we see how dangerous it is for schools
  to depend completely on governmental aid. Educational institutions should depend on
  public help and not on governmental help." The Buddhist viharas (Nalanda,
  Vikramashila, Taksashila) were helped "only by the kings," so "within only one hundred
  years of the end of the Buddhist states, all the viharas in India ceased to exist."
- **One pandit, one small university.** "Each pandita set up his educational system and
  curriculum according to his wishes and his own teaching… every catuspathii was a small
  university in itself." The catuspáthiis "were not the flag bearers or proponents of
  any set dogma or ism."
- **Sanskrit as vehicle of culture.** "Sanskrit is the vehicle of cultural history of
  the entire India, Tibet and South East Asia. To go away from that culture means to
  lose a vital link and to destroy oneself."
- **The protective fence.** On the new gurukul: "So whenever you make something,
  establish a protective fence to protect it from the evil forces" — the gurukul "has
  already been established" and must be strengthened "with collective efforts."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** gurukul is the proper term for university; "Vishvavidalaya" is a
  misnomer; schools must depend on public, not governmental, help; chátra, ácárya and
  kulapati have strict technical meanings Sarkar restores.
- **"Vishvavidalaya"** is Sarkar's own transliteration (DNE 18) — keep it rather than
  normalizing to the standard *vishvavidyalaya*.
- **Could not verify:** the DNE 19 philology (Gárgaka = Garga + yujak) was not
  cross-checked against a separate source; the enrollment scale (Pathshála ~100 …
  Gurukul 500,000) is as stated in DNE 18, with Shri Amar Singh's cap of 500,000.
