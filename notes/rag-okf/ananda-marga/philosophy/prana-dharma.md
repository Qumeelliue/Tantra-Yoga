# Definition

**Prāṇa Dharma** is "the sine qua non of human existence": **internal discipline in
the psychic sphere of individual life** (the Prāṇa Dharma of individual life) and,
when national characteristics are expressed "in a particular vein," the **Prāṇa
Dharma of the nation** — its distinctive soul-character. (1967, AMPiN 4/8.)

# Explanation

- **Individual and national character.** People in similar environments, eras and
  cultures acquire group characteristics which, mixed within a society, develop
  national characteristics — "an entire national psychology." Differences between
  nations are "internal rather than external"; through proper education and integrated
  social living, internal qualities can be properly developed.
- **The nation's Prāṇa Dharma can be struck at.** Foreign rule, diplomacy, defective
  education, capitalism and communism all attack it:
  - **British diplomacy in India:** rather than attack directly, the British created
    "a group of native supporters who would remain Indian by birth and complexion but
    European in manners, customs, education, taste and culture" — an education system
    that made Indian youth "cynical, materialistic and atheistic," alienated from
    their own masses (the "neither Indian, nor civil, nor service" ICS).
  - **British opium in China:** cheap opium made the energetic, pious Chinese "idle and
    indolent," deviating them from their Prāṇa Dharma — the British were "responsible
    for the annihilation of the Chinese Prāṇa Dharma," which the communists then
    finished.
  - **Capitalism:** "although capitalism does not directly oppose Prāṇa Dharma, the
    all-devouring exploitation of capitalism robs the people of their possessions and
    drives them into the street as beggars," making it "impossible for the people to
    follow their Prāṇa Dharma."
  - **Communism:** materialism "can never be the base of human life"; communism is
    "diametrically opposed to Indian Prāṇa Dharma," letting "the human soul stand
    unrecognized."
- **Ananda Marga wants to establish the Prāṇa Dharma of the entire universe.** Its
  spiritual/social treatises, ethics, spiritual cult, socio-economic theory and
  education system all serve Prāṇa Dharma. Unlike the Vedāntic illusion-school, Ananda
  Marga "accepts this world as a relative truth and strives to cope with its problems"
  — hence the elimination of capitalism and the guarantee of minimum needs, so "every
  human being will get ample opportunity to follow Prāṇa Dharma."
- **Education and Prāṇa Dharma.** The Ananda Marga education system "is based on the
  principle of Prāṇa Dharma" — modern knowledge plus reverence, good manners, humility,
  dignity of labour and social consciousness — which the Western system "has miserably
  failed to inculcate."

# In *Discourses on Neohumanist Education* (1969; 1979) — the core definition and its defence

*Talks on Education – Excerpt B* (dne-11, 1969, Ranchi) gives the node's core
definition and the doctrine of enmity by deprivation; a later DNE chapter uses the
same words in a different sense (flagged below).

- **The core definition.** "The words práńa dharma mean the cardinal characteristic of
  a person which differentiates one person from another"; an entire race too inheres
  "some traits which distinguish that particular race from other." "The gradual
  development of internal discipline springs directly from the mode of living and
  education. This internal discipline is known as práńa dharma. To be more clear,
  when the vital expression of a race takes a particular course of manifestation, that
  course of manifestation is known as práńa dharma."
- **The best way of enmity is deprivation of práńa dharma.** "The best way of enmity
  against a person or a race is to deprive the person or the race of the freedom to
  cultivate their práńa dharma, and to prevent them from channelizing their potentiality
  accordingly" — the bird-in-a-cage analogy: "The long confinement in the cage, which
  is against the práńa dharma of the bird, will deprive it of the capacity to fly."
- **India's subjective práńa dharma.** India's people are "basically subjective in
  their approach to life and the world… parabhimukhi from the very inception of
  childhood"; the ancient brahmacarya-gārhasthya-vānaprastha education made this "the
  práńa dharma of the people of India."
- **Capitalism, communism, and the British.** "Capitalism and communism are both
  ultravires to the práńa dharma of the people of the world." The British deprived
  India of its práńa dharma "by the English objective education system" — producing "a
  peculiar group of people who were neither Indian nor civilian nor serviceable"; in
  China they used opium to make the "laborious and dexterous as well as religious"
  people "inactive and indolent."
- **Curriculum and PROUT.** "Our Ananda Marga school curriculum is based on the práńa
  dharma of the people of India, and furthermore, it strengthens the people in their
  práńa dharma." "The different races will assimilate PROUT philosophy according to
  their práńa dharma. There is wide scope for adjustment." Grounded in *"Svadharme
  nidhanaḿ shreyah paradharma bhayabahah"* ("It is better to die while pursuing one's
  Dharma than to be led to catastrophe by following adharma").
- **⚠ DISTINCT SENSE — flag:** dne-22 (*Plants, Animals and Human Beings*, 15 Oct 1979)
  uses *Prāṇa Dharma* / *Jiivan Dharma* in a **different** sense: "There are some
  special common attributes of plants, animals and human beings which can be termed
  their Práńa Dharma or Jiivan Dharma… Inactivity, rest, the need for security,
  reproduction and death are common characteristics" — the life-attributes of all
  living beings, not the distinguishing soul-character of this node. Keep the two
  senses distinct.

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** Prāṇa Dharma = internal psychic discipline (individual) and the
  national soul-character; "wealth and poverty force people to digress from their
  original Prāṇa Dharma."
- **Sarkar's historical claims** (British diplomacy, the opium trade, the ICS
  anecdote, Chinese deviation → communism) are his own framing (1967) — keep dated,
  not as independent history.
- **Could not verify in the cited passage:** a formal curriculum of the "Prāṇa
  Dharma-based education" beyond the qualities named.
- **DNE 11 gives the core definition verbatim** (prāṇa dharma = "the cardinal
  characteristic of a person which differentiates one person from another") and the
  enmity-by-deprivation thesis; **DNE 22 uses "Prāṇa Dharma / Jiivan Dharma" in a
  DIFFERENT sense** (the common life-attributes of plants, animals and humans) — keep
  the two senses distinct, as flagged in the DNE sub-section.
