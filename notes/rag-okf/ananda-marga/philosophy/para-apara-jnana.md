# Definition

The pair **parā-jñāna / aparā-jñāna** is Sarkar's division of knowledge itself:
"Knowledge is of two types: *parájñāna*, or *áptajñāna* [transcendental] and
*aparájñāna*, or *práptajñāna* [non-transcendental]." Transcendental knowledge
"operates in the purely spiritual world, and derives its inspiration from the Cosmic
Centre, the Universal Nucleus… It is also called self-knowledge." Aparā-jñāna
"may be defined as the internal projection of the external physicality," while
parā-jñāna "is the internal projection of the internal or cosmic spirituality." The
same pair is named *paravidyā*/*aparavidyā* in the eastern-education discourses and
*prápta vákyá*/*ápta vákyá* in the epistemology discourse. (22 March 1982, DNE 7.)

# Explanation

The pair appears across the DNE corpus in several near-synonymous terminologies which
must be kept source-specific:

- **Parájñāna / áptajñāna vs. aparájñāna / práptajñāna** (DNE 7). The purest statement:
  transcendental knowledge "derives its inspiration from the Cosmic Centre, the
  Universal Nucleus. It has nothing whatever to do with worldly gain or loss,
  exploitation or administration"; it "is also called self-knowledge." Aparā-jñāna is
  "non-transcendental knowledge related to this material world" — and study-derived
  knowledge carries two defects: "defect due to ignorance" and "defect due to change in
  tempor [time]." "That which establishes one in the spiritual realm is transcendental
  knowledge. It is also called *ápta vákyá* in Sanskrit."
- **Paravidyā vs. aparavidyā** (DNE 10, DNE 11). In the eastern guru-house system "the
  child used to learn mainly *paravidyā* or spiritual knowledge and some *aparavidyā* or
  mundane knowledge from the Guru" — spiritual knowledge was primary. The contrast is
  framed civilisational: "The East is predominantly subjective in outlook, whereas the
  West has a mainly objective bent of mind"; the West's "clear and unilateral emphasis on
  mundane knowledge" is one-sided. Ananda Marga "believes in one universal society with
  one ideology and one cosmic goal."
- **Prápta vákyá vs. ápta vákyá** (DNE 39). "Whatever people learn from the external
  world — be it from a book of facts or a learned discourse, or any source of knowledge —
  is 'prápta vákyá' or 'relative knowledge'. It is sometimes correct, sometimes
  incorrect." The three sources of relative knowledge are "direct perception, inference
  and authority"; all are defective ("with the slightest defect in the knower, knowable
  and knowledge, real knowledge is not possible"). "When human beings, through
  psycho-spiritual practice, make their minds as expansive as the Comic Mind, they can
  receive instructions or directions directly from Him due to His proximity. The
  knowledge thus acquired is called 'ápta vákyá' or 'absolute knowledge'… Absolute
  knowledge is the direct message from God." Its mark of identity: "it must be universal,
  rational and psychological." "Whenever there is a conflict between the brain and the
  heart, intelligent people should respond to the call of the heart."
- **Aparā-jñāna vs. parā-jñāna as internal projection** (DNE 40). "Mundane knowledge may
  be defined as the internal projection of the external physicality. And Parā-jñāna or
  spiritual knowledge is the internal projection of the internal or cosmic spirituality."
  The shloka seals it:

  > *Átmajiṋáṋaḿ vidurjiṋáṋaḿ jiṋáṋányányáni yánitu;
  > Táni jiṋáṋávaḿásáni sárasyanaeva bodhanát.*

  "Excepting self-realization or Átma-jiṋáṇa – all other knowledge is of no value…
  Every object has two types of shadow: the umbra and penumbra… all mundane knowledge is
  only a shadow it will not help you recognize the real thing." And in the same breath
  the summation: "know all – know one" — "if you want to know all – know one. Then only
  everything will be known to you."

# Notes — fidelity

- **Direct teaching:** transcendental knowledge is self-knowledge "received directly from
  the Cosmic Mind"; all mundane knowledge is umbra/penumbra shadow; "know all – know
  one." Keep the shloka exact.
- **Several terminologies, one pair — do not conflate.** *parájñāna/áptajñāna*
  (DNE 7), *paravidyā/aparavidyā* (DNE 10–11), *prápta vákyá/ápta vákyá* (DNE 39),
  *aparā-jñāna/parā-jñāna* (DNE 40) are the same contrast in different vocabularies.
  The node maps them but preserves each source's terms.
- **Typo preserved:** "the Comic Mind" appears verbatim in DNE 39 for the Cosmic Mind;
  not silently corrected here.
- **Ágama has two senses — flagged.** In DNE 39 "ágaṋa" is "authoritative documents"
  (scripture as a source of relative knowledge, unreliable unless grounded in spiritual
  realization); in DNE 41 *Ágama* is the name of Shiva's replies (vs. Párvatii's
  questions, *Nigama*). These are different uses of the same word; do not merge them.
- **Could not verify in the cited passages:** an identification of áptajñāna with any
  particular scripture; Sarkar states the principle, not a canon.
